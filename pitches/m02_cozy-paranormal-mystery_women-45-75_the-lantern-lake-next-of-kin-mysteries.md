# The Lantern Lake Next-of-Kin Mysteries

**Genre:** Cozy paranormal mystery · **Readers:** Women 45–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Edie Farrow, 57, sees the shadows of close blood relatives lean toward each other. She traces heirs for a Finger Lakes court: one unclaimed estate and one suspicious death per book.

**The setting.** Harrow County, New York: Lantern Lake, hillside vineyards, a courthouse whose brass lamps are lit for every hearing. Six families still argue over who sold the canal lock land. Die without a will and the court must find blood kin, or the state inherits.

**The lead.** Edie is the court-appointed genealogist, paid from the estate: no heir, no fee. A foundling left on the courthouse steps in 1969, her shadow has leaned only toward her daughter; she has never searched, in case nobody was looking. Dry, exact, she talks to file boxes: "Ninety years quiet. Take your time."

**The cast.** Tess, 29, Edie's daughter, a genetic counselor who thinks her mother merely observant, until book 3; Lois Pavlik, 61, court clerk, who reads Edie's mail upside down; Ambrose Teague, 74, county historian, who swept the courthouse at 17; Stan Dombrowski, 63, dairy farmer and coroner, who brings Edie coffee to death scenes.

**The series engine.** Shadows lean only between parent and child or siblings, half or full. The sight gives one fact, kin or not, never how, and needs light and both people alive in the room. Each death looks accidental; several people gain money, land or silence. Proof is censuses, baptism books and deeds; consented DNA only confirms.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Kin by Porch Light* — Alma Vandermark, 81, falls downstairs; her supposed half-sister casts no lean toward Alma's brother Otto, because she is an actress hired by Otto's thieving guardian.
2. *Heirs at High Noon* — Swimmer Carl Hoffner, 58, drowns; Stan's shadow leans toward Carl's sister, making Stan a half-brother, heir and suspect, until Edie proves a neighbor forged a deed and drowned Carl.
3. *Deeds Under Glass* — Hollis Ostrander dies before willing his greenhouse to his gardener; his brother's shadow leans toward the young man, a half-brother owed half; Edie proves which baptism page the brother forged.
4. *Wills by Lamplight* — Midwife Agnes Pruyn dies in a blackout, one birth ledger missing; Edie finds whose entry was worth killing for, and another leads to a kitchen where her own shadow leans.

*Running thread:* who left a newborn at the courthouse; each book adds one fact (a monogrammed blanket, a cousin match, Ambrose's name on the 1969 report) and book 4 answers, giving Edie living kin.

**Why readers come back.** You see each shadow when Edie does. Every case closes: heir named, death explained, nobody harmed on the page. Edie tells family news privately first; its owner decides what the court hears. Thursdays, Lois and Edie split one pie slice and argue over the bigger half.

**What makes it different.** The gift is a question, never an answer. The detecting is real genealogy, by a researcher whose family tree begins on a courthouse step.
