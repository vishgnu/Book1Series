# Under Pilotage

**Genre:** Science fiction adventure (space) · **Readers:** Men and women 35–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete transit per book

**Logline.** Treaty pilot Maren Achterberg, 44, boards troubled ships and steers them 31 hours through a shifting ice lane to the Caird system's only fuel port. She holds the conn and nothing else.

**The setting.** Kettle Station melts ring ice into propellant. The one way in is the Passage, where the moonlet Hob drags a wave of ice, the Comb, across faster than tables update. By treaty no ship runs it without a pilot, boarded by cutter, who serves any flag and holds only the conn.

**The lead.** Maren, nineteen years a Passage pilot, once delivered the *Anna Reyes* on the minute to a seizure crew, as the rule required. Its master lost his license; she still answers his daughter's letters and wants that rule to be worth it. She reads ice better than people. A ladder fall cost her a knee. She calls the lane six hours ahead from forty minutes of radar returns; a near miss is "closer than I'd choose."

**The cast.** Ines Okonjo, 51, cutter coxswain, at the helm the day Maren fell, who jokes about everything except whether Maren blames her. Pilot Master Ruth Calloway, 63, who trained Maren and signed the Reyes seizure. Dev Pradhan, 23, apprentice, who wants beacons since a piloted ship killed his mother. Emil Sorensen, 39, inspector and failed pilot candidate, who wants Maren to say what she sees.

**The series engine.** Every book is one transit: one ship, 31 hours, a crew in trouble, a closing lane. Maren solves the navigation with burns, margins and timing, and settles the quarrel aboard only through heading, speed and arrival time.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Outer Marker* — The liner *Corazon Vale*, 2,100 aboard, a dying captain, first officer Lena Varga right to relieve him and wrong about when. Maren times the Hob window, nine minutes short of the tables, so the handover follows the last hard burn.
2. *Dead Freight* — The grain hauler *Tuck Hanley*, cargo shifted and yawing, hides eleven miners who saw tug burns near Hob. Maren spends margin so Sorensen's shift meets the ship, and tells no one.
3. *No Register* — A 300-meter warship with a blank transponder takes the one open lane, under Hob, where its radar records the moonlet being pushed. Maren refuses its captain a false pilot's log.
4. *Inner Marker* — The Service tender *Sable Wren* carries Calloway and the thruster packs. Maren tells Ines first, then refuses the conn until the push logs go to every flag, then runs the narrowest lane on record to bring Varga's ship in.

*Running thread:* who is nudging Hob, and why; book 4 answers that the Pilot Service has pushed it for forty years to keep the lane too erratic for one flag's beacon net.

**Why readers come back.** A full story every time, outer marker to the Kettle, no cliffhanger. Physics that holds, danger without cruelty, Ines at the ladder. After book 4 Hob stays erratic for decades, so the cutter keeps boarding, under open logs.

**What makes it different.** The hero commands nothing: she cannot arrest, order or take sides, only steer. Each book is a sealed-ship story told by the one neutral stranger aboard.
