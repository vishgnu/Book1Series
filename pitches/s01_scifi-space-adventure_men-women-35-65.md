# Under Pilotage

**Genre:** Science fiction adventure (space) · **Readers:** Men and women 35–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete transit per book

**Logline.** Treaty pilot Maren Achterberg, 44, boards troubled ships at the outer marker and steers them 31 hours through a shifting ice lane to the Caird system's only fuel port. She holds the conn and nothing else.

**The setting.** Kettle Station melts the ring ice of Morrow into propellant. The one way in is the Passage, a lane where the moonlet Hob drags a ripple of ice, the Comb, across faster than tables update. By treaty no ship runs it without a pilot, boarded by cutter. Pilots serve any flag and hold only the conn; the master stays liable.

**The lead.** Maren, nineteen years a Passage pilot, has never lost a ship. She once delivered one on the minute to a seizure crew, as the rule required, and wants that rule to be worth it. A boarding-ladder fall cost her a knee. She calls the lane six hours ahead from forty minutes of radar returns and talks in headings, numbers and dry understatement; a near miss is "closer than I'd choose."

**The cast.** Ines Okonjo, 51, cutter coxswain, at the helm the day Maren fell, who jokes about everything else. Pilot Master Ruth Calloway, 63, guardian of the Service's neutrality. Dev Pradhan, 23, an apprentice who thinks beacons would be safer than pilots. Emil Sorensen, 39, the port inspector who wants Maren to say what she sees.

**The series engine.** Every book is one transit: one ship, 31 hours, a crew in trouble, a closing lane. Maren solves the navigation with burns, margins and timing the reader can follow, and settles the quarrel aboard only through heading, speed and arrival time.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Outer Marker* — The liner *Corazon Vale*, 2,100 aboard, a dying captain, a first officer right to relieve him and wrong about when. Maren times the Hob window, nine minutes short of the tables, so the handover follows the last hard burn.
2. *Dead Freight* — The grain hauler *Tuck Hanley* hides eleven miners who saw tug burns near Hob. Maren's speed decides which inspector's shift meets the ship.
3. *No Register* — A 300-meter warship with a blank transponder wants fuel and silence. Maren's heading runs it close under Hob, where its own radar records the moonlet being pushed.
4. *Inner Marker* — The Service tender *Sable Wren* carries Calloway and the thruster packs. Maren refuses the conn until the push logs go to every flag, then runs the narrowest lane on record.

*Running thread:* who is nudging Hob, and why; book 4 answers that the Pilot Service has pushed it for forty years, keeping the lane too erratic for a beacon net one flag could own.

**Why readers come back.** A full story every time, outer marker to the Kettle, no cliffhanger. A professional good at a hard job, physics that holds, danger without cruelty. After book 4 the Passage stays open and the cutter keeps boarding.

**What makes it different.** The hero commands nothing: she cannot arrest, order or take sides, only steer. Each book is a sealed-ship story told by the one neutral stranger aboard, and the last puts her own neutrality on trial.
