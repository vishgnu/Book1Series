# The Hob Passage

**Genre:** Science fiction adventure (space) · **Readers:** Men and women 35–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete transit per book

**Logline.** Treaty pilot Maren Achterberg, 44, boards ships in trouble and steers each one down the Passage, 31 hours of moving ice no chart can track. She holds the conn and nothing else.

**The setting.** Kettle Station melts a gas giant's ice ring into propellant. The one gap in is the Passage. Hob, a two-kilometer rubble moonlet, orbits there, and its gravity drags a breaker of ice, the Comb, across the lane faster than tables update or beacons survive. By treaty every ship takes a pilot, who boards by cutter and serves any flag.

**The lead.** Maren calls the lane six hours ahead from forty minutes of radar. She could have docked the *Anna Reyes* late and let its master run; she arrived on the minute, as the rule required, to a waiting seizure crew, and he lost his license. She answers his daughter's letters and still seeks the transit that proves the rule right. A ladder fall cost her a knee; a near miss is "closer than I'd choose."

**The cast.** Ines Okonjo, 51, cutter coxswain, counts ladder rungs for her and jokes about everything except whether Maren blames her for the fall. Pilot Master Ruth Calloway, 63, trained Maren. Dev Pradhan, 23, apprentice, built the longest-lived beacon, nineteen days. Emil Sorensen, 39, inspector, rechecks the tables by hand and suspects they are rigged.

**The series engine.** Every book is one transit: one ship, a crew in trouble, a closing lane. Maren solves the navigation with burns, margins and timing, and settles the quarrel aboard only through heading, speed and arrival time.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Outer Marker* — On the liner *Corazon Vale*, its captain dying, Maren times the last burn so First Officer Varga takes command after it, then logs Hob moving nine minutes ahead of every table.
2. *Dead Freight* — Maren docks a yawing grain hauler two hours late, on purpose, so eleven hidden witnesses reach Sorensen.
3. *No Register* — Maren threads an unmarked warship under Hob; when its captain demands she erase the radar record of a tender beside the moonlet, she files it.
4. *Inner Marker* — Maren catches the tender pushing Hob, withholds the conn until Dev sends the logs to every flag, then brings the *Corazon Vale* home through the narrowest lane on record.

*Running thread:* who is nudging Hob, and are they right; book 4 answers that the Pilot Service has pushed it with small burns for forty years so no flag can own the lane.

**Why readers come back.** Each book ends at the dock and adds one fact about Hob: nine minutes early, a tender on radar, a name. Readers return for the next ship and for Ines counting rungs. After book 4 Hob stays erratic, so the cutter keeps boarding, under open logs.

**What makes it different.** The hero commands nothing: she cannot arrest, order or take sides, only steer. Each book is a sealed-ship story told by the one neutral stranger aboard.
