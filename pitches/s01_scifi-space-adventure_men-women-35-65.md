# The Hob Passage

**Genre:** Science fiction adventure (space) · **Readers:** Men and women 35–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete transit per book

**Logline.** Treaty pilot Maren Achterberg, 44, boards troubled ships and steers them 31 hours past the Comb, a breaker of ice no chart keeps up with. She holds the conn and nothing else.

**The setting.** Kettle Station sits inside a gas giant's ice ring, melting it into propellant. One gap leads in: the Passage. Hob, a two-kilometer rubble moonlet, orbits in the gap, and its gravity drags a white breaker of ice, the Comb, across the lane faster than tables update or beacons survive. By treaty no ship runs it without a pilot, boarded by cutter, who serves any flag and holds only the conn.

**The lead.** Maren calls the lane six hours ahead from forty minutes of radar returns. She once delivered the *Anna Reyes* on the minute to a seizure crew, as the rule required. Its master lost his license; she still answers his daughter's letters and wants that rule to be worth it. A ladder fall cost her a knee. A near miss is "closer than I'd choose."

**The cast.** Ines Okonjo, 51, cutter coxswain, who counts the ladder rungs for her and jokes about everything except whether Maren blames her for the fall. Pilot Master Ruth Calloway, 63, who trained Maren and signed the *Reyes* seizure. Dev Pradhan, 23, apprentice, who boards with her and built the longest-lived beacon, nineteen days. Emil Sorensen, 39, inspector and failed pilot candidate, who rechecks tables by hand.

**The series engine.** Every book is one transit: one ship, 31 hours at safe speed, a crew in trouble, a closing lane. Maren solves the navigation with burns, margins and timing, and settles the quarrel aboard only through heading, speed and arrival time.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Outer Marker* — On the liner *Corazon Vale*, its captain dying, Maren times the last hard burn so First Officer Varga takes command after it, then logs Hob nine minutes early.
2. *Dead Freight* — Maren nurses a yawing grain hauler through and docks two hours late, on purpose, so eleven hidden witnesses reach Sorensen.
3. *No Register* — Maren threads an unmarked warship under Hob and refuses to erase its radar record of a tender pushing the moonlet.
4. *Inner Marker* — Maren catches Calloway's tender pushing Hob, withholds the conn until Dev sends the logs to every flag, then brings Varga's *Corazon Vale* home through the narrowest lane on record.

*Running thread:* who is nudging Hob, and whether they are right to; book 4 answers that the Pilot Service has pushed it with small burns for forty years so no flag can own the lane.

**Why readers come back.** A full story every time, no cliffhanger, and one new piece of the Hob question. Physics that holds, danger without cruelty, Ines at the ladder. After book 4 Hob stays erratic, so the cutter keeps boarding, under open logs.

**What makes it different.** The hero commands nothing: she cannot arrest, order or take sides, only steer. Each book is a sealed-ship story told by the one neutral stranger aboard.
