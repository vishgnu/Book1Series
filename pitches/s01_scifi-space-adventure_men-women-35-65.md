# Under Pilotage

**Genre:** Science fiction adventure (space) · **Readers:** Men and women 35–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete transit per book

**Logline.** Maren Achterberg, 44, cannot arrest, order or take sides aboard the troubled ships she pilots through a shifting ice lane to the Caird system's only fuel port. For 31 hours she holds the conn, and one wrong heading kills everyone aboard.

**The setting.** Kettle Station melts ring ice into propellant in the rings of the gas giant Morrow. The one way in is the Passage, a lane through ice and shepherd moonlets that shifts by the hour: the moonlets' pull and the Kettle's own mining stir it faster than tables update. A hull that misses its window is holed in minutes. By treaty no ship runs it without a pilot, boarded by cutter at the outer marker. Pilots serve any flag and hold the conn, nothing else.

**The lead.** Maren Achterberg, 44, nineteen years a Passage pilot, has never lost a ship. She wants that record kept and her nephew Joost, 13, raised by someone who comes home. The work cost her a knee in a boarding-ladder fall. She calls the lane six hours ahead from forty minutes of radar returns and talks in headings, numbers and dry understatement; a near miss is "closer than I'd choose."

**The cast.** Ines Okonjo, 51, cutter coxswain, at the helm the day Maren fell, a subject neither raises. Pilot Master Ruth Calloway, 63, who guards the Service's neutrality. Dev Pradhan, 23, an apprentice with fast hands and no fear yet. Emil Sorensen, 39, the port inspector who can act where Maren cannot.

**The series engine.** Every book is one transit: one ship, 31 hours, a crew in trouble, a lane closing around them. Maren solves the navigation with burns, margins and timing the reader can follow, and touches the quarrel aboard only through heading, speed and arrival time. Every flag needs fuel, so another ship is always waiting.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Outer Marker* — The liner *Corazon Vale*, 2,100 aboard: a dying captain, a first officer right to want him relieved and wrong about when, and a window past the moonlet Hob nine minutes shorter than the tables say.
2. *Dead Freight* — The grain hauler *Tuck Hanley* hides eleven stowaways, ring-ice miners who saw tug burns near Hob.
3. *No Register* — A 300-meter warship with a blank transponder wants fuel and silence; its radar shows Maren that Hob is being pushed.
4. *Inner Marker* — The yacht *Sable Wren* carries consortium chair Corwin Hale, 58, and the surveyor who measured the pushes, held against her will; through the narrowest lane on record, Maren's arrival hour delivers the proof.

*Running thread:* who is nudging the shepherd moonlet Hob, and why; book 4 names a fuel consortium making the lane too erratic for human pilots, so every ship must rent its beacon net.

**Why readers come back.** A full story every time: board at the outer marker, tie up at the Kettle, no cliffhanger. A professional good at a hard job, physics that holds, danger without cruelty. After book 4 the Passage stays open and the cutter keeps boarding.

**What makes it different.** The hero commands nothing; she can only steer. Each book is a sealed-ship story told by the one neutral stranger aboard, whose single lever is where the ship is and when.
