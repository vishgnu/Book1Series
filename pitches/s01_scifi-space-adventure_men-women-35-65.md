# The Hob Passage

**Genre:** Science fiction adventure (space) · **Readers:** Men and women 35–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete transit per book

**Logline.** Treaty pilot Maren Achterberg, 44, boards ships in trouble and steers each one down the Passage, 31 hours of moving ice no chart can track.

**The setting.** Kettle Station melts a gas giant's ice ring into propellant. The one gap in is the Passage. Hob, a two-kilometer rubble moonlet, orbits there, and its gravity drags a breaker of ice, the Comb, across the lane faster than tables update or beacons survive. By treaty every ship takes a pilot, who boards by cutter and serves any flag.

**The lead.** Maren calls the lane six hours ahead from forty minutes of radar. She once docked the indebted *Anna Reyes* on the minute, as the rule required, into a waiting seizure crew; docking late would have saved its master's license. She answers his daughter's letters and still seeks the transit that proves the rule right. A ladder fall cost her a knee; a near miss is "closer than I'd choose."

**The cast.** Imani Okonjo, 51, cutter coxswain, counts ladder rungs for her and jokes about everything except whether Maren blames her for the fall. Pilot Master Vera Castellan, 63, trained Maren. Dev Pradhan, 23, apprentice, built the longest-lived beacon, nineteen days. Anders Sorensen, 39, inspector, rechecks the tables by hand and suspects they are rigged.

**The series engine.** Every book is one transit: one ship, a crew in trouble, a closing lane. Maren solves the navigation with burns, margins and timing, and settles the quarrel aboard only through heading, speed and arrival time.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Outer Marker* — With the liner *Corazon Vale*'s captain dying and the Comb closing nine minutes early, Maren times the last burn so First Officer Solano takes command with the ship clear.
2. *Dead Freight* — Maren nurses a yawing grain hauler past the Comb and docks two hours late, on purpose, the delay she refused the *Anna Reyes*, so eleven witnesses to table-rigging reach Sorensen.
3. *No Register* — Maren threads an unmarked warship under Hob; when its captain demands she erase the radar record of a tender beside the moonlet, she files it.
4. *Inner Marker* — Maren learns Castellan ordered the burns, withholds the conn, a pilot's one lever, until the logs reach every flag, then brings the *Corazon Vale* through the narrowest lane on record.

*Running thread:* who is nudging Hob, and are they right; book 4 answers that the Pilot Service has pushed it for forty years so no flag can own the lane, and Maren judges the aim right, the secrecy wrong.

**Why readers come back.** Each book ends at the dock and adds one fact about Hob: nine minutes early, a tender on radar, Castellan's name. Readers return for the next ship and for Imani counting rungs. After book 4 Hob stays erratic, so the cutter keeps boarding, under open logs.

**What makes it different.** The hero commands nothing: she cannot arrest, order or take sides, only steer. Each book is a sealed-ship story told by the one neutral stranger aboard.
