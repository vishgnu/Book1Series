# The Night Notary

**Genre:** Urban amateur-sleuth mystery · **Readers:** Women and men 40–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Gloria Ibarra, 47, a Los Angeles night notary, refuses a widower's 2 a.m. deed; by morning another notary has stamped it and he is dead. Her only legal power is to say no and write it down.

**The setting.** After 9 p.m.: loan papers on a Pacoima kitchen table, a power of attorney at a hospital bedside. A California notary gives no legal advice: she identifies the signer, journals the date and time, takes a thumbprint for deeds, and may refuse anyone confused or coerced.

**The lead.** Nineteen years a superior court clerk. Six years ago she notarized an impostor's deed on good ID and the Batistes lost their house; she keeps that journal page and has never faced them. A second bad stamp would cost her commission. Days belong to her father Ernesto, 79, a retired locksmith losing his memory. Dry, exact, polite: "Sir, I asked her, not you."

**The cast.** Ruth Adeyemi, 58, hospital night chaplain, who calls when a patient is rushed to sign and argues mercy over rules. Nadine Kovac, 52, whose signing service books Gloria's jobs and who talks her home at 3 a.m. Warren Tsai, 44, the district attorney's fraud investigator, who subpoenas her pages. Dale Purcell, 61, the escrow officer who closes her loans.

**The series engine.** Every case starts at a signing: a few people, one lying. Police have a medical examiner's ruling and no probable cause; Gloria's journal, recorded deeds and court files give Tsai grounds to open a file.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Witness My Hand* — The death is ruled a fall, but the son-in-law's alibi witness once signed Gloria's journal under another name; the son-in-law killed for the house.
2. *Sworn Before Me* — A jail inmate names a deed impostor, recants, then fatally overdoses; the recantation's journal entry falls during the jail count, and the impostor's brother carried in paper and pills.
3. *Penalty of Perjury* — At Ruth's call Gloria notarizes a dying widow's X before two witnesses; days later a deed carries a fluent signature, forged by the niece who withheld her medication.
4. *Satisfactory Evidence* — A deed with Gloria's seal, absent from her journal, evicts a family; Ernesto signs away his own house; her four journals show every bad ID shares one issue date.

*Running thread:* Who sends a second notary each time Gloria refuses. Nadine, Tsai and Purcell see every refusal; Tsai looks likeliest. Book 4: Nadine, whose failing service sold each refused signer to a notary who would not refuse; her book 1 invoice billed the refused deed as closed. Nadine is charged, and Gloria sits at the Batistes' table as they win a judgment.

**Why readers come back.** One mystery solved per book, every clue on a page the reader has already seen. Gloria wins by attention, not force.

**What makes it different.** No badge, no law license, no gun: a seal, a journal, and the power to say no.
