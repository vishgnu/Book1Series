# The Night Notary

**Genre:** Urban amateur-sleuth mystery · **Readers:** Women and men 40–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Gloria Ibarra, 47, a Los Angeles night notary, can only refuse a bad signing and write it down. In every book, that record is evidence.

**The setting.** Los Angeles County after 9 p.m.: loan papers on a Pacoima kitchen table, a power of attorney at a hospital bedside, a sworn declaration at the downtown jail. California's rule: a notary gives no legal advice. She identifies the signer, journals the act, takes a thumbprint for deeds and powers of attorney, and may refuse a signer who seems confused or coerced.

**The lead.** Gloria Ibarra, 47, clerked in superior court for nineteen years and now works nights from a Corolla. Six years ago she notarized a deed for an impostor with good ID, and the Batiste family lost their house; she keeps that journal page. Days belong to her father Ernesto, 79; she judges capacity at breakfast too. She catches a coached answer and says so: "Sir, I asked her, not you."

**The cast.** Ruth Adeyemi, 58, night chaplain at the county hospital, who calls when a patient is rushed to sign. Nadine Kovac, 52, the dispatcher who texts Gloria her jobs. Warren Tsai, a district attorney's fraud investigator who needs her clean for the stand. Helen Marsh, 66, who trained Gloria and keeps the county's surrendered notary journals.

**The series engine.** Gloria's stake is lawful: her journal line is evidence, and a bad record costs her $15,000 bond, her commission and her living. The clues are entries the reader has already seen: a name, a time, a thumbprint. She works only from journals and recorded deeds. Work comes nightly by Nadine's text and Ruth's call.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Witness My Hand* — A widower deeds away his house at 2 a.m. and dies by morning, ruled a fall. His witness signed Gloria's journal in March under another name, breaking the son-in-law's alibi; Tsai starts listening.
2. *Sworn Before Me* — A jailed witness names a deed impostor, recants, then swears he never signed. Gloria's entry proves he did; visitor logs against her times name the paralegal who reached him. Her commission draws a complaint.
3. *Under Penalty of Perjury* — Gloria refuses to license a handyman's bedside marriage to a dying widow; another notary arrives within the hour. Her entry from four nights before, the widow lucid, proves coercion. Strangers bring Ernesto papers.
4. *Personally Appeared* — The Okafors face eviction on a deed with Gloria's seal and no line in her journal. Suspended, she lays four journals side by side: every bad ID shares one issue date.

*Running thread:* Who runs the signing mill that manufactures signatures to take houses and sent Gloria an impostor six years ago. Book 4: Nadine, copying from surrendered journals Helen let her scan. Nadine is charged, the Batistes win a judgment, and Ruth calls again.

**Why readers come back.** One mystery solved per book, with every clue in ink where the reader can check it. A sleuth in her forties who wins by paying attention, not force.

**What makes it different.** Her only legal power is to say no and write it down. No badge, no law license, no gun: a seal, a journal, an ink pad.
