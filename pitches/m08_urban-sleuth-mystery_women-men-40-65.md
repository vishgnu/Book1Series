# The Night Notary

**Genre:** Urban amateur-sleuth mystery · **Readers:** Women and men 40–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Gloria Ibarra, 47, a Los Angeles night notary, refuses a widower's 2 a.m. deed; by morning another notary has stamped it and he is dead. Her only legal power is to say no and write it down.

**The setting.** After 9 p.m.: loan papers on a Pacoima kitchen table, a power of attorney at a hospital bedside. A California notary gives no legal advice: she identifies the signer, journals the act, takes a thumbprint for deeds and powers of attorney, and may refuse anyone confused or coerced. A print matches only people already booked; it never finds a stranger.

**The lead.** Nineteen years a superior court clerk. Six years ago she notarized an impostor's deed on good ID and the Batistes lost their house; she keeps that journal page and has never faced them. Days belong to her father Ernesto, 79, a retired locksmith losing his memory. Dry, exact, polite: "Sir, I asked her, not you."

**The cast.** Ruth Adeyemi, 58, hospital night chaplain, who calls when a patient is rushed to sign and argues mercy over rules. Nadine Kovac, 52, her dispatcher, who talks her home at 3 a.m. Warren Tsai, 44, a fraud investigator who copies her pages. Helen Marsh, 66, her trainer, now keeper of the county clerk's surrendered journals.

**The series engine.** Every case starts at a signing: a few people, one lying. Police call it a fall or a civil matter. Anyone can subpoena the journal; only Gloria was in the room. A bad stamp costs her commission, and the surety that pays her $15,000 bond pursues her.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Witness My Hand* — The death is ruled a fall. Daughter, son-in-law and caregiver sat at the table. The caregiver signed Gloria's journal in March as a witness under another name; the son-in-law's alibi goes with her.
2. *Sworn Before Me* — A jail inmate swears a declaration naming a deed impostor, recants, then fatally overdoses. Three visitors came between. The recantation's notary line is timed during the jail count; the visitor log names the paralegal who carried it.
3. *Under Penalty of Perjury* — Over Ruth's plea, Gloria refuses a handyman and a dying widow a confidential marriage license; another notary obliges; the widow dies. Handyman, niece and caregiver gain. Four nights earlier the widow signed Gloria's journal left-handed; the license is signed right-handed. Ernesto signs for a stranger.
4. *Personally Appeared* — The Okafors face eviction on a deed bearing Gloria's seal but absent from her journal; a second deed names Ernesto's house. Suspended, she lays four journals out: every bad ID shares one issue date.

*Running thread:* Who runs the signing mill that sent Gloria her impostor. Tsai looks likeliest. Book 4: Nadine, paying her son's debts with journals Helen let her scan. Nadine is charged, Helen resigns, and Gloria sits at the Batistes' table as they win a judgment.

**Why readers come back.** One mystery solved per book, every clue in ink. A sleuth who wins by attention, not force.

**What makes it different.** No badge, no law license, no gun: a seal, a journal, and the power to say no.
