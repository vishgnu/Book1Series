# The Night Notary

**Genre:** Urban amateur-sleuth mystery · **Readers:** Women and men 40–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Gloria Ibarra, 47, a mobile notary working nights across Los Angeles County, may only check ID, watch the pen and write it down. In each book one signing goes wrong, and her journal is the evidence.

**The setting.** Los Angeles County after 9 p.m.: loan papers on a Pacoima kitchen table, a power of attorney at a hospital bedside, a sworn declaration at the downtown jail. California's rule: a notary gives no legal advice. She identifies the signer, journals the act, takes a thumbprint for deeds and powers of attorney, and may refuse a signer who seems confused or coerced.

**The lead.** Gloria Ibarra, 47, clerked in superior court for nineteen years and now works nights from a Corolla. She wants one six-year-old signing put right. Days belong to her father Ernesto, 79, whose memory is slipping. She catches a wrong date or a coached answer and says so like a court record: dry, exact, polite.

**The cast.** Ruth Adeyemi, 58, night chaplain at the county hospital, who calls when a patient is rushed to sign. Lonnie Pruitt, 61, a bail-bonds clerk who owes Gloria favors. Mateo, 19, her nephew, who scans her journals. Warren Tsai, a sheriff's fraud detective who wishes she would go home.

**The series engine.** Every book opens with a wrong signing and closes that case. Gloria's stake is lawful: her journal line is evidence, and her commission and $15,000 bond are exposed. The clues are entries the reader has already seen: a name, a time, a thumbprint. Los Angeles signs papers nightly, so calls keep coming.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Witness My Hand* — A widower deeds away his house at 2 a.m. and dies by morning; Gloria's time entry breaks an alibi, and Tsai starts listening.
2. *Sworn Before Me* — A jailed witness recants, then swears he never signed; her journal settles it, and Lonnie's ledger names the company paying her fee.
3. *Under Penalty of Perjury* — Gloria refuses a dying widow's bedside marriage license; another notary issues it, and proving coercion ties him to that company.
4. *Personally Appeared* — A deed carries Gloria's seal but has no line in her journal; the cloned stamp leads to the probate supervisor who trained her.

*Running thread:* Who runs the signing mill that sent an impostor to Gloria six years ago and took the Batiste family's house; answered in book 4, its clients still unnamed.

**Why readers come back.** One mystery solved per book, with every clue in ink where the reader can check it. A sleuth in her forties who wins by paying attention, not force. Los Angeles at night, from other people's kitchens. One old wrong, answered in book four.

**What makes it different.** Her only legal power is to say no and write it down. No badge, no law license, no gun: a seal, a journal, an ink pad. Her journal doubles as the reader's clue sheet.
