# The Night Notary

**Genre:** Urban amateur-sleuth mystery · **Readers:** Women and men 40–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** A widower deeds away his house at 2 a.m. and is dead by morning. Gloria Ibarra, 47, a Los Angeles night notary, can only refuse a bad signing and write it down. That record is the evidence.

**The setting.** Los Angeles County after 9 p.m.: loan papers on a Pacoima kitchen table, a power of attorney at a hospital bedside. By California law a notary gives no legal advice: she identifies the signer, journals the act, takes a thumbprint, and may refuse a signer who seems confused or coerced.

**The lead.** Gloria clerked in superior court for nineteen years, now works nights from a Corolla. Six years ago she notarized a deed for an impostor with good ID and the Batistes lost their house; she keeps that journal page and has never faced them. Days belong to her father Ernesto, 79, a retired locksmith whose memory is slipping. Dry, exact, polite: "Sir, I asked her, not you."

**The cast.** Ruth Adeyemi, 58, county hospital night chaplain, who calls when a patient is rushed to sign and argues mercy over the rule. Nadine Kovac, 52, the dispatcher who found Gloria this work and talks her home at 3 a.m.; her son's debts sit on her house. Warren Tsai, 44, a fraud investigator who has watched notaries sell their stamps. Helen Marsh, 66, who trained Gloria and keeps the county's surrendered journals.

**The series engine.** A bad record costs Gloria her $15,000 bond, her commission and her living. The clues are journal entries the reader has already seen: a name, a time, a thumbprint.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Witness My Hand* — The widower's death is ruled a fall. His witness signed Gloria's journal in March under another name, breaking the son-in-law's alibi; Tsai starts listening.
2. *Sworn Before Me* — A jailed witness names a deed impostor, then swears he never signed. Gloria's entry and the jail's visitor log name the paralegal who reached him. Her commission draws a complaint.
3. *Under Penalty of Perjury* — Over Ruth's plea, Gloria refuses to license a handyman's bedside marriage to a dying widow; another notary arrives within the hour. Her entry from four nights before, the widow lucid, proves coercion. Ernesto, too polite to refuse, signs for a stranger.
4. *Personally Appeared* — The Okafors face eviction on a deed with Gloria's seal and no line in her journal; a second deed names Ernesto's house. Suspended, she lays four journals out: every bad ID shares one issue date.

*Running thread:* Who runs the signing mill that sent Gloria her impostor. Book 4: Nadine, paying her son's debts with journals Helen let her scan. Nadine is charged, Helen resigns, and Gloria, done hiding behind the record, sits at the Batistes' table as they win a judgment.

**Why readers come back.** One mystery solved per book. A sleuth in her forties who wins by paying attention, not force, and argues it out with Ruth over 4 a.m. coffee.

**What makes it different.** No badge, no law license, no gun: a seal, a journal, an ink pad, and the power to say no.
