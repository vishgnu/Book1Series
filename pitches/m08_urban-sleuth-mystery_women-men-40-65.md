# The Night Notary

**Genre:** Urban amateur-sleuth mystery · **Readers:** Women and men 40–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** A widower signs away his house at 2 a.m. and is dead by morning; the only honest record is the journal of Gloria Ibarra, 47, a Los Angeles night notary. She can only refuse and write it down; in each book, that record is evidence.

**The setting.** Los Angeles County after 9 p.m.: loan papers on a Pacoima kitchen table, a power of attorney at a hospital bedside, a sworn declaration at the downtown jail. California's rule: a notary gives no legal advice. She identifies the signer, journals the act, takes a thumbprint for deeds and powers of attorney, and may refuse a signer who seems confused or coerced.

**The lead.** Gloria Ibarra, 47, clerked in superior court for nineteen years and now works nights from a Corolla. Six years ago she notarized a deed for an impostor with good ID, and the Batiste family lost their house; she keeps that journal page. Days belong to her father Ernesto, 79, whose memory is slipping. She catches a coached answer and says so, dry, exact, polite: "Sir, I asked her, not you."

**The cast.** Ruth Adeyemi, 58, night chaplain at the county hospital, who calls when a patient is rushed to sign. Lonnie Pruitt, 61, a bail-bonds clerk who owes Gloria favors. Warren Tsai, a sheriff's fraud detective who wishes she would go home. Helen Marsh, 66, the probate supervisor who trained Gloria and still takes her calls for advice.

**The series engine.** Every book opens with a wrong signing and closes that case. Gloria's stake is lawful: her journal line is evidence, and a bad record costs her commission and her living. The clues are entries the reader has already seen: a name, a time, a thumbprint. She compares entries across nights, pulls recorded deeds, and hands Tsai what holds up in court. Los Angeles signs papers nightly, so calls keep coming.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Witness My Hand* — A widower deeds away his house at 2 a.m. and dies by morning; Gloria's time entry breaks an alibi, and Tsai starts listening.
2. *Sworn Before Me* — A jailed witness recants, then swears he never signed; her journal settles it, and Lonnie's ledger shows one company paid for the signing.
3. *Under Penalty of Perjury* — Gloria refuses to issue a dying widow's bedside confidential marriage license; another notary does, and proving coercion ties him to that company.
4. *Personally Appeared* — A deed carries Gloria's seal but has no line in her journal; the cloned stamp leads to Helen Marsh, and the mill is shut down.

*Running thread:* Who runs the signing mill, a company that manufactures signatures to take houses, and sent an impostor to Gloria six years ago; answered in book 4, when Helen is charged and the Batistes win a judgment.

**Why readers come back.** One mystery solved per book, with every clue in ink where the reader can check it. A sleuth in her forties who wins by paying attention, not force. Los Angeles at night, from other people's kitchens.

**What makes it different.** Her only legal power is to say no and write it down. No badge, no law license, no gun: a seal, a journal, an ink pad. Her journal doubles as the reader's clue sheet.
