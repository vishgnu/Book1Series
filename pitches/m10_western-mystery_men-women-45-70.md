# The Camas Fork Mysteries

**Genre:** Western mystery · **Readers:** Men and women 45–70 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Marty Voss, 54, holds the keys to three hundred headgates along her 24 miles of Idaho canal. In every book, water turns up where it should not be, and someone has died over it.

**The setting.** Harrow County, Idaho: potato cellars and dairies over black lava rock, nine inches of rain a year. The Camas Fork is split among fourteen ditches, the oldest dug in 1884. The rule: first in time, first in right. In a short year the oldest ditch fills before a newer one gets a drop.

**The lead.** Marty Voss, 54, was the deputy who served the 2001 shutoff that cost Walt Pruitt his farm. She rides ditch now, no badge, only the right to lock a headgate, and wants to know that shutoff was right. She reads a wet bank like a crime scene and states the measurement before the opinion: "Four tenths low. What I think comes after."

**The cast.** Sheriff Caleb Ortega, 47, the trainee who drove her to the Pruitt shutoff; Dale Hinckley, 61, watermaster, who taught her to read a weir and keeps the ledger in his late wife's recipe tins; Ruth Aldecoa, 67, Basque herder's granddaughter on the oldest ditch, no heir, quietly leasing her water; Gene Voss, 58, Marty's brother, farming under the newest.

**The series engine.** The death is Ortega's case; the water is Marty's. Each break comes from what only a ditch rider may lawfully do: measure, lock, sign the log, testify. Every summer runs short; this year pages of a lost 1911 ledger are surfacing, and every priority date is in play.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *First in Time* — April: the buyer of the Pruitt farm dies in his potato cellar, his headgate open, Marty's padlock cut. Her log shows it opened after he died.
2. *Call on the River* — July: a river call makes Marty lock out her brother Gene, and a neighbor dies beside a shaved flume. A defense lawyer calls her readings a sister's log; the state gauge backs them. Gene, cleared, stops speaking to her.
3. *Beneficial Use* — Harvest: a dairy pond fills at night, and its silt gives up Walt Pruitt, missing since 2001. Her delivery sheets show the pond dry until 2003: murder. Gene sends his son to the headgate.
4. *Use It or Lose It* — Winter: stock water runs in a ditch locked since October; Hinckley lies under the ice at its headgate, his tins emptied. Her lock log names a shareholder she has known thirty years. Gene drives her to court.

*Running thread:* Someone altered a date in the 1911 decree ranking 214 rights, and a family has farmed on the lie. Book 4 settles it between Ruth's ditch and Gene's, apart from the killing, and Marty says under oath that her 2001 shutoff was wrong.

**Why readers come back.** A complete, fair mystery: readers see each gauge reading when Marty does and can solve it beside her. One valley through a full year, smart working people who know their ground, and no cliffhangers.

**What makes it different.** The detective is a ditch rider whose only power is a padlock on a headgate, her evidence cubic feet per second in her own lock log.
