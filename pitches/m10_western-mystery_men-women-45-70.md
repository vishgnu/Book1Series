# The Camas Fork Mysteries

**Genre:** Western mystery · **Readers:** Men and women 45–70 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Marty Voss, 54, holds the keys to three hundred headgates along her 24 miles of Idaho canal. In every book, water turns up where it should not be, and someone has died over it.

**The setting.** Harrow County, Idaho: potato cellars and dairies over black lava rock, nine inches of rain a year. Nothing grows without the Camas Fork, a river split among fourteen ditches, the oldest dug in 1884. The rule: first in time, first in right. In a short year the oldest ditch fills before a newer one gets a drop.

**The lead.** Marty Voss, 54, was the deputy who served the 2001 shutoff that cost Walt Pruitt his farm. She rides ditch now for the honest numbers, with no badge, only the right to lock a headgate. Widowed, she thinks the old families confuse being first with being right. She reads a wet bank like a crime scene and states the measurement before the opinion.

**The cast.** Sheriff Caleb Ortega, 36, once her trainee, who wants her kept to the water; Dale Hinckley, 61, watermaster, keeper of the priority list; Ruth Aldecoa, 67, potato grower on the oldest ditch; Gene Voss, 58, Marty's brother, farming under the newest.

**The series engine.** Each book opens with water that is not where the delivery sheet says: a headgate chained open, a flume shaved half an inch, a pond that fills at night. Each break comes from what only a ditch rider may lawfully do: measure, lock, sign the log, testify. Every summer runs short; this year pages of a lost 1911 ledger are surfacing, and every priority date is in play.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *First in Time* — April: the buyer of the Pruitt farm dies in his potato cellar, his headgate open four miles off, Marty's padlock cut. Her log shows it opened after he died. Ortega lets her sign evidence.
2. *Call on the River* — July: a river call makes Marty lock out her brother Gene, and a neighbor dies beside a shaved flume. Her readings date the theft before the call; Gene, cleared, stops speaking to her.
3. *Beneficial Use* — Harvest: a dairy pond fills at night, and its silt gives up Walt Pruitt, missing since 2001. Her delivery sheets show the pond dry until 2003: murder. Marty becomes a witness.
4. *Use It or Lose It* — Winter: stock water runs in a ditch locked since October; Hinckley lies under the ice at its headgate. Her lock log names who opened it the night his ledger vanished; she testifies against one of her own.

*Running thread:* Someone altered a date in the 1911 decree ranking 214 rights, and a family has farmed on the lie. Each book's ledger page points at a different family; book 4 settles it between Ruth Aldecoa and Gene Voss.

**Why readers come back.** A complete, fair mystery every time: readers see each gauge reading when Marty does. One valley through a full year, smart working people who know their ground, and no cliffhangers.

**What makes it different.** The detective is a ditch rider whose only power is a padlock on a headgate. Her evidence is cubic feet per second in her own lock log.
