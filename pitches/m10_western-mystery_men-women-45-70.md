# The Camas Fork Mysteries

**Genre:** Western mystery · **Readers:** Men and women 45–70 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Marty Voss rides ditch on Idaho's Camas Fork with the keys to three hundred headgates. In each book water turns up where it should not be, and someone has died over it.

**The setting.** Harrow County, Idaho: lava rock and nine inches of rain a year. Fourteen ditches split the Camas Fork. First in time is first in right: in a short year the oldest ditch fills before a newer one gets a drop.

**The lead.** As a deputy, Marty Voss, 54, served the 2001 shutoff that cost Walt Pruitt his farm. Now deputy watermaster, she has no badge, only the right to lock a headgate on any of the fourteen ditches, and wants to know that shutoff was right. She says the old families confuse first with right. "Four tenths low. What I think comes after."

**The cast.** Sheriff Caleb Ortega, 47, the trainee who drove her to the Pruitt shutoff; Dale Hinckley, 61, watermaster, who taught her the weir and keeps the ledger in recipe tins; Ruth Aldecoa, 67, on the oldest ditch, heirless, leasing her water; Gene Voss, 58, Marty's brother, on the newest.

**The series engine.** The death is Ortega's case; the water is Marty's. Each break comes from what only a ditch rider may do: measure, lock, sign, testify. Ortega acts on nothing she has not signed and tells her when she is off her ditch. This year pages of a lost 1911 ledger are surfacing, and people kill over a priority date.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *First in Time* — April: the Pruitt farm's buyer dies in his potato cellar, headgate open, Marty's padlock cut. The weir's recorder chart shows the water came six hours after death.
2. *Call on the River* — July: a river call, the oldest ditch demanding its water, makes Marty lock out Gene; a neighbor dies beside a flume shaved to read low. The defense calls her log a sister's; the state gauge backs it. Gene, cleared, stops speaking to her.
3. *Beneficial Use* — Harvest: a dairy pond fills at night; its silt gives up Walt Pruitt, missing since 2001. Her delivery sheets, countersigned by Hinckley, show the pond dry until 2003: he was put there later. Gene sends his son to the headgate rather than face her.
4. *Use It or Lose It* — Winter: water runs in a ditch locked since October; Hinckley lies under the ice at its headgate, his tins emptied. Her lock log names a shareholder she has known thirty years. Gene drives her to court, knowing her testimony may cost him water.

*Running thread:* A date altered in the 1911 decree put one ditch ahead of another; the courthouse copy burned, and every record since repeats it. The 2001 shutoff enforced that order. Book 4 settles it between Ruth's ditch and Gene's, apart from the killing, and Marty says under oath her shutoff was wrong.

**Why readers come back.** A complete, fair mystery each time: readers see every gauge reading when Marty does. One valley, one year, no cliffhangers.

**What makes it different.** A detective whose only power is a padlock on a headgate, her evidence cubic feet per second.
