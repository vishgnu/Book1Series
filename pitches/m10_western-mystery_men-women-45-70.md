# The Camas Fork Mysteries

**Genre:** Western mystery · **Readers:** Men and women 45–70 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Marty Voss has no badge, only the padlocks on three hundred headgates, the gates that send Idaho's Camas Fork to its farms. When water runs where she locked it out, somebody is dead beside it.

**The setting.** Harrow County, Idaho: potato cellars and dairies over black lava rock, nine inches of rain a year. Fourteen ditches split the Camas Fork, the oldest dug in 1884. First in time is first in right: in a dry year the oldest ditch fills before a newer one gets a drop.

**The lead.** Marty Voss, 54, is the ditch rider: she opens and locks the gates that send river water to each farm. As a deputy she served the 2001 shutoff that cost Walt Pruitt his farm, and she wants to know it was right. She reads a wet bank like a crime scene. "Four tenths low. What I think comes after."

**The cast.** Sheriff Caleb Ortega, 47, the trainee who drove her to the Pruitt shutoff; Dale Hinckley, 61, watermaster, who keeps the ledger in his late wife's recipe tins; Ruth Aldecoa, 67, a Basque herder's granddaughter on the oldest ditch; Gene Voss, 58, Marty's brother, on the newest.

**The series engine.** The death is Ortega's case; the water is Marty's. Each break comes from what only a ditch rider may do: measure, lock, sign the log, testify. Every case turns on two records that cannot both be true, and the reader sees both. In a dry valley a place in line is worth killing for, so cases keep coming.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *First in Time* — April: the old Pruitt farm's buyer lies dead in his potato cellar, Marty's padlock cut, yet the gauge chart says the water came six hours after he died.
2. *Call on the River* — July: ordered to lock out her brother Gene, Marty finds his neighbor dead beside a flume shaved to cheat the gauge; her log clears Gene, who stops speaking to her.
3. *Dead Storage* — Harvest: a dairy pond filling at night gives up Walt Pruitt, missing since 2001, but Marty's delivery sheets show the pond stood dry until 2003, so someone moved him.
4. *Forfeit* — Winter: Hinckley lies under the ice at a headgate locked since October, his tins emptied; the log says only Marty's key opened it, and Gene drives her to court.

*Running thread:* A date forged in 1911 decided which ditch gets water first, and Marty enforced it in 2001. The lost ledger pages prove it; in book 4 she says under oath her shutoff was wrong.

**Why readers come back.** Each case closes in its own book, no cliffhangers. Each book also turns up one page of the lost 1911 ledger, moving who stands first in line, and pushes Gene further from his sister. Smart working people who know their ground.

**What makes it different.** A detective whose only power is a padlock on a headgate, her evidence cubic feet per second. Every cover carries a cut padlock and the line "A Camas Fork Mystery."
