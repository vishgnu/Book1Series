# The Camas Fork Mysteries

**Genre:** Western mystery · **Readers:** Men and women 45–70 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Marty Voss, 54, a former deputy turned ditch rider on Idaho's Snake River Plain, patrols 62 miles of canal with keys to three hundred headgates. In every book, water turns up where it should not be, and someone dies over it.

**The setting.** Harrow County, Idaho: potato cellars, beet fields and dairies laid over black lava rock, nine inches of rain a year. Nothing grows without the Camas Fork Canal, dug in 1884. The rule is older than the state: first in time, first in right. In a short year the oldest water right is filled in full before a newer one gets a drop.

**The lead.** Marty Voss, 54, was a sheriff's deputy for 22 years, quit after a shooting the review board cleared and she has not, and now rides ditch. She wants work with honest numbers. It costs her neighbors: no badge, only the right to lock a headgate. She reads a wet bank like a crime scene and states the measurement before the opinion.

**The cast.** Sheriff Caleb Ortega, 36, her former trainee, who needs her eye and resents it; Dale Hinckley, 61, state watermaster, keeper of the priority list; Ruth Aldecoa, 67, third-generation potato grower; Dr. Priya Nair, 38, a hydrologist whose well data the old families distrust.

**The series engine.** Each book opens with water that is not where the delivery sheet says: a headgate chained open, a flume shaved half an inch, a pond that fills at night. Marty follows it to a death and to who needed the water. Cases keep coming because every summer runs short and she sees every back gate.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *First in Time* — April: a headgate chained open and a 71-year-old shareholder drowned below it put Marty back beside the sheriff.
2. *Call on the River* — July: a call dries up junior fields, a deputy watermaster dies, and a shaved flume costs Marty a friend.
3. *Beneficial Use* — Harvest: a dairy pond filling at night and an irrigator dead in the beets expose water claimed for acres never planted.
4. *Use It or Lose It* — Winter: a forfeiture fight in water court, a lawyer dead in the dry canal, and the missing ledger entered as evidence.

*Running thread:* The 1911 decree ranks 214 valley rights from a surveyor's ledger unseen since; book 4 finds it and settles whose right came first, with next April's water still to deliver.

**Why readers come back.** A complete, fair mystery every time: readers see each gauge reading when Marty does and can solve it beside her. One valley through a full year, March wind to first frost, and smart working people who know their ground. No cliffhangers.

**What makes it different.** The detective is a ditch rider whose only power is a padlock on a headgate. Her evidence is measured in cubic feet per second, and every suspect's place in line was fixed in 1911.
