# The Honest Pound

**Genre:** Cozy fantasy mystery · **Readers:** Women 25–45 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** In Brindlemarket, magic is sold by the ounce and only a scale cannot be fooled. Weights inspector Wren Calloway catches culprits by arithmetic, and suspects the one reading she never questioned: the one that ruined her father.

**The setting.** Brindlemarket is a canal city of fog and warm bread, where a noon bell tells every stall to true its scale. Luck is sold by the ounce, weather by the gill, sleep by the dram. A glamour fools eyes, not bronze: the beam only says how much.

**The lead.** Wren Calloway, 34, is Junior Inspector Third Class at the Office of Weights and Measures. She apologizes first, then corrects you to the grain, marzipan in hand. Eleven years ago she found her father's scale three grains short and signed the condemnation; asking to recheck it keeps her third class. He returns her letters unopened. She has never unpacked her trunk.

**The cast.** Bargewoman Dot Marrow shaves her tonnage and calls Wren "Ounces." Her senior Osric Pell keeps the master pound. Assayer Lucan Ashby, 36, trusts fire over scales, disputes her evidence and saves her a seat. Landlady Henna Finch, Bakers' Guild warden, keeps supper warm.

**The series engine.** Every book opens with something that weighs wrong and ends with a culprit caught by arithmetic, in a short sum the reader can solve too. One trade from behind the counter; one glamour scam, funny until someone is hurt. Wren can condemn a measure and compel a ledger, never arrest; the canal watch needs her proof.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Short Weight* — A tallyman is dismissed over eleven ounces of saffron against a ledger's twelve; of five manifest signers, Wren's sum clears him and Dot and finds a seal the Office never struck.
2. *Baker's Dozen* — Henna's prize loaf holds three drams of sleep where the guild allows one; Wren must audit the woman who feeds her, and sack counts pick one of four bakers with oven keys.
3. *False Bottom* — An honest apothecary's measure fails; three people handled it since sealing, Lucan sides with Wren against his Hall, and its false seal matches the weight that tested her father's scale.
4. *Full Measure* — The noon bell rings late: the master pound is a grain light and its keeper, Osric, is arrested. Wren's sum finds the thief, then the forger among six who held a seal die, none at her table; she overturns her verdict, her father writes back, she earns a district and unpacks.

*Running thread:* Someone is faking the Office's stamp, and the weight that tested her father's scale may be a fake; answered in *Full Measure*.

**Why readers come back.** A city worth moving into, and a spare chair at the boardinghouse table that slowly becomes Wren's. Supper teasing and a slow burn. A heroine who wins by being careful, not chosen. A fair puzzle and a full ending every time.

**What makes it different.** The detective is a civil servant with a set of weights, and every clue is a quantity. Magic is what gets faked, never what solves the case. The verdict she most needs to overturn is her own.
