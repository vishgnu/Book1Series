# Luck by the Ounce: The Brindlemarket Mysteries

**Genre:** Cozy fantasy mystery · **Readers:** Women 25–45 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** In Brindlemarket, magic sells by the ounce and only a scale cannot be fooled. Weights inspector Wren Calloway solves crimes by arithmetic, finds a family at a boardinghouse table, and doubts the reading that ruined her father.

**The setting.** Brindlemarket is a canal city where a noon bell tells every stall to true its scale. Luck sells by the ounce, weather by the gill, sleep by the dram: licensed salts that go flat in a month. A glamour can make a penny loaf look a pound but cannot fool bronze.

**The lead.** Wren Calloway, 38, is the best inspector at the Office of Weights and Measures and still Junior Inspector Third Class. She apologizes first, then corrects you to the grain. Eleven years ago she found her father's tea scale three grains short and signed the condemnation. Asking to recheck it keeps her third class. He returns her letters unopened. She has never unpacked her trunk.

**The cast.** Bargewoman Dot Marrow shaves her tonnage and calls Wren "Ounces." Landlady Henna Finch, Bakers' Guild warden, keeps supper warm and keeps score. Her senior Osric Pell keeps the master pound. Assayer Lucan Ashby, 40, widowed and wary of wanting anything twice, disputes her evidence and saves her a seat.

**The series engine.** Every book opens with something that weighs wrong and someone Wren likes blamed. She can condemn a measure and compel a ledger, never arrest; the canal watch needs her proof. The last click is a sum the reader can solve too: eleven ounces against twelve. Nobody dies.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *A Grain of Luck* — A chest of luck arrives an ounce short and Dot is blamed; Wren clears her and finds a seal the Office never struck.
2. *A Dram of Sleep* — Henna's bake-off loaf puts the judges to sleep; Wren audits the woman who feeds her, and Lucan's furnace finds the same false seal.
3. *A Scruple of Mercy* — A generous apothecary loses her shop to a swapped measure; Wren restores it, Lucan defies his Hall, and the seal turns up in her father's file.
4. *A Pound of Truth* — The noon bell rings late: the master pound is a grain light and Osric is blamed; Wren names the forger, overturns her verdict, answers Lucan and unpacks.

*Running thread:* Who forges the Office's seal, and did it condemn her father's scale? Answered in *A Pound of Truth*.

**Why readers come back.** A spare boardinghouse chair that slowly becomes Wren's. A slow burn, one step per book: a saved seat, a shared furnace, a defied Hall, an answer. A heroine who wins by being careful, not chosen. Each case ends fully; each last page adds one fact about the forged seal.

**What makes it different.** The detective is a civil servant with weights; every clue is a quantity. Magic gets faked, never solves the case. The verdict she most needs to overturn is her own.
