# The Honest Pound

**Genre:** Cozy fantasy mystery · **Readers:** Women 25–45 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** In the canal city of Brindlemarket, where magic is sold by the ounce and only a scale cannot be fooled, shy weights inspector Wren Calloway follows each short measure to a murder, and toward the forged seal that ruined her father.

**The setting.** Brindlemarket is three hundred bridges and a charm stall on every quay. Magic is a trade: luck by the ounce, weather by the gill, sleep by the dram. A glamour makes goods look, smell and taste like more, but fails on the dull bronze master weights, older than the first charm. No spell tells truth from lie; the beam only says how much.

**The lead.** Wren Calloway, 34, is Junior Inspector Third Class at the Office of Weights and Measures. Eleven years ago a false-scale verdict ruined her spice-merchant father; she wants it overturned, and asking keeps her third class. She reads ledgers, seals and residues like handwriting. She apologizes first, then corrects you to the grain, marzipan in hand.

**The cast.** Osric Pell, 61, her kind, idle senior inspector, better connected than he admits. Dot Marrow, bargewoman, who carries what the city would rather not weigh. Henna Finch, landlady and Bakers' Guild warden, supplier of supper and gossip. Lucan Ashby, Assay Hall rival, smug and occasionally right.

**The series engine.** Every book opens with something that weighs wrong and ends with a murderer caught by arithmetic. In between: one trade seen from behind the counter, a death filed as accident, and proof built from quantities, seals, ledgers and residues. Every scale in Brindlemarket is resealed yearly, so Wren's rounds and their trouble never end.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Short Weight* — Saffron on the Spice Quay runs an ounce light and a tallyman drowns; Wren finds a seal the Office never struck.
2. *Baker's Dozen* — A guild flour-master dies and Henna Finch is accused; Wren clears her by sack count and traces every false seal to one die.
3. *False Bottom* — An apothecary who reported sleeping draughts three drams light is found dead; the doctored measure bears the seal from Wren's father's condemned scale.
4. *Full Measure* — A granary balances on paper, not on the beam, and its auditor is dead; Wren names the forger inside the Office, clears her father and earns her own district.

*Running thread:* Who is striking counterfeit Office seals, and whether one condemned her father's scale; answered in *Full Measure*.

**Why readers come back.** A city worth moving into: canal fog, warm bread, a spare chair at the boardinghouse table. A heroine who wins by being careful, not chosen. A fair puzzle, solvable from the numbers on the page, and a full ending every time.

**What makes it different.** The detective is a civil servant with a set of weights, and every clue is a quantity. Magic is what gets faked, never what solves the case, so each answer is a sum the reader can check.
