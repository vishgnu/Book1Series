# The Honest Pound

**Genre:** Cozy fantasy mystery · **Readers:** Women 25–45 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** In the canal city of Brindlemarket, magic is sold by the ounce and only a scale cannot be fooled. When a stall sells an ounce light, someone ends up dead, and weights inspector Wren Calloway apologizes first, then proves it to the grain.

**The setting.** Brindlemarket is three hundred bridges, canal fog, warm bread and a charm stall on every quay. Magic is a trade: luck by the ounce, weather by the gill, sleep by the dram. A glamour makes a small loaf look, smell and taste like a large one, but fails on the dull bronze master weights. No spell tells truth from lie; the beam only says how much.

**The lead.** Wren Calloway, 34, is Junior Inspector Third Class at the Office of Weights and Measures. Eleven years ago a false-scale verdict ruined her spice-merchant father; she wants it overturned, and asking keeps her third class. She reads ledgers, seals and residues like handwriting, and has never unpacked her trunk at the boardinghouse. She apologizes first, then corrects you to the grain, marzipan in hand.

**The cast.** Osric Pell, 61, her senior inspector, naps through rounds and signs whatever she asks. Dot Marrow, bargewoman, hauls condemned goods and gives Wren blunt advice. Henna Finch, landlady and Bakers' Guild warden, keeps her supper warm and nags her to unpack. Lucan Ashby, Assay Hall rival, smug, occasionally right, forever saving her a seat.

**The series engine.** Every book opens with something that weighs wrong and ends with a murderer caught by arithmetic. In between: one trade seen from behind the counter, a glamour scam that is funny until someone dies, a death filed as accident, and supper at Henna's table. Every scale in Brindlemarket is resealed yearly, so Wren's rounds and their trouble never end.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Short Weight* — Saffron on the Spice Quay runs an ounce light and a tallyman drowns; Wren finds a seal the Office never struck, and a seat at Henna's table.
2. *Baker's Dozen* — At the Loaf Fair a guild flour-master dies and Henna is accused; Wren clears her by sack count and learns only three people can cut a seal die.
3. *False Bottom* — An apothecary who reported sleeping draughts three drams light is found dead; the doctored measure bears the seal that condemned Wren's father, so someone gained by his ruin.
4. *Full Measure* — A granary balances on paper, not on the beam, and its auditor is dead; Wren names the forger at a public hearing, clears her father, earns her own district and unpacks.

*Running thread:* Who is striking counterfeit Office seals, and why one condemned her father's scale; answered in *Full Measure*.

**Why readers come back.** A city worth moving into, and a spare chair at the boardinghouse table that slowly becomes Wren's. A heroine who wins by being careful, not chosen. A fair puzzle, solvable from the numbers on the page, and a full ending every time.

**What makes it different.** The detective is a civil servant with a set of weights, and every clue is a quantity. Magic is what gets faked, never what solves the case, so the reader can solve it too.
