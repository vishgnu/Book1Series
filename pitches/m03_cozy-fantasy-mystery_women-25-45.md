# The Honest Pound

**Genre:** Cozy fantasy mystery · **Readers:** Women 25–45 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** In Brindlemarket, magic is sold by the ounce and only a scale cannot be fooled. Weights inspector Wren Calloway catches culprits by arithmetic, and suspects the one reading she never questioned: the one that ruined her father.

**The setting.** Brindlemarket is a canal city of fog and warm bread, where a noon bell tells every stall to true its scale. Luck is sold by the ounce, weather by the gill, sleep by the dram. A glamour makes a penny loaf look a pound but fails on the bronze master weights; the beam only says how much.

**The lead.** Wren Calloway, 34, is Junior Inspector Third Class at the Office of Weights and Measures. She apologizes first, then corrects you to the grain, marzipan in hand. Eleven years ago she found her father's scale an ounce false and signed the condemnation; asking to recheck it keeps her third class. She has never unpacked her trunk.

**The cast.** Bargewoman Dot Marrow shaves her tonnage and calls Wren "Ounces." Only three people can cut a seal die: her senior Osric Pell, who once passed a scale he should have failed; Lucan Ashby of the Assay Hall, who disputes her evidence and saves her a seat; and landlady Henna Finch, Bakers' Guild warden, who keeps supper warm.

**The series engine.** Every book opens with something that weighs wrong and ends with a culprit caught by arithmetic, in a short sum the reader can solve too. One trade seen from behind the counter and a glamour scam that is funny until someone is hurt. Scales are resealed yearly, and no case touching a sealed measure closes without an Office certificate.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Short Weight* — A drowned tallyman carries eleven ounces of saffron where his ledger says twelve; the missing ounce leads to his killer, Dot's shaved tonnage and a seal the Office never struck.
2. *Baker's Dozen* — Henna's prize loaf holds three drams of sleep where the guild allows one, and a flour-master barely wakes; sack counts clear Henna, name the poisoner and find the false seal again.
3. *False Bottom* — An honest apothecary is shut down when her own measure fails; Wren and Lucan prove who doctored it, and its false seal matches the weight that condemned her father.
4. *Full Measure* — The noon bell rings late: the master pound is a grain light and its keeper, Osric, is arrested. Wren's sum finds the thief, then the forger; she overturns her verdict, earns a district and unpacks.

*Running thread:* Someone is faking the Office's stamp, and the weight that condemned Wren's father may be a fake; answered in *Full Measure*.

**Why readers come back.** A city worth moving into, and a spare chair at the boardinghouse table that slowly becomes Wren's. Supper teasing and a slow burn. A heroine who wins by being careful, not chosen. A fair puzzle and a full ending every time.

**What makes it different.** The detective is a civil servant with a set of weights, and every clue is a quantity. Magic is what gets faked, never what solves the case. The verdict she most needs to overturn is her own.
