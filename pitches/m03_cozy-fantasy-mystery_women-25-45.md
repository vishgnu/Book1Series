# The Honest Pound

**Genre:** Cozy fantasy mystery · **Readers:** Women 25–45 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** In Brindlemarket, magic is sold by the ounce and only a scale cannot be fooled. Weights inspector Wren Calloway catches culprits by arithmetic, and suspects the one reading she never questioned: the one that ruined her father.

**The setting.** Brindlemarket is a canal city of fog and warm bread, where a noon bell tells every stall to true its scale. Luck is sold by the ounce, weather by the gill, sleep by the dram: licensed salts that go flat in a month, so nobody hoards. A glamour makes a penny loaf look a pound but cannot fool bronze: the beam only says how much.

**The lead.** Wren Calloway, 34, is Junior Inspector Third Class at the Office of Weights and Measures. She apologizes first, then corrects you to the grain, marzipan in hand. Eleven years ago she found her father's tea scale three grains short and signed the condemnation. No inspector may reopen her own verdict; asking keeps her third class. He returns her letters unopened. She has never unpacked her trunk.

**The cast.** Bargewoman Dot Marrow shaves her tonnage and calls Wren "Ounces." Her senior Osric Pell keeps the master pound. Assayer Lucan Ashby, 36, trusts fire over scales, disputes her evidence and saves her a seat. Landlady Henna Finch, Bakers' Guild warden, keeps supper warm and keeps score.

**The series engine.** Every book opens with something that weighs wrong and someone blamed. Wren learns who lied and for whom; the last click is a short sum the reader can solve too. Each has one glamour scam, funny until someone is hurt. Wren can condemn a measure and compel a ledger, never arrest; the canal watch needs her proof.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Short Weight* — At the saffron fair, a tallyman drowns beside a chest an ounce short; Dot's barge carried it. Wren clears Dot, names the killer and finds a seal the Office never struck.
2. *Baker's Dozen* — At the midwinter bake-off, Henna's prize loaf puts the judges to sleep. Wren must audit the woman who feeds her; the flour sacks name the baker.
3. *False Bottom* — An apothecary who doses the quarter on credit loses her shop when her measure fails. Wren finds who swapped it, Lucan sides with her against his Hall, and its false seal matches the weight that tested her father's scale.
4. *Full Measure* — The noon bell rings late: the master pound is a grain light and Osric is arrested. Wren finds the thief, then the forger; she overturns her verdict, her father writes back, she earns a district and unpacks.

*Running thread:* Someone is faking the Office's stamp, perhaps on the weight that tested her father's scale; answered in *Full Measure*.

**Why readers come back.** A spare chair at the boardinghouse table that slowly becomes Wren's. A slow burn argued out over supper. A heroine who wins by being careful, not chosen. A fair puzzle and a full ending every time.

**What makes it different.** The detective is a civil servant with a set of weights, and every clue is a quantity. Magic is what gets faked, never what solves the case. The verdict she most needs to overturn is her own.
