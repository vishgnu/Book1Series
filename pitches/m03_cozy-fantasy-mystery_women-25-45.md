# The Honest Pound

**Genre:** Cozy fantasy mystery · **Readers:** Women 25–45 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** In Brindlemarket, magic is sold by the ounce and only a scale cannot be fooled. Weights inspector Wren Calloway catches murderers by arithmetic, and suspects the one reading she never questioned: the one that ruined her father.

**The setting.** Brindlemarket is laid out like a ledger, from Dram Row to the Standard Beam, where a noon bell tells every stall to true its scale. Magic is a trade, bound in salt, wax or oil: luck by the ounce, weather by the gill, sleep by the dram. A glamour fools the senses but fails on the dull bronze master weights, older than the first charm, and the beam only says how much.

**The lead.** Wren Calloway, 34, is Junior Inspector Third Class at the Office of Weights and Measures. She apologizes first, then corrects you to the grain, marzipan in hand. Eleven years ago she found her spice-merchant father's scale an ounce false and signed the condemnation; asking to recheck it keeps her third class. She reads seals and residues like handwriting, and has never unpacked her trunk.

**The cast.** Osric Pell, her senior, once passed a scale he should have failed. Dot Marrow, bargewoman, is paid by tonnage and shaves it. Henna Finch, landlady and Bakers' Guild warden, sets the legal loaf and keeps supper warm. Lucan Ashby of the Assay Hall tests purity, not weight, disputes her evidence and saves her a seat.

**The series engine.** Every book opens with something that weighs wrong and ends with a culprit caught by arithmetic, in a sum the reader can check. In between: one trade seen from behind the counter and a glamour scam that is funny until someone is hurt. Scales are resealed yearly, so Wren's rounds never end.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Short Weight* — A drowned tallyman carries eleven ounces of saffron where his ledger says twelve; the missing ounce leads to his killer and a seal the Office never struck.
2. *Baker's Dozen* — A prize loaf holds three drams of sleep where the guild allows one, and a flour-master barely wakes; sack counts name the poisoner, and only three people can cut a seal die.
3. *False Bottom* — An apothecary who reported draughts three drams light is found dead; her doctored measure bears the false seal from the check weight Wren used against her father.
4. *Full Measure* — A granary weighs forty sacks short of its books and its auditor is dead; Wren names the forger, overturns her own verdict, earns a district and unpacks.

*Running thread:* Who strikes counterfeit Office seals, and whether Wren's reading of her father's scale was made with a false weight; answered in *Full Measure*.

**Why readers come back.** A city worth moving into, and a spare chair at the boardinghouse table that slowly becomes Wren's. A heroine who wins by being careful, not chosen. A fair puzzle and a full ending every time.

**What makes it different.** The detective is a civil servant with a set of weights, and every clue is a quantity. Magic is what gets faked, never what solves the case. The verdict she most needs to overturn is her own.
