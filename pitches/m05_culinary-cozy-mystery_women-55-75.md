# The Open Class Mysteries

**Genre:** Culinary cozy mystery · **Readers:** Women 55–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Dot Engelhardt, 67, a retired flour-mill test cook, judges pies, preserves and breads at Upper Midwest fairs from a 1994 Winnebago. At each fair one death is called an accident, and the proof otherwise is on her judging table.

**The setting.** One summer, four fairs: Tamarack County in June, Sorrel in July, Pike Lake in August, then the State Fair. Each is a week-long town: dairy barn, grandstand, midway, and an exhibit hall of numbered jars and pies. The same bakers, carnies and hog men follow the circuit. Entries carry a number, never a name, and nothing leaves the hall until Sunday release; then the evidence gets eaten.

**The lead.** Dot spent thirty years in the Halstad Mills test kitchen. She wants one summer with her granddaughter, who moves to Seattle in September. Speaking up embarrasses her hosts, and a troublesome judge is not invited back. She can tell lard from shortening and one kitchen from another. She talks like a recipe card: "Shortening. I'd have forgiven the lie before the crust."

**The cast.** Kayla Engelhardt, 19, drives the Winnebago and asks what Dot won't. Oleo, an elderly basset hound, howls at every Ferris wheel. Harlan Voight, 70, widowed livestock judge, keeps turning up with coffee. Lorraine Tvedt, 72, enters pie at every fair and knows every county's grudges. Deputy Curtis Lindahl, 41, has a badge in one county and Berkshire hogs at all four fairs.

**The series engine.** Every book is one fair, entry day to Sunday release: judging, a death found quietly off the page and called an accident, Dot's proof by taste, and two or three recipes. Voss & Daughter Amusements works all four fairs and pays one local at each to sign ride-safety papers unread, disguised as a $500 sponsor's prize. The state holds ninety county fairs.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *White Ribbon Jam* — Tamarack's ride inspector, Walt Brenner, is found beside the Ferris wheel; store-bought jam breaks an alibi; Dot inherits his notebook.
2. *Red Ribbon Crust* — Sorrel's dairy superintendent is found in the milk house; two lard crusts from one kitchen name the killer; Harlan stops being a coincidence.
3. *Blue Ribbon Starter* — Pike Lake's demolition-derby announcer never returns to his booth; a sourdough younger than its story exposes the liar; Kayla cracks half the case.
4. *Purple Ribbon Butter* — State Fair: the midway bookkeeper is found beside the butter sculpture; Dot matches the last entry number and names the paymaster.

*Running thread:* Walt's notebook lists one exhibit entry number per fair, each a sponsor's-prize winner; who at the midway picks them, and who wanted him stopped.

**Why readers come back.** A week at the fair without the heat: lemon meringue under glass, Dot and Kayla bickering over the map, Oleo stealing a corn dog. Every case closes in its own book, the clue on the page before Dot names it.

**What makes it different.** The detective's instrument is her palate: clues are tasted in fat, sugar, pectin and a starter's age. The village travels: a fairground full on Monday, an empty field a week later, the same neighbors parked at the next one.
