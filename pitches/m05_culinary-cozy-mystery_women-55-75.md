# The Open Class Mysteries

**Genre:** Culinary cozy mystery · **Readers:** Women 55–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Dot Engelhardt, 67, retired flour-mill test cook, judges numbered, nameless pies and preserves at Upper Midwest fairs. At each, one death is called an accident, and the proof otherwise is on her table until Sunday, when it gets eaten.

**The setting.** One summer, four fairs: Tamarack County, Sorrel, Pike Lake, then the State Fair. Each is a week-long town: dairy barn, grandstand, midway, exhibit hall. Judges bunk in the 4-H dormitory; the same bakers and carnies follow the circuit. Entries carry a number, never a name; nothing leaves the hall until Sunday.

**The lead.** Dot spent thirty years in the Halstad Mills test kitchen and can tell lard from shortening, one kitchen from another. She judges people like crusts: once. She wants one summer with her granddaughter, who moves to Seattle in September; every hour on a case comes out of it. She talks like a recipe card: "Shortening. I'd have forgiven the lie before the crust."

**The cast.** Kayla Engelhardt, 19, drives the Buick and hasn't baked since Dot called her crust tough. Harlan Voight, 70, livestock judge, brings coffee, his basset hound Oleo and the wrong suspect. Lorraine Tvedt, 72, has lettered other women's ribbons thirty years. Rita Voss, 58, runs her Ferris wheel half-empty since Walt died. Bev Aasen, 61, secretary, got Dot the job and bakes the breakfast rolls.

**The series engine.** Every book is one fair, entry day to Sunday release: judging, a death called an accident, one numbered entry that contradicts a story, and Dot matching number to maker by taste before the hall empties. A receipt, lid or ledger confirms each taste. Supper in the dormitory; two or three recipes. The state holds ninety fairs.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Jam No. 41* — Tamarack: Rita's inspector and friend Walt Brenner dies under her Ferris wheel; store-bought jam breaks an alibi; Dot inherits his notebook.
2. *Pie No. 117* — Sorrel: Dot disqualifies a board man's pie and calls him a cheat; he is found in the milk house. Harlan reads a hog by hand; its leaf lard names the killer. The pie was Walt's number, and the man had meant to confess.
3. *Sourdough No. 9* — Pike Lake: the derby announcer, Harlan's fishing partner, never returns to his booth; his winning sourdough, younger than its story, exposes the liar. Kayla cracks half the case; Lorraine, entered under a niece's number, is a suspect.
4. *Butter No. 302* — State Fair: the treasurer is found beside the butter sculpture. Four winners share one oven's scorched corner with the breakfast rolls; Dot names Bev, who signs her contract, and Bev's paymaster. Rita's wheel is inspected; Dot, asked back, drives Kayla to the train and asks her for a pie.

*Running thread:* Someone bakes $500 prize winners for local officials who sign ride-safety papers unread; Walt's notebook lists one per fair. Who cooks, who pays.

**Why readers come back.** A week at the fair without the heat: dill pickles held to the light, the dormitory percolator at five, Harlan's chair a little closer each fair. The clue is on the page before Dot names it.

**What makes it different.** The detective's instrument is her palate: clues are tasted in fat, sugar, pectin and a starter's age. The village travels: full fairground Monday, empty field a week later.
