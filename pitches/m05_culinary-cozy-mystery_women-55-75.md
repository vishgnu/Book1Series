# The Open Class Mysteries

**Genre:** Culinary cozy mystery · **Readers:** Women 55–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Dot Engelhardt, 67, retired flour-mill test cook, judges numbered, nameless pies and preserves at Upper Midwest fairs. At each, one death is called an accident, and the proof otherwise is on her table until Sunday, when it gets eaten.

**The setting.** One summer, four fairs: Tamarack County, Sorrel, Pike Lake, then the State Fair. Each is a week-long town: dairy barn, grandstand, midway, exhibit hall. Judges bunk in the 4-H dormitory; the same bakers, carnies and hog men follow the circuit. Entries carry a number, never a name; nothing leaves the hall until Sunday.

**The lead.** Dot spent thirty years in the Halstad Mills test kitchen and can tell lard from shortening, one kitchen from another. She wants one summer with her granddaughter, who moves to Seattle in September; every hour on a case comes out of it. She talks like a recipe card: "Shortening. I'd have forgiven the lie before the crust."

**The cast.** Kayla Engelhardt, 19, drives the Buick, argues the map and asks what Dot won't. Harlan Voight, 70, livestock judge, brings coffee, his basset hound Oleo and the wrong suspect. Lorraine Tvedt, 72, Dot's clerk, wants one purple ribbon of her own. Rita Voss, 58, owns the Ferris wheel and wants it inspected. Bev Aasen, 61, association secretary, signs every judge's contract.

**The series engine.** Every book is one fair, entry day to Sunday release: judging, a death called an accident, one numbered entry that contradicts a story, and Dot matching number to maker by taste before the hall empties. Supper nightly in the dormitory kitchen; two or three recipes. Each solved case earns the next fair's invitation; the state holds ninety.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *White Ribbon Jam* — Tamarack: ride inspector Walt Brenner dies under the Ferris wheel; store-bought jam breaks an alibi; Dot inherits his notebook.
2. *Red Ribbon Pie* — Sorrel: the board man whose pie Dot disqualified is found in the milk house; leaf lard from a hog only Harlan can place names the killer; the pie was Walt's number, and Dot's ruling cost its owner $500.
3. *Blue Ribbon Sourdough* — Pike Lake: the derby announcer, who signed the ride papers, never returns to his booth; his winning sourdough, younger than its story, exposes the liar; Kayla cracks half the case; Dot learns the winners are baked elsewhere.
4. *Purple Ribbon Butter* — State Fair: the treasurer is found beside the butter sculpture; Dot tastes one kitchen in four winners and names the cook, who signs her contract, and her paymaster; Rita's wheel is inspected before it turns; Dot is asked back for next summer.

*Running thread:* Someone bakes $500 prize winners for local officials who sign ride-safety papers unread; Walt's notebook lists one per fair. Who cooks, who pays.

**Why readers come back.** A week at the fair without the heat: dill pickles held to the light, the dormitory percolator at five, two pie bakers feuding since 1989, Harlan's chair a little closer each fair. The clue is on the page before Dot names it.

**What makes it different.** The detective's instrument is her palate: clues are tasted in fat, sugar, pectin and a starter's age. The village travels: full fairground Monday, empty field a week later.
