# The Fair Judge Mysteries

**Genre:** Culinary cozy mystery · **Readers:** Women 55–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Dot Engelhardt, 67, retired test cook, judges numbered, nameless pies and preserves at Upper Midwest fairs. At each, a death is called an accident; the proof otherwise is on her table until Sunday, when it gets eaten.

**The setting.** One summer, four fairs: Tamarack County, Sorrel, Pike Lake, then the big Lakeland Exposition. Each is a week-long town: dairy barn, grandstand, midway. Judges bunk in the 4-H dormitory; the same bakers and ride crew follow the circuit. Nothing leaves the exhibit hall until Sunday.

**The lead.** Thirty years in the Norquist Mills test kitchen taught Dot to tell lard from shortening, and one kitchen from another. She wants this summer with her granddaughter, Seattle-bound in September; every hour on a case comes out of it. Judging, she talks like a recipe card: "Shortening. I'd have forgiven the lie before the crust." To Kayla: "Sit. You get the corner piece."

**The cast.** Kayla Engelhardt, 19, drives the Buick and hasn't baked since Dot called her crust tough. Harlan Voight, 70, livestock judge, reads a limp across a show ring and brings coffee, his basset Oleo and the wrong suspect. Bev Aasen, 61, fair secretary, and Lorraine Tvedt, 72, ribbon letterer, share the dormitory oven. Rita Voss runs the Ferris wheel.

**The series engine.** Every book is one fair: judging, a death, and one numbered entry that contradicts someone's story. Every taste clue is also visible to the reader: in book 1 the "homemade" jam sets clear as factory jelly, and its lid carries price-sticker glue. Dormitory supper, two or three recipes. The region holds ninety fairs.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Jam No. 41 Under the Ferris Wheel* — Ride inspector Walt dies under Rita's wheel; Dot blames Rita, apologizes with pie, breaks the real alibi with store-bought jam, and keeps Walt's notebook.
2. *The Lard in Pie No. 117* — A fair-board man dies in the milk house; the ride crew is suspected, leaf lard in one pie points to an insider, and Harlan's chair moves closer.
3. *Sourdough No. 9 and the Empty Booth* — The fair announcer, Harlan's fishing partner, dies; Lorraine is suspected until Kayla spots one oven's scorched corner on two winners' loaves.
4. *Butter No. 302, Best in Show* — The Exposition treasurer dies beside the butter sculpture; Bev admits baking the winners, Dot names the chairman who paid and killed, then asks Kayla for a pie.

*Running thread:* Why Walt listed blue-ribbon entry numbers: a chairman has winners baked for officials, who sign ride-safety papers unread. Bev baked to keep Tamarack's fair open, keeps her seat at supper, and Dot is asked back.

**Why readers come back.** A week at the fair without the heat: dill pickles held to the light, the percolator at five. Every case closes Sunday; what carries over is the weeks before Seattle, whether Kayla bakes again, and the next number in Walt's notebook.

**What makes it different.** The detective's instrument is her palate and the evidence is edible. No shop, no café, no hometown: the village travels, fairground Monday, empty field the next.
