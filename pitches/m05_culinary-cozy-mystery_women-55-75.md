# The Open Class Mysteries

**Genre:** Culinary cozy mystery · **Readers:** Women 55–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Dot Engelhardt, 67, retired flour-mill test cook, judges numbered, nameless pies and preserves at Upper Midwest fairs. At each, one death is called an accident, and the proof otherwise is on her table until Sunday, when it gets eaten.

**The setting.** One summer, four fairs: Tamarack County in June, Sorrel in July, Pike Lake in August, then the State Fair. Each is a week-long town: dairy barn, grandstand, midway, exhibit hall. Judges bunk in the 4-H dormitory; the same bakers, carnies and hog men follow the circuit. Entries carry a number, never a name; nothing leaves the hall until Sunday.

**The lead.** Dot spent thirty years in the Halstad Mills test kitchen and can tell lard from shortening, one kitchen from another. She wants one summer with her granddaughter, who moves to Seattle in September; every hour on a case comes out of it. A troublesome judge is not invited back. She talks like a recipe card: "Shortening. I'd have forgiven the lie before the crust."

**The cast.** Kayla Engelhardt, 19, drives the Buick and asks what Dot won't. Harlan Voight, 70, livestock judge, reads a hog by hand as Dot reads a crust by tongue, and brings coffee and the wrong suspect. Lorraine Tvedt, 72, clerks Dot's table. Rita Voss, 58, owns the Ferris wheel and wants it inspected. Bev Aasen, 61, association secretary, signs every judge's contract.

**The series engine.** Every book is one fair, entry day to Sunday release: judging, a death found off the page and called an accident, one numbered entry that contradicts a story, and Dot matching number to maker by taste before the hall empties. Two or three recipes. The state holds ninety county fairs.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Entry 114, Strawberry Jam* — Tamarack's ride inspector, Walt Brenner, dies under the Ferris wheel; store-bought jam breaks an alibi; Dot inherits his notebook.
2. *Entry 62, Two-Crust Pie* — Sorrel: a baker Dot disqualified is found in the milk house; two lard crusts from one kitchen name the killer; Dot is suspected first.
3. *Entry 9, Sourdough* — Pike Lake's derby announcer never returns to his booth; Dot's own scorecard backs the wrong story until a sourdough younger than claimed exposes the liar; Kayla cracks half the case.
4. *Entry 301, Butter* — State Fair: the association treasurer is found beside the butter sculpture; Dot tastes one kitchen in four prize winners and names the paymaster, who signs her contract.

*Running thread:* Walt's notebook lists one entry number per fair; each won a $500 prize and belongs to the local who signed the ride-safety papers unread. Who cooked the winners.

**Why readers come back.** A week at the fair without the heat: dill pickles held to the light, the dormitory percolator at five. The clue is on the page before Dot names it.

**What makes it different.** The detective's instrument is her palate: clues are tasted in fat, sugar, pectin and a starter's age. The evidence is numbered, nameless and eaten on Sunday. The village travels: full fairground Monday, empty field a week later.
