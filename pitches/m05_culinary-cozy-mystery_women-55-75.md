# The Open Class Mysteries

**Genre:** Culinary cozy mystery · **Readers:** Women 55–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Dot Engelhardt, 67, a retired flour-mill test cook, judges pies, preserves and breads at Upper Midwest fairs from a 1994 Winnebago. At each fair one death is called an accident, and the proof otherwise is on her judging table.

**The setting.** One summer, four fairs: Tamarack County in June, Sorrel County in July, Pike Lake County in August, then the State Fair. Each is a town that lasts a week: dairy barn, grandstand, midway, and an exhibit hall of numbered jars and pies. The rule: entries carry a number, never a name, and nothing leaves the hall until Sunday release, after which the evidence gets eaten.

**The lead.** Dot Engelhardt, 67, spent thirty years in the Halstad Mills test kitchen and now judges open-class foods. She wants one summer with her granddaughter. Saying what she tastes costs neighbors ribbons and alibis, and fair boards do not rehire trouble. She can tell lard from shortening and one kitchen from another. She talks like a recipe card: short, in order.

**The cast.** Kayla Engelhardt, 19, drives the Winnebago and asks what Dot won't. Harlan Voight, 70, widowed livestock judge, keeps turning up with coffee. Lorraine Tvedt, 72, Tamarack's fair-board secretary, knows every county's grudges. Deputy Curtis Lindahl, 41, has a badge in one county and Berkshire hogs at all four fairs.

**The series engine.** Every book is one fair, entry day to Sunday release: judging, a death the sheriff calls an accident, Dot's proof by taste, and two or three recipes. Cases keep arriving because Voss & Daughter Amusements works all four fairs, and in each county someone local took its money. The state holds ninety county fairs.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *White Ribbon Jam* — Ride inspector Walt Brenner dies under the Ferris wheel; store-bought jam breaks an alibi; Dot inherits his notebook.
2. *Red Ribbon Crust* — A dairy-barn death; two lard crusts from one kitchen name the killer; Harlan stops being a coincidence.
3. *Blue Ribbon Starter* — A derby-night grandstand fall; a sourdough younger than its story exposes the liar; Kayla cracks half the case.
4. *Purple Ribbon Butter* — State Fair: the midway bookkeeper dies in the butter cooler; Dot matches the last entry number and names the paymaster.

*Running thread:* Walt's notebook lists one exhibit entry number per fair; what he was tracking, and who at the midway wanted him stopped.

**Why readers come back.** A week at the fair without the heat: lemon meringue under glass, dill pickles held to the light, Dot and Kayla bickering over the map. Every case closes in its own book, with the clue on the page before Dot names it. Then the Winnebago pulls out.

**What makes it different.** The detective's instrument is her palate: clues are tasted, in fat, sugar, pectin and the age of a starter. The village is new each book, a fairground that fills on Monday and is an empty field a week later.
