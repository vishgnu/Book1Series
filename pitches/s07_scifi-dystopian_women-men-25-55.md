# The Signature Laws

**Genre:** Dystopian science fiction · **Readers:** Women and men 25–55 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** In the Lakes Commonwealth, adulthood is a license renewed by exam. Fail, and you cannot sign your name: a Guardian company signs for your bank account, lease and front door. Former Ward Ines Calder takes the appeals.

**The setting.** Port Stannard, 2084: an ore port of cranes and boarding houses. Begun as elder fraud protection, the Assessment now tests every adult every five years: a timed household budget, a count of addresses, a sworn list of missed payments, checked against bank records. One citizen in nine fails. Your lock then opens only on a Guardian's countersignature, billed to your wages.

**The lead.** Ines Calder, 37, works from the back of the Blue Drum laundromat. At nineteen she admitted one missed payment and spent five years a Ward; her Guardian sold her mother's house. She has conceded nothing since. She wants her brother's signature back, and every case puts her own renewal under Lakehead Guardian's eye. Exact, dry, funniest when furious.

**The cast.** Sam Calder, 41, her brother, crane mechanic and Ward, told her not to file again and still fixes her dryers. Teo Brandt, 29, Lakehead case officer, signed his mother into Wardship and believes it saved her. Tribunal chair Aila Kangas, 58, has ruled against Ines eleven times and reads every page. Paul Rennick, 54, runs Lakehead Guardian and signs for his own father.

**The series engine.** Each book: a failed Assessment, a hearing in thirty days. Scores are sealed, and a third lost appeal is permanent. Ines learns why her client failed and who profits, while Rennick leans on her: the laundromat's account frozen, Sam's crane ticket pulled.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Seventy-One* — Baker Danuta Wojcik, 44, has dyscalculia; Ines saves her shop and finds her 71 was failed against 74, not the published 70.
2. *Countersigned* — Union archivist Walt Deering, 73, loses his appeal; Ines commits her one crime, back-dating a deed to before his Wardship so his archive and its fee ledgers reach the union.
3. *Winter Address* — Deckhand Jan Hruska, 46, failed for moving boarding houses each winter; Ines wins back his bunk and his postcode's score sheets, Sam's among them.
4. *Her Own Hand* — Teo reports the back-dated deed, then brings Ines Lakehead's fee tables; at her own renewal she concedes the forgery, loses her signature, and puts the hidden schedule on the record.

*Running thread:* Who moves the pass mark by postcode. Book 4 finds a schedule passed in open session, never gazetted, priced on Guardian fee income. The Assessment stands with one published mark; 1,400 Wards sign again, Sam on his own lease.

**Why readers come back.** Each book ends on a verdict and one thing won back, leaves one document for the next, and brings Sam nearer his lease.

**What makes it different.** A dystopia run on hearings, with no uprising. The only law broken is broken by the heroine, and she pays for it.
