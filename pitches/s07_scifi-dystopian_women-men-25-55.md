# The Right to Sign

**Genre:** Dystopian science fiction · **Readers:** Women and men 25–55 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** In the Lakes Commonwealth, adulthood is a license renewed by exam. Fail, and you cannot sign your name: a Guardian company signs for your bank account, lease and front door. Former Ward Ines Calder takes one failed citizen per book, with thirty days to win.

**The setting.** Meridian is a freshwater ore port. The Assessment began as elder fraud protection; now everyone sits it every five years. Half the score is a stability index built from bank records and Guardian-reported address changes. Laid-off dock crews change boarding houses each winter, and every move costs points. Lawyers are barred; lay advocates argue appeals.

**The lead.** Ines Calder, 37, takes appeals behind the Blue Drum laundromat. At nineteen she admitted a missed payment on her Assessment and spent five years a Ward while her Guardian sold her mother's house. She argued herself out; Wardship taught her which form gets filed out of order. She wants her brother's signature back and never concedes. Exact, dry, funniest when furious.

**The cast.** Sam Calder, 41, crane mechanic, her brother, a Ward on 73, told her not to file a third time and still fixes the Blue Drum's dryers. Teo Brandt, 29, Lakehead case officer, believes rules protect his Ward mother, who has never missed rent. Tribunal chair Marian Voss, 58, has ruled against Ines eleven times and reads every page. Paul Rennick, 54, runs Lakehead Guardian and signs for his own father.

**The series engine.** Every book opens with a failed Assessment and a hearing thirty days out. Ines learns why this person failed and who bills them, then argues the appeal in a room built to say no. One citizen in nine fails. No cliffhangers.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Short Count* — Lena Marsh, 44, a baker with dyscalculia, feeds the dock canteen on credit while Lakehead lists her shop for sale; Ines wins by reading the local pass mark, 74 not 70, into the record; Lakehead audits her standing.
2. *Record Copy* — Walt Deering, 71, a union archivist, fails memory and sleeps beside the fee ledgers Rennick wants; Ines loses the appeal and saves the archive by signing its deed for him after the ruling.
3. *Raw Score* — Cal Hruska, 46, a deckhand, passed the test and failed on winter moves between Lakehead boarding houses, and his wages freeze; Ines's win puts raw sheets on record, Sam's 73 among them; Teo reports the Deering deed.
4. *Her Own Hand* — The deed fails Ines's renewal; Teo hands her Lakehead's fee tables and loses his job. She concedes the deed, her first admission since nineteen, takes a year's probation and wins on the postcode schedule, which Voss orders published; Sam signs his own lease.

*Running thread:* Who moves the pass mark by postcode; book 4 finds a schedule passed in open session, never gazetted, priced on Guardian fee income. The Assessment stands with one published mark, 72.

**Why readers come back.** Each book gives a person you want to see keep their keys, a paper trail and a verdict. Each moves Sam and Ines one conversation closer.

**What makes it different.** The dystopia is administrative and lawful; the one rule broken is the heroine's. The advocate who returns other people's keys loses her own.
