# The Right to Sign

**Genre:** Dystopian science fiction · **Readers:** Women and men 25–55 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** In the Lakes Commonwealth, adulthood is a license renewed by exam. Fail, and a Guardian company signs for your bank account, lease and front door. Ines Calder takes one failed citizen per book, with thirty days to win.

**The setting.** Meridian is a freshwater ore port. The Assessment began as elder fraud protection; now everyone sits it every five years. Laid-off dock crews change boarding houses each winter, and every move costs points. Wards can work and marry but cannot sign, and their Guardian bills them for it. Lawyers are barred to keep hearings cheap; lay advocates argue appeals.

**The lead.** Ines Calder, 37, was a Ward from nineteen to twenty-four. She takes appeals behind Dot Okafor's Blue Drum laundromat. She has twice lost her brother's appeal. Wardship taught her which form gets filed out of order. Exact, dry, funniest when furious.

**The cast.** Paul Rennick, 54, runs Lakehead Guardian; he co-wrote the first elder-protection rules and signs for his own father. Teo Brandt, 29, his best case officer; his Ward mother has never missed rent. Tribunal chair Marian Voss, 58, has ruled against Ines eleven times and reads every page. Sam Calder, 41, her brother, still a Ward, scored 73.

**The series engine.** Every book opens with a failed Assessment and a hearing thirty days out. Ines learns why this person failed and who bills them, then argues the appeal in a room built to say no. One citizen in nine fails.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Short Count* — Lena Marsh, 44, a baker with dyscalculia, cannot sign for flour while Lakehead bills her till; Ines wins by reading the local pass mark, 74 not 70, into the record; Lakehead audits her standing.
2. *Record Copy* — Walt Deering, 71, a union archivist, fails memory while holding fee ledgers Rennick wants; Ines loses the appeal and saves the archive by signing its deed for him after the ruling, the series' one broken rule.
3. *Raw Score* — Cal Hruska, 46, a deckhand, passed the test and failed on winter moves between Lakehead boarding houses; Ines's win puts raw sheets on record, Sam's 73 among them; Teo reports the Deering deed.
4. *Her Own Hand* — The deed fails Ines's renewal; unable to sign her filing, she concedes it, takes a year's probation and wins on the postcode schedule, which Voss orders published; Sam signs his own lease.

*Running thread:* Who moves the pass mark by postcode, and on what figures; book 4 finds a schedule passed in open session, never gazetted, priced on Guardian fee income. The Assessment stands with one published mark, 72.

**Why readers come back.** Each book gives a person you want to see keep their keys, a paper trail and a verdict. Every outcome changes how Meridian treats the next client and what Lakehead does to Ines.

**What makes it different.** The dystopia is administrative and lawful; the one rule broken is the heroine's. The weapons are a lease, a score sheet and a filing deadline. The advocate who returns other people's keys loses her own.
