# The Thrale Landings

**Genre:** First-contact science fiction · **Readers:** Women and men 30–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete exchange per book

**Logline.** Every four hundred days a capsule lands on a sub-Antarctic island carrying one object from unseen senders. Tobias Eze, 58, customs appraiser, has forty days to choose Earth's reply and learn why they hold a human cup.

**The setting.** Thrale Island: black gravel, seals, a seam of red clay under the weather station. A farmer's saved seed brought a living flower extinct since 1912, and forty nations signed the treaty. A flag brought three empty capsules, and half left. Now replies must be made or mended on Thrale, and one more empty capsule closes the station.

**The lead.** Tobias spent thirty years appraising seized antiquities by wear and weight. He trusts provenance over making, so anything new reads as fake. He chose the flag. He told his daughter, a shoemaker, she sells copies; her letters sit unanswered. He wants one reply he can stand behind. He talks like a condition report: his marriage is "stable, with old repairs."

**The cast.** Saoirse Lim, 31, textile conservator: he asks where a thing has been, she asks what they can make. Renata Voss, 44, treaty indemnity officer, demoted for insuring the flag. Hana Kovač, cook, throws pots and runs the betting pool Tobias has never won.

**The series engine.** Each cycle, Tobias and Saoirse test candidates against the working rule: replies that claimed failed, replies that showed use were answered. Each new object breaks part of it. Every ending changes who may sign or block the next reply.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Thumbprint Cup* — A clay cup bears a nine-thousand-year-old human thumbprint; Tobias catches a swapped replica by four grams and replies with Hana's mended bowl; the theft hands Voss a veto.
2. *The Broken Tally* — Half a tally stick has one notch more than recorded landings; Voss vetoes Tobias's miscounted reply until he carves a corrected half, and the log gains a landing.
3. *The Unworn Shoe* — A child's shoe in leather from no animal, the first gift made new; Tobias calls it a copy until Saoirse's stitch count proves otherwise; she sews its mate and co-signs.
4. *The Weighted Box* — An empty box, too heavy by the mass of Earth's last three replies; Tobias adds a cup Hana threw that week, which he cannot authenticate.

*Running thread:* Why the senders hold a human cup. Book 4: it was an earlier human reply; they want things made now, not kept. Tobias writes to his daughter. Who the senders are stays open.

**Why readers come back.** Each book hands readers one object and every clue Tobias has, so they can beat him to the answer and bet with the crew. Every ending closes its case and yields one fact about the senders.

**What makes it different.** First contact through objects: no signal, no ship, no translated speech. The hero is an appraiser with loupe and scale; Earth's replies come from a cook's kiln.
