# In Kind

**Genre:** First-contact science fiction · **Readers:** Women and men 30–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete exchange per book

**Logline.** Every four hundred days a capsule lands on a sub-Antarctic island carrying one object from unseen senders. Tobias Eze, 58, customs appraiser, reads it like a suspect antique and has forty days to choose Earth's reply; his last cost 1,200 days of silence.

**The setting.** Thrale Island: black gravel, seals, and a weather station decommissioned in 1987. Capsules appear at dawn, cold, on no radar. A seed sample brought a living flower extinct since 1912. A flag brought three cycles of silence. Now nothing goes back unless made or mended on Thrale.

**The lead.** Tobias spent thirty years as a customs appraiser and museum registrar, reading wear, weight, repair and intent. He trusts provenance over making, so anything new reads as fake. He countersigned the flag over the station chief's written objection, and leaves his daughter's letters unanswered. He wants one reply he can stand behind; another silence closes the station. He sounds like a condition report.

**The cast.** Saoirse Lim, 31, textile conservator, who unpicks her own stitching nightly; Ingrid Haugen, 63, station chief, who hired Tobias and keeps the flower alive; Renata Voss, 44, treaty indemnity officer, who signed the flag payout; Hana Kovač, the cook, who throws pots from island clay and runs the betting pool Tobias has never won.

**The series engine.** Each book is one cycle and ends with the reply sent. Tobias and Saoirse test each reply against every past result: replies that claimed failed, replies that showed use were answered. He asks where a thing has been; she asks what they can make. Every ending changes the station's rules.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Thumbprint Cup* — A clay cup bears a nine-thousand-year-old human thumbprint; a treaty curator swaps in a replica to keep it on Earth; Tobias catches the four-gram difference and answers with a much-mended bowl from Hana's kitchen. The theft hands Voss a veto.
2. *The Split Tally* — Half a tally stick, one notch per landing. Voss will insure only a final reply, and her audit catches his miscounted notch; Tobias signs alone for a corrected driftwood half, and the next capsule lands forty days early.
3. *The Unworn Shoe* — A child's shoe, the first gift made rather than kept. Tobias calls it a copy; Saoirse's stitch count proves him wrong, and Ingrid takes joint signature on the reply.
4. *The Heavy Box* — An empty box, too heavy by exactly the mass of Earth's last three replies; ordered to cut it open, Tobias fills it, last with a cup Hana threw that week, her thumbprint fresh, which he cannot authenticate. The next capsule lands on time: an uncut tally.

*Running thread:* What the senders want, and why they hold a human cup. Book 4 reads four gifts as one message; how the cup reached them stays open.

**Why readers come back.** Each book hands the reader one strange object and every clue Tobias has, so they can beat him to the answer and bet on the reply with the crew. Every ending closes its case and adds one piece to the message.

**What makes it different.** First contact through objects: no signal, no ship, no translated speech. The hero is a registrar with a loupe and a scale, and Earth's replies come out of a cook's kiln.
