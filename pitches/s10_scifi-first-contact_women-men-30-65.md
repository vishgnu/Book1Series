# In Kind

**Genre:** First-contact science fiction · **Readers:** Women and men 30–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete exchange per book

**Logline.** Every four hundred days a capsule lands on a sub-Antarctic island carrying one object from senders nobody has seen. Tobias Eze, 58, former customs appraiser, has forty days to choose Earth's reply; his last one cost 1,200 days of silence.

**The setting.** Thrale Island: black gravel, seals, and a weather station decommissioned in 1987. Capsules appear at dawn, cold, on no radar. One object arrives; one goes back within forty days. A seed sample brought a living flower extinct since 1912. A flag brought three cycles of silence. Now nothing goes back unless made or mended on Thrale.

**The lead.** Tobias spent thirty years as a customs appraiser and museum registrar, reading wear, weight, repair and intent. He countersigned the flag over the station chief's written objection. He wants one reply he can stand behind; another silence and the treaty states close the station. He sounds like a condition report.

**The cast.** Saoirse Lim, 31, a textile conservator who trusts a stitch count over his instinct; Ingrid Haugen, 63, station chief, her objection still on file; Renata Voss, 44, treaty indemnity officer, who prices every reply; Hana Kovač, the cook, who throws pots from island clay and runs the betting pool Tobias has never won.

**The series engine.** Each book is one cycle and ends with the reply sent. Tobias and Saoirse test each reply against every past result: replies that claimed failed, replies that showed use were answered. Every ending changes the station's rules, and the tally counts down to book 4.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Thumbprint Cup* — A clay cup bears a nine-thousand-year-old human thumbprint; Tobias catches a replica swap by a four-gram difference and answers with a much-mended bowl from Hana's kitchen. The theft hands Voss a veto.
2. *The Split Tally* — Half a tally stick, one notch per landing, two spaces left. Voss will indemnify only a closing receipt; Tobias signs alone for a driftwood half notched to fit, and the next capsule lands forty days early.
3. *The Unworn Shoe* — A child's shoe, the first gift made rather than kept. Tobias calls it a copy; Saoirse's stitch count proves him wrong, and Ingrid takes joint signature on the reply.
4. *The Heavy Box* — An empty box, too heavy by exactly the mass of Earth's last three replies; ordered to cut it open, Tobias fills it, last with a cup Hana threw that week, her thumbprint fresh. The next capsule lands on time: an uncut tally.

*Running thread:* What the senders want, and why they hold a human cup. Book 4 reads four gifts as one message: we kept yours, the account is open, we made this, fill it. The cup was taken from use; its route stays theirs.

**Why readers come back.** Each book hands the reader one strange object and every clue Tobias has, so they can beat him to the answer and bet on the reply. Every ending closes its case and adds one piece to the message.

**What makes it different.** First contact through objects: no signal, no ship, no translated speech. The hero is a registrar with a loupe and a scale, and Earth's replies come out of a cook's kiln.
