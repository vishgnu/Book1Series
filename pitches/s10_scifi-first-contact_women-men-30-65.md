# In Kind

**Genre:** First-contact science fiction · **Readers:** Women and men 30–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete exchange per book

**Logline.** Every four hundred days a capsule lands on a sub-Antarctic island carrying one object from unseen senders. Tobias Eze, 58, customs appraiser, reads it like a suspect antique and has forty days to choose Earth's reply; his last cost 1,200 days of silence.

**The setting.** Thrale Island: black gravel, seals, and a weather station decommissioned in 1987. Capsules appear at dawn, cold, on no radar. A farmer's saved seed brought a living flower extinct since 1912. A flag brought three cycles of silence. Now nothing goes back unless made or mended on Thrale, and a navy waits offshore.

**The lead.** Tobias spent thirty years as a customs appraiser, reading wear, weight, repair and intent. He trusts provenance over making, so anything new reads as fake. He chose the flag, and leaves his daughter's letters unanswered. He wants one reply he can stand behind. He sounds like a condition report: he calls his marriage "stable, with old repairs."

**The cast.** Saoirse Lim, 31, textile conservator, who unpicks her own stitching nightly; Ingrid Haugen, 63, station chief, who argued against the flag and keeps the flower alive; Renata Voss, 44, treaty indemnity officer, who paid for the flag and bets against him; Hana Kovač, the cook, who throws pots from island clay and runs the betting pool Tobias has never won.

**The series engine.** Tobias and Saoirse test each reply against every past result: replies that claimed failed, replies that showed use were answered. He asks where a thing has been; she asks what they can make. Every ending changes the station's rules.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Thumbprint Cup* — A clay cup bears a nine-thousand-year-old human thumbprint. A treaty curator swaps in a replica; Tobias catches the four-gram difference and answers with a much-mended bowl from Hana's kitchen. The theft hands Voss a veto.
2. *The Split Tally* — Half a tally stick, one notch per landing, two spaces left. Voss's recount catches Tobias's miscounted notch; he carves a corrected driftwood half, and the next capsule lands forty days early, the senders' first hurry.
3. *The Unworn Shoe* — A child's shoe in leather from no animal, the first gift made rather than kept. Tobias calls it a copy; Saoirse's stitch count proves him wrong; she sews its mate and signs the reply beside him.
4. *The Heavy Box* — An empty box, too heavy by exactly the mass of Earth's last three replies; Tobias fills it, last with a cup Hana threw that week, her thumbprint fresh, which he cannot authenticate. The next capsule lands on time: an uncut tally, a new count. He answers his daughter.

*Running thread:* What the senders want, and why they hold a human cup. Book 4 answers: the cup was an earlier reply, and the four gifts ask for things made now, not kept. Who sent it stays open.

**Why readers come back.** Each book hands the reader one strange object and every clue Tobias has, so they can beat him to the answer and bet on the reply with the crew. Every ending closes its case.

**What makes it different.** First contact through objects: no signal, no ship, no translated speech. The hero is an appraiser with a loupe and a scale, and Earth's replies come out of a cook's kiln.
