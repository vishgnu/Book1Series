# In Kind

**Genre:** First-contact science fiction · **Readers:** Women and men 30–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete exchange per book

**Logline.** Every four hundred days a capsule lands on a sub-Antarctic island carrying one object from senders nobody has seen. Senior Assessor Tobias Eze, 58, has forty days to read it and choose what Earth sends back.

**The setting.** Thrale Island, South Atlantic: black gravel, elephant seals, constant wind, a weather station decommissioned in 1987. The capsule lands beside its anemometer mast; twenty-two Exchange staff live in the huts. The rule: one object arrives, one goes back within forty days. Replies have consequences. A seed sample brought a living flower extinct since 1912. A flag brought three cycles of silence.

**The lead.** Tobias Eze, 58, was thirty years a customs appraiser and museum registrar. He reads wear, repair, provenance and intent the way a detective reads a room. He countersigned the flag and wants one reply he can stand behind. The island cost him his marriage. He sounds like a condition report: dry, exact.

**The cast.** Dr. Saoirse Lim, 31, a linguist certain the gifts have grammar; Ingrid Haugen, 63, station chief, fourteen months from pension; Colonel Dale Pruitt, military liaison, who wants the reply to be a warning; Hana Kovač, the cook, who throws pots from island clay.

**The series engine.** Each book is one cycle: a capsule lands, and Tobias and Saoirse work the gift from physical evidence inside forty days. Someone tries to steal, fake or hijack the reply; Tobias stops it with evidence, not force. Each ends with the gift understood and the reply sent. While Earth answers in kind, capsules keep coming.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Thumbprint Cup* — A clay cup bears a nine-thousand-year-old human thumbprint; Tobias exposes a replica swap and answers with a much-mended bowl.
2. *The Counting Clock* — A clock runs down to the reply deadline two cycles ahead, a term, not a threat; Tobias sends a chronometer, not Pruitt's shell casing.
3. *The Unworn Shoe* — A child's shoe, never worn, is the first gift made rather than kept; Tobias disproves an auditor's planted evidence of a hoax.
4. *The Heavy Box* — An empty box is too heavy by exactly the mass of Earth's last three replies; ordered to cut it open, Tobias fills it.

*Running thread:* The gifts form one sentence: we kept this, the keeping has a term, for those who come after, tell us what to hold next. Hana's new cup, with a fresh thumbprint, answers it and opens a second term.

**Why readers come back.** Each book hands the reader one strange object and every clue Tobias has, so they can reach the answer a page before he does. The aliens never explain themselves. Every ending closes its case and adds a clause to the larger sentence.

**What makes it different.** First contact conducted through objects: no signal, no ship, no translated speech. The hero is a 58-year-old registrar with a loupe and a scale; the aliens prove to be keepers, like him.
