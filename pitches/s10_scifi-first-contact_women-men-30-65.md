# In Kind

**Genre:** First-contact science fiction · **Readers:** Women and men 30–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete exchange per book

**Logline.** Every four hundred days a capsule lands on a sub-Antarctic island carrying one object from senders nobody has seen. Tobias Eze, 58, a former customs appraiser, has forty days to read it like a suspect antique, by wear, weight and repair, and choose Earth's reply.

**The setting.** Thrale Island, South Atlantic: a weather station decommissioned in 1987, where twenty-two Exchange staff live in the huts. One object arrives; one goes back within forty days. A seed sample brought a living flower extinct since 1912. A flag brought three cycles of silence. Since the flag, nothing goes back unless made or mended on Thrale.

**The lead.** Tobias spent thirty years as a customs appraiser and museum registrar, reading wear, repair, provenance and intent. He countersigned the flag over the station chief's written objection and has not left Thrale since. He wants one reply he can stand behind; another silence and the nine treaty states close the station. He sounds like a condition report: dry, exact.

**The cast.** Dr. Saoirse Lim, 31, a textile conservator who trusts a stitch count over his instinct; Ingrid Haugen, 63, station chief, whose objection to the flag is still on file; Renata Voss, 44, treaty indemnity officer, who prices every reply and can withhold it; Hana Kovač, the cook, who throws pots from island clay and runs the betting pool Tobias has never won.

**The series engine.** Each book is one cycle: Tobias and Saoirse work the gift from physical evidence, then test a reply against every past result: replies that claimed failed, replies that showed use were answered. The pressure changes each time: a theft, a staff split, Tobias's own wrong reading, a direct order.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Thumbprint Cup* — A clay cup bears a nine-thousand-year-old human thumbprint; Tobias catches a replica swap by a four-gram difference and answers with a much-mended bowl from Hana's kitchen.
2. *The Split Tally* — Half a tally stick, notched by two hands; the staff divide over bill or receipt until Saoirse matches the cuts to past landings, and Tobias sends a driftwood half notched to fit.
3. *The Unworn Shoe* — A never-worn child's shoe, the first gift made rather than kept. Tobias calls it a copy; Saoirse's stitch evidence proves him wrong, and Ingrid stakes her post on the corrected reply.
4. *The Heavy Box* — An empty box, too heavy by exactly the mass of Earth's last three replies; ordered to cut it open, Tobias fills it, last with a cup Hana threw that week, her thumbprint fresh.

*Running thread:* Four gifts, one message: what the senders ask of Earth, and how a human cup reached them. Book 4 answers the first; the second stays theirs.

**Why readers come back.** Each book hands the reader one strange object and every clue Tobias has, so they can beat him to the answer and bet on the reply with the crew. The aliens never explain themselves; every ending closes its case and adds one piece to the message.

**What makes it different.** First contact through objects: no signal, no ship, no translated speech. The hero is a registrar with a loupe and a scale, and Earth's replies come out of a cook's kiln.
