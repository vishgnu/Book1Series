# The Thrale Landings

**Genre:** First-contact science fiction · **Readers:** Women and men 30–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete exchange per book

**Logline.** Every four hundred days a capsule lands on a sub-Antarctic island with one object inside. Customs appraiser Tobias Eze, 58, has forty days to choose Earth's reply, and no idea why the senders kept a human cup.

**The setting.** Thrale Island: black gravel, seals, a seam of red clay under the weather station. Earth sends a reply; the senders answer with a new object or an empty capsule. A farmer's seed was answered with a flower extinct since 1912. A flag got three empty capsules, and half the treaty nations left. One more empty closes the station.

**The lead.** Tobias spent thirty years appraising seized antiquities by wear and weight. He trusts a thing's history, not its making, so anything new reads as fake. The flag was his choice. He told his daughter, a shoemaker, she sells copies; her letters sit unanswered. He talks like a condition report: his marriage is "stable, with old repairs."

**The cast.** Saoirse Lim, 31, textile conservator, unpicks her own stitching nightly and asks what they can make. Renata Voss, 44, treaty indemnity officer, demoted for insuring the flag. Hana Kovač, 52, cook, throws pots and runs the betting pool Tobias has never won.

**The series engine.** Tobias and Saoirse test each candidate against one rule: the senders answer what has been used, never what claims. Each new object breaks part of it. Every ending changes who may sign or block the next reply.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Thumbprint Cup* — A clay cup bears a nine-thousand-year-old thumbprint; Tobias catches a swapped replica by four grams; Hana's mended bowl is answered, so repair counts; Renata gains a veto.
2. *The Broken Tally* — Half a tally stick has one notch more than the station's logged landings; Tobias finds the unrecorded one and carves its match, proving they keep count; Renata lifts her veto.
3. *The Unworn Shoe* — A child's shoe, never worn, in leather from no animal; Tobias calls it a copy until Saoirse's stitch count proves the senders made it; she sews its mate and co-signs.
4. *The Weighted Box* — An empty box, too heavy by the mass of Earth's last three replies; Renata rules it the closing empty; Tobias answers anyway with Hana's new cup, which he cannot authenticate.

*Running thread:* Why the senders kept a human cup. Book 4: it was an earlier human reply; they answer what is made or grown now, not what is kept. Who they are stays open. Tobias writes to his daughter.

**Why readers come back.** Each book hands readers one object and every clue Tobias has, so they can beat him to the answer and bet with the crew. Every ending yields one fact about the senders.

**What makes it different.** First contact through objects: no signal, no ship, no translated speech. The hero is an appraiser with loupe and scale; Earth's replies are made by hand on the island. The senders never explain themselves.
