# The Thrale Landings

**Genre:** First-contact science fiction · **Readers:** Women and men 30–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete exchange per book

**Logline.** Every four hundred days a capsule lands on a sub-Antarctic island carrying one object from unseen senders. Tobias Eze, 58, customs appraiser, has forty days to choose Earth's reply and learn why they hold a human cup.

**The setting.** Thrale Island: black gravel, seals, an old weather station. Capsules appear at dawn, cold, on no radar. A farmer's saved seed brought a living flower extinct since 1912, and forty nations joined the treaty. A flag brought three cycles of silence, and half left. Now nothing goes back unless made or mended on Thrale, and another silence closes the station.

**The lead.** Tobias spent thirty years as a customs appraiser, reading wear, weight, repair and intent. He trusts provenance over making, so anything new reads as fake. He chose the flag, and leaves his daughter's letters unanswered. He wants one reply he can stand behind. He sounds like a condition report: he calls his marriage "stable, with old repairs."

**The cast.** Saoirse Lim, 31, textile conservator, who unpicks her own stitching nightly; Renata Voss, 44, treaty indemnity officer, whose office repays the nations if a reply fails; Hana Kovač, the cook, who throws pots from island clay and runs the betting pool Tobias has never won.

**The series engine.** Each book is one cycle and ends with the reply sent. Tobias and Saoirse test each candidate against every past result: replies that claimed failed, replies that showed use were answered. Tobias signs, Voss's office insures, and every ending changes who may sign or block the next reply.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Thumbprint Cup* — A clay cup bears a nine-thousand-year-old human thumbprint; Tobias catches a swapped replica by four grams and replies with Hana's mended bowl, and the theft hands Voss a veto.
2. *The Broken Tally* — Half a tally stick, one notch per landing; Voss vetoes Tobias's miscounted reply until he carves a corrected half, and the next capsule comes forty days early.
3. *The Unworn Shoe* — A child's shoe in leather from no animal, the first gift made new; Tobias calls it a copy, Saoirse proves him wrong, sews its mate and co-signs.
4. *The Weighted Box* — An empty box, too heavy by exactly the mass of Earth's last three replies; Tobias adds a cup Hana threw that week, which he cannot authenticate.

*Running thread:* Why the senders hold a human cup. Book 4 answers: it was an earlier human reply, and they want things made now, not kept; Tobias answers his daughter. Who sent it stays open.

**Why readers come back.** Each book hands the reader one strange object and every clue Tobias has, so they can beat him to the answer and bet on the reply with the crew. Every ending closes its case and yields one new fact about the senders.

**What makes it different.** First contact through objects: no signal, no ship, no translated speech. The hero is an appraiser with a loupe and a scale, and Earth's replies come out of a cook's kiln.
