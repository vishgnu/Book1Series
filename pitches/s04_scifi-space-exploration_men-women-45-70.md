# The Closed Worlds

**Genre:** Science fiction (space exploration) · **Readers:** Men and women 45–70 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete posting per book

**Logline.** June Iwasaki, 66, ecologist, and her husband Walt, 68, mining geologist, are world-sitters: the only two people on each closed planet, for one local year. Each year the logbooks and June's count disagree.

**The setting.** The Reserve Service keeps sixty-three living worlds closed. Each has a hut, a skiff, a year of sealed stores and a shelf of paper logbooks. The rule: nothing brought in, nothing taken out. The relief ship is six weeks away. Any departure from standing orders is logged as a breach under both names; a fourth ends their postings unless the review board strikes one.

**The lead.** At twenty-four June watched threads climb from a lifeless salt lake. Her recorder caught nothing, her chief wrote "mirage," and she taught for thirty years. She wants one more look, with a witness, yet trusts no count but her own, Walt's included. She counts twice and talks like a field note: dry, exact, funny.

**The cast.** Walt, forty-one years married, won't sign for what he hasn't measured, so has never said whether he believes her. Odalys Brandt, 52, postings officer, sends June to logbooks nobody audits. Emil Strand, 79, wrote "mirage" because the recorder was blank, would again, and chairs the review board.

**The series engine.** Every book is one posting: one marvel, and one place where the logbooks and June's count disagree. Walt mends, June waits and counts, and each book one of them is wrong. Each posting is settled before the relief ship lands and leaves one new lead toward June's lake.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Thirty-Year Reef* — Orrin's under-ice reef blooms nine years early; Walt seals the tide crack as ordered, the reef dims, and he dives to reopen it, breach one.
2. *The Walking Forest* — Tamsin's forest walks eight meters a year after groundwater and has stopped; orders say water it, but logbooks show it stops to seed, breach two.
3. *The Night Singer* — Calder's logbooks describe a night chorus no recorder holds; June proves the recorder filters song as noise and refuses a collector's permit, breach three.
4. *The Quiet Basin* — On Selah, June's salt lake, Walt's levels show a survey well draining it; they cap it, breach four, in front of Strand, who stands beside Walt as the threads rise.

*Running thread:* what June saw at twenty-four, and whether anyone else can be shown it. Book 4: June lets Walt's numbers stand; two witnesses, "mirage" and one breach struck, and a name: the lake's dormant life leaving on silk, once in forty-two years. Eleven lakes unchecked.

**Why readers come back.** A new world every book, a marriage that argues well, and a puzzle solved with evidence to a finished ending. Each book also brings June one lead closer to her lake and one breach closer to losing the work.

**What makes it different.** A couple in their sixties doing custodial work: counting, mending, writing it down. Each planet is solved by reading decades of other couples' handwriting. Nothing gets conquered, settled or shot.
