# The World-Sitters

**Genre:** Science fiction (space exploration) · **Readers:** Men and women 45–70 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete posting per book

**Logline.** June Iwasaki, 66, ecologist, and her husband Walt, 68, mining geologist, are world-sitters: the only two people on each closed planet, for one local year. At twenty-four her first count was filed as "mirage."

**The setting.** The Reserve Service keeps sixty-three living worlds closed to settlement, mining and tourism. Each has a hut, a skiff and a shelf of paper logbooks. The rule: nothing brought in, nothing taken out. Help is six weeks away, and every fix is logged as a breach under their names.

**The lead.** At twenty-four June watched threads climb from a lifeless salt lake at dawn. Her recorder caught nothing, her chief wrote "mirage," and she taught for thirty years. She wants one more look, with a witness. She counts twice and talks like a field note: dry, exact, funny.

**The cast.** Walt, forty-one years married, who has never said whether he believes her. Odalys Brandt, 52, postings officer, who gives June the worlds nobody wants. Maren, 11, the granddaughter whose messages arrive in batches. Emil Strand, 79, who wrote "mirage" because the recorder was blank, would again, and now chairs her review board.

**The series engine.** Every book is one posting: one marvel, and one place where the logbooks and June's count disagree. Two people with a rock hammer and a year measure, argue and decide whether to touch it. Odalys sends them wherever the logs stop adding up.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Thirty-Year Reef* — Under Orrin's ice a reef grows once in thirty years, now blooming nine years early. Walt shutters the tide crack, a logged breach, and it blooms on; growth bands in the rock show every third cycle runs early. Life can sleep for decades.
2. *The Walking Forest* — Tamsin's forest walks eight meters a year after groundwater, and has stopped. The Service orders it watered; the logbooks show it stopped once before, to seed. They wait. A forest can move for water; Walt starts looking too.
3. *The Night Singer* — Calder's logbooks describe a night chorus no recorder holds; a collector lands with a permit for one singer. June proves the Service recorder filters the song as noise and the chorus fails a voice short. Strand requests her blank recording.
4. *The Quiet Basin* — On Selah a warden wrote "threads at dawn" forty-two years before June. The lake must brim by this year's dawn; a forgotten Service well has bled it for decades. They cap it, their largest breach, and Strand, come early to close her file, stands beside Walt as the threads rise.

*Running thread:* what June saw at twenty-four, and whether anyone else can be shown it. Book 4: two witnesses, "mirage" struck, and a name: the lake's dormant life leaving on silk, once in forty-two years. Eleven lakes unchecked.

**Why readers come back.** A new world every book, through expert eyes. A marriage that works. A puzzle solved with evidence and a finished ending. Leads the reader's own age.

**What makes it different.** Retired grandparents doing custodial work: counting, mending, writing it down. Each planet is solved by reading decades of other couples' handwriting. Nothing gets conquered, settled or shot.
