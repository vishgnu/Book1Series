# The Closed Worlds

**Genre:** Science fiction (space exploration) · **Readers:** Men and women 45–70 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete posting per book

**Logline.** June Iwasaki, 66, and her husband Walt, 68, are world-sitters: the only people on each closed planet for one local year. Each world holds a marvel gone wrong, and one clue to what June saw at twenty-four.

**The setting.** The Reserve Service keeps sixty-three living worlds closed, each with a hut and a shelf of paper logbooks. Relief is six weeks away; messages go monthly. Any departure from standing orders is a breach. Four breaches and the sitters are sent home for good; only the review board can cancel one.

**The lead.** At twenty-four June, an ecologist, watched threads climb from a lifeless salt lake. Her recorder caught nothing, her chief wrote "mirage," and she taught thirty years. She wants one more look, with a witness, yet trusts no count but her own. She talks in field notes: "Reef light down 40 percent since Walt's repair. The repair is excellent."

**The cast.** Walt, mining geologist, forty-one years married, won't sign for what he hasn't measured, so has never said whether he believes her; he answers in questions: "Forty percent of what?" Odalys Brandt, 52, postings officer, sends them to worlds whose logbooks nobody has checked, before the Service pulps them. Emil Strand, 79, June's old chief, chairs the review board.

**The series engine.** Something goes wrong; Walt mends, June waits and counts, and the answer lies in decades of other couples' handwriting, checked against rock and water. In each book June or Walt is proved wrong, and one entry points toward June's lake.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Thirty-Year Reef* — On Orrin an under-ice reef blooms nine years early; Walt seals the tide crack as ordered, learns early blooms are normal, and reopens it: breach one.
2. *The Walking Forest* — On Tamsin a forest that walks after groundwater has stopped; June waters one grove, it alone fails to seed, and the board rewrites the order: no breach.
3. *The Night Singer* — On Calder the logbooks describe a night chorus no recorder holds; June finds the recorder filters song as noise, rewires it and won't restore it: breaches two and three.
4. *The Quiet Basin* — On Selah, June's salt lake, Walt's levels show a survey well draining the basin; they cap it with Strand watching: breach four.

*Running thread:* can anyone else be shown what June saw? In book 3 a warden's entry, "threads at dawn," points to Selah. In book 4 June lets Walt's numbers stand, and three people watch dormant brine life hatch and leave on silk, once in forty-two years. Strand still calls "mirage" right for a blank recorder, and cancels the fourth breach: the Service drilled the well itself.

**Why readers come back.** A new world each time, a marriage that argues well, a puzzle closed by evidence, and a breach count that rises when they're right and holds when they're wrong.

**What makes it different.** A couple in their sixties doing custodial work: counting, mending, writing it down. Nothing gets conquered, settled or shot.
