# The Hut Warden Mysteries

**Genre:** Traditional mystery (Alpine setting) · **Readers:** Women and men 45–70 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** At a Swiss alpine hut 2,740 meters up, warden Ursi Feuz, 52, must find the killer among the names in the hut book before police can climb up, while feeding, bunking and roping down every guest, the killer included.

**The setting.** The Schwarzhorn Hut, Bernese Oberland: 34 bunks, one stove, one radio. Guests walk up four hours and sign the hut book. When the path closes or the helicopter cannot fly, police are a day away, and by custom the warden answers for everyone under her roof.

**The lead.** Ursi worked the rescue winch twenty years until her knee ended it; the hut is now her only address. The alpine club gave her one trial year, and every death counts against her. In 2019 she took another's head count and winched a guest off too late; now she counts everyone herself at dark. She talks short and reads guests by what does not fit.

**The cast.** Res von Allmen, 57, supply pilot, flew the 2019 mission and has never said whose count it was. Nora Gilgen, 22, geology student, argues about everything but evidence. Wachtmeister Beat Zumbrunn, valley sergeant, can open no case without evidence, so he needs her log. Vreni Kaufmann, 71, the last warden's widow, kept the hut book thirty years and trades each answer for a favor.

**The series engine.** Each book opens with a hut book page. One death passes for an accident until Ursi reads boot rack, bed plan and radio log. She cannot hold anyone or lock anyone out; she logs times and statements. New cases come because people with a stake in an old entry climb up to read it.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The August Page* — August: a storm holds eleven guests; a notary carrying a 1961 estate paper lies below the terrace, his boots dry on the rack.
2. *The Closing Party* — October: snow shuts in eight club volunteers; the treasurer dies in the winter room, its flue capped a day early. Res flew the cap up; Ursi logs him too.
3. *The Winter Room* — March: danger level four pins six tourers; guide Lukas Morf, Emil's grandson, lies buried, transceiver off, though Ursi's door check logged it sending. Ursi lets Nora's count stand; Zumbrunn opens a linked file.
4. *The Hut Book* — June: the club audits the hut books before voting on Ursi; the archivist dies on the cellar stairs and the 1961 volume vanishes. Without evidence Zumbrunn must file a fall; Ursi has sixty August pages.

*Running thread:* In 1961 a rope of three left; two came back. Emil Morf did not, yet "E. Morf" is signed every August in the same hand, shakier each decade. Each victim came for those pages; book 4 names the signer, has Res answer for 2019 and settles whether Ursi keeps the hut.

**Why readers come back.** A complete puzzle in every book, solvable from clues on the page; no cliffhanger, no gore. Rope and weather done right, rösti on the stove, a capable woman doing a hard job well.

**What makes it different.** The suspect list is an object: the hut book page. The detective is the host, and her duty of care covers the killer too: she cooks breakfast for the person she is about to name.
