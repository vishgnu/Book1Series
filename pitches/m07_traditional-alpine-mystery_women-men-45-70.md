# The Alpine Hut Mysteries

**Genre:** Traditional mystery (Alpine setting) · **Readers:** Women and men 45–70 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** At a Swiss hut 2,740 meters up, warden Ursi Feuz, 52, must find the killer among the hut book's names before police can climb up, while feeding and bunking every guest, the killer included.

**The setting.** The Schwarzhorn Hut, Bernese Oberland, is a staffed mountain refuge: 34 bunks, one stove, one radio. Guests walk up four hours and sign the hut book, which never leaves the hut. When weather shuts path and helicopter, police are a day away.

**The lead.** Twenty years on the rescue winch ended with Ursi's knee. The club gave her one trial year: a fatal accident ends it, a proven murder does not. So she logs what points to accident first. In 2019 she trusted another's count of five on a ledge; the sixth died overnight. Now she counts herself, at dark. She talks short: "Guests lie about fitness and boots."

**The cast.** Res von Allmen, supply pilot, flew in 2019 and has never said whose count it was. Nora Gilgen, hut assistant, argues about everything but evidence. Wachtmeister Beat Zumbrunn takes from her log only what he can check.

**The series engine.** A dead man has signed the hut book every August for sixty years. Each book, someone climbs up to read the 1961 page, the only proof of who owns the alp below, and does not walk down. Ursi proves murder from boot rack, bed plan and radio log. Suspects are the book's new signers; regulars are witnesses with gaps. She can hold no one, only log.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Death on the August Page* — August storm: a notary lies below the terrace, boots dry on the rack; this year's "E. Morf" is fresh ink.
2. *Death at the Closing Party* — October snow: the treasurer dies in the winter room, its flue capped a day early; Ursi logs Res too; the prosecutor seizes the 1961 volume.
3. *Death at Level Four* — March, avalanche level four: guide Lukas Morf, Emil's grandson, lies buried, transceiver off, though Ursi's door check logged it sending; police post an officer.
4. *Death Under the Last Signature* — June fog before the club votes on Ursi: the court returns the volume, the archivist carrying it dies on the cellar stairs, and the volume vanishes.

*Running thread:* In 1961 a rope of three came back as two, without Emil Morf, yet "E. Morf" is still signed in the same hand, shakier each decade. Book 4 names the signer, shows whose 2019 count failed, and settles whether Ursi keeps the hut.

**Why readers come back.** A complete puzzle per book, solvable from the page; no cliffhanger, no gore. Each case adds one checkable fact about the signature, the reason to borrow the next. Rope and weather done right, rösti on the stove, a capable woman at work.

**What makes it different.** The suspect list is an object: the hut book page. The detective is the host: she cooks breakfast for the person she is about to name.
