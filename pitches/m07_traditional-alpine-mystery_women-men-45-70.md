# The Hut Warden Mysteries

**Genre:** Traditional mystery (Alpine setting) · **Readers:** Women and men 45–70 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** At a Swiss alpine hut 2,740 meters up, warden Ursi Feuz, 52, must find the killer among the names in the hut book before police can climb up, while feeding, bunking and roping down every guest, the killer included.

**The setting.** The Schwarzhorn Hut, Bernese Oberland: 34 bunks, one stove, one radio. Guests walk up four hours and sign the hut book, which by club rule never leaves the hut. When the path closes and the helicopter cannot fly, police are a day away, and the warden answers for everyone under her roof.

**The lead.** Ursi worked the rescue winch twenty years until her knee ended it. The club gave her one trial year: an accident counts against her, a proven killing does not. In 2019 she trusted another's head count, five on a ledge; the sixth died overnight. Now she counts everyone herself at dark. She talks short and reads guests by what does not fit.

**The cast.** Res von Allmen, 57, supply pilot, flew in 2019, has never said whose count it was, and brings her the crossword. Nora Gilgen, 22, hut assistant, argues about everything but evidence. Wachtmeister Beat Zumbrunn needs her log, or the prosecutor rules accident. Vreni Kaufmann, 71, kept the hut book thirty years and trades answers for favors.

**The series engine.** One death passes for an accident until Ursi reads boot rack, bed plan and radio log. She cannot hold anyone or lock anyone out; she logs times and statements. Suspects are the hut book's new signers; regulars are witnesses with gaps. Cases come because an estate, a lease and a family name rest on the 1961 page, readable only here.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The August Page* — August: a storm holds eleven guests; a notary carrying a 1961 estate paper lies below the terrace, his boots dry on the rack.
2. *The Closing Party* — October: snow shuts in eight volunteers; the treasurer dies in the winter room, its flue capped a day early. Res flew the cap up; Ursi logs him too.
3. *The Winter Room* — March: danger level four pins six tourers; guide Lukas Morf lies buried, transceiver off, though Ursi's door check logged it sending: someone beside him switched it off.
4. *The Hut Book* — June: late snow and fog seal the hut before the club votes on Ursi; the archivist dies on the cellar stairs and the 1961 volume vanishes. The prosecutor expects a fall; Ursi has sixty August pages.

*Running thread:* In 1961 a rope of three left; two came back. Emil Morf did not, yet "E. Morf" is signed every August in the same hand, shakier each decade; wardens thought it a memorial. Book 4 names the signer, has Res answer for 2019 and settles whether Ursi keeps the hut.

**Why readers come back.** A complete puzzle in every book, solvable from clues on the page; no cliffhanger, no gore. Rope and weather done right, rösti on the stove, a capable woman doing a hard job well.

**What makes it different.** The suspect list is an object: the hut book page. The detective is the host, and her duty of care covers the killer too: she cooks breakfast for the person she is about to name.
