# The Hut Warden Mysteries

**Genre:** Traditional mystery (Alpine setting) · **Readers:** Women and men 45–70 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** At a Swiss alpine hut 2,740 meters up, warden Ursi Feuz, 52, must find the killer among the names in the hut book before police can climb up, while feeding, bunking and roping down every guest, the killer included.

**The setting.** The Schwarzhorn Hut, Bernese Oberland: 34 bunks, one stove, one radio. Guests walk up four hours and sign the hut book, which by club rule never leaves the hut. When the path closes and the helicopter cannot fly, police are a day away.

**The lead.** Ursi worked the rescue winch twenty years until her knee ended it. The club gave her one trial year: a fatal accident ends it, a proven murder does not. In 2019 she trusted another's head count, five on a ledge; the sixth died overnight. Now she counts everyone herself at dark. She talks short: "Guests lie about two things, fitness and boots."

**The cast.** Res von Allmen, 57, supply pilot, flew in 2019, has never said whose count it was, and brings her the crossword. Nora Gilgen, 22, hut assistant, argues about everything but evidence. Wachtmeister Beat Zumbrunn needs her log, or the prosecutor rules accident; her job rides on murder, so he takes only what he can check. Vreni Kaufmann, 71, the last warden's widow, trades hut book answers for favors.

**The series engine.** One death passes for an accident until Ursi reads boot rack, bed plan and radio log. She cannot hold anyone or lock anyone out; she logs times and statements. Cases come because only the 1961 page records who roped up with Emil Morf, whose alp the survivor's family took and the hut now leases. Courts accept only the original, so claimants climb up.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The August Page* — August: a storm holds eleven guests; a notary sent to read the 1961 page lies below the terrace, his boots dry on the rack.
2. *The Closing Party* — October: snow shuts in eight volunteers; the treasurer dies in the winter room, its flue capped a day early. Res flew the cap up; Ursi logs him too.
3. *The Winter Room* — March: danger level four pins six tourers; guide Lukas Morf, Emil's grandson, lies buried, transceiver off, though Ursi's door check logged it sending. Zumbrunn counts three deaths around one page.
4. *The Hut Book* — June: fog seals the hut before the club votes on Ursi; the archivist dies on the cellar stairs and the 1961 volume vanishes, leaving Ursi sixty Augusts of one signature.

*Running thread:* In 1961 a rope of three left; two came back. Emil Morf did not, yet "E. Morf" is signed every August in the same hand, shakier each decade; wardens thought it a memorial. Book 4 names the signer, proves from flight log and winch sheet whose 2019 count failed, and settles whether Ursi keeps the hut.

**Why readers come back.** A complete puzzle, solvable from clues on the page; no cliffhanger, no gore. Rope and weather done right, rösti on the stove, a capable woman doing a hard job well.

**What makes it different.** The suspect list is an object: the hut book page. The detective is the host, and her duty of care covers the killer too: she cooks breakfast for the person she is about to name.
