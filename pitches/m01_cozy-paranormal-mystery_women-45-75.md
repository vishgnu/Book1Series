# The Skarv Sound Piano Tuner Mysteries

**Genre:** Cozy paranormal mystery · **Readers:** Women 45–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Signe Halvorsen, 61, a widowed church organist, tunes pianos on six ferry-linked Salish Sea islands. At first touch she hears the last music played in anger, grief or fear; sometimes it contradicts a death certificate, and once she tunes, it is gone.

**The setting.** Skarv Sound, Washington: six cedar-dark islands, a shuttered cannery and one car ferry, the *Kittiwake*, four round trips a day. Nobody crosses unseen: the timetable fixes who was where, when. The mainland sheriff reopens no ruled death without physical evidence.

**The lead.** Signe drives her late husband Arne's tuning round in his 1989 Volvo with his cat, Purser. She wants to finish his round, not to ask why he stopped. She has not touched their parlor upright since he died. Perfect pitch, dry tongue. To Mateo: "Good. You still cannot count to four."

**The cast.** Britt Halvorsen, 66, Arne's sister, sells tickets and cardamom buns, his thermos behind the window; she and Signe bicker instead of crying. Mateo Ruiz, 26, deputy, tone-deaf, her worst pupil. Opal Tanaka, 58, deckhand, remembers every car, says little about Arne's. Desmond Pryce, 49, cheerful mainland rival, has no idea what he erases.

**The series engine.** Each piano holds one echo: the last time anyone played it in strong feeling, usually plain grief. A few bars and the touch of the hands, never words or faces. Nothing older than the last dated tuning card survives. Signe names the piece; hands and timetables do the proving. A tuner enters every house, and someone wants it tuned by Friday.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Discord in the Choir Loft* — Choir director Lenore Vance's death is ruled a fall; her grand holds a furious hymn in a left hand spanning a tenth, and Lenore reached an octave.
2. *Lullaby at Last Call* — A tavern owner drowns after the last ferry and gossip blames his widow; the bar piano's lullaby, heard by neighbors at ten, clears her.
3. *Abide with Malice* — Walter Coyle, 84, dies days after a new will cuts out his daughter; the same bars of "Abide with Me" linger in another late-will house.
4. *The Piano She Wouldn't Tune* — The cannery caretaker's fall repeats a bookkeeper's in 1987; Signe touches her parlor upright, hears Arne's frightened last waltz, forgives his silence and plays again.

*Running thread:* Why Arne drove to the ferry with no appointments, leaving eleven starred pianos untuned. Book 4: he heard echoes too, starred what he could not prove, and his heart failed crossing to tell the sheriff. Ten stars remain.

**Why readers come back.** A dry, clever woman over sixty, cross with the husband she misses. Ferries, rain, cardamom buns, Bach too loud in an empty church, and a fair puzzle. Each book ends one fact nearer Arne's last crossing.

**What makes it different.** The witness is a piano, and it testifies once. Signe destroys evidence by doing her job, so each book she must choose when to tune. No witches, no talking cat: one quiet psychic gift with strict limits.
