# The Tuner's Ear Mysteries

**Genre:** Cozy paranormal mystery · **Readers:** Women 45–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Signe Halvorsen, 61, a widowed church organist, tunes pianos on six ferry-linked Salish Sea islands. At first touch she hears the last music played in anger, grief or fear; sometimes it contradicts a death certificate, and once she tunes, it is gone.

**The setting.** Skarv Sound, Washington: six islands, 2,900 residents, a shuttered cannery and one car ferry, the *Kittiwake*, four round trips a day from Lund Harbor. Rain means every piano needs tuning twice yearly. The timetable fixes who was where, when.

**The lead.** Signe, organist at Lund Harbor Lutheran, drives her late husband Arne's tuning round in his 1989 Volvo wagon with his cat, Purser. Perfect pitch, dry tongue. To Mateo, after an arrest: "Good. You still cannot count to four."

**The cast.** Mateo Ruiz, 26, the islands' only deputy, tone-deaf and her worst pupil, checks her hunches against the vehicle log. Britt Halvorsen, 66, Arne's sister, sells tickets and cardamom buns dockside and criticizes Signe's driving. Opal Tanaka, 58, deckhand, remembers every car. Desmond Pryce, 49, cheerful mainland rival, has no idea what he erases.

**The series engine.** The echo is a few bars and the touch of the hands, never words or faces: a reach, a stumble, facts readers can match to suspects. A tuner is welcome in every house, so cases keep arriving. The gift only points; proof comes from timetables and gossip.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *A Wolf in the Choir Loft* — Choir director Lenore Vance, 79, dies on her stairs, ruled a fall; her grand holds a furious left hand spanning a tenth, and Signe measures hands at coffee hour before Desmond's Thursday booking. Opal recalls Arne in line for the cannery island.
2. *Lullaby at Last Call* — A tavern owner drowns after the last ferry; the bar piano's lullaby, in his widow's small hands and heard by neighbors at ten, clears her. One of four who missed the boat did it. Arne's mileage book shows unbilled calls.
3. *The Same Four Bars* — A summer resident dies in his sleep; Desmond tunes his piano first, but the same four bars survive on a piano Arne starred two islands away. One player, two houses; the vehicle log names the car. Desmond learns what he erases.
4. *The Piano She Wouldn't Tune* — Signe touches her parlor upright: Arne's last bars match the starred cannery-office piano, tying a bookkeeper's 1987 death to a new one. Ticket stubs convict; then she tunes the upright, her farewell.

*Running thread:* Why Arne drove to the ferry on a day without appointments, and why he left eleven starred pianos untuned. Book 4: he heard echoes too, and was going to tell Mateo when his heart failed. Ten stars remain.

**Why readers come back.** A dry, clever woman over sixty. Ferries, rain, cardamom buns and Bach in an empty church. A puzzle solvable with a timetable and a good ear. Each book returns a little more of Arne.

**What makes it different.** The witness is a piano, and it testifies once. Signe destroys evidence by doing her job, so each book she must choose when to tune.
