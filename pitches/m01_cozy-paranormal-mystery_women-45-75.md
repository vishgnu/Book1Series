# The Tuner's Ear Mysteries

**Genre:** Cozy paranormal mystery · **Readers:** Women 45–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Signe Halvorsen, 61, a widowed church organist, tunes pianos on six ferry-linked Salish Sea islands. At first touch she hears the last music played in anger, grief or fear; sometimes it contradicts a death certificate, and once she tunes, it is gone.

**The setting.** Skarv Sound, Washington: six cedar-dark islands, 2,900 residents, a shuttered cannery and one car ferry, the *Kittiwake*, four round trips a day from Lund Harbor. Nobody crosses unseen: the timetable fixes who was where, when. The mainland sheriff reopens no ruled death without physical evidence.

**The lead.** Signe drives her late husband Arne's tuning round in his 1989 Volvo with his cat, Purser. She wants to finish his round, not to ask why he stopped. She has not touched their parlor upright since he died. Perfect pitch, dry tongue. To Mateo: "Good. You still cannot count to four."

**The cast.** Mateo Ruiz, 26, resident deputy, tone-deaf, her worst pupil. Britt Halvorsen, 66, Arne's sister, sells tickets and cardamom buns, his thermos behind the window. Opal Tanaka, 58, deckhand, remembers every car. Desmond Pryce, 49, cheerful mainland rival, has no idea what he erases.

**The series engine.** Each piano holds one undated echo: the last time anyone played it in strong feeling, usually plain grief. A few bars and the touch of the hands, never words or faces. Signe can name the piece, so anyone can check whose hands fit; timetables do the proving. A tuner enters every house, and someone wants it tuned by Friday.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *A Wolf in the Choir Loft* — Choir director Lenore Vance, 79, dies on her stairs, ruled a fall. Her grand holds a hymn in a furious left hand spanning a tenth; Lenore reached an octave. Signe measures four choristers' hands at coffee hour before Desmond's Thursday booking.
2. *Lullaby at Last Call* — A tavern owner drowns after the last ferry. Neighbors heard the bar piano's lullaby at ten, in his widow's small hands, clearing her; of four stranded patrons, one bought a one-way ticket.
3. *The Same Four Bars* — No murder: Walter Coyle, 84, leaves a late will and four helpers with keys; Desmond tunes first. The same bars of "Abide with Me" survive on a piano Arne starred, in another late-will house. The vehicle log puts one witness off-island on signing day.
4. *The Piano She Wouldn't Tune* — The cannery caretaker's ruled fall repeats bookkeeper Ingrid Dahl's in 1987. Signe touches her parlor upright: Arne's last bars match the starred office piano's frightened waltz. A 1987 stub breaks one of four foremen's alibis. She forgives him and tunes the upright, her farewell.

*Running thread:* Why Arne drove to the ferry with no appointments and left eleven starred pianos untuned. Book 4: he heard echoes too, starred what he could not prove, and his heart failed crossing to tell the sheriff. Ten stars remain for later books.

**Why readers come back.** A dry, clever woman over sixty, cross with the husband she misses. Ferries, rain, cardamom buns, Bach too loud in an empty church, and a fair puzzle.

**What makes it different.** The witness is a piano, and it testifies once. Signe destroys evidence by doing her job, so each book she must choose when to tune.
