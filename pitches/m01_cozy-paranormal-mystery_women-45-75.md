# The Tuner's Ear Mysteries

**Genre:** Cozy paranormal mystery · **Readers:** Women 45–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Signe Halvorsen, 61, a widowed church organist, tunes pianos across six ferry-linked islands in Washington's Salish Sea. Each piano gives her, once, the last music played on it in strong feeling; sometimes it contradicts a death certificate.

**The setting.** Skarv Sound: six cedar-dark islands, 2,900 year-round residents and one car ferry, the *Kittiwake*, four round trips a day beginning with the 7:10 from Lund Harbor. Britt's Dock Bakery sells cardamom buns from 6:30. Constant rain means every piano needs tuning twice a year. Nobody crosses the water unseen: the timetable fixes who could have been on which island, and when.

**The lead.** Signe Halvorsen, 61, organist at Lund Harbor Lutheran, has taken over her late husband Arne's tuning round and his 1989 Volvo wagon. She wants to know why he died in the ferry line on a day without appointments. The cost: her work erases every echo she hears. She has perfect pitch and speaks in short, dry sentences.

**The cast.** Mateo Ruiz, 26, the islands' only deputy and her worst-ever pupil. Britt Halvorsen, 66, Arne's sister, who runs the dock bakery and hears everything first. Opal Tanaka, 58, *Kittiwake* deckhand, who remembers every car. Desmond Pryce, 49, a mainland tuner with no idea what he erases.

**The series engine.** When Signe's tuning lever first touches a piano, it returns a few bars and the touch of the hands: never words or faces. Tuning erases the echo for good. A tuner is welcome in every house, so each book brings a death ruled natural, one piano that disagrees, and proof built from timetables and gossip.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Wolf Interval* — Choir director Lenore Vance, 79, dies on her stairs; her grand returns a stranger's furious left hand, and Signe confides in Mateo.
2. *False Beats* — A tavern owner drowns after the last ferry; Signe misreads the bar piano's lullaby and learns echoes can mislead.
3. *Sympathetic Strings* — After a summer resident's death, pianos on two islands return the same four bars; Arne's ledger surfaces, eleven pianos starred.
4. *The Pitch Raise* — A starred piano links a cannery bookkeeper's death to a new one; to finish Arne's case, Signe tunes the parlor upright.

*Running thread:* What Arne played, his last morning, on the upright she refuses to tune, and why he then drove to the ferry; book 4 answers both, leaving ten starred pianos.

**Why readers come back.** A dry, clever woman over sixty who is welcome in every house. Ferries, rain, hymns and warm buns. A puzzle you can solve with a timetable and a good ear. Every case closes, and each book returns a little more of Arne.

**What makes it different.** The witness is a piano, and it testifies once. Signe destroys her evidence by doing her job, so the gift only points; proof still takes ferry tickets, neighbors and nerve, and the clue is always music.
