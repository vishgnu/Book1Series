# The Tuner's Ear Mysteries

**Genre:** Cozy paranormal mystery · **Readers:** Women 45–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Signe Halvorsen, 61, a widowed church organist, tunes pianos on six ferry-linked Salish Sea islands. At first touch she hears the last music played in anger, grief or fear; sometimes it contradicts a death certificate, and once she tunes, it is gone.

**The setting.** Skarv Sound, Washington: six cedar-dark islands, 2,900 residents, a shuttered cannery and one car ferry, the *Kittiwake*, four round trips a day from Lund Harbor. Rain means every piano needs tuning twice yearly. Nobody crosses unseen: the timetable fixes who was where, when.

**The lead.** Signe drives her late husband Arne's tuning round in his 1989 Volvo wagon with his cat, Purser. She wants to finish his round, not to ask why he stopped. She has not touched their parlor upright since he died. Perfect pitch, dry tongue. To Mateo, after an arrest: "Good. You still cannot count to four."

**The cast.** Mateo Ruiz, 26, sole deputy, tone-deaf, her worst pupil, checks her hunches against the vehicle log. Britt Halvorsen, 66, Arne's sister, sells tickets and cardamom buns, keeps his thermos behind the window and criticizes how Signe drives his Volvo. Opal Tanaka, 58, deckhand, remembers every car and kept quiet about Arne's. Desmond Pryce, 49, cheerful mainland rival, pays for his mother's care home and has no idea what he erases.

**The series engine.** The echo is a few bars and the touch of the hands, never words or faces: a reach, a stumble. A tuner is welcome in every house, so cases keep arriving, each with a customer who wants it tuned by Friday. The gift only points; proof comes from timetables and gossip.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *A Wolf in the Choir Loft* — Choir director Lenore Vance, 79, Signe's thorn, dies on her stairs, ruled a fall; her grand holds a furious left hand spanning a tenth. Signe measures hands at coffee hour before Desmond's Thursday booking, and hides why from Mateo.
2. *Lullaby at Last Call* — A tavern owner drowns after the last ferry; the bar piano's lullaby, in his widow's small hands, clears her. Signe tells Mateo what she hears; four stranded patrons remain. Opal admits Arne's odd crossings.
3. *The Same Four Bars* — A summer resident dies in his sleep and Desmond tunes first, but the same four bars survive on a piano Arne starred; the vehicle log names the driver. Signe fears the stars mean another woman. Desmond, told, starts phoning ahead.
4. *The Piano She Wouldn't Tune* — Signe touches her parlor upright: Arne's last bars match the starred cannery-office piano, tying a bookkeeper's 1987 death to a new one. Ticket stubs convict. She forgives him and tunes the upright, her farewell.

*Running thread:* Why Arne drove to the ferry on a day without appointments and left eleven starred pianos untuned. Book 4: he heard echoes too, and never told her. Ten stars remain.

**Why readers come back.** A dry, clever woman over sixty, cross with the husband she misses. Ferries, rain, cardamom buns, Bach too loud in an empty church. A puzzle solvable with a timetable and a good ear. Each book returns a little more of Arne.

**What makes it different.** The witness is a piano, and it testifies once. Signe destroys evidence by doing her job, so each book she must choose when to tune.
