# The Tuner's Ear Mysteries

**Genre:** Cozy paranormal mystery · **Readers:** Women 45–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Signe Halvorsen, 61, a widowed church organist, tunes pianos across six ferry-linked Salish Sea islands. At first touch she hears the last music played on each in anger, grief or fear; sometimes it contradicts a death certificate, and once she tunes, it is gone.

**The setting.** Skarv Sound, Washington: six cedar-dark islands, 2,900 residents, reef-net boats, strawberry fields, a shuttered cannery and one car ferry, the *Kittiwake*, four round trips a day from Lund Harbor. Rain means every piano needs tuning twice yearly. Nobody crosses unseen: the timetable fixes who was on which island, when.

**The lead.** Signe, organist at Lund Harbor Lutheran, has taken over her late husband Arne's tuning round and 1989 Volvo wagon. She wants to know why he died in the ferry line on a day without appointments. She has perfect pitch, plays Bach too loud in the empty church, and talks dry. To Mateo, after an arrest: "Good. You still cannot count to four."

**The cast.** Mateo Ruiz, 26, the islands' only deputy, tone-deaf and her worst pupil, tests her hunches against the *Kittiwake*'s vehicle log. Britt Halvorsen, 66, Arne's sister, sells tickets and freight at the dock booth, with cardamom buns from 6:30. Opal Tanaka, 58, deckhand from a strawberry-farming family, remembers every car. Desmond Pryce, 49, a cheerful mainland rival tuner, has no idea what he erases.

**The series engine.** The echo is a few bars and the touch of the hands, never words or faces, and tuning erases it for good. Each book brings a death ruled natural, a piano that disagrees, and someone who wants it tuned by Friday. The clue is always music; proof comes from timetables and gossip.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *A Wolf in the Choir Loft* — Choir director Lenore Vance, 79, dies on her stairs, ruled a fall; her grand returns a stranger's furious left hand, and the heirs have booked Desmond for Thursday.
2. *Lullaby at Last Call* — A tavern owner drowns after the last ferry; Signe hears guilt in the bar piano's lullaby, and it proves to be the suspected widow's alibi.
3. *The Same Four Bars* — A summer resident dies in his sleep; Desmond has already tuned his piano, but the same four bars survive two islands away. Arne's ledger surfaces.
4. *The Piano She Wouldn't Tune* — A starred piano in the cannery office links a bookkeeper's old death to a new one; to finish Arne's case, Signe tunes the upright in her own parlor.

*Running thread:* Why Arne, who heard echoes too, starred eleven pianos in his ledger and left them untuned, and what he played his last morning on the upright she refuses to tune. Book 4 answers both; the other ten starred pianos are future cases.

**Why readers come back.** A dry, clever woman over sixty. Ferries, rain, cardamom buns and Bach in an empty church. A puzzle solvable with a timetable and a good ear. Every case closes; each book returns a little more of Arne.

**What makes it different.** The witness is a piano, and it testifies once. Signe destroys her evidence by doing her job, so every book makes her choose when to tune, and a cheerful rival may get there first. The gift only points; proof still takes ferry tickets, neighbors and nerve.
