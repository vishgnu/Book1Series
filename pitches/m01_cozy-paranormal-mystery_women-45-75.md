# The Tuner's Ear Mysteries

**Genre:** Cozy paranormal mystery · **Readers:** Women 45–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Signe Halvorsen, 61, a widowed organist, tunes pianos across six ferry-linked islands in Washington's Salish Sea, and at first touch hears the last music played on each piano in anger, grief or fear. Sometimes it contradicts a death certificate; then her tuning erases it.

**The setting.** Skarv Sound: six cedar-dark islands, 2,900 residents and one car ferry, the *Kittiwake*, four round trips a day from Lund Harbor. Rain means every piano needs tuning twice yearly. Nobody crosses the water unseen: the timetable fixes who was on which island, when.

**The lead.** Signe, organist at Lund Harbor Lutheran, has taken over her late husband Arne's tuning round and 1989 Volvo wagon. She wants to know why he died in the ferry line on a day without appointments. She has perfect pitch, plays Bach too loud in the empty church, and talks dry. To Mateo, after an arrest: "Good. You still cannot count to four."

**The cast.** Mateo Ruiz, 26, the islands' only deputy and her worst-ever pupil. Britt Halvorsen, 66, Arne's sister, runs the dock bakery, hears everything first and criticizes Signe's driving. Opal Tanaka, 58, *Kittiwake* deckhand, remembers every car; her cat, Purser, rides in the Volvo uninvited. Desmond Pryce, 49, a cheerful mainland rival tuner with no idea what he erases.

**The series engine.** The echo is a few bars and the touch of the hands: never words or faces, and tuning erases it for good. A tuner is welcome in every house, so each book brings a death ruled natural, one piano that disagrees, and proof built from timetables and gossip.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *A Wolf in the Choir Loft* — Choir director Lenore Vance, 79, dies on her stairs, ruled a fall; her grand returns a stranger's furious left hand, and Signe confides in Mateo.
2. *Lullaby at Last Call* — A tavern owner drowns after the last ferry; Signe misreads the bar piano's lullaby, suspects the wrong woman, and learns echoes can mislead.
3. *The Same Four Bars* — A summer resident dies in his sleep; pianos on two islands return the same four bars. Arne's ledger surfaces, starring eleven pianos whose deaths he doubted.
4. *The Piano She Wouldn't Tune* — A starred piano links a cannery bookkeeper's old death to a new one; to finish Arne's case, Signe tunes the upright in her own parlor.

*Running thread:* What Arne played, his last morning, on the upright she refuses to tune, and why he then drove to the ferry. Book 4 answers both and leaves Signe at peace; the other ten starred pianos are future cases.

**Why readers come back.** A dry, clever woman over sixty. Ferries, rain, cardamom buns and a cat in the Volvo. A puzzle solvable with a timetable and a good ear. Every case closes; each book returns a little more of Arne.

**What makes it different.** The witness is a piano, and it testifies once. Signe destroys her evidence by doing her job, so the gift only points; proof still takes ferry tickets, neighbors and nerve.
