# The Skarv Sound Piano Tuner Mysteries

**Genre:** Cozy paranormal mystery · **Readers:** Women 45–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Signe Halvorsen, 61, widowed church organist, tunes pianos on six ferry-linked Salish Sea islands. At first touch she hears the last music played on each in anger, grief or fear; once she tunes, it is gone.

**The setting.** Skarv Sound, Washington: cedar-dark islands, a shuttered cannery and one car ferry, the *Kittiwake*, four round trips daily. The timetable fixes who was where, when. The mainland sheriff reopens no ruled death without physical evidence.

**The lead.** Signe drives her husband Arne's tuning round in his 1989 Volvo with his cat, Purser. She wants to finish it, not ask why he stopped. She has not touched their parlor upright since he died. To her worst piano pupil: "Good. You still cannot count to four."

**The cast.** Britt Halvorsen, 66, Arne's sister, sells tickets and cardamom buns, Arne's thermos still behind her window; she and Signe bicker instead of crying. Mateo Ruiz, 26, deputy, tone-deaf, that worst pupil. Opal Tanaka, 58, deckhand, remembers every car and won't discuss Arne's last crossing. Desmond Pryce, 49, cheerful mainland rival, has no idea what he erases.

**The series engine.** Most echoes are plain grief: a few bars and the touch of the hands, never words or faces. Sometimes one contradicts a death certificate. Signe names the piece; hands and timetables do the proving. A tuner enters every house, and someone always wants the piano done by Friday.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Discord in the Choir Loft* — Choir director Lenore Vance's death is ruled a fall; her grand holds a furious hymn in a left hand spanning a tenth; Lenore reached an octave.
2. *Lullaby at Last Call* — A tavern owner drowns after the last ferry; gossip blames his widow, who always plays the closing lullaby, but the bar piano holds it in stiff, arthritic hands.
3. *Abide with Malice* — Walter Coyle dies after cutting his daughter from his will; his piano holds "Abide with Me" in a right hand that never uses its ring finger.
4. *The Piano She Wouldn't Tune* — The cannery caretaker dies beside a piano Arne starred; her parlor upright holds Arne's frightened last waltz, four-hands, and Signe knows the second pair before she tunes it.

*Running thread:* Why Arne drove to the ferry with no appointments, leaving eleven pianos starred in his ledger. Book 4 answers: he heard echoes too, starred what he could not prove, and his heart failed crossing to tell the sheriff; Opal promised never to tell Signe he was afraid. Four stars are settled; seven wait.

**Why readers come back.** A dry, clever woman over sixty, cross with the husband she misses. Ferries, rain, too-loud Bach in an empty church, a puzzle that plays fair. Every case closes in its book; each ends one fact nearer Arne's last crossing.

**What makes it different.** No witches, no talking cat: one quiet gift. The witness is a piano that testifies once. Signe destroys evidence by doing her job, so each book she must choose when to tune.
