# The Tally Counts

**Genre:** Cozy science fiction (space) · **Readers:** Women and men 30–60 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** The register at Pell's Orchard says 212; census taker Nell Abara, 53, counts 213 and puts the kettle on. Each book she rights one habitat's wrong number, while one midwife keeps turning up counted twice.

**The setting.** The Outer Reaches: orchard rings and sixty-person ice drums. Scrubbers lose some of every breath, so the Air Board ships make-up air a year ahead, billed by the head-year; gauges show how much is breathed, never by whom. Every person must be seen and asked: the last orbital count cut a living town's air. Assembly seats need four million heads; the tally stands 650 over.

**The lead.** Nell Abara, twenty-nine years unbribed. She wants her pocket book, one stroke per person, to balance. Counting people spares her staying with them: her late husband Ade asked her to stop; she took one more district. Home is the cutter *Tally* and the galley he fitted for two. "I don't doubt you. I count you."

**The cast.** Davi Serrano, 22, apprentice, raised in the town whose air was cut, sure better math would have saved it. Registrar Imogen Park, 61, trained Nell and needs the four million. Oksana Bright, 67, mail-barge skipper, rearranges Nell's galley and stays for supper.

**The series engine.** Park sends the cutter *Tally* wherever count and register disagree. Nell and Davi work through kitchens, ration books, who sings at whose funeral. Every wrong number has a sound reason; every case ends with the count made right.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *One Heartbeat Over* — Pell's Orchard's 213th is a tug hand declared dead to dodge eleven years of back air; the town splits the bill 213 ways.
2. *Forty Borrowed Breaths* — Saint Brine's goats breathe air drawn for forty dead; Davi's bedtime gauge check, a habit from his cut town, catches it, and Nell wins a livestock allowance.
3. *Ninety-Four Loaves* — Quiet Harbor bakes for ninety-four but lists ninety, because a ledger once sold them; Davi asks instead of calculating, and Nell counts four children as strokes, no names.
4. *Six Hundred Twelve Under Way* — The outbound hauler *Marigold Parish* asks to be counted as its own town, striking 612 from home registers; Park asks Nell to round up; she rules straight.

*Running thread:* Midwife Junia Vasko, 41, is seen and asked twice in one week, a district apart. She keeps two hearths, half a year each; Nell changes the form to one stroke, two half-shares of air. The seats hold by two, thanks to a tug hand and four children.

**Why readers come back.** A warm galley, a fair puzzle, a society small enough to learn by name. Each book ends on Nell's pocket book: the new seat margin, and one Junia theory struck out. In book 4 Nell sets Ade's galley for two again, Oksana in the second chair.

**What makes it different.** The detective is a census taker whose only power is to knock and ask; her oath: everyone counts, once. The stakes are air and votes, never murder.
