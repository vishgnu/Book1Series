# Off by One

**Genre:** Cozy science fiction (space) · **Readers:** Women and men 30–60 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** The register at Pell's Orchard says 212; census taker Nell Abara, 53, counts 213 and puts the kettle on. Each book she rights one space habitat's wrong number, while Junia Vasko keeps turning up counted twice.

**The setting.** The Outer Reaches: 2,300 habitats, orchard rings and ice drums with sixty people and one kettle. At each ten-year census, no sensor, satellite or proxy counts. A person must be seen and asked, because the last census run from orbit counted an empty drum as a town and cut a living town's air. Air subsidy and Assembly seats follow the headcount.

**The lead.** Nell Abara, 53, senior enumerator, twenty-nine years unbribed. The work has cost her any home but the cutter *Tally* and the galley her late husband Ade fitted for two. She wants her pocket book, one stroke per person counted, to balance. She talks plainly and asks twice: "I don't doubt you. I count you."

**The cast.** Davi Serrano, 22, apprentice with a statistics degree, sure a satellite could finish by lunch. Registrar Imogen Park, 3,000 heads above the four million that keep the Reaches' seats. Oksana Bright, 67, mail-barge skipper, with opinions on Nell's galley.

**The series engine.** Park sends the *Tally* wherever count and register disagree, and there are 2,300 airlocks. Nell and Davi work without warrants or weapons: kitchens, ration books, who sings at whose funeral. Every society has a sound reason for its wrong number; every case ends with the count made right, even when right costs.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *One Heartbeat Over* — Pell's Orchard, a hand-pollinated apple ring, registers 212; Nell counts 213: a tug hand declared dead, because reviving him bills eleven years of air. The town splits the bill 213 ways; Davi sees a count protect someone.
2. *Forty Quiet Names* — Saint Brine, a salt-mining drum, draws air for forty dead; cheesemaker Hollis Brandt's goats breathe it. Davi's sums nearly cut the drum's air; Nell stakes her record on a livestock allowance.
3. *The Uncounted House* — Quiet Harbor admits to ninety former debtors and bakes for ninety-four; a ledger once sold them, so four children go unwritten. Nell doubts her oath; Davi asks instead of calculating; she counts strokes, no names.
4. *A Town Under Way* — The hauler *Marigold Parish*, 611 aboard, asks to be counted as a town; an honest ruling risks the four million. Nell makes it; the seats hold by forty.

*Running thread:* Midwife Junia Vasko, 41, was seen and asked twice in one week, at opposite ends of the district, by Nell and an honest colleague. Books 1 to 3 rule out twin, forgery and miscount; book 4 answers with the two-hearth custom, a chair kept at two tables since book 1.

**Why readers come back.** A warm galley, a puzzle that plays fair, and a society small enough to learn by name. Nobody gets hurt. Every book closes its case; book 4 answers Junia Vasko and fills the galley's second chair with Oksana.

**What makes it different.** Her only power is the right to knock and ask; her oath is three words: everyone counts, once. The series turns on the third. The stakes are air and votes, never murder.
