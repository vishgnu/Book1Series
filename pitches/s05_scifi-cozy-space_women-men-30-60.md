# Off by One

**Genre:** Cozy science fiction (space) · **Readers:** Women and men 30–60 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** The register at Pell's Orchard says 212; census taker Nell Abara, 53, counts 213 and puts the kettle on. Each book she rights one habitat's wrong number, while Junia Vasko keeps turning up counted twice.

**The setting.** The Outer Reaches: 2,300 habitats, orchard rings and sixty-person ice drums. Scrubbers lose some of every breath, so the Air Board ships make-up air per registered head, billed by the head-year; gauges show how much is breathed, never by whom. Each census, every person must be seen and asked: the last orbital count cut a living town's air. Assembly seats need four million heads; the tally stands 650 over.

**The lead.** Nell Abara, twenty-nine years unbribed. She wants her pocket book, one stroke per person, to balance. Her late husband Ade asked her to stop; she took one more district. Home is the cutter *Tally* and the galley he fitted for two. "I don't doubt you. I count you."

**The cast.** Davi Serrano, 22, apprentice, raised in the town whose air was cut, sure better math would have saved it; he scores Nell's hunches (71 percent, disputed). Registrar Imogen Park, 61, trained Nell and needs the four million. Oksana Bright, 67, mail-barge skipper, rearranges Nell's galley and stays for supper.

**The series engine.** Park sends the disputes cutter *Tally* wherever count and register disagree. Nell and Davi work through kitchens, ration books, who sings at whose funeral. Every wrong number has a sound reason; every case ends with the count made right.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *One Heartbeat Over* — Pell's Orchard, an apple ring: the 213th is a tug hand declared dead, because relisting him bills eleven years of back air. The town splits the bill 213 ways.
2. *Forty Quiet Names* — Saint Brine, a salt drum, draws air for forty dead; the goats breathe it. Davi's sums catch what Nell's knocking missed; she strikes forty and wins a livestock allowance.
3. *The Uncounted House* — Quiet Harbor lists ninety former debtors and bakes for ninety-four; a ledger once sold them, so four children go unwritten. Davi asks instead of calculating; Nell, doubting her oath, counts strokes, no names.
4. *A Town Under Way* — The hauler *Marigold Parish*, 612 aboard, asks to be counted as a town. Park asks Nell, once, to round up; tempted, she rules straight. The 650 margin, 615 after three cases, loses her 612 and Junia's second stroke: the seats hold by two, thanks to a tug hand and four children.

*Running thread:* Midwife Junia Vasko, 41, was seen and asked twice in one week, a district apart: "I answered true both times." Book 1's extra place setting was the clue. She keeps two hearths, half a year each, and striking her costs a drum its midwife. Nell changes the form: one stroke, two half-shares of air.

**Why readers come back.** A warm galley, a fair puzzle, a society small enough to learn by name. In book 4 Nell sets Ade's galley for two again, Oksana in the second chair.

**What makes it different.** The detective is a census taker whose only power is to knock and ask; her oath: everyone counts, once. The stakes are air and votes, never murder.
