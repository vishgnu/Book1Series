# Off by One

**Genre:** Cozy science fiction (space) · **Readers:** Women and men 30–60 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** In the 2260s, census enumerator Nell Abara, 53, flies an old cutter among 2,300 habitats where every person must be seen and asked. Each book, one habitat's count is wrong, and she stays to learn why.

**The setting.** Enumeration District 114, Outer Reaches: 2,300 habitats strung across forty astronomical units, orchard rings and ice drums with sixty people and one kettle. Every ten years comes the census, and no sensor, satellite or proxy counts. A person must be seen and asked. Air subsidy and Assembly seats follow the headcount, and the Reaches keep their seats only above four million.

**The lead.** Nell Abara, 53, senior enumerator, twenty-nine years unbribed. She wants one clean census; it has cost her any home but the cutter *Tally* and the galley her late husband Ade, a pilot, fitted for two. She notices the extra place setting, the ration book in new handwriting. She talks plainly, asks twice, and knits while she waits.

**The cast.** Davi Serrano, 22, apprentice, a statistics graduate sure a satellite could finish by lunch. Registrar Imogen Park, 61, keeper of the district ledger and Nell's budget. Tobias Renner, 44, the honest enumerator next district over. Oksana Bright, 67, mail-barge skipper and gossip.

**The series engine.** Each book opens with the *Tally* docking where count and register disagree. Nell and Davi work without warrants or weapons: kitchens, ration books, who sings at whose funeral. Every society has a sound reason for its wrong number; every case ends with the count made right. There are 2,300 airlocks to knock on.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *One Heartbeat Over* — Pell's Orchard registers 212 and Nell counts 213; a newborn hidden for fear of an air fine shows Davi what satellites miss.
2. *Forty Quiet Names* — Saint Brine draws air for forty dead; Nell finds forty paperless ice-cutters breathing it and registers them by name.
3. *The Uncounted House* — Quiet Harbor's ninety former company debtors refuse any ledger; Davi finds the lawful way to see and ask them on their terms.
4. *A Town Under Way* — The hauler *Marigold Parish*, 611 aboard, asks to be counted as a town; Nell's ruling settles the double count and the Reaches' seats.

*Running thread:* Junia Vasko was seen and asked twice in one week, nine astronomical units apart, by Tobias and by Nell; book 4 proves both honest: twin sisters sharing one register line.

**Why readers come back.** A kettle on, a puzzle that plays fair, and a society small enough to learn by name. Nobody gets hurt; nobody is a villain for being odd. Every book closes its case, and book 4 answers the series' one large question.

**What makes it different.** The detective is a census taker. Her only power is the right to knock and ask; her oath is three words: everyone counts, once. The stakes are air and votes, never murder.
