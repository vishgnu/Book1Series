# Off by One

**Genre:** Cozy science fiction (space) · **Readers:** Women and men 30–60 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** The register at Pell's Orchard says 212; census taker Nell Abara, 53, counts 213 and puts the kettle on. Each book, one space habitat's count is wrong, and she stays to learn why.

**The setting.** The Outer Reaches: 2,300 habitats, orchard rings and ice drums with sixty people and one kettle. At each ten-year census, no sensor, satellite or proxy counts. A person must be seen and asked, because the last census run from orbit counted an empty drum as a town and cut a living town's air. Air subsidy and Assembly seats follow the headcount.

**The lead.** Nell Abara, 53, senior enumerator, twenty-nine years unbribed, on her last census before retirement. She wants it clean; the work has cost her any home but the cutter *Tally* and the galley her late husband Ade, a pilot, fitted for two. She notices the extra place setting, the ration book in new handwriting. She talks plainly, asks twice, and knits while she waits.

**The cast.** Davi Serrano, 22, apprentice, a statistics graduate sure a satellite could finish by lunch, and often half right. Registrar Imogen Park, who holds Nell's budget and needs a high count whatever the truth. Tobias Renner, the honest enumerator next district over, who envies her record. Oksana Bright, 67, mail-barge skipper and gossip.

**The series engine.** Each book opens with the *Tally* docking where count and register disagree. Nell and Davi work without warrants or weapons: kitchens, ration books, who sings at whose funeral. Every society has a sound reason for its wrong number; every case ends with the count made right, even when right costs. There are 2,300 airlocks to knock on.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *One Heartbeat Over* — Pell's Orchard registers 212 and Nell counts 213; a newborn hidden for fear of an air fine shows Davi what satellites miss.
2. *Forty Quiet Names* — Saint Brine draws air for forty dead; somebody breathes it, and a plain count would cut the whole drum's supply.
3. *The Uncounted House* — Quiet Harbor's ninety former company debtors refuse any ledger, since a ledger once sold them; Nell doubts her oath until Davi finds a lawful way to ask on their terms.
4. *A Town Under Way* — The hauler *Marigold Parish*, 611 aboard, asks to be counted as a town; the honest ruling may cost the Reaches their seats, and Nell makes it.

*Running thread:* Junia Vasko was seen and asked twice in one week, at opposite ends of the district, by Tobias and by Nell; book 4 proves both honest, by a custom of the Reaches that Nell's oath must make room for.

**Why readers come back.** A kettle on, a puzzle that plays fair, and a society small enough to learn by name. Nobody gets hurt; nobody is a villain for being odd. Every book closes its case; book 4 answers the series' one large question and whether the *Tally* stays a galley for one.

**What makes it different.** The detective is a census taker. Her only power is the right to knock and ask; her oath is three words: everyone counts, once. The stakes are air and votes, never murder.
