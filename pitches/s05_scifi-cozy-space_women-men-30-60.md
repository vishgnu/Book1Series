# Off by One

**Genre:** Cozy science fiction (space) · **Readers:** Women and men 30–60 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** The register at Pell's Orchard says 212; census taker Nell Abara, 53, counts 213 and puts the kettle on. Each book she rights one habitat's wrong number, while Junia Vasko keeps turning up counted twice.

**The setting.** The Outer Reaches: 2,300 habitats, orchard rings and sixty-person ice drums. Every hull leaks, so the Air Board ships make-up air per registered head, billed by the head-year; gauges show how much is breathed, never by whom. Each census, every person must be seen and asked: the last orbital count cut a living town's air. Assembly seats need four million heads; the tally stands 650 over.

**The lead.** Nell Abara, twenty-nine years unbribed. She wants her pocket book, one stroke per person, to balance. Counting people spares her staying with them: her late husband Ade asked her to stop; she took one more district. Home is the cutter *Tally* and the galley he fitted for two. "I don't doubt you. I count you."

**The cast.** Davi Serrano, 22, apprentice, raised in the town whose air was cut, sure better math would have saved it. Registrar Imogen Park, 61, trained Nell and needs the four million. Oksana Bright, 67, mail-barge skipper, wants a table to return to.

**The series engine.** Local enumerators count; Park sends the disputes cutter *Tally* wherever count and register disagree. Nell and Davi work through kitchens, ration books, who sings at whose funeral. Every wrong number has a sound reason; every case ends with the count made right, even when right costs.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *One Heartbeat Over* — Pell's Orchard, an apple ring: the 213th is a tug hand declared dead, because relisting him bills eleven years of back air. The town splits the bill 213 ways.
2. *Forty Quiet Names* — Saint Brine, a salt drum, draws air for forty dead; the goats breathe it. Davi's sums catch what Nell's knocking missed; she strikes forty and wins a livestock allowance.
3. *The Uncounted House* — Quiet Harbor lists ninety former debtors and bakes for ninety-four; a ledger once sold them, so four children go unwritten. Davi asks instead of calculating; Nell, doubting her oath, counts strokes, no names.
4. *A Town Under Way* — The hauler *Marigold Parish*, 612 aboard and outbound, asks to be counted as a town; yes strikes 612 from home registers. Park asks Nell, once, to round up; Nell rules straight. From 615, the seats hold by three: a tug hand and four children.

*Running thread:* Midwife Junia Vasko, 41, was seen and asked twice in one week, a district apart. Books 1–3 rule out twin, forgery and miscount; the answer sat on a book 1 table, the extra place setting: by two-hearth custom she keeps half a year in each drum. Nell enters her once: one stroke, two half-shares of air.

**Why readers come back.** A warm galley, a fair puzzle, a society small enough to learn by name. In book 4 Nell sets Ade's galley for two again, Oksana in the second chair.

**What makes it different.** The detective is a census taker whose only power is to knock and ask; her oath: everyone counts, once. The stakes are air and votes, never murder.
