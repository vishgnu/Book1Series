# Off by One

**Genre:** Cozy science fiction (space) · **Readers:** Women and men 30–60 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** The register at Pell's Orchard says 212; census taker Nell Abara, 53, counts 213 and puts the kettle on. Each book she rights one habitat's wrong number, while Junia Vasko keeps turning up counted twice.

**The setting.** The Outer Reaches: 2,300 habitats, orchard rings and ice drums with sixty people and one kettle. At each ten-year census, no sensor, satellite or proxy counts. A person must be seen and asked, because the last census run from orbit counted an empty drum as a town and cut a living town's air. Air subsidy and Assembly seats follow the headcount.

**The lead.** Nell Abara, 53, senior enumerator, twenty-nine years unbribed. The work has cost her any home but the cutter *Tally* and the galley her late husband Ade, a pilot, fitted for two. She notices the extra place setting, the ration book in new handwriting. She wants her pocket book, one stroke per person counted, to balance. She talks plainly and asks twice: "I don't doubt you. I count you."

**The cast.** Davi Serrano, 22, apprentice, raised in the town whose air the orbital census cut; he trusts arithmetic, not enumerators. Registrar Imogen Park, who must count four million to hold the Reaches' seats and has 3,000 to spare. Tobias Renner, the honest enumerator next district over. Oksana Bright, 67, mail-barge skipper on Nell's route, with opinions on her galley.

**The series engine.** Each book opens with the *Tally* docking where count and register disagree. Nell and Davi work without warrants or weapons: kitchens, ration books, who sings at whose funeral. Every society has a sound reason for its wrong number; every case ends with the count made right, even when right costs.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *One Heartbeat Over* — Pell's Orchard registers 212 and Nell counts 213: a tug hand declared dead eleven years ago, fed quietly because reviving him on paper bills eleven years of air.
2. *Forty Quiet Names* — Saint Brine draws air for forty dead; Davi's arithmetic finds who breathes it, and a plain count would cut the whole drum's supply.
3. *The Uncounted House* — Quiet Harbor's ninety former company debtors refuse any ledger, since a ledger once sold them; Davi finds a lawful way to ask on their terms.
4. *A Town Under Way* — The hauler *Marigold Parish*, 611 aboard, asks to be counted as a town; the honest ruling could drop the Reaches below four million, and Nell makes it.

*Running thread:* Junia Vasko was seen and asked twice in one week, at opposite ends of the district, by Tobias and by Nell. Each book adds a dated sighting; book 4 proves both honest, by a custom of the Reaches that Nell's oath must make room for.

**Why readers come back.** A warm galley, a puzzle that plays fair, and a society small enough to learn by name. Nobody gets hurt; nobody is a villain for being odd. Every book closes its case; book 4 answers Junia Vasko and the galley's second chair.

**What makes it different.** The detective is a census taker. Her only power is the right to knock and ask; her oath is three words: everyone counts, once. The series turns on the third. The stakes are air and votes, never murder.
