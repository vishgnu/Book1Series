# s04: The World-Sitters

Revision log. Genre: Science fiction (space exploration). Target readers: Men and women 45–70.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: The World-Sitters. A protected world is closed to everyone except two resident wardens, posted for one local year and then relieved. The Reserve Service cannot pay much, so it recruits retired field scientists; people call them world-sitters. Leads: June Iwasaki, 66, field ecologist, and Walt Iwasaki, 68, mining geologist, married forty-one years: the only two human beings on each planet they keep. The work is counting, mending, noticing and writing it down. Each book is one posting on one world, with one marvel that made it a reserve (a reef that grows under the ice once in thirty years; a forest that walks eight meters a year; a night singer no instrument has recorded) and one thing that should not be there: a prospector's drone, a tourist yacht with a dead transponder, a line of bootprints, a silence where the dawn chorus used to be. With no help closer than six weeks, they work out what is happening and stop it, using field science, patience and four decades of finishing each other's arguments; the grandchildren's messages arrive in batches. No sapient aliens: animals, plants and stranger things, behaving as living things do. Each book delivers one real wonder and one real danger and ends when the relief ship comes. Thread: at twenty-four June saw something on her first survey that nobody believed, and it cost her a career; she picks each posting for what it can tell her about where to look, and on the fourth world she finds it.

**Originality audit of the seed** (web searches, 15 November 2026):

> Earlier versions of this seed were audited. Found: Becky Chambers, To Be Taught, If Fortunate (a humane ecological survey of four worlds); John Scalzi, Fuzzy Nation, after H. Beam Piper's Little Fuzzy (mining rights hinge on certified native life); Alan Dean Foster, Voyage to the City of the Dead (married scientists' expedition); Janet Kagan, Mirabile (an older biologist solving ecological puzzles, from memory). 'The Life List' is taken as a title and has already been dropped. The seed has already been rebuilt around a retired couple posted as the only two wardens on a protected world. One search on that version found no matching novel; the nearest results were Elizabeth Moon, Remnant Population (an elderly woman alone on a colony world) and Jack Vance's Cadwal novels (human Conservators of a planet kept as a natural preserve). Never let a case turn on sentience.

### Round 1: First contact

**cold-reader (Pat).** Two retired field scientists minding closed planets with a rock hammer and paper logbooks is exactly my shelf; I would borrow book 1, though the intrusions look samey. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 8, originality 8, engine 7, character 8, setting 8, fit 9, clarity 7.

- Keep: June herself: 'counts twice and talks like a field note: dry, exact, funny', plus the chief who wrote 'mirage' and thirty years of teaching instead; The custodial job and its kit: 'a prefab hut, a greenhouse, a skiff and a shelf of paper logbooks going back decades', 'two people with a rock hammer and a year', 'without a shot fired'; The marvels with real numbers on them: a reef that grows once in thirty years, a forest that walks eight meters a year, and the ending's 'eleven lakes unchecked'
- Problem 1: The page gives away the answer to the running thread: 'book 4 shows a brine animal ballooning skyward to breed'. I now know what June saw before reading a word. Why it matters: The mystery was my main reason to read four books instead of one; knowing it is a spider-style ballooning animal takes the pull out. Suggested fix: On the reader-facing page, stop at 'what June saw on her first survey is answered in book 4, with a witness'. Keep the brine animal for the book.
- Problem 2: Three of four intrusions are the same kind of thing: a prospector's drone, a stray yacht, a collector, then a forgotten well. Human trespass or leftover hardware each time. Why it matters: By book 3 I would predict the shape. I read this genre for ecological puzzles, not for policing poachers on a different backdrop. Suggested fix: Make at least one intrusion a natural or ecological puzzle: an introduced organism off a boot, a station greenhouse escape, or an earlier warden's well-meant mistake found in the logbooks.
- Problem 3: The marvels are named but not explained. What is the reef made of under ice, why once in thirty years, how does a forest walk, why did the dawn chorus stop when a lake drained? Why it matters: I want accuracy with the wonder. One line of mechanism would tell me the author knows ecology; without it these could be pretty labels. Suggested fix: Give each book one clause of mechanism, e.g. the reef builds only when a thirty-year tidal cycle opens the ice, or the forest roots forward after retreating groundwater.
- Problem 4: 'Help is six weeks away' and 'one local year' with only two people, aged 66 and 68, but the pitch never says what the year costs them physically or in the marriage. Why it matters: A marriage that works is appealing, but Walt never saying whether he believes her for forty-one years needs some heat, or it reads as cozy and static. Suggested fix: Add one line on the stake between them per book: what Walt risks by looking, what June would do if he saw nothing, and one honest bodily limit of doing fieldwork at their age.
- Reminded them of: Becky Chambers, To Be Taught, If Fortunate; Kim Stanley Robinson's field-science passages (Red Mars, Antarctica); Ursula Le Guin, The Word for World Is Forest (minus the war); Fire lookout and refuge-caretaker memoirs (Philip Connors, Fire Season); Firewatch (game); Sue Burke, Semiosis (for the ecology)

**ten-second-browser (a subscription reader scrolling on a phone).** A warm, competent, grown-up exploration series I would borrow tonight; the job and the couple sell it, but the stakes and the mystery are stated too quietly. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 8, originality 8, engine 7, character 8, setting 8, fit 9, clarity 7.

- Keep: The job itself: 'the only two people on each closed planet they keep for one local year' plus 'a shelf of paper logbooks going back decades' and 'Help is six weeks away'; June's wound and voice: 'her chief wrote "mirage," and she taught for thirty years instead'; 'counts twice and talks like a field note'; Walt 'has never said whether he believes her'; The marvels in the book lines: 'a reef that grows once in thirty years', 'a forest that walks eight meters a year', and Walt seeing the threads rise in book 4
- Problem 1: In ten seconds my eye caught 'world-sitters', the ages and 'only two people on each closed planet'. Then 'something turns up that should not be there' is vague; I nearly swiped there. Why it matters: That phrase could be any mystery on the shelf. I did not yet know it meant poachers and drones, nor that June has a thirty-year-old sighting to prove. Suggested fix: Make the logline's second sentence concrete: name an intruder (a prospector's drone, bootprints) and add June's stake, e.g. the thing she saw at twenty-four that her chief logged as 'mirage'.
- Problem 2: The intrusions are small and human: a drone, a drifting yacht, a collector, a forgotten well. What goes wrong for June and Walt personally if they fail is never stated. Why it matters: Humane is what I want, but two people in their late sixties six weeks from help should feel some risk. Right now it reads cosy to the point of low pulse. Suggested fix: Give each book line one clause of cost or clock: what the reef, forest or singer loses and by when, and one bodily or marital risk for the wardens, such as ice, isolation or injury with no help.
- Problem 3: The running thread is spelled out before I have started: 'a brine animal ballooning skyward to breed'. Books 1 to 3 advance it only by 'one thread', 'Walt starts looking', 'file reopens'. Why it matters: Knowing the answer on the series page removes my reason to reach book 4, and the middle steps feel like markers, not discoveries. Suggested fix: On the reader-facing page keep the answer back: say only that book 4 shows what rose from the lake. Give books 2 and 3 a physical piece of evidence each, not just a change of attitude.
- Problem 4: Four books, same shape: marvel, intruder, stop it, relief ship lands. Emil Strand, who wrote 'mirage' and chairs the review board, never appears in the four book lines. Why it matters: By book 3 I can guess the pattern. The one person with a grudge to settle is sitting unused, and he is the human conflict I would follow. Suggested fix: Break the pattern once: in book 3 or 4 let the intrusion come from inside the Service, or put Strand on the ground or on the relief ship, so the 'mirage' quarrel is settled face to face.
- Reminded them of: Becky Chambers, To Be Taught, If Fortunate; Ursula K. Le Guin, The Word for World Is Forest (minus the war); Fire lookout memoirs / the game Firewatch; Kim Stanley Robinson, Aurora (ecology detail); Nevada Barr's Anna Pigeon park-ranger mysteries; Adrian Tchaikovsky, Alien Clay (the biology, not the tone)

**Rewrite: accepted (slight).**

- Changed: Logline: replaced 'something turns up that should not be there' with a drone, a bootprint and June's 'mirage' stake (ten-second-browser, problem 1).
- Changed: Running thread: withheld the brine-animal answer; the page now promises only that book 4 answers it before two witnesses (both critics).
- Changed: Intrusions varied: book 2 is an earlier warden's escaped greenhouse vine found in the logbooks, book 3's collector holds a permit Strand's board signed, and book 4's well is the Service's own (both critics, sameness).
- Changed: Strand put on the ground in book 4, beside Walt as the threads rise; books 2 and 3 each get physical progress on the thread (a second thread, the reopened file) (ten-second-browser, problems 3 and 4).
- Changed: One clause of mechanism per marvel (thirty-year tide, retreating groundwater, draining lake) and a bodily cost (broken ankle in the setting rule, Walt's wrecked knee in book 3); cut the last sentence of 'What makes it different' to pay for it (cold-reader, problems 3 and 4).
- Declined: A stated marital stake in every book line (what Walt risks, what June would do if he saw nothing): no room on one page, and Walt's silence is a named strength best left unexplained here.
- Declined: A clock or deadline clause in every book line: only book 1 got one ('mid-bloom'); four would crowd lines already carrying a mechanism each.
- Declined: A mechanism for the night singer: 'no instrument has recorded' it is the marvel, and explaining it would undo it.
- For the author to decide: The answer to the running thread (a brine animal ballooning skyward to breed) is now off the page, as both critics asked. If this one-pager is also your working document rather than reader-facing copy, say so and I will restore it.
- For the author to decide: Book 4 now has Strand landing early on the relief ship and witnessing the threads with Walt. Is it acceptable for a third person to be on the ground at the climax, or should the 'two wardens, nobody else' rule hold to the last page?

**Gate (reader: Pat, blind A/B)** preferred the rewrite (slight). A's ecology is real science: a reef triggered by a thirty-year tide, a forest following groundwater, an escaped greenhouse vine traced through logbooks. Varied problems, not four trespassers. Strand standing witness pays off the grudge. And A keeps the mystery unspoiled. What the losing version did better: B's logline is cleaner and more inviting ("something turns up that should not be there"); A's is cramped. B's book lines are quicker to read, and "Walt starts looking too" is warmer than "bags a second thread."
