# s02: Ballast

Revision log. Genre: Science fiction adventure (racing). Target readers: Men and women 30–65.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: Ballast. In the 2190s the great sport of the outer system is band racing: three-person crews in gondolas slung under envelopes of heated gas, riding the jet streams of the giant planets. There are no engines and no steering. A crew can only climb or sink to find a wind going its way, and it pays for every move in ballast or heat; a boat that runs out of both sinks into the dark. The season is four races: the Venus Cloud Classic, a four-day lap of the planet in the acid layer; the Titan Endurance; the Red Spot Rim around Jupiter's great storm; and the Great Band, once around Saturn's equator in nine days. Lead: Anselm Okoro, 48, the best wind-reader of his generation until a race killed his brother and co-pilot six years ago; he comes back to fly the Marigold for the Pallas Cooperative, a miners' entry paid for with ore tithes and bake sales, against the corporate works teams. Crew ideas: a 19-year-old burner prodigy who manages the heat; a sixty-year-old rigger who can patch an envelope by feel; a sponsor's representative nobody wants at the launch field. Each book is one complete race (tactics, breakages, weather) plus one act of cheating or sabotage to expose before the finish. Thread: the season championship, and the truth about the envelope failure that killed his brother, which the federation signed off as pilot error. Sport the reader can follow: one rule, visible stakes, underdogs.

**Originality audit of the seed** (web searches, 15 November 2026):

> Closest found: Bud Sparhawk, Distant Seas (professional sailors in a corporate-sponsored wind race across Jupiter's atmosphere); Arthur C. Clarke, A Meeting with Medusa (a hot-hydrogen balloon in Jupiter's atmosphere); Robert L. Forward, Saturn Rukh (a balloon-borne crew at Saturn); Michael McCollum, The Clouds of Saturn (floating cities); Iain M. Banks, The Algebraist (gas-giant regattas, from memory). No balloon-racing season was found. No sails or keels; keep the miners' cooperative at the center, not hired professionals. This seed replaced a solar-sail racing concept that was too close to Clarke's Sunjammer.

### Round 3: Series engine

**series-editor.** A distinctive, well-funded engine with a real running question, but books 1 and 4 lack visible results and the thread does not advance in the first two books. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 7, originality 8, engine 6, character 7, setting 8, fit 8, clarity 7.

- Problem 1: The thread moves only in books 3 and 4. "Acid Lap" and "Cold Fuel" contain no step toward why Tobias signed; the seam splitting "where Tobias's did" is the first evidence. Why it matters: Binge readers need the question to tighten every book; otherwise books 1-2 read as episodes and the thread as a book-4 add-on. Suggested fix: Give each book one named clue: book 1, Hesper finds the envelope's lot marking cut out; book 2, Priya's audit turns up who at Pallas bought that fabric; book 3, the seam; book 4, the name.

- Problem 3: Book 2, "Cold Fuel", is weakest: its crisis is a protest and a frozen entry cleared by "Dita's ledger". The race is decided by paperwork, and no in-air failure or weather break is named. Why it matters: Eleven days is the longest race and the mid-series slump risk. An audit climax breaks the engine's own promise of "a failure repaired with what is aboard". Suggested fix: Keep the protest as the mid-book turn, but make the ending physical: the freeze costs a day's start, so Marigold must cross Titan's slow polar air on short fuel, dropping ore Dita must sign for.
- Problem 4: The books do not visibly change the crew. Hesper "bets against her own boat", Dita signs for ore, Dray "said nothing", but no book shows any of these relationships shifting. Why it matters: Four races with a static crew are four episodes. The arc needs a different Marigold at the start of each book. Suggested fix: Assign one change per book: book 1 Hesper stops betting against; book 2 Priya moves from threat to ally; book 3 Dray breaks silence; book 4 the membership must vote knowing the name is one of theirs.
