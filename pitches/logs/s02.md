# s02: Ballast

Revision log. Genre: Science fiction adventure (racing). Target readers: Men and women 30–65.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: Ballast. In the 2190s the great sport of the outer system is band racing: three-person crews in gondolas slung under envelopes of heated gas, riding the jet streams of the giant planets. There are no engines and no steering. A crew can only climb or sink to find a wind going its way, and it pays for every move in ballast or heat; a boat that runs out of both sinks into the dark. The season is four races: the Venus Cloud Classic, a four-day lap of the planet in the acid layer; the Titan Endurance; the Red Spot Rim around Jupiter's great storm; and the Great Band, once around Saturn's equator in nine days. Lead: Anselm Okoro, 48, the best wind-reader of his generation until a race killed his brother and co-pilot six years ago; he comes back to fly the Marigold for the Pallas Cooperative, a miners' entry paid for with ore tithes and bake sales, against the corporate works teams. Crew ideas: a 19-year-old burner prodigy who manages the heat; a sixty-year-old rigger who can patch an envelope by feel; a sponsor's representative nobody wants at the launch field. Each book is one complete race (tactics, breakages, weather) plus one act of cheating or sabotage to expose before the finish. Thread: the season championship, and the truth about the envelope failure that killed his brother, which the federation signed off as pilot error. Sport the reader can follow: one rule, visible stakes, underdogs.

**Originality audit of the seed** (web searches, 15 November 2026):

> Closest found: Bud Sparhawk, Distant Seas (professional sailors in a corporate-sponsored wind race across Jupiter's atmosphere); Arthur C. Clarke, A Meeting with Medusa (a hot-hydrogen balloon in Jupiter's atmosphere); Robert L. Forward, Saturn Rukh (a balloon-borne crew at Saturn); Michael McCollum, The Clouds of Saturn (floating cities); Iain M. Banks, The Algebraist (gas-giant regattas, from memory). No balloon-racing season was found. No sails or keels; keep the miners' cooperative at the center, not hired professionals. This seed replaced a solar-sail racing concept that was too close to Clarke's Sunjammer.

**First draft:** “Ballast”, 507 words.

### Round 1: First contact

**cold-reader (Greg).** Balloon racing on the gas giants with no engines and two gauges is the kind of yarn I grew up on; I would borrow book one tonight. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 8, originality 8, engine 8, character 6, setting 8, fit 9, clarity 8.

- Keep: "No engines, no steering. A crew can only climb or sink to find a wind going its way" and the two gauges, ballast and heat: I understood the whole sport in two sentences.; One complete race per book with a result and carried standings, plus the plain-spoken answer promised in book 4: "a fabric lot number and a name."; The working-class underdog: a miners' cooperative boat "paid for with ore tithes and bake sales", a 48-year-old lead and a 60-year-old rigger who "patches seams by feel".
- Problem 1: The arc list gives away the results: "Marigold places third", "Marigold wins", "Hesper's patch holds". Three of four races are spoiled before I open book 1. Why it matters: A race story lives on not knowing who finishes where. If the page tells me, I have less reason to turn pages. Suggested fix: State each race's danger and the question it poses instead of the outcome: what the acid layer does to the boat, what Dita and Anselm disagree about, what is at stake if the seam goes.
- Problem 2: "One act of cheating exposed before the finish" in every book reads like a formula. By book 3 I will be waiting for the cheat to turn up on schedule. Why it matters: A series I settle into needs familiar comfort but also surprise. Four identical beats in a row feels like a television procedural. Suggested fix: Vary it: let one cheat go unproven until a later book, make one involve the Marigold's own side, and hint that the cheats connect to whoever signed off Tobias's failure.
- Problem 3: The cast is jobs and ages, not people. Lucan Dray "flew against Tobias" and Priya is the auditor "nobody wants", but I cannot tell what either wants or whether Dray is a villain. Why it matters: I come back to a series for the crew's company as much as the races. Right now only Anselm and Hesper feel like somebody. Suggested fix: Give each one a want and a friction in a clause: what Dita is proving, why Priya is really there, whether Dray is rival, suspect or the only man who believes Anselm.
- Problem 4: The wonder is undersold. Venus, Titan, Jupiter and Saturn get "acid layer" and "orange haze" and little else; "sinks into the dark" is the only line that gave me a chill. Why it matters: Sense of wonder is why I read science fiction. I want to know what a man sees from a gondola at the rim of the Red Spot. Suggested fix: Add one concrete sight or hazard per world to the arc lines: the view, the scale, the thing that can kill you there and nowhere else.
- Reminded them of: Arthur C. Clarke, A Meeting with Medusa; Arthur C. Clarke, The Wind from the Sun (solar yacht race); Dick Francis racing mysteries; Jules Verne, Five Weeks in a Balloon; Ben Bova, Jupiter; Gordon Bennett Cup gas balloon race

**ten-second-browser (a subscription reader scrolling on a phone).** Engineless balloon racing on gas giants with two gauges to watch is a real draw; the logline is overloaded and the lead is grief-shaped rather than vivid, but I would borrow it. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 7, originality 8, engine 8, character 6, setting 8, fit 9, clarity 7.

- Keep: "No engines, no steering. A crew can only climb or sink to find a wind going its way" plus "two gauges, ballast and heat" - I understood the whole sport in one read.; "A boat that spends both sinks into the dark" - the stakes in one line, weather and physics as the enemy, no war needed.; One whole race per book with standings carrying forward, and the underdog paid for with "ore tithes and bake sales"; Anselm "talks like a forecast".
- Problem 1: In ten seconds my eye caught "engineless balloon" and "four races on four worlds", then nearly swiped at "for a miners' cooperative ... exposing one cheat per race while hunting the truth". The logline carries five ideas and no danger. Why it matters: I still did not know where the balloons fly or what happens if you lose. The best lines sit one paragraph lower, past my ten seconds. Suggested fix: Lead the logline with the sport and the risk: no engines, no steering, the jet streams of Venus to Saturn, spend your ballast and heat and you sink. Then Anselm and his brother in one clause.
- Problem 2: "One act of cheating exposed before the finish" in every book, announced up front, with the cheat named for books 1 to 3: hidden ballast tank, doctored fuel, falsified wind feed. Why it matters: By book 2 I would be waiting for the cheat instead of watching the race. It reads like a formula, and the surprises are already spent. Suggested fix: Keep cheating as the thread but vary its role: one book where Marigold is accused, one where exposing it costs the placing. Hold back the method in the book lines.
- Problem 3: Book 4 line is vague where the others are concrete: "the championship is settled on the last day" and the running thread ends on "a fabric lot number and a name". Why it matters: A lot number is paperwork. I want to know the finale is a race against someone, and Lucan Dray, who "flew against Tobias", is never used in the arc. Suggested fix: Say who Marigold races for the title on Saturn and tie Dray to the answer or to the last-day duel, so the truth about Tobias lands during the flying, not in a hearing.
- Problem 4: Anselm is defined by a dead brother and an inquiry; the cast is a list of ages and skills. Priya, "the ore buyer's auditor nobody wants", sounds the most interesting and has no stated role. Why it matters: I will follow a crew for four books only if they rub against each other. Only "Dita overrules Anselm" hints at that. Suggested fix: Give one line of friction per crew member: what Dita wants that Anselm will not risk, why Hesper stayed, what Priya can shut down. Say whether Priya flies or watches from the ground.
- Reminded them of: Ford v Ferrari (works team versus underdog); Kim Stanley Robinson-style planetary hard SF; Gordon Bennett Cup gas balloon race; Speed Racer / Redline for the multi-world circuit; The Martian (repair with what is aboard); Dick Francis (sport plus a cheat uncovered each book)

**Rewrite: accepted (clear).**

- Changed: Logline now leads with the sport and the risk (no engines, no steering, four worlds, out of ballast and heat you sink), then Anselm and his brother in one clause; cheat-per-race and other load removed (ten-second-browser problem 1).
- Changed: Arc lines no longer give results or cheat methods; each states the race, one hazard unique to that world, and the open question (cold-reader problems 1 and 4, browser problem 2).
- Changed: Cheating varied instead of 'one cheat exposed per book': never the same way twice, Marigold is the boat protested on Titan, and proving a foul costs a placing on Jupiter (both critics).
- Changed: Cast given a want or friction each: Dita wants the route calls, Hesper rigged Tobias's last boat, Priya is ground-side and can pull the entry, Dray was last to see Tobias aloft (both critics).
- Changed: Book 4 made a named duel, Marigold against Dray's Corvane boat level on points, and the running thread is answered in the air with a name Dray has kept (browser problem 3).
- Declined: Dropping 'a fabric lot number and a name' as paperwork: the cold reader named it a strength, so it stays and is delivered mid-race instead of in a hearing.
- Declined: Hinting that every cheat connects to whoever signed off Tobias's failure: it would turn four sporting fouls into one conspiracy and cost the variety the same critic asked for.
- Declined: A full sight-from-the-gondola description per world: no room within the length cap; one hazard or scale detail per arc line instead.
- For the author to decide: Dray's role is now leaning toward witness (he has kept the name for six years) rather than villain; confirm, or say if he should be the man responsible.
- For the author to decide: Hesper having rigged Tobias's last boat adds a quiet suspicion inside the crew; keep it, or should her reason for staying be plain loyalty.

**Gate (reader: Greg, blind A/B)** preferred the rewrite (clear). A gives me the wonder: acid cloud eating rigging, minus 290, a storm wider than Earth, 1,100 mph ring-shadow wind. Its cast has stakes (Hesper rigged Tobias's boat, Dray saw him last) and it doesn't give away every finish. B reads like a results sheet. What the losing version did better: B's logline is one clean sentence; A's "and a boat out of ballast and heat sinks" is garbled. B's concrete cheats (hidden ballast tank, doctored fuel load, falsified wind feed) and "Hesper's patch holds" are worth keeping.
