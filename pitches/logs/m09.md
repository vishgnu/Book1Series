# m09: Cause of Failure: The Duluth Collapse Mysteries

Revision log. Genre: Techno-mystery. Target readers: Men and women 40–65.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: Factor of Safety. Elias Brandt, 52, was a principal at a big structural engineering firm until a parking garage he had flagged in peer review came down and the firm let him carry it. Now he works out of a rented bay on the Duluth waterfront as a forensic engineer for insurers, small towns and the occasional plaintiff: he says why it fell. He does not work alone, and the series is the three of them: Naomi Adeyemi, 29, a metallurgist with a portable lab and no patience, and Dutch Kowalczyk, 68, a retired crane operator who knows how steel is really put up. Each book is one failure that everyone calls an accident (a ski-lift tower, a grain elevator, an ore-dock conveyor, a small dam's spillway gate) and the physical evidence that says a person made it fail, or let it: beach marks on a fracture face, the wrong bolt grade, a weld inspection signed on a day it rained. The reader sees every piece of evidence and gets the engineering explained plainly. Thread: the garage collapse. Someone changed a drawing after it was sealed, and each case hands Elias a piece of how. Titles from engineering terms (Factor of Safety, Load Path, Fatigue Limit, Single Point of Failure).

**Originality audit of the seed** (web searches, 15 November 2026):

> Closest found: Aileen Schumacher's Tory Travers series (a structural-engineer sleuth in New Mexico; collapse as accident or sabotage); John Billheimer's Owen Allison series (a federal failure analyst in West Virginia); Robbie Parsons, Fracture Critical (a Pittsburgh bridge inspector, a kidnap thriller). The seed has already been moved from Pittsburgh to Duluth, keeps bridges out of the four cases and leans on the three-person team, which none of these has.

**First draft:** “Cause of Failure”, 506 words.
