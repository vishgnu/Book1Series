# m07: The Hut Warden Mysteries

Revision log. Genre: Traditional mystery (Alpine setting). Target readers: Women and men 45–70.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: The Hut Warden. Ursi Feuz, 52, spent twenty years as a mountain-rescue paramedic until her knee ended it; now she runs the Schwarzhorn hut, a 34-bed alpine club hut at 2,740 meters in Switzerland's Bernese Oberland: four hours on foot from the valley, supplied by helicopter, one radio, no road. When weather closes the passes the hut is a sealed room full of strangers who all signed the hut book, and the cantonal police are a day away; hut custom makes the warden responsible for everyone under her roof. Each book is a closed-circle mystery among one hut's worth of guests: guides and clients, a wedding party, ski tourers, a film crew. The four books follow one hut year: high summer, the autumn closing, the winter room in February, the June reopening. She reads people the way she read casualties: fast, by what does not fit. Cast ideas: Res, the supply-helicopter pilot and her old rescue partner; Nora, 22, hut assistant and geology student; Wachtmeister Beat Zumbrunn of the valley police, mostly a voice on the radio; a guide who thinks the hut should have been his. Thread: every August since 1961 someone has signed the hut book as E. Morf, in the same hand, and no warden has ever seen it done. The answer, found in book four, is human, not supernatural.

**Originality audit of the seed** (web searches, 15 November 2026):

> Closest found: Philipp Gurt, Bündner Sturm (German-language; Graubünden, a glacier body, a hut warden found dead; the sleuth is a police officer); Gérard Muller, Le refuge maudit (French-language; hut guardians find a body, a gendarme investigates); Sarah Pearse, The Sanatorium (a storm-sealed Swiss hotel). No warden-as-sleuth series found in English, German or French. The seed has already been moved from Graubünden to the Bernese Oberland and its glacier-body thread replaced.

**First draft:** “The Hut Warden Mysteries”, 513 words.

### Round 1: First contact

**cold-reader (Margaret).** A hut I would happily climb to every evening, with a clever hut-book idea, but I need more warmth in Ursi and her people before I would miss them. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 8, originality 7, engine 8, character 6, setting 9, fit 8, clarity 8.

- Keep: The hut book: one page of names opening each volume, and "E. Morf" signing every August "in the same hand since 1961"; The Schwarzhorn Hut itself: "red-and-white shutters, 34 bunks, one stove, one radio", rösti on the stove, four hours above the last postbus stop; Ursi as host, not police: "a capable woman of 52 doing a hard job well" whose "duty of care covers the killer too"
- Problem 1: The regulars are a list of jobs and ages. Res, Nora, Zumbrunn and Köbi get a line each, and nothing tells me who is fond of whom or what an ordinary evening feels like. Why it matters: I read for company. I come back for people I would miss, and so far I would only miss the building. Suggested fix: Give each regular one human detail and one tie to Ursi: what Res brings up unasked in the helicopter, what Nora and Ursi argue about at the stove, why Zumbrunn bothers to radio.
- Problem 2: Ursi is all competence. "She talks short and counts twice" is good, but I learn nothing of her life: family, loss, what she loves about the place, why this hut. Why it matters: A woman who only works is admirable but cold. I want to sit with her after the guests are in bed. Suggested fix: Add one or two sentences on her private side: who she left in the valley, what the knee cost her, one small pleasure she keeps at the hut.
- Problem 3: The arc lines give away too much. Köbi is "cleared" in book 2, Ursi "wins a five-year lease", and the Morf answer is told outright as "a family keeping a dead man alive on paper". Why it matters: The Morf signature was the thing that made me lean forward, and I was told the answer before I had opened book one. Suggested fix: Keep the question and withhold the answers: say book 4 settles who signs for Morf and whether Ursi keeps the hut. Drop "cleared" from book 2.
- Problem 4: Four deaths in one trial year at one small hut, with "every death counts against her", strains belief, and book 3's body buried outside with "transceiver switched off" sounds bleaker and more technical than the others. Why it matters: If the hut starts to feel cursed or grim I stop wanting to visit. I like the cosiness to hold against the murder. Suggested fix: Have the pitch acknowledge it: the club and the valley gossip about the unlucky hut, and Ursi feels it. For book 3, name the warm indoor life of the winter room alongside the death.
- Reminded them of: Louise Penny's Three Pines books; Agatha Christie, And Then There Were None; Agatha Christie, The Sittaford Mystery; Martin Walker's Bruno, Chief of Police; Nevada Barr's Anna Pigeon; Donna Leon's Brunetti

### Round 2: Originality

**originality-auditor.** A familiar snowed-in closed circle made fresh by the guest-book page and a host-detective with duty of care; the lead's backstory and book 4's film crew are stock. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 8, originality 7, engine 7, character 6, setting 8, fit 8, clarity 8.

- Keep: The hut book page as the suspect list that opens each volume: 'The suspect list is an object'; Host as detective under a trial year: 'her duty of care covers the killer too' and 'every death counts against her'; Evidence from hut routine: 'boot rack, bed plan, radio log', plus 'Four books, one hut year'
- Problem 1: Most generic element: 'When storm or snow closes the path, the cantonal police are a day away.' Weather-sealed mountain lodging is the shelf's commonest frame (The Sanatorium, The Hunting Party, One by One), and it is used four times. Why it matters: A binge reader meets the same isolation mechanism in every book; the pitch's own distinctive devices are doing less of the sealing than the weather is. Suggested fix: Vary the seal with hut-specific causes: storm in book 1, a grounded helicopter and closed path in 2, avalanche danger level in 3, and in 4 let the club's vote, not weather, keep everyone up.
- Problem 2: 'duty of care covers the killer too' is the most distinctive idea but appears only in the last line. No book summary shows Ursi having to feed, shelter or rescue her suspect. Why it matters: Without it on the page, the series reads as a standard amateur sleuth in a lodge; this is what Gurt, Muller and Pearse do not have. Suggested fix: Put it in the logline and in each book: e.g. in book 3 the killer is the injured tourer Ursi must keep alive overnight; in book 1 she ropes a suspect down.
- Problem 3: Book 4's 'six filmmakers come for the hut's bad year', with the recordist dying, is the stock documentary-crew-at-the-scene device (from memory: common in recent crime fiction and TV, e.g. Only Murders in the Building's podcast frame). Why it matters: The finale should be the series' most particular book; instead it borrows its most second-hand visitor group, and it sits oddly with these readers. Suggested fix: Replace the film crew with the alpine club's own inspection party and archivist, up to audit the hut books before the vote; the archivist dies and the 1961 volume vanishes.
- Problem 4: Ursi's 'knee ended it' backstory, the 'daughter in Thun wants her down', and rival Köbi 'who thinks the hut should be his' are standard cozy furniture; 'E. Morf' risks reading as a ghost tease. Why it matters: The lead resembles many sidelined-professional sleuths (from memory: Nevada Barr's Anna Pigeon, Vera-type leads); the Morf thread could breach the no-supernatural promise. Suggested fix: Give Ursi a hut-book stake: a name on a past page she failed to bring down alive. Signal early that Morf is a human custom, e.g. the ink and pen change over decades.
- Reminded them of: Sarah Pearse, The Sanatorium (storm-sealed Swiss lodging); Philipp Gurt, Bündner Sturm (dead hut warden, police sleuth); Gérard Muller, Le refuge maudit (hut guardians, gendarme); Agatha Christie, The Sittaford Mystery / And Then There Were None (snowbound closed circle; list of the doomed) (from memory); Ruth Ware, One by One; Lucy Foley, The Hunting Party (snowed-in chalet/lodge) (from memory); Nevada Barr, Anna Pigeon series (capable middle-aged woman, wilderness duty post) (from memory)
- Search note: No web search this round. Resemblances beyond the three titles in the supplied audit are from memory and marked so; none shares premise plus setting plus the hut-book device. Titles unchecked.

**superfan-reader (Diane).** A well-built snowbound puzzle box with one fresh idea, the guest-book page as suspect list, wrapped around a cast I have met many times; I would borrow book 1. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 8, originality 6, engine 7, character 6, setting 8, fit 9, clarity 8.

- Keep: The hut book page that opens each volume, and the "E. Morf" signature since 1961: a document as suspect list is the thing I have not seen, and a paper trail is my kind of clue.; "her duty of care covers the killer too" and working from "boot rack, bed plan, radio log": a host-detective with a real professional stake and evidence that belongs to the place.; "Four books, one hut year": the seasons give each book its own weather and method, especially The Winter Room with five round the stove and the sixth under snow.
- Problem 1: The supporting cast is all stock: gruff old partner with "unasked advice", sparky young assistant, valley sergeant "more curious than he admits", rival who "thinks the hut should be his", worried daughter. Why it matters: I can write their scenes myself before opening the book. None of them has a secret, a trade or a view that surprises me. Suggested fix: Replace Köbi first. Make the rival someone with a rightful claim: the previous warden's widow, who kept the hut book for thirty years and knows more about Morf than she tells.
- Problem 2: Ursi has competence and a bad knee but no point of view. "Talks short and counts twice" is manner, not a way of seeing the world or the people who come up. Why it matters: I follow a sleuth for her opinions. Twenty years on a winch should have left her with convictions about risk, tourists, guides and who gets blamed. Suggested fix: Give her one hard belief from rescue work that the cases test, for instance that mountain deaths are almost always called accidents too quickly, and one rescue she still argues with herself about.
- Problem 3: The Morf thread is a curiosity with no stakes or history yet. Why 1961? What happened that year? Nothing ties it to the hut, the valley or Ursi until book 4. Why it matters: This is the world-opening part I read for. Without a 1961 event behind it, it is a gimmick I will forget between books. Suggested fix: Name the 1961 event in the pitch (a lost rope party, a hut-building death, a border matter) and let each book's case turn up one dated fragment of it.
- Problem 4: Four killings in one year at one hut strains belief, and book 2's "Köbi serviced that stove" plus the wedding party read like any snowed-in country-house setup moved uphill. Why it matters: A traditional mystery asks me to believe the place. A hut with that record would be shut by the club, and the pitch only half admits it. Suggested fix: Make the improbability the subject: have Zumbrunn and the club ask whether the deaths are connected, and give book 2 a cause of death only a hut closing could produce.

- Changed: Duty of care moved to the center (auditor): now in the logline, the setting rule ('guilty or not'), the engine, and shown in books 1 and 3 (roping the killer down, nursing an injured suspect).

**Gate (editor, blind A/B)** preferred the rewrite (slight). A's running thread is a real mystery (1961 rope of three, signature shakier each decade, a fragment per book), which gives binge readers a reason to continue. Its hook lands the host's duty to the killer, and its mountain detail is sharper: transceiver off, flue capped, danger level four. What the losing version did better: B is warmer and more varied: daughter in Thun, dawn coffee, red-and-white shutters, postbus; Köbi as rival and book 2 suspect; outsider casts (wedding guests, filmmakers) instead of two club-insider books; a cleaner, clearer logline.
