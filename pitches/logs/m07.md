# m07: The Alpine Hut Mysteries

Revision log. Genre: Traditional mystery (Alpine setting). Target readers: Women and men 45–70.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: The Hut Warden. Ursi Feuz, 52, spent twenty years as a mountain-rescue paramedic until her knee ended it; now she runs the Schwarzhorn hut, a 34-bed alpine club hut at 2,740 meters in Switzerland's Bernese Oberland: four hours on foot from the valley, supplied by helicopter, one radio, no road. When weather closes the passes the hut is a sealed room full of strangers who all signed the hut book, and the cantonal police are a day away; hut custom makes the warden responsible for everyone under her roof. Each book is a closed-circle mystery among one hut's worth of guests: guides and clients, a wedding party, ski tourers, a film crew. The four books follow one hut year: high summer, the autumn closing, the winter room in February, the June reopening. She reads people the way she read casualties: fast, by what does not fit. Cast ideas: Res, the supply-helicopter pilot and her old rescue partner; Nora, 22, hut assistant and geology student; Wachtmeister Beat Zumbrunn of the valley police, mostly a voice on the radio; a guide who thinks the hut should have been his. Thread: every August since 1961 someone has signed the hut book as E. Morf, in the same hand, and no warden has ever seen it done. The answer, found in book four, is human, not supernatural.

**Originality audit of the seed** (web searches, 15 November 2026):

> Closest found: Philipp Gurt, Bündner Sturm (German-language; Graubünden, a glacier body, a hut warden found dead; the sleuth is a police officer); Gérard Muller, Le refuge maudit (French-language; hut guardians find a body, a gendarme investigates); Sarah Pearse, The Sanatorium (a storm-sealed Swiss hotel). No warden-as-sleuth series found in English, German or French. The seed has already been moved from Graubünden to the Bernese Oberland and its glacier-body thread replaced.

**First draft:** “The Hut Warden Mysteries”, 513 words.

- Problem 1: Four killings in under a year at one hut, all tied to one 1961 page. Zumbrunn "counts three deaths around one page" by book 3 and the hut is still open with guests signing in. Why it matters: Any prosecutor I ever worked with would seize that book and shut the place after the second body. I'd snort. Suggested fix: Show why the state cannot simply take the book or close the hut: a court order pending, a certified copy rejected, police posting a man there by book 3. - Problem 2: The engine paragraph is hard to follow: "only the 1961 page records who roped up with Emil Morf, whose alp the survivor's family took and the hut now leases." I read it three times. Why it matters: The motive for all four books lives in that sentence. If a shopper can't parse it, the series reads as coincidence. Suggested fix: Say it plain in two sentences: who owns the alp now, who stands to gain or lose if the page shows a different rope partner, and why only the original counts.
- Problem 3: Her job condition, "a fatal accident ends it, a proven murder does not", gives the lead a personal stake in calling murder. The pitch notes it but only Zumbrunn guards against it. Why it matters: A witness with a motive to find homicide gets torn apart by any defense lawyer. I want to see her know that. Suggested fix: Give Ursi her own rule about it: she logs facts that cut against murder too, and in one book she records something that hurts her own case. That is a code.
- Problem 4: Titles are quiet and two blur together: "The Winter Room" is book 3 but the winter-room death is in book 2; "The Hut Book" and "The August Page" sound like the same thing. Why it matters: On a shelf of thumbnails I would not know these are murder mysteries or which order to take them in. Suggested fix: Rename book 3 for its own clue (the transceiver, the door check, level four) and add a subtitle line like "A Hut Warden Mystery, Book 1" with a death word somewhere in the set.
