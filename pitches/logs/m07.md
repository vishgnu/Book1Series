# m07: The Hut Warden Mysteries

Revision log. Genre: Traditional mystery (Alpine setting). Target readers: Women and men 45–70.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: The Hut Warden. Ursi Feuz, 52, spent twenty years as a mountain-rescue paramedic until her knee ended it; now she runs the Schwarzhorn hut, a 34-bed alpine club hut at 2,740 meters in Switzerland's Bernese Oberland: four hours on foot from the valley, supplied by helicopter, one radio, no road. When weather closes the passes the hut is a sealed room full of strangers who all signed the hut book, and the cantonal police are a day away; hut custom makes the warden responsible for everyone under her roof. Each book is a closed-circle mystery among one hut's worth of guests: guides and clients, a wedding party, ski tourers, a film crew. The four books follow one hut year: high summer, the autumn closing, the winter room in February, the June reopening. She reads people the way she read casualties: fast, by what does not fit. Cast ideas: Res, the supply-helicopter pilot and her old rescue partner; Nora, 22, hut assistant and geology student; Wachtmeister Beat Zumbrunn of the valley police, mostly a voice on the radio; a guide who thinks the hut should have been his. Thread: every August since 1961 someone has signed the hut book as E. Morf, in the same hand, and no warden has ever seen it done. The answer, found in book four, is human, not supernatural.

**Originality audit of the seed** (web searches, 15 November 2026):

> Closest found: Philipp Gurt, Bündner Sturm (German-language; Graubünden, a glacier body, a hut warden found dead; the sleuth is a police officer); Gérard Muller, Le refuge maudit (French-language; hut guardians find a body, a gendarme investigates); Sarah Pearse, The Sanatorium (a storm-sealed Swiss hotel). No warden-as-sleuth series found in English, German or French. The seed has already been moved from Graubünden to the Bernese Oberland and its glacier-body thread replaced.

**First draft:** “The Hut Warden Mysteries”, 513 words.

### Round 1: First contact

**cold-reader (Margaret).** A hut I would happily climb to every evening, with a clever hut-book idea, but I need more warmth in Ursi and her people before I would miss them. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 8, originality 7, engine 8, character 6, setting 9, fit 8, clarity 8.

- Keep: The hut book: one page of names opening each volume, and "E. Morf" signing every August "in the same hand since 1961"; The Schwarzhorn Hut itself: "red-and-white shutters, 34 bunks, one stove, one radio", rösti on the stove, four hours above the last postbus stop; Ursi as host, not police: "a capable woman of 52 doing a hard job well" whose "duty of care covers the killer too"
- Problem 1: The regulars are a list of jobs and ages. Res, Nora, Zumbrunn and Köbi get a line each, and nothing tells me who is fond of whom or what an ordinary evening feels like. Why it matters: I read for company. I come back for people I would miss, and so far I would only miss the building. Suggested fix: Give each regular one human detail and one tie to Ursi: what Res brings up unasked in the helicopter, what Nora and Ursi argue about at the stove, why Zumbrunn bothers to radio.
- Problem 2: Ursi is all competence. "She talks short and counts twice" is good, but I learn nothing of her life: family, loss, what she loves about the place, why this hut. Why it matters: A woman who only works is admirable but cold. I want to sit with her after the guests are in bed. Suggested fix: Add one or two sentences on her private side: who she left in the valley, what the knee cost her, one small pleasure she keeps at the hut.
- Problem 3: The arc lines give away too much. Köbi is "cleared" in book 2, Ursi "wins a five-year lease", and the Morf answer is told outright as "a family keeping a dead man alive on paper". Why it matters: The Morf signature was the thing that made me lean forward, and I was told the answer before I had opened book one. Suggested fix: Keep the question and withhold the answers: say book 4 settles who signs for Morf and whether Ursi keeps the hut. Drop "cleared" from book 2.
- Problem 4: Four deaths in one trial year at one small hut, with "every death counts against her", strains belief, and book 3's body buried outside with "transceiver switched off" sounds bleaker and more technical than the others. Why it matters: If the hut starts to feel cursed or grim I stop wanting to visit. I like the cosiness to hold against the murder. Suggested fix: Have the pitch acknowledge it: the club and the valley gossip about the unlucky hut, and Ursi feels it. For book 3, name the warm indoor life of the winter room alongside the death.
- Reminded them of: Louise Penny's Three Pines books; Agatha Christie, And Then There Were None; Agatha Christie, The Sittaford Mystery; Martin Walker's Bruno, Chief of Police; Nevada Barr's Anna Pigeon; Donna Leon's Brunetti

**ten-second-browser (a subscription reader scrolling on a phone).** A snowed-in Swiss hut with the suspect list on page one is exactly my shelf; I would borrow book 1, though the arc list gives away too much. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 8, originality 8, engine 8, character 7, setting 9, fit 9, clarity 7.

- Keep: First ten seconds: "When weather seals it and a guest dies, she must find the killer among the names in the hut book" caught my eye and I knew the whole deal. Keep the hut book page as suspect list.; Ursi herself: "worked the rescue winch for twenty years until her knee ended it", "talks short and counts twice", one trial year where "every death counts against her". A capable woman my age with something to lose.; Concrete place: "2,740 meters", "one stove, one radio", "boot rack, bed plan, radio log", the police "a day away". It reads like someone who has slept in a hut.
- Problem 1: The arc list spoils outcomes: "Köbi, cleared", "Ursi wins a five-year lease", and the thread answer "a family keeping a dead man alive on paper". I now know the rival is innocent and how book 4 ends. Why it matters: I read these to guess. Being told who is cleared and what the long mystery turns out to be removes my reason to reach book 4. Suggested fix: Keep the question, cut the answers: end the thread at "unseen by any warden" and say only that book 4 answers it. Replace "Köbi, cleared" with Köbi as prime suspect.
- Problem 2: Near swipe point: the cast line "Wachtmeister Beat Zumbrunn, valley police sergeant, mostly a radio voice". Four names in a row of Swiss surnames and ages, none doing anything, right after a strong lead paragraph. Why it matters: On a phone at night a roll call of unfamiliar names is where my thumb moves. I stopped knowing nobody but Ursi. Suggested fix: Give each one a hook in relation to Ursi and trim to one line each, as Köbi already has: "the guide who thinks the hut should be his". Say what Res and Nora want or hide.
- Problem 3: A hut where a guest is murdered four times in one year, while "every death counts against her", strains belief. The pitch never says why the club keeps her or why guests still come. Why it matters: I accept a body count in a village series over years. Four killings in twelve months at one 34-bed hut makes me smile in the wrong way. Suggested fix: Acknowledge it inside the story: make the club's mounting pressure and the hut's growing notoriety part of the trial-year stakes, and let book 4's filmmakers come partly because of the deaths.
- Problem 4: After ten seconds I still did not know what the E. Morf thread was; it first appears in book 4's line as "the Morf story" before it is explained, and it sits below the arc. Why it matters: The yearly signature "in the same hand since 1961" is the most intriguing thing here and the reason to read all four, yet I nearly missed it. Suggested fix: Move one sentence about the E. Morf signature up into the setting or engine paragraph, before the book list, so the four titles are read with that question already in mind.
- Reminded them of: Agatha Christie, And Then There Were None / The Sittaford Mystery; Louise Penny, Three Pines (A Fatal Grace, The Beautiful Mystery); Martin Walker, Bruno, Chief of Police; Donna Leon, Brunetti (sense of place); Ruth Ware, One by One; Hunkeler / Swiss TV Krimi such as Der Bestatter

**Rewrite: accepted (clear).**

- Changed: Logline now leads with the hut-book page as suspect list (round focus: most unusual thing first); the engine's duplicate opening sentence was cut to pay for it.
- Changed: E. Morf signature moved up into the setting, so the four titles are read with the question in mind (ten-second-browser, problem 4).
- Changed: Arc and running thread no longer give answers: Köbi is now the obvious suspect in book 2, the lease outcome and the Morf solution are withheld (both critics).
- Changed: Cast lines each carry a habit and a tie to Ursi; Ursi gets a daughter in Thun and dawn coffee on the terrace (cold-reader problems 1-2, browser problem 2).
- Changed: Four deaths in one year acknowledged inside the story: valley talk and club pressure in the engine, filmmakers drawn by the hut's bad year in book 4; book 3 now shows the five around the stove and drops the transceiver detail (both critics).
- Declined: Fuller private history for Ursi (loss, what the knee cost her): held to one sentence to stay inside the length limit and keep her terse voice.
- Declined: Explaining why the club keeps her and why guests still come: would add mechanism; the pressure is shown instead.
- For the author to decide: The transceiver-switched-off clue was cut from the book 3 line for tone and length; decide whether it stays as that book's central fair-play clue in the outline.
- For the author to decide: Ursi's daughter in Thun is new and not in the recurring cast; confirm you want her, or name a different private tie.

**Gate (reader: Margaret, blind A/B)** preferred the rewrite (clear). B gives me people to miss: the daughter in Thun, dawn coffee on the terrace, Res flying up her newspaper, Nora arguing, Zumbrunn radioing nightly. It also keeps the Morf answer and Ursi's fate unspoiled, so I have a reason to reach book 4. What the losing version did better: A's logline is plainer and places me at the hut first. "Supplies arrive by helicopter" and the transceiver switched off are good concrete details, and "in the same hand since 1961" sits well in the running thread.
