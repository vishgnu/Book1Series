# m05: The Fair Judge Mysteries

Revision log. Genre: Culinary cozy mystery. Target readers: Women 55–75.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: Open Class. Dot Engelhardt, 67, spent thirty years in the test kitchen of a big flour mill and now spends each summer driving a 1994 Winnebago around the county fairs of a fictional Upper Midwest state as a certified open-class foods judge: pies, preserves, breads. This year her 19-year-old granddaughter Kayla comes along to drive and earn college money. A county fair is a closed world for one week (the same families, the same rivalries, 4-H kids, the midway company, the demolition derby), and Dot's palate is evidence: she can taste store-bought jam in a homemade jar, tell lard from shortening, date a sourdough, and say which entry came out of which kitchen. Each book is one fair and one death passed off as an accident (the dairy barn, the grandstand, the Ferris wheel), and the answer runs through the exhibit hall. The four books are one summer: county fairs in June, July and August, then the State Fair. Thread: the same traveling midway company works all four fairs, and its ride inspector is the first to die. Cast ideas: a widowed livestock judge who keeps turning up; a fair-board secretary who knows everything; a deputy who shows hogs. No judge is murdered and nobody is poisoned by a pie.

**Originality audit of the seed** (web searches, 15 November 2026):

> Closest found: Joanne Fluke, Key Lime Pie Murder (a baking judge at a Minnesota county fair, one book); Livia J. Washburn's Fresh-Baked Mysteries (a retired-teacher sleuth, baking contests, recipes); Kylie Logan's Chili Cook-Off Mysteries (a traveling contest circuit, a new venue per book). No traveling fair-judge series found. Titles with 'Blue Ribbon' are crowded. The seed's lead has already been changed from a retired teacher to a retired test-kitchen home economist.

**First draft:** “The Open Class Mysteries”, 514 words.
