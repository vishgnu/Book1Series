# s10: In Kind

Revision log. Genre: First-contact science fiction. Target readers: Women and men 30–65.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: In Kind. The Senders have never landed and never spoken. Every four hundred days a sealed capsule comes down beside a decommissioned weather station on a fictional sub-Antarctic island, carrying one object; the Exchange has forty days to put one object in the capsule and send it back. Nobody knows the language, only that replies have consequences. After a seed sample went up, the next gift was a living flower extinct since 1912. After a flag, nothing came for three cycles. Lead: Tobias Eze, 58, Senior Assessor, thirty years a customs appraiser and museum registrar: he reads objects (wear, repair, provenance, intent) the way a detective reads a room. With him: Dr. Saoirse Lim, 31, who believes in grammar; a station chief counting the days to her pension; a military liaison who wants the reply to be a warning; the cook. Each book is one cycle: one gift to understand (a clay cup bearing a nine-thousand-year-old human thumbprint; a clock counting down to an unknown date; a child's shoe, never worn; a box that is empty and too heavy) and one human scheme to steal, fake or hijack the reply. The gift's meaning and the right reply are settled within the book. Thread: the four gifts are one sentence, and the last reply is the answer to it.

**Originality audit of the seed** (web searches, 15 November 2026):

> Closest found: Andrew Lane and Nigel Foster, Netherspace (aliens nobody can talk to; object-for-object trades with no visible logic); Clifford Simak, The Visitors (1980; inscrutable visitors who pay in gifts). No timed single-object exchange or appraiser lead found. The aliens have already been renamed. Keep one object, forty days and the reading of provenance central.

**First draft:** “In Kind”, 514 words.

### Round 1: First contact

**cold-reader (Aisha).** Contact by exchanged objects, read by a registrar, is an idea I haven't met; I'd borrow book 1, but the repeated sabotage plot and tidy final sentence worry me. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 8, originality 8, engine 6, character 6, setting 8, fit 8, clarity 7.

- Keep: Contact through objects with consequences: 'A seed sample brought a living flower extinct since 1912. A flag brought three cycles of silence.'; Tobias as a 58-year-old registrar who 'reads wear, repair, provenance and intent' and 'countersigned the flag' -- competence plus a real mistake, not a lone genius; The gifts themselves, especially the nine-thousand-year-old human thumbprint and the box 'too heavy by exactly the mass of Earth's last three replies'
- Problem 1: The running thread is spelled out as a neat English sentence ('we kept this, the keeping has a term...') and 'the aliens prove to be keepers, like him.' Why it matters: That makes them museum curators with human motives. The strangeness I came for drains out, and the pitch spoils its own puzzle. Suggested fix: Leave the sentence partly unreadable on the page: Tobias's reading is the best fit to evidence, with one clause that does not resolve. Cut 'like him'.
- Problem 2: Every book has the same human plot: 'Someone tries to steal, fake or hijack the reply; Tobias stops it with evidence.' Replica swap, shell casing, planted hoax, order to cut. Why it matters: By book 3 I will see the saboteur coming. The object puzzle is fresh each time; the opposition is not. Suggested fix: Vary the pressure: one book where the staff honestly disagree on the reading, one where Tobias's own first reading is wrong and Saoirse's evidence corrects him.
- Problem 3: The cast is a set of job titles. Saoirse is 'certain the gifts have grammar', Pruitt 'wants the reply to be a warning', Ingrid is 'fourteen months from pension'. Tobias gets a lost marriage and nothing else. Why it matters: I want people I believe. Pruitt reads as the stock hawk colonel, and Saoirse as the assistant who is there to be told. Suggested fix: Give Saoirse a reading that beats Tobias's at least once, and Pruitt a reason a sensible person would share. Say what Tobias risks personally beyond regret over the flag.
- Problem 4: Nothing says how a nine-thousand-year-old human thumbprint came to be with the senders, or what the forty-day tests actually look like. 'Reasoning the reader can follow' is promised, not shown. Why it matters: I work in a lab. If the dating and provenance are hand-waved, I stop trusting the puzzle, and the thumbprint is the biggest claim on the page. Suggested fix: Add one concrete line of method for book 1: what Tobias measures, what it rules out, and that the question of how they got the cup is live in the series.
- Reminded them of: Arrival / Story of Your Life (Ted Chiang); Roadside Picnic (Strugatsky); Rendezvous with Rama; Contact (Sagan); The Southern Reach trilogy; The Dig (isolated-site expert procedural)

**ten-second-browser (a subscription reader scrolling on a phone).** A quiet, clever object-puzzle first contact I would borrow, though the stakes are thin and the pitch gives away its own ending and gets cryptic in the middle. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 8, originality 8, engine 7, character 7, setting 8, fit 8, clarity 6.

- Keep: The reply rule and its consequences: "A seed sample brought a living flower extinct since 1912. A flag brought three cycles of silence." That is what stopped my thumb.; The objects themselves, especially "a nine-thousand-year-old human thumbprint" and the box "too heavy by exactly the mass of Earth's last three replies".; Tobias as "a 58-year-old registrar with a loupe and a scale" who "countersigned the flag" on Thrale Island: a grown-up lead with a mistake to make good, in a place I can see.
- Problem 1: In ten seconds I got capsule, island, forty days, but not what happens if he chooses wrong. The worst outcome named is "three cycles of silence"; the logline ends on "choose what Earth sends back" with no cost. Why it matters: I nearly swiped at the logline's end. Silence as the penalty sounds mild; I need to feel what Earth loses before I borrow. Suggested fix: Put the flower and the flag in the logline, and say what silence cost: what the world lost, or feared, during those three empty cycles and what a fourth would mean.
- Problem 2: The running thread spells out the whole answer: "we kept this, the keeping has a term... tell us what to hold next", plus Hana's cup and "the aliens prove to be keepers". Why it matters: The promise is that I solve it a page before Tobias. Here I am handed the series solution before book 1, so the wonder is spent. Suggested fix: On a reader-facing page, keep only the question: the gifts add up to one sentence, completed in book 4. Cut the sentence itself, Hana's cup and "keepers, like him".
- Problem 3: The book lines turn cryptic: "a term, not a threat", "the first gift made rather than kept", "Earth answers in kind, capsules keep coming". I had to reread to parse them, and book 2's clock sentence still confuses me. Why it matters: Late at night on a phone, a line I must reread is a line where I leave. Books 2 and 3 sound weaker than 1 and 4. Suggested fix: Give each book one plain question a reader can hold: why is the clock counting to a date two landings away, and who is the shoe for? Drop the compressed interpretive phrases.
- Problem 4: The human opposition repeats: a "replica swap", Pruitt's shell casing, "an auditor's planted evidence", an order to cut the box. Four times someone interferes and Tobias proves them wrong. Why it matters: Bingeing four books, I worry the middle will feel like the same case twice. Pruitt and the auditor read as stock obstacles, and Saoirse and Ingrid do nothing here. Suggested fix: Make each book's pressure a different kind and name the person: Ingrid's pension on the line in one, Saoirse wrong about the grammar in another, so the cast changes and the threat escalates.
- Reminded them of: Arrival / Story of Your Life (Ted Chiang); Roadside Picnic (Strugatsky); Rendezvous with Rama; Contact (Carl Sagan); Solaris; The Detectorists (tone: quiet people reading objects)
- Search note: No search done; resemblances are from my own reading memory. First ten seconds: my eye caught "Every four hundred days a capsule lands on a sub-Antarctic island" and "one object". I would have hovered to swipe at "choose what Earth sends back" because nothing yet said what goes wrong. What I still did not know at that point: what a bad reply costs, whether the senders are ever met, and whether this is a thriller or a quiet book. Reading on, the setting paragraph delivered and more (the flower and flag are the real hook and sit too low), the arc mostly delivered, and the thread paragraph over-delivered by telling me the ending.

**Rewrite: accepted (clear).**

- Changed: Logline now states the cost of a wrong reply (1,200 days of silence), and the lead adds Tobias's personal stake: another silence hands the Exchange to the military (browser: no cost; cold reader: what Tobias risks).
- Changed: Running thread no longer spells out the message, Hana's cup or 'keepers, like him'; it keeps the question, promises the answer in book 4 and leaves how the cup reached the senders unresolved (both critics: spoiled puzzle, aliens too human).
- Changed: Human pressure now differs per book: theft in 1, honest disagreement in 2, Tobias's own wrong reading corrected by Saoirse in 3 with Ingrid's pension at risk, a direct order in 4; the auditor hoax plot is gone (both critics: repeated saboteur).
- Changed: Books 2 and 3 are restated as plain situations and the compressed phrases ('a term, not a threat', 'made rather than kept', 'answers in kind') are removed; book 1 gains a concrete method beat: dating the firing and a four-gram weight difference (browser: cryptic lines; cold reader: method not shown).
- Changed: Cast: Saoirse is a linguist whose readings sometimes beat Tobias's, and Pruitt has an argument a sensible person could share: senders holding a human relic have been here before (cold reader: stock cast).
- Declined: Putting the flower and the flag in the logline: it would exceed the 45-word limit and duplicate the setting lines both critics named as the strongest on the page.
- Declined: A fuller account of the forty-day tests and the cup's provenance: there is no word budget, so one method beat stands in and the provenance question moves to the running thread.
- Declined: Spelling out what the world lost during the three silent cycles: it would need a new mechanism, so the stake is carried by the 1,200 days and the military takeover instead.
- For the author to decide: Is 'another silence hands the Exchange to the military' the personal stake you want for Tobias, or should it be something closer to him, such as removal from the island?
- For the author to decide: Should the question of how a nine-thousand-year-old human cup reached the senders stay open after book 4, as now, to seed a second series, or be answered there?

**Gate (reader: Aisha, blind A/B)** preferred the rewrite (clear). A shows me the reasoning (a four-gram difference, stitch evidence), lets Tobias be wrong and Saoirse right, varies the pressure per book, and keeps the senders strange. B spells out the whole message and calls the aliens keepers like him: humans in costume, puzzle spoiled. What the losing version did better: Hana's new cup with a fresh thumbprint as the final reply is a warm, earned closing image; "the first gift made rather than kept" is a sharp observation about the shoe. Keep both without spelling out the sentence.
