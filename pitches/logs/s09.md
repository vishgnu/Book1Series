# s09: Aurochs Country

Revision log. Genre: Climate science fiction adventure. Target readers: Women and men 25–55.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: Aurochs Country. 2084. After the floods and the long emptying of the countryside, nine thousand square kilometers between the Elbe and the Oder were given back to water and grass. People call it the Bruch, the old word for the fen. Back-bred aurochs, wisent, elk, wild horses and wolves now move through drowned villages and birch-grown autobahn, watched by a small ranger service with river barges, sensor mycelium and drones that fly like starlings. Lead: Hanka Sommer, 34, ranger-veterinarian, born to a family that left a village now under reeds; blunt, tireless, better with animals than committees. With her: Tomasz Wrona, 57, a tracker who trusts his feet over any sensor; a first-year ranger from the city; the station cook who really runs the place. Each book is one season, one species and one crime or rescue: a surveyor missing in the winter marsh; horn poachers at the spring calving; a summer fire and the settlers who lit it; an animal in the autumn migration that nobody bred and nobody will admit to releasing. Fieldcraft, weather, animals that behave like animals. Thread: at the end of the year the charter that protects the Bruch comes up for a vote, and everything the rangers do becomes evidence for or against; meanwhile the water is falling, and the roofs of Hanka's family village are coming back into the light. Hopeful, physical, outdoors: a future worth living in, with work to do.

**Originality audit of the seed** (web searches, 15 November 2026):

> Closest found: Ian McDonald, The Wilding (2024; a rookie ranger at a rewilding project, folk horror); E. J. Swift, When There Are Wolves Again (2025; hopeful rewilding science fiction); Brogen Murphy, Wildlands (2025; children's; a rewilded zone with wolves, bison and poachers). No Elbe-Oder setting or ranger-veterinarian series found. 'The Wilding' is taken as a title and has already been dropped, and the zone renamed. Lead with aurochs and drowned villages, not wolves.

**First draft:** “Aurochs Country”, 519 words.
