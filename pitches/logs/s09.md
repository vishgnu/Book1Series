# s09: Aurochs Country

Revision log. Genre: Climate science fiction adventure. Target readers: Women and men 25–55.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: Aurochs Country. 2084. After the floods and the long emptying of the countryside, nine thousand square kilometers between the Elbe and the Oder were given back to water and grass. People call it the Bruch, the old word for the fen. Back-bred aurochs, wisent, elk, wild horses and wolves now move through drowned villages and birch-grown autobahn, watched by a small ranger service with river barges, sensor mycelium and drones that fly like starlings. Lead: Hanka Sommer, 34, ranger-veterinarian, born to a family that left a village now under reeds; blunt, tireless, better with animals than committees. With her: Tomasz Wrona, 57, a tracker who trusts his feet over any sensor; a first-year ranger from the city; the station cook who really runs the place. Each book is one season, one species and one crime or rescue: a surveyor missing in the winter marsh; horn poachers at the spring calving; a summer fire and the settlers who lit it; an animal in the autumn migration that nobody bred and nobody will admit to releasing. Fieldcraft, weather, animals that behave like animals. Thread: at the end of the year the charter that protects the Bruch comes up for a vote, and everything the rangers do becomes evidence for or against; meanwhile the water is falling, and the roofs of Hanka's family village are coming back into the light. Hopeful, physical, outdoors: a future worth living in, with work to do.

**Originality audit of the seed** (web searches, 15 November 2026):

> Closest found: Ian McDonald, The Wilding (2024; a rookie ranger at a rewilding project, folk horror); E. J. Swift, When There Are Wolves Again (2025; hopeful rewilding science fiction); Brogen Murphy, Wildlands (2025; children's; a rewilded zone with wolves, bison and poachers). No Elbe-Oder setting or ranger-veterinarian series found. 'The Wilding' is taken as a title and has already been dropped, and the zone renamed. Lead with aurochs and drowned villages, not wolves.

**First draft:** “Aurochs Country”, 519 words.

### Round 1: First contact

**cold-reader (Aisha).** A rewilded German fen where recovery is the conflict is a place I want to go; the people are still job titles, and the ending is given away. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 7, originality 7, engine 7, character 5, setting 9, fit 8, clarity 8.

- Keep: "The conflict comes from recovery, not collapse: the land is healing and people want back in" plus Zerbow's roofs surfacing as the water falls: the best idea on the page; The Charter rule ("nobody lives here, nothing leaves, and rangers handle an animal only to undo human harm") and cases that turn on "veterinary evidence, tracks and water levels, not guns"; The texture: river barges, "birch-grown autobahn", sensor mycelium in the peat, a crew and station kitchen instead of a lone genius
- Problem 1: Hanka is a skill set and a position. "Wants the Bruch kept wild" never wavers, and Zerbow rising is stated as backdrop, not as something that tempts or changes her. Why it matters: I want people I believe. A lead who is simply right for four books is a competent-hero cutout, however good her eye for a limping wisent. Suggested fix: Give her a real doubt and a cost: one case where her own evidence helps the settlers or her mother's claim, and show what she does with the report.
- Problem 2: The cast is a list of types: old tracker who distrusts sensors, young drone kid bad in mud, cook with the last word. Only Deniz gets a beat in the arc. Why it matters: "A crew worth a year" is promised but not shown; I have met all three of these people in other books. Suggested fix: Give each one a want that conflicts with Hanka or the Charter, such as Tomasz born in a drowned village, or Margit holding rosters that hide something, and place it in a specific book.
- Problem 3: The pitch gives away the series answer, "a ten-year renewal and new terms", and it sounds like a committee outcome. The settlers and the mother have no visible stake in it. Why it matters: The vote is the reason to read four books; knowing it ends in a tidy compromise drains the tension before I start. Suggested fix: Pose the thread as a question and hint at the price: what the new terms cost Hanka personally and what happens to Zerbow, without stating the result.
- Problem 4: The falling water is the scientific heart, proved in book 1, but nothing says why it falls or what it does to the animals; books 2 and 4 read as standard poacher and culprit plots. Why it matters: As a biologist I came for the ecology driving the plot. Without it the cases could be any game-warden mystery with nicer scenery. Suggested fix: Tie each case to the drying fen: calving grounds shifting toward the edge, peat dry enough to burn, the bear and elk following changed water. One clause per book would do it.
- Reminded them of: C.J. Box's Joe Pickett novels (game-warden case per book); Nevada Barr's Anna Pigeon series; Kim Stanley Robinson, The Ministry for the Future (rewilding strand); Charlotte McConaghy, Once There Were Wolves; Becky Chambers's Monk and Robot (hopeful tone); Oostvaardersplassen / real aurochs back-breeding projects

**ten-second-browser (a subscription reader scrolling on a phone).** The rewilded fen with drowned villages and aurochs sold me by the second paragraph; the logline nearly lost me first by ending on a vote instead of a danger. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 6, originality 8, engine 8, character 6, setting 9, fit 9, clarity 7.

- Keep: The Bruch itself: "wolves move through drowned villages and birch-grown autobahn", rangers on river barges, "drones that flock like starlings"; "The conflict comes from recovery, not collapse: the land is healing and people want back in" plus Zerbow's roofs surfacing as the water falls; One season, one species, one case per book, solved by "veterinary evidence, tracks and water levels, not guns"
- Problem 1: In ten seconds I caught "2084", "ranger-veterinarian", "rewilded fen", then hit "each case becomes evidence in the vote on its future" and my thumb moved. The logline ends on procedure, with no danger or person. Why it matters: I came for adventure and animals. "Evidence in the vote" reads like a committee. I did not yet know what Hanka risks or who is against her. Suggested fix: End the logline on the personal stake that is now buried at the bottom: her own family's drowned village is surfacing inside the land she guards, and people want back in. Move the vote to the running thread.
- Problem 2: Hanka is a job and a skill set. "Talks in short orders and field notes" and reading a limping wisent are competence; the mother's table is the only warmth and it gets one sentence. Why it matters: I binge four books for a person, not a patrol. Right now I respect her but I do not yet miss her between books. Suggested fix: Give her one want or flaw that pulls against the Charter, such as being tempted to see Zerbow's roofs herself or to break the handling rule for one animal, and show it costing her in a specific book.
- Problem 3: The book lines state outcomes flatly: "the rangers take crew and buyer without a shot", "book 4 answers with a ten-year renewal and new terms". Every ending is told, including the series finale. Why it matters: Knowing no animal gets hurt is reassuring, but being told the final vote result removes my reason to reach book 4. Suggested fix: Keep the promise of resolution and safe herds, but pose each book as a question with a cost: what Hanka must give up or risk. For book 4 say the vote is decided, not how.
- Problem 4: Opposition is faceless. Poachers, "settler families" and whoever released the bear change every book; no recurring human adversary or tempting counter-argument carries across the year. Cast are all colleagues. Why it matters: The settlers wanting their land back is the most interesting moral pressure here, and it only shows up in book 3. Suggested fix: Name one recurring figure on the other side, ideally someone from Zerbow or Hanka's own mother as a voice of the returners, who appears in all four books and is partly right.
- Reminded them of: C.J. Box's Joe Pickett game-warden novels; Nevada Barr's Anna Pigeon park-ranger mysteries; Becky Chambers' Monk & Robot (hopeful tone); Kim Stanley Robinson's The Ministry for the Future (policy thread); Oostvaardersplassen / Rewilding Europe documentaries; All Creatures Great and Small (station kitchen, vet casework)

**Rewrite: accepted (clear).**

- Changed: Logline now ends on the personal stake (water falling, her family's drowned village rising inside the land she guards) instead of the vote, which moved to the engine and running thread (ten-second-browser 1).
- Changed: Hanka gets a doubt and a cost: her own field reports keep proving the returners partly right and she files them anyway; this replaces the old closing line of 'What makes it different' (both critics on the lead).
- Changed: Her mother, Renate Sommer, 66, is now the named recurring voice of the returners and leads the settlers in book 3; Ines Brandt left the cast list and stays in book 1 (ten-second-browser 4).
- Changed: Tomasz and Margit each get a want that pulls against Hanka or the Charter: he would leave a finding out, she feeds settlers at the back door (cold-reader 2).
- Changed: Each book is tied to the drying fen (calving pushed to the edge, peat dry enough to burn, bear and elk on the last deep channels), and the running thread no longer states the vote result, only that book 4 decides vote and Zerbow at a price to Hanka (cold-reader 3 and 4, ten-second-browser 3).
- Declined: Posing every book line as an open question with the outcome withheld: the brief promises a complete mission resolved per book, so the lines still say each case is solved.
- Declined: Explaining why the water is falling: a cause would add a mechanism the page has no room for; the pitch shows only its effects on each case.
- Declined: Placing a Tomasz or Margit beat in a specific named book: no room within the word limit; book 4 only points toward the station.
- For the author to decide: Book 4 now implies that whoever released the bear is connected to Station Erlenhof (Tomasz is the obvious candidate). Is that the culprit you want, or should the trail lead to the returners or an outside party?
- For the author to decide: In book 3 the fire is now an accident from Renate's settler camp, not a fire the settlers light on purpose. Confirm which you want; deliberate arson makes Renate much harder to keep 'partly right'.

**Gate (reader: Aisha, blind A/B)** preferred the rewrite (clear). B gives Hanka a real bind: her own honest field data argues against the cause she serves, and her mother leads the other side. Falling water causally links all four cases. A's people are job descriptions and its conflict stays external. What the losing version did better: A states the book 4 answer concretely (ten-year renewal with new terms) where B goes vague with "at a price to Hanka". A also keeps hydrologist Ines Brandt in the cast and the nine-thousand-square-kilometer scale in the logline.
