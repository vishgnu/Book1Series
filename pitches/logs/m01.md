# m01: The Tuner's Ear Mysteries

Revision log. Genre: Cozy paranormal mystery. Target readers: Women 45–75.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: The Tuner's Ear. Signe Halvorsen, 61, a church organist, takes over her late husband Arne's piano-tuning round among the ferry-linked islands of a fictional sound in Washington State's Salish Sea: six islands, one ferry timetable, cedar, rain, cardamom buns at the dock bakery. Since his death she hears what he must have heard: when her tuning lever first touches a piano, it gives back the last thing played on it in strong feeling: a few bars and the touch of the hands, never words or faces. Tuning erases the echo for good, so each piano can testify once, and only to her. A tuner is let into every house, church, school, tavern and summer cabin, and island damp means every piano needs her; where a death looks natural, the piano sometimes says otherwise. The ferry timetable decides who could have been where. Lead: perfect pitch, stubborn, drives Arne's old Volvo wagon onto the 7:10 boat. Cast ideas: a young deputy who was her worst pupil; her sister-in-law, who runs the dock bakery; a ferry deckhand who sees who boards; a rival tuner from the mainland. Thread: one piano she will not tune, the one Arne played on his last morning; it holds his last music and perhaps the truth about his death, and she can hear it only once. Book titles can be tuning terms (wolf interval, false beats, sympathetic strings).

**Originality audit of the seed** (web searches, 15 November 2026):

> Closest found: Brian Harvey's Frank Ryan mysteries (a piano-tuner sleuth, no paranormal element); Joe Cosentino's Player Piano Mysteries (a player-piano ghost helps solve murders); Ann Yost's Bait & Stitch series (Finnish-American Upper Peninsula cozies). The piano-echo device was not found anywhere. The seed has already been moved from Michigan's Upper Peninsula to ferry islands in Washington State to leave Yost's ground.

**First draft:** “The Tuner's Ear Mysteries”, 511 words.

### Round 1: First contact

**cold-reader (Linda).** A dry sixty-one-year-old piano tuner on rainy ferry islands with a strictly limited gift: I would borrow book one tonight, though the book blurbs are murky. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 8, originality 8, engine 8, character 7, setting 9, fit 8, clarity 6.

- Keep: The gift's limits: 'a few bars and the touch of the hands: never words or faces', heard once, and 'Signe destroys her evidence by doing her job'. She still has to think.; Skarv Sound and the ferry timetable as the fair-play puzzle: 'Nobody crosses the water unseen', plus Britt's cardamom buns from 6:30 and Opal who 'remembers every car'.; Signe herself: 61, widowed, 'short, dry sentences', Arne's 1989 Volvo, and 'the upright she refuses to tune'. That last one got me.
- Problem 1: The four book lines read like notes to the author. 'The Wolf Interval', 'The Pitch Raise', 'eleven pianos starred', 'leaving ten starred pianos': I do not know what any of that means or promises. Why it matters: I choose a series by whether book one sounds like a good evening. Jargon titles and cryptic fragments make me work, and I may just scroll on. Suggested fix: Give each book two plain sentences: who died, why it looks natural, what the piano gave Signe, who is under suspicion. Explain a musical title in half a line, and say what a starred piano is.
- Problem 2: It is all rain, widowhood, death certificates and hymns. The only joke is Mateo being 'her worst-ever pupil'. I am told she is dry but never hear her say anything. Why it matters: Cozy means I smile. A grieving organist on dark wet islands could turn out mournful, and I do not borrow mournful. Suggested fix: Put one actual line of Signe's dry talk on the page, and one funny recurring situation with Britt or Mateo. Show me the warmth, do not just list 'warm buns'.
- Problem 3: No animal anywhere, and the food stops at one bun. The townsfolk are a list of jobs and ages; Desmond Pryce 'with no idea what he erases' is a puzzle, not a person. Why it matters: I come back to a series for the people and the creatures as much as the murders. Four names with ages is thin company for four books. Suggested fix: Give Signe or the ferry a pet with a habit, give Britt a second thing she bakes, and tell me in one line whether Desmond is rival, nuisance or suspect.
- Problem 4: Ending on 'leaving ten starred pianos' sounds like the story is not finished after book four, and Arne's death in the ferry line might turn the last book sad rather than satisfying. Why it matters: I was promised the running thread answered. If I read four books and get a door left open for more, I feel cheated. Suggested fix: Say plainly that book four tells us what Arne played and why he died, and that Signe ends in a good place. Mention the other pianos as a bonus, not a loose end.
- Reminded them of: Murder, She Wrote (Cabot Cove); Aunt Dimity series; Louise Penny's Three Pines, minus the darkness; Lighthouse Library Mysteries; Shetland ferry-timetable alibis, gentler; Haunted Bookshop Mysteries

**ten-second-browser (a subscription reader scrolling on a phone).** A widowed organist whose pianos remember is a fresh, warm hook with a real engine; a few muddy sentences and a thin Signe keep it from a must-borrow. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 8, originality 8, engine 8, character 7, setting 9, fit 9, clarity 6.

- Keep: First ten seconds: 'widowed church organist, tunes pianos across six ferry-linked islands' and 'sometimes it contradicts a death certificate'. That caught me and I stopped scrolling.; 'The witness is a piano, and it testifies once' plus 'Signe destroys her evidence by doing her job': a gift with a cost I understand at once, and it cannot solve the case alone.; The ferry timetable as a fair puzzle ('Nobody crosses the water unseen'), with Britt's cardamom buns, the 1989 Volvo and 'the upright she refuses to tune'.
- Problem 1: My near-swipe was 'gives her, once, the last music played on it in strong feeling'. I read it twice. I did not yet know she hears it, or that tuning wipes it. Why it matters: The logline is all I read in ten seconds; if the gift is fuzzy there, I only half understand what I am borrowing. Suggested fix: Say it plainly: 'The first time she touches a piano she hears the last thing played on it in anger, grief or fear. Then her tuning erases it. Sometimes the music contradicts the death certificate.'
- Problem 2: After ten seconds I knew nothing of Signe's warmth or humor. 'Speaks in short, dry sentences' tells me she is funny without showing it, and the page never lets her speak. Why it matters: I come back to cozies for the woman's company. Here she is a job, a grief and a gift, not yet a friend. Suggested fix: Give one line of Signe's own voice or one small habit, such as what she says to a neglected piano or to Mateo, and one thing she enjoys besides duty.
- Problem 3: The book 3 and 4 lines and the running thread get cryptic: 'eleven pianos starred', 'leaving ten starred pianos', 'answers both', 'the parlor upright'. I could not tell whose parlor without rereading. Why it matters: The Arne mystery is my reason to finish all four, and I had to do sums to follow it. The ten left over sounded like unfinished business. Suggested fix: State it straight: Arne marked eleven pianos he believed held evidence of a wrong death. Book 4 solves his and tells why he died; say the remaining ten are future cases, not loose ends.
- Problem 4: The cast is a list of ages and jobs. Desmond Pryce, 'a mainland tuner with no idea what he erases', is intriguing but I cannot tell if he is rival, threat or friend. No hint of companionship. Why it matters: The close community is half the promise. Mateo as 'her worst-ever pupil' is the only relationship with any spark or fun in it. Suggested fix: Give each a line of relationship like Mateo's: what Britt and Signe bicker about, what Opal owes her, and whether Desmond is a nuisance, a danger or a slow-growing friendship.
- Reminded them of: Louise Penny's Three Pines (community, older cast); The Thursday Murder Club; Murder, She Wrote; Ghost Whisperer (a gift with limits); Debbie Macomber's Cedar Cove (Washington waterfront town); Agatha Raisin

**Rewrite: accepted (clear).** It ran over the length limit and was trimmed by a line editor before the gate.

- Changed: Logline now says plainly that she hears the music at first touch, that it comes from anger, grief or fear, and that her tuning erases it (ten-second-browser's near-swipe sentence).
- Changed: Book titles changed from tuner jargon to plain ones, and each line now gives the official ruling or Signe's mistake; book 3 says what a starred piano is (cold-reader's and browser's top complaint about cryptic blurbs).
- Changed: Running thread now states that book 4 answers both questions, leaves Signe at peace, and that the other ten starred pianos are future cases, not loose ends (both critics).
- Changed: Lead now has one line of Signe's own dry talk and one thing she enjoys, loud Bach in the empty church (both critics: told she is dry, never heard).
- Changed: Cast gains relationships and a creature: Britt criticizes Signe's driving, Opal's cat Purser rides in the Volvo uninvited, Desmond is named as a cheerful rival (thin company, no animal, unclear Desmond).
- Declined: Two full sentences per book with a named suspect: the template asks for one line each and the page is already near the length limit.
- Declined: A second thing Britt bakes: no room, and the cardamom buns are the detail both critics remembered.
- Declined: Explaining each musical title in half a line: replaced the jargon titles with plain ones instead, which costs fewer words.
- For the author to decide: I replaced all four book titles (The Wolf Interval, False Beats, Sympathetic Strings, The Pitch Raise) with plain ones; do you want to keep the musical-term titles and gloss them instead?
- For the author to decide: The page is now roughly 560 words by my estimate, above the 520 target but under the 580 cap; if it must come down, the cat or Signe's quoted line are the cheapest cuts, and I would cut the cat first.

**Gate (reader: Linda, blind A/B)** preferred the rewrite (clear). B gives me Signe's voice ("You still cannot count to four"), a cat named Purser riding in the Volvo, Britt nagging about her driving, and titles that sound like cozies I'd borrow. A's titles are tuner jargon and its people are a list. What the losing version did better: A's setting had the bakery by name, cardamom buns from 6:30 and the 7:10 ferry: small-town texture B thinned. "Welcome in every house" and "the clue is always music" were nice closing touches too.
