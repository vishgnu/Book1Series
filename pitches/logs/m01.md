# m01: The Tuner's Ear Mysteries

Revision log. Genre: Cozy paranormal mystery. Target readers: Women 45–75.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: The Tuner's Ear. Signe Halvorsen, 61, a church organist, takes over her late husband Arne's piano-tuning round among the ferry-linked islands of a fictional sound in Washington State's Salish Sea: six islands, one ferry timetable, cedar, rain, cardamom buns at the dock bakery. Since his death she hears what he must have heard: when her tuning lever first touches a piano, it gives back the last thing played on it in strong feeling: a few bars and the touch of the hands, never words or faces. Tuning erases the echo for good, so each piano can testify once, and only to her. A tuner is let into every house, church, school, tavern and summer cabin, and island damp means every piano needs her; where a death looks natural, the piano sometimes says otherwise. The ferry timetable decides who could have been where. Lead: perfect pitch, stubborn, drives Arne's old Volvo wagon onto the 7:10 boat. Cast ideas: a young deputy who was her worst pupil; her sister-in-law, who runs the dock bakery; a ferry deckhand who sees who boards; a rival tuner from the mainland. Thread: one piano she will not tune, the one Arne played on his last morning; it holds his last music and perhaps the truth about his death, and she can hear it only once. Book titles can be tuning terms (wolf interval, false beats, sympathetic strings).

**Originality audit of the seed** (web searches, 15 November 2026):

> Closest found: Brian Harvey's Frank Ryan mysteries (a piano-tuner sleuth, no paranormal element); Joe Cosentino's Player Piano Mysteries (a player-piano ghost helps solve murders); Ann Yost's Bait & Stitch series (Finnish-American Upper Peninsula cozies). The piano-echo device was not found anywhere. The seed has already been moved from Michigan's Upper Peninsula to ferry islands in Washington State to leave Yost's ground.

**First draft:** “The Tuner's Ear Mysteries”, 511 words.
