# m01: The Skarv Sound Piano Tuner Mysteries

Revision log. Genre: Cozy paranormal mystery. Target readers: Women 45–75.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: The Tuner's Ear. Signe Halvorsen, 61, a church organist, takes over her late husband Arne's piano-tuning round among the ferry-linked islands of a fictional sound in Washington State's Salish Sea: six islands, one ferry timetable, cedar, rain, cardamom buns at the dock bakery. Since his death she hears what he must have heard: when her tuning lever first touches a piano, it gives back the last thing played on it in strong feeling: a few bars and the touch of the hands, never words or faces. Tuning erases the echo for good, so each piano can testify once, and only to her. A tuner is let into every house, church, school, tavern and summer cabin, and island damp means every piano needs her; where a death looks natural, the piano sometimes says otherwise. The ferry timetable decides who could have been where. Lead: perfect pitch, stubborn, drives Arne's old Volvo wagon onto the 7:10 boat. Cast ideas: a young deputy who was her worst pupil; her sister-in-law, who runs the dock bakery; a ferry deckhand who sees who boards; a rival tuner from the mainland. Thread: one piano she will not tune, the one Arne played on his last morning; it holds his last music and perhaps the truth about his death, and she can hear it only once. Book titles can be tuning terms (wolf interval, false beats, sympathetic strings).

**Originality audit of the seed** (web searches, 15 November 2026):

> Closest found: Brian Harvey's Frank Ryan mysteries (a piano-tuner sleuth, no paranormal element); Joe Cosentino's Player Piano Mysteries (a player-piano ghost helps solve murders); Ann Yost's Bait & Stitch series (Finnish-American Upper Peninsula cozies). The piano-echo device was not found anywhere. The seed has already been moved from Michigan's Upper Peninsula to ferry islands in Washington State to leave Yost's ground.

**First draft:** “The Tuner's Ear Mysteries”, 511 words.

### Round 2: Originality

**originality-auditor.** A genuinely fresh device (a one-time, self-erasing piano witness) wrapped in a very familiar widow-on-a-small-island cozy frame; the resemblance to existing work is trope-level, not premise-level. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 8, originality 8, engine 7, character 7, setting 6, fit 9, clarity 8.

- Problem 1: Most generic element: the widow who inherits her late husband's trade and investigates his unexplained death as the four-book thread. Readers of this shelf have met it more often than any other part of the pitch. Why it matters: The running thread is what sells books 2 to 4, and its outline is the one a binge reader can predict from the logline. Suggested fix: Make Arne's secret device-specific: he heard echoes too and tuned some away on purpose. The ledger's eleven stars then mark pianos he left untuned, and the thread asks which echoes he erased, and for whom.
- Problem 2: The most distinctive element, erasure, is stated but never dramatized. No book in the arc turns on the choice to tune or not, and Desmond appears only in the cast list. Why it matters: Without erasure in the plots, the device reads as ordinary psychometry (touch an object, sense its past), which is common on this shelf. Suggested fix: Give each book a clock: book 1, the heirs book Desmond to tune Lenore's grand before the sale, so Signe has two ferry crossings to hear it again. In book 3, one of the two matching pianos is already tuned.
- Problem 3: The supporting cast and comforts are stock Pacific Northwest island cozy: bakery-owning sister-in-law who "hears everything first", young lone deputy, uninvited cat, "cardamom buns", rain. Why it matters: Stripped of the piano, this could be any island cozy; Scandinavian bakery plus cat is the shelf's default furniture. Suggested fix: Tie each to sound or the ferry. Britt keeps the church's hymn board and knows who requested what; Mateo, tone-deaf, checks Signe's claims against the Kittiwake's vehicle log. Keep Opal as is; she is already specific.
- Problem 4: "Lund Harbor Lutheran", Halvorsen, Arne, Britt, cardamom: the move from Michigan to Washington kept the Nordic-American small-town texture that defined the seed's closest neighbor (Yost). Why it matters: Changing the state but not the cultural flavor leaves the resemblance half in place for a reader who knows both. Suggested fix: Keep Signe Norwegian-American but root the islands in what is locally particular: the cannery of book 4, reef-net fishing, a Japanese-American farming history through Opal Tanaka's family. Let the church be the one Nordic institution.
