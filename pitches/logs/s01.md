# s01: Under Pilotage

Revision log. Genre: Science fiction adventure (space). Target readers: Men and women 35–65.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: Under Pilotage. The only fueling port in the Caird system lies inside the rings of its gas giant, and the one way in is the Passage: a lane through ice and shepherd moonlets that shifts by the hour. By treaty no ship may run it without a licensed pilot of the Passage Pilot Service, boarded from a cutter at the outer marker. Pilots are neutral, like doctors: they bring through any ship of any flag. On the bridge the pilot has the conn and nothing else: no authority over crew, cargo or quarrels. Lead: Maren Achterberg, 44, nineteen years a pilot, never lost a ship, dry, exact, a bad knee from a boarding-ladder fall, raising her nephew on the station. Each book is one 31-hour transit aboard one troubled ship where she is the only stranger: a liner whose captain is dying and whose first officer wants him gone, a grain hauler with stowaways, a warship that is on no register, a yacht with a passenger who is not there willingly. She cannot interfere, but nobody gets through without her. Cast ideas: her cutter coxswain; the Pilot Master; an apprentice; a port inspector. Thread: the Passage is getting worse on purpose; someone is nudging a shepherd moonlet. Real problem-solving, orbital mechanics that behave, competence under pressure.

**Originality audit of the seed** (web searches, 15 November 2026):

> No boarding-pilot lead found. Nearest pieces: Ginger Booth, Spaceship Thrive (a captain crossing a gas giant's rings); the Pilots' Guild in C. J. Cherryh's Foreigner (the name only); Nevil Shute's novella Pilotage (the title only). Call it a pilot service, never a guild.

**First draft:** “Under Pilotage”, 514 words.

### Round 1: First contact

**cold-reader (Tom).** A harbor pilot in space who holds the conn and nothing else: that is a real job with real limits, and I would borrow book 1 tonight. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 8, originality 8, engine 8, character 7, setting 8, fit 9, clarity 7.

- Keep: "holds the conn, nothing else" - the hero commands nothing and can only steer; her one lever is where the ship is and when; One transit per book, 31 hours, board at the outer marker and tie up at the Kettle, with navigation solved by "burns, margins and timing the reader can follow"; Maren herself: nineteen years, never lost a ship, the knee, "headings, numbers and dry understatement", and Ines at the helm the day she fell
- Problem 1: The villain's plan does not add up as stated. A fuel consortium closing the only lane to the only fuel port loses its own customers, and "paid beacons" cannot guide ships through a closed lane. Why it matters: I drop books when the scheme behind the plot makes no engineering or business sense. This is the four-book payoff. Suggested fix: Say the pushes make the lane too erratic for a human to call six hours ahead, so only the consortium's beacon net can run it. One clause in the running thread does it.
- Problem 2: Why the lane shifts "by the hour" and why a pilot can read it from "forty minutes of radar returns" when ship computers and tables cannot is asserted, not explained. Why it matters: Ring dynamics are slow and predictable in my head. If the pilot's edge over automation is hand-waved, the whole premise wobbles. Suggested fix: Add one sentence of mechanism: what stirs the ice (shepherd resonances, the Kettle's own mining, collisions) and why local judgment beats onboard prediction.
- Problem 3: Book 1 has the weakest hazard line. "A drift the tables cannot explain" is vague, and the captain-versus-first-officer quarrel is the most familiar of the four. Why it matters: Book 1 is the one I decide on. Books 2 and 3 sound better than the one I would actually borrow. Suggested fix: Name the concrete navigation problem in Outer Marker: what the liner must clear, by when, and what relieving the captain mid-lane would cost in the lane.
- Problem 4: Every ship arrives already in trouble, and the first officer and the yacht's owner both read as possible fools or villains. The pitch never says the crews are competent. Why it matters: I want capable people on both sides of the conn. A pilot who is only good because the ship's officers are bad gets old fast. Suggested fix: Give one ship's officer per book a line showing skill and a defensible reason for their position, starting with the Corazon Vale's first officer.
- Reminded them of: Nathan Lowell, Solar Clipper series (working merchant spacers); Mark Twain, Life on the Mississippi (river pilots reading a shifting channel); The Expanse (burns and physics that hold); C. J. Cherryh, Merchanter novels (stations, flags, neutral trade); Patrick O'Brian, Aubrey-Maturin (seamanship as plot); Columbia River bar pilots / Panama Canal pilotage

**ten-second-browser (a subscription reader scrolling on a phone).** A harbor pilot in space with one lever is a fresh, solid premise, but the opening lines describe a job, not a danger, so I nearly swiped. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 6, originality 8, engine 8, character 6, setting 8, fit 9, clarity 7.

- Keep: "The hero commands nothing. She cannot arrest, order or take sides; she can only steer." This is the real hook and it is what sold me.; The engine: "one ship, 31 hours, a crew in trouble, a lane closing around them", with a complete story and no cliffhanger each time.; The Passage and Kettle Station: a lane "that shifts by the hour", pilot boarded by cutter, any flag served. Concrete, believable, easy to picture.
- Problem 1: First ten seconds: my eye caught "pilots ships through the shifting ice lane" and "31 hours". I would have swiped at "holding the conn and no other authority" because it reads as a job description. Why it matters: I still did not know what goes wrong, what is at stake, or why her lack of authority is exciting. The best line sits at the very bottom. Suggested fix: Lead with the constraint as trouble: she cannot arrest, order or take sides, only steer, and this ship has a dying captain and a mutinous first officer in a lane that is closing.
- Problem 2: Stakes stay abstract. "A crew in trouble" and "a drift the tables cannot explain" never say what happens if she gets it wrong: nobody states that ships break up or people die in the Passage. Why it matters: I want tension. "Has never lost a ship" only matters if I can see what losing one looks like. Suggested fix: Add one concrete line of consequence in the setting or engine: what the ice does to a hull that misses its window, and how many are aboard the Corazon Vale.
- Problem 3: Maren is a résumé more than a person: 44, nineteen years, a knee, a nephew. "Dry understatement" is asserted but never shown, and Ines being "at the helm the day Maren fell" is left hanging. Why it matters: I follow a series for the person. I cannot yet hear her, and I do not know if she and Ines are friends or carrying a grudge. Suggested fix: Give one line of Maren's actual speech in the lead paragraph, and say in a clause what stands between her and Ines since the fall.
- Problem 4: The running thread ends on "a fuel consortium that wants paid beacons in place of pilots". After a warship and a moved moonlet, the villain is a faceless company, and book 4 is a yacht. Why it matters: Four books of build-up should pay off with someone I can dislike and a final transit that sounds like the hardest, not the smallest. Suggested fix: Name one person who fronts the consortium and put them aboard the Sable Wren, and state that the lane in book 4 is the narrowest it has ever been.
- Reminded them of: Nathan Lowell, Solar Clipper series (working-life space fiction); The Expanse (ring ice, physics that holds, minus the war); C. J. Cherryh, merchanter novels; Patrick O'Brian / Hornblower-style sea procedure; Murder on the Orient Express (sealed vessel, outsider aboard); Becky Chambers, Wayfarers (danger without cruelty)

- Changed: Setting adds one clause of mechanism (moonlets' pull plus the Kettle's mining outrun the tables) and one line of consequence (a missed window holes the hull in minutes); answers the cold reader on why a pilot beats tables and the browser on abstract stakes.
- Changed: Book 1 line made concrete: 2,100 aboard, a window past Hob nine minutes shorter than the tables say, and a first officer who is right about relieving the captain but wrong about when; answers the weak-hazard and competent-crew points and seeds Hob from book 1.
- Changed: Running thread and book 4 reworked: the consortium makes the lane too erratic for human pilots so ships must rent its beacon net, chair Corwin Hale, 58, is aboard the Sable Wren, and the lane is the narrowest on record; answers the villain-logic and faceless-payoff points.
- Changed: Lead gains one line of Maren's speech ("closer than I'd choose") and the cast says she and Ines never raise the fall; answers the resume-not-a-person point.
- Declined: A line of skill and defensible motive for one officer in every book: done for book 1 only, since doing all four would overrun the length limit.
- Declined: Naming collisions as a third cause of lane shift: two causes are enough to take in on one pass.
- For the author to decide: Is Corwin Hale, 58, acceptable as the consortium's face, and should he appear or be mentioned before book 4 so the payoff is planted?
- For the author to decide: The pitch is now roughly 550 words, above the 520 target though under the 580 cutoff; if it must come down, the candidates are the Ines clause and the passenger count.

**Gate (reader: Tom, blind A/B)** preferred the rewrite (clear). B tells me why the lane shifts (moonlet pull, mining), what failure costs (holed in minutes, 2,100 aboard), and gives a real number: a window nine minutes short. The first officer being right but mistimed promises no idiots. The beacon-net motive makes engineering sense. What the losing version did better: A's logline is cleaner and plainer; B's opens on a clumsy negative. A also keeps book 4 leaner without naming the consortium chair, and its "cannot arrest, order or take sides" sits better in the closing section.

### Round 2: Originality

**originality-auditor.** A genuinely fresh seat (the neutral pilot who can only steer) bolted to a very familiar villain and lead; the device is original, the conspiracy is not. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 7, originality 7, engine 8, character 6, setting 7, fit 9, clarity 8.

- Keep: "The hero commands nothing; she can only steer" and "touches the quarrel aboard only through heading, speed and arrival time": the constraint is the series, and I know no space series built on it (from memory).; The lane as a physical system: "calls the lane six hours ahead from forty minutes of radar returns", the window "nine minutes shorter than the tables say", Hob being pushed. The mystery is readable in the navigation data.; One sealed transit per book with a different ship type each time (liner, grain hauler, blank-transponder warship, yacht), boarded by cutter at the outer marker.
- Problem 1: The running thread's answer, "a fuel consortium making the lane too erratic for human pilots, so every ship must rent its beacon net", is the stock corporate-manufactured-crisis villain, with a chair on a yacht. Why it matters: It is the most generic element on the page: The Expanse, Outland and Alien-style company plots have trained this shelf to guess 'the corporation did it' by book 2. Suggested fix: Keep the pushes, change the hand: make them come from inside the Pilot Service or the treaty powers, e.g. Calloway's predecessors nudging Hob for decades to keep the lane unpilotable by machine. Maren's neutrality then becomes the thing on trial.
- Problem 2: The distinctive lever, "where the ship is and when", only becomes the plot's resolution in book 4 ("Maren's arrival hour delivers the proof"). Books 1-3 read as navigation problems with a quarrel alongside. Why it matters: If the steering-only constraint decides just one ending, the series reads as a standard competent-captain procedural with a rank change, and the unique selling point is decoration. Suggested fix: State for each book the one thing Maren does with heading, speed or arrival time that settles the shipboard quarrel: e.g. book 1, she times the Hob window so the captain's relief falls after the point of no return.
- Problem 3: The lead's kit is shelf-standard: "has never lost a ship", an old injury, an orphaned nephew to come home to, a colleague tied to the accident, "a subject neither raises", plus a fearless apprentice. Why it matters: Perfect record plus guilt-linked partner plus rookie is the default veteran package from Hornblower-in-space to every rescue procedural; nothing here belongs only to a pilot. Suggested fix: Replace the spotless record with a pilot-specific scar: one ship she brought in on time that should have been delayed, because neutrality forbade her to act. Make that, not the knee, what she and Ines do not raise.
- Problem 4: Setting components are individually familiar: ice-to-propellant station in gas-giant rings, a single chokepoint lane, a neutral service by treaty. The audit's nearest neighbor, Spaceship Thrive, already crosses gas-giant rings. Why it matters: Ring ice mining is the Belt/Saturn default since The Expanse (from memory); the resemblance is trope-level, but the setting alone will not distinguish the series on a retailer page. Suggested fix: Lean on the maritime-pilotage law, not the ice: put the pilot's legal position (advises, holds the conn, master stays liable, serves any flag) in the logline and setting paragraph, and give the Passage one named hazard unique to shepherd-moonlet dynamics.
- Reminded them of: The Expanse, James S. A. Corey: ring/ice economy, corporate conspiracy moving rocks (from memory) - shared tropes, not premise; Ginger Booth, Spaceship Thrive: gas-giant ring crossing (per audit) - setting detail only; Mark Twain, Life on the Mississippi: the river pilot reading a shifting channel, apprentice 'cub' (from memory) - the real ancestor, shared craft not plot; Nathan Lowell, Solar Clipper / Quarter Share: low-violence working-spacer competence fiction for the same KU readers (from memory) - shared tone; C. J. Cherryh, Merchanter / Alliance-Union books: station-and-ship politics, neutral trade services (from memory) - shared trope; Hornblower / Honor Harrington lineage: naval procedure in space, but there the hero commands; here she does not (from memory)
- Search note: No web search this round. All comparisons are from memory except Spaceship Thrive, Cherryh's Pilots' Guild and Shute's Pilotage, which come from the supplied audit. I recall no existing space series whose lead is a boarding pilot with conn-only authority; resemblance to all listed works is trope-level, not premise plus setting plus device. Titles (Under Pilotage, Outer Marker, Dead Freight, No Register, Inner Marker) were not checked; 'Dead Freight' is also a Breaking Bad episode title (from memory) and a standard shipping term.

**superfan-reader (Sam).** A harbor pilot in space with one lever is a real rule-driven idea; the villain and the cast are off the shelf, and nobody here is funny yet. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 7, originality 7, engine 8, character 6, setting 8, fit 8, clarity 8.

- Keep: The constraint as a game rule: "The hero commands nothing; she can only steer" and touches the quarrel "only through heading, speed and arrival time". This is the one thing I have not met before.; The Passage itself: a lane that "shifts by the hour", stirred "faster than tables update", pilots boarded by cutter under treaty, serving any flag. Rules I can learn and then watch get bent.; Book 1's knot: a first officer "right to want him relieved and wrong about when" plus a window "nine minutes shorter than the tables say". Specific, fair, no villain needed.
- Problem 1: The running thread ends at a greedy consortium chair on a yacht making people "rent its beacon net". Evil corporation manufacturing a crisis to sell the fix is the most stock piece on the page. Why it matters: I guessed it by book 2's "tug burns near Hob". Three books of setup deserve an answer I could not predict from the first clue. Suggested fix: Replace this first. Make the pushers sympathetic or inside the house: the Pilot Service itself, or miners nudging Hob to save the Kettle from a worse drift, so Maren's neutrality is what gets tested.
- Problem 2: The cast is a set of job titles: stern master guarding neutrality, apprentice with "fast hands and no fear yet", inspector "who can act where Maren cannot", nephew as reason to come home. Why it matters: I read for crews. None of these people wants anything that collides with Maren, and I have met the fearless apprentice and the at-home kid many times. Suggested fix: Give each one a want that costs Maren something: Dev thinks beacons would be safer than pilots, Calloway has bent neutrality once before, Sorensen needs her to break the rule she lives by.
- Problem 3: The single lever could run thin. Four books in which the answer is always arriving early or late risk the same trick, and book 4's "arrival hour delivers the proof" already sounds like book 2's move. Why it matters: As a designer I want one mechanic with new verbs each level. If the lever never changes shape, I stop at book 2. Suggested fix: State a different use of the lever per book: delay in 1, a chosen route past witnesses in 2, what her radar is allowed to see in 3, refusing the conn in 4.
- Problem 4: Humor is one sample of dry understatement, "closer than I'd choose", and Maren's flawless record "has never lost a ship" plus bad knee is the standard veteran kit. Why it matters: Thirty-one sealed hours with a taciturn perfect professional can read as a manual. I need someone to laugh with and a flaw that bites. Suggested fix: Let Okonjo or Dev carry real banter on the cutter, and give Maren a costly habit, such as trusting her own read over the tables even when the tables are right.

Cherryh, Merchanter novels (station and ship politics); The Expanse (ring ice, belt labor, corporate plot); Patrick O'Brian, Aubrey-Maturin (seamanship as plot); FTL: Faster Than Light (one ship, one run, closing lane)

**Rewrite: accepted (clear).** It ran over the length limit and was trimmed by a line editor before the gate.

- Changed: Running thread: the fuel consortium and its chair on a yacht are gone; the Pilot Service itself has pushed Hob for forty years to keep the lane beyond any beacon net one flag could own (both critics' top problem, stock corporate villain). Book 4 is now the Service's own tender with Calloway aboard.
- Changed: Each book line now states what Maren does with the lever, a different verb each time: sequence of the handover (1), speed and so the arrival shift (2), heading past Hob (3), withholding the conn (4) (auditor: lever only decided book 4; superfan: same trick four times).
- Changed: Lead: the nephew is cut and replaced by a pilot-specific scar, a ship delivered on the minute to a seizure crew because the rule required it; record, knee, radar read and voice kept word for word (both critics: shelf-standard veteran kit).
- Changed: Cast: each person now pulls against Maren or adds humor: Ines jokes about everything but the fall, Dev thinks beacons are safer than pilots, Sorensen wants her to say what she sees (superfan: job titles, no humor).
- Changed: Setting and logline: pilotage law moved forward (holds the conn, master stays liable, any flag) and one named shepherd-moonlet hazard added, the Comb; logline rewritten plain and positive, and 'cannot arrest, order or take sides' restored to the closing section (auditor's setting point; carried-forward judge note).
- Declined: Dropping 'has never lost a ship' and moving the unspoken subject between Maren and Ines from the fall to the scar: both were praised by earlier readers, so the scar was added beside them instead.
- Declined: Giving Maren the habit of trusting her read over correct tables, and Calloway a prior breach of neutrality in the cast line: cut for length, and Calloway's role is already carried by book 4.
- Declined: Making sympathetic miners the pushers: the Service-as-culprit option tests the hook (neutrality) more directly and keeps book 2's miners as witnesses.
- For the author to decide: Book 4 has Maren refuse the conn until the push logs are filed, which is her first act that is not steering. Decide whether the treaty gives a pilot the right to decline a ship (keeps her inside the rules) or whether this is her one deliberate breach (costs her something in later books).
- For the author to decide: The nephew Joost is cut to make room for the delivered-ship scar. If you want a home life for Maren as a series-long warmth, say so and it can return in place of one cast clause.

What the losing version did better: B's logline carries stakes ("one wrong heading kills everyone aboard").
