# m03: The Honest Pound

Revision log. Genre: Cozy fantasy mystery. Target readers: Women 25–45.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: The Honest Pound. In the canal city of Brindlemarket magic is a trade like any other, sold by weight and measure: luck by the ounce, bottled weather by the gill, sleep by the dram. Magic can make anything look, feel, smell and taste like more than it is. The one thing it cannot touch is the dull bronze of the city's master weights, cast before the first charm was sold, so the scale is the only place in Brindlemarket where a glamour fails. The Office of Weights and Measures keeps the masters, seals every scale and measure in the city, and is cordially disliked by everyone who sells anything. Lead: Wren Calloway, 34, Junior Inspector Third Class, exact, shy, fond of marzipan; daughter of a spice merchant ruined for a false scale, a verdict she has never believed. Every case starts with something that weighs wrong (short-weight saffron at the docks, a sleeping draught three drams light, a granary that balances on paper and not on the beam) and ends with a murder solved through quantities, seals, ledgers and residues. Cast ideas: her kind, idle senior inspector; a canal bargewoman who carries what the city would rather not weigh; her landlady of the Bakers' Guild; a rival from the Assay Hall. Thread: someone is forging the Office's own seals, and her father's ruin is tied to it. Titles from measure idioms (Short Weight, Baker's Dozen, False Bottom, Full Measure). An honest clerk, a set of weights and a city that would rather not be measured.

**Originality audit of the seed** (web searches, 15 November 2026):

> Closest found: Eliza Quill, Murder, Magic & a Midnight Market (a cozy fantasy mystery at a night market, with a Guild Inspector as victim); Seanan McGuire, In an Absent Dream (a market whose magic enforces fair value); Joseph Roth, Weights and Measures (an inspector lead, literary, no fantasy). No inspector-sleuth or sealed-scale device found. The seed has already shrunk the lantern night market and dropped a magic-enforced fair bargain.

**First draft:** “The Honest Pound”, 513 words.

### Round 1: First contact

**cold-reader (Priya).** A weights inspector solving murders by arithmetic in a city that sells luck by the ounce is fresh and rule-tight; I'd borrow it, though the found family feels thin. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 8, originality 8, engine 8, character 6, setting 8, fit 7, clarity 8.

- Keep: The magic rule: "luck by the ounce, weather by the gill, sleep by the dram" plus glamour that "fails on the dull bronze master weights" and "the beam only says how much". Clear limits, and I can see how clues work.; Wren's voice in one line: "She apologizes first, then corrects you to the grain, marzipan in hand."; "A murderer caught by arithmetic" and "every clue is a quantity": a civil servant detective with a fair, checkable puzzle.
- Problem 1: The cast reads as a list of job titles. Nobody has a relationship with Wren beyond function; "supplier of supper and gossip" and "smug and occasionally right" are stock roles. Why it matters: Found family is why I read cozy. I see a spare chair at a table but not who loves whom or what is at risk between them. Suggested fix: Give each cast member one line of specific bond or friction with Wren: what Osric owes her father, why Dot trusts her, what Henna nags her about. Show the family forming across books.
- Problem 2: Four murders, a drowning, a ruined father and a corrupt Office. The pitch promises warmth but the arc list is all corpses and forgery; no humor shows up in the book blurbs. Why it matters: It tilts toward procedural rather than cozy. I can't tell if I'll laugh or feel hugged, which is what I borrow for. Suggested fix: Add one warm or funny beat per book line (a festival, a disastrous charm, a boardinghouse subplot) and let the tone of the blurbs show the humor the brief promises.
- Problem 3: Books 2 to 4 all advance by 'another false seal found'. The thread looks like the same clue repeated, and the forger being "inside the Office" is guessable from the logline. Why it matters: If I can call the ending from the series page, the running thread stops pulling me through four books. Suggested fix: Make each book change the question: who can strike a die, why her father was targeted, who benefited. Hint at a personal cost in book 4 without naming where the forger sits.
- Problem 4: Wren's inner life is one grievance. "Shy" and careful are stated, but I don't know what she wants for herself beyond clearing her father, or what the shyness costs her. Why it matters: A heroine defined only by a dad's old case feels like a plot device; I need a small private want to root for. Suggested fix: Add one personal, low-stakes desire or flaw that grows across the series: a friendship she avoids, a room she won't unpack, a fear of speaking at hearings that she overcomes.
- Reminded them of: Legends & Lattes; The Tainted Cup; Discworld's Ankh-Morpork (Going Postal); The Undertaking of Hart and Mercy; Blades in the Dark (canal city vibe); The Spellshop

**ten-second-browser (a subscription reader scrolling on a phone).** A genuinely fresh cozy premise with a lovely city, but the first lines sell arithmetic and murder before they sell warmth or a person I want to follow. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 7, originality 8, engine 8, character 6, setting 8, fit 7, clarity 6.

- Keep: "magic is sold by the ounce" with "luck by the ounce, weather by the gill, sleep by the dram" — the world in one line, and it caught my eye first; "She apologizes first, then corrects you to the grain, marzipan in hand" — the only line where Wren becomes a person; move it up, never cut it; "Magic is what gets faked, never what solves the case" plus the titles Short Weight / Baker's Dozen / False Bottom / Full Measure
- Problem 1: In ten seconds I caught "magic is sold by the ounce" and "shy weights inspector", then nearly swiped at "follows each short measure to a murder". I had to reread it and still did not know what a short measure was. Why it matters: The logline is all I read before deciding. Jargon plus a clunky verb costs the borrow, and "shy" alone gives me nobody to love yet. Suggested fix: Say it plainly: when a stall sells an ounce light, someone ends up dead, and Wren is the only one who checks. Swap "shy" for the apologizes-then-corrects-you behavior.
- Problem 2: Warmth arrives last. "canal fog, warm bread, a spare chair at the boardinghouse table" is the coziest line and sits near the bottom; the top half is seals, ledgers, residues and four corpses. Why it matters: I pick cozy for the feeling. After ten seconds I knew the mechanism and the murders but not whether this would be comforting or dry. Suggested fix: Put the boardinghouse table, Henna's suppers and the marzipan into the logline or the first setting sentence. One sensory comfort beat before any mention of ledgers or seals.
- Problem 3: "caught by arithmetic", "every clue is a quantity", "a sum the reader can check" is repeated three ways. It starts to sound like homework, and no humor is actually shown anywhere on the page. Why it matters: Late at night I want charm and a puzzle I can enjoy guessing, not a promise that I will be doing sums. The brief promises humor; I saw none. Suggested fix: State the fair-puzzle promise once. Use the freed space for one funny concrete example of a glamour scam, like a loaf that tastes like more than it is, caught on the bronze weight.
- Problem 4: Found family is a list, not a feeling. Dot "carries what the city would rather not weigh" is vague, Osric is "kind, idle", and there is no hint of affection, friction or romance between anyone. Why it matters: I binge four books for the people around the table. Book 2 hinges on caring that Henna is accused, and right now she is a job description. Suggested fix: Give each cast member one line of relationship to Wren: what Henna nags her about, what Osric covers for, whether Lucan's rivalry has any spark. Make Dot's cargo concrete.
- Reminded them of: Legends & Lattes (cozy trade-and-found-family feel); The Spellshop; The Undertaking of Hart and Mercy (civil-servant fantasy); Discworld city watch books, especially Going Postal; The Thursday Murder Club (fair cozy puzzle, ensemble); Miss Percy's Pocket Guide

**Rewrite: accepted (clear).**

- Changed: Logline rewritten in plain words: 'short measure' and 'shy' are gone, replaced by 'sells an ounce light, someone ends up dead' and Wren's apologize-then-prove behavior (ten-second-browser, problem 1).
- Changed: Warmth moved up: canal fog and warm bread now open the setting, with a concrete funny glamour scam (a small loaf passing as a large one); the engine now names a comic scam and supper at Henna's table per book (ten-second-browser 2 and 3, cold-reader 2).
- Changed: Each cast member now has a bond or friction with Wren: Osric signs whatever she asks, Dot hauls condemned goods and gives blunt advice, Henna nags her to unpack, Lucan keeps saving her a seat (both critics on found family).
- Changed: Wren gets a small private want: the trunk she has never unpacked, paid off in book 4 along with speaking at a public hearing; the spare chair 'slowly becomes Wren's' (cold-reader 4).
- Changed: The running thread now changes question each book (a seal the Office never struck, only three people can cut a die, who gained by her father's ruin) and 'inside the Office' is removed from book 4; the fair-puzzle promise is stated less often (cold-reader 3, ten-second-browser 3).
- Declined: Removing 'caught by arithmetic' and 'every clue is a quantity': both critics named them as strengths, so only the third repetition was cut.
- Declined: A festival or comic subplot in every book line: only book 2 got one (the Loaf Fair), since four would break the one-line format and the length cap.
- Declined: Adding the father thread back into the logline: it cost the plain wording the browser asked for, and the lead and running thread carry it.
- For the author to decide: Lucan 'forever saving her a seat' hints at a slow-burn romance. Do you want one across the four books, or should he stay a friendly rival?
- For the author to decide: Book 4 no longer says where the forger sits. Should the forger be someone in the found family's orbit (a personal cost for Wren) or an Office figure outside it, which keeps the ending gentler?

**Gate (reader: Priya, blind A/B)** preferred the rewrite (clear). B gives me the found family: the unpacked trunk paying off in book 4, Henna nagging, Lucan saving her a seat, supper at the table. The small-loaf glamour is more specific, and the cast do things instead of being described. A reads like a casebook. What the losing version did better: A's logline carries the father's forged seal, so the personal stake hooks at once. "Older than the first charm" explains why the weights resist glamour. "A sum the reader can check" is sharper than B's closing line.
