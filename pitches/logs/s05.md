# s05: The Tally Counts

Revision log. Genre: Cozy science fiction (space). Target readers: Women and men 30–60.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: Off by One. In the 2260s the Outer Reaches hold a census every ten years, and the law says a person must be seen and asked; no sensor counts. Assembly seats and the air subsidy both follow the headcount. Enumeration District 114 is 2,300 habitats strung across forty astronomical units, and its senior enumerator is Nell Abara, 53: twenty-nine years in the service, unbribable, a knitter, widow of a pilot named Ade, flying the old patrol cutter Tally with a galley she is proud of. Her new apprentice, Davi, 22, has a statistics degree and thinks the whole thing could be done by satellite. The enumerator's oath: everyone counts, once. Each book is one habitat where the count is wrong. One heartbeat more than the register. Forty names drawing air who have not breathed in years. A community that refuses to be counted at all. A ship that swears it is a town. Finding out why means sitting in kitchens, reading ration books and noticing who sets an extra place. Small strange societies, each with its own good reasons. Thread: one name counted twice in the same week, in two habitats nine astronomical units apart, by two honest enumerators; Nell has one census and four books to find out how. In the background, the Reaches keep their Assembly seats only if the total clears a threshold. Kind, curious, funny; nobody is a villain for being odd.

**Originality audit of the seed** (web searches, 15 November 2026):

> No space-census series found. Census-taker leads elsewhere: Clifford Simak, Census (1944); Frederik Pohl, The Census Takers (1956, the census as a cull); China Miéville, This Census-Taker; Jesse Ball, Census (a traveling census taker and his son); the Doctor Who audio Order of the Daleks (a Galactic Census assessor with a probationary assessor). Keep the stakes mechanical and the tone comic, away from Ball's elegiac register.

**First draft:** “Off by One”, 509 words.

### Round 1: First contact

**cold-reader (Aisha).** A census taker whose only power is to knock and ask is an idea I have not met; I would borrow book 1, though the twin reveal worries me. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 8, originality 8, engine 7, character 7, setting 8, fit 9, clarity 8.

- Keep: The premise and its rule: "no sensor, satellite or proxy counts. A person must be seen and asked" with stakes of "air and votes, never murder"; "Every society has a sound reason for its wrong number" — each case is a small anthropology, especially Saint Brine's forty paperless ice-cutters and Quiet Harbor's debtors who refuse any ledger; Nell at 53: "twenty-nine years unbribed", "the galley her late husband Ade, a pilot, fitted for two", noticing "the ration book in new handwriting"
- Problem 1: The running thread resolves as "twin sisters sharing one register line". That is the first guess any reader makes about one person seen twice, and the pitch gives it away. Why it matters: Four books of waiting for the oldest answer in mystery fiction; a series promising a puzzle that "plays fair" should not end on one I solved on the series page. Suggested fix: Keep Junia Vasko seen twice, but make the answer a social fact of the Reaches: why a family or habitat needs one line for two people, and what Nell's oath costs them. Do not state it in the pitch.
- Problem 2: The rule that carries everything, why "no sensor, satellite or proxy counts", is never explained. Davi's objection that "a satellite could finish by lunch" is reasonable and goes unanswered. Why it matters: I work with measurement; a rule with no reason reads as author convenience, and Davi becomes a strawman set up to be taught a lesson. Suggested fix: Add one sentence of history: a past census falsified by sensor data or proxies, habitats erased or invented, so that being seen and asked is a hard-won right rather than a quaint custom.
- Problem 3: Books 1 and 2 share a shape: hidden people, kind enumerator registers them, all is well. "Every case ends with the count made right" promises that Nell is never wrong and counting never harms. Why it matters: Being counted is not always a kindness. If the oath is never tested, the warmth turns sugary and I stop believing these people by book 2. Suggested fix: Let one case cost Nell something: the correct count drops a habitat's subsidy or the Reaches below four million, and she must choose. Book 3's refusers are the place to make her doubt the ledger itself.
- Problem 4: The supporting cast are job titles with one adjective: "the honest enumerator next district over", "mail-barge skipper and gossip". Imogen Park has a budget but no want. Davi's arc seems finished in book 1. Why it matters: I come back for people. Only Nell is one yet, and an apprentice who learns his lesson in the first book has nowhere to go for three more. Suggested fix: Give each a want that rubs against Nell's: Park needs the four million whatever the truth, Tobias envies her record, Davi keeps being partly right about data through book 3.
