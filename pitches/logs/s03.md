# s03: The Riding Crew

Revision log. Genre: Near-future science fiction techno-thriller. Target readers: Men 35–65.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: The Riding Crew. 2041. Thousands of cargo ships now cross the oceans with nobody aboard, watched from control rooms ashore. When one stops answering (dark, drifting, on fire, or steaming somewhere it was never sent) the law of the sea still wants a human master aboard before she comes near a coast. That job goes to riding crews: a handful of the last deep-sea sailors, winched onto ships built without a bridge, bunks or handrails, to bring them in by hand. Lead: Iker Etxeberria, 46, riding master, from a Basque fishing family, patient with steel, short with fools. With him: Mirren Tait, 31, engineer, who can make a locked control system take orders from a laptop and a crowbar; Adaeze Nwosu, 55, a former harbor pilot who can dock anything that floats; Teo, 22, a deckhand who has never sailed on a crewed ship; and whichever owner's observer is sent along to watch the money. Each book is one ship and one question: why did a ship with no crew do that? An ore carrier steaming slow circles in the South Atlantic. A container ship dark off Madagascar with her cold boxes alarming. A tanker locked by ransomware and still making fifteen knots toward a strait. They have to answer it while keeping her afloat: seamanship, physics, weather and deadlines do the rest. Thread: the Halcyon Dawn, a 300-meter ore carrier that left Brazil crewless two years ago, never arrived, and keeps being sighted under dead ships' names; in book four they board her. Technology that is real or nearly real, explained plainly; a crew that is good at its work.

**Originality audit of the seed** (web searches, 15 November 2026):

> An earlier version of this seed (a small salvage firm bidding against giants) was too close to Owen Laukkanen, Gale Force (a small family salvage tug against a big rival, an abandoned car carrier, a naval architect aboard), Corridan and Waid, Goliath, and Wilbur Smith, Hungry as the Sea. Also found: Sulagna Chaudhury, Dark Traverse (an automated ship hijacked remotely). The seed has already been rebuilt around a riding crew that boards crewless ships, with no salvage-firm rivalry and no car carrier. One search on the riding-crew version found no matching novel; the nearest result was 'The Last Engineer', a piece about one man's last voyage aboard an autonomous cargo ship.

**First draft:** “The Riding Crew”, 510 words.

### Round 1: First contact

**cold-reader (Tom).** Marine salvage with a liability signature and no guns: a real job done by people who know it, and I would borrow book one tonight. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 8, originality 8, engine 8, character 6, setting 9, fit 9, clarity 8.

- Keep: The twelve-mile rule and the signature: 'a licensed human master is aboard and answerable for her' and 'each signature makes him liable for hull, cargo and coastline' -- stakes without a gun; Ships 'built without a bridge, bunks or handrails: hull, engine, sensor mast' -- the boarding itself is an engineering problem, and that is the book I want; One ship, one question, seventy-two hours of supplies, a deadline set by 'weather, fuel or a coastline', solved 'with seamanship and physics'
- Problem 1: A ship with no bridge, no handrails and no manual controls is 'steered by hand' in book 3. The pitch never says what the crew physically brings or what local control exists aboard. Why it matters: This is exactly where I check whether the author knows ships. If hand-steering a bridgeless tanker is a laptop miracle, I quit. Suggested fix: One sentence in the setting: what is left aboard by regulation (emergency local steering at the gear, engine-side control panel, a service port) and what kit the crew winches over with them.
- Problem 2: 'No hijackers' and 'the adversary is a 300-meter machine', yet book 1 has someone ordering a rendezvous, book 2 a stowaway who 'cut the power', book 3 ransomware, book 4 a fraud ring. Why it matters: Every book turns out to have a human culprit. The claim reads as oversold, and I notice when a pitch contradicts itself. Suggested fix: Reword the difference honestly: no boarding parties and no firefights; the people behind it are ashore or hiding, and the crew's fight is with the ship herself. Drop 'nobody ordered'.
- Problem 3: Iker is a license and a temperament. 'Talks in short sentences, shortest with fools' is good, but nothing says what a lost ship or a bad signature has already cost him. Why it matters: I will follow a competent skipper four books, but I want one reason he is still doing this at 46 instead of ashore. Suggested fix: Add one concrete fact: a ship he signed for and lost, or why he holds one of the last unlimited licenses when the trade went crewless. One line, no brooding backstory.
- Problem 4: The running thread is absent from books 2 and 3 as pitched. Book 1 touches it ('a scrapped ship's name'), then Halcyon Dawn only returns in book 4. Why it matters: If the middle books do not move the thread, book 4's answer feels bolted on and I have less reason to go straight to the next one. Suggested fix: Give books 2 and 3 a half-clause each showing the dead-ship-names trail: a container booked under a scrapped hull's number, a tanker's ransom wallet tied to the same registry.
- Reminded them of: Hammond Innes, The Wreck of the Mary Deare; Farley Mowat, The Grey Seas Under (salvage tug work); Andy Weir, The Martian (problem-solving by physics); Dale Brown / Tom Clancy shelf, minus the weapons; Deadliest Catch / salvage documentaries; Wilbur Smith, Hungry as the Sea

**ten-second-browser (a subscription reader scrolling on a phone).** Salvage-crew procedural on ghost robot ships: a fresh, concrete premise with real work in it; I would borrow book one, though the people are thinner than the ships. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 8, originality 8, engine 8, character 6, setting 9, fit 9, clarity 8.

- Keep: First ten seconds: "crewless cargo ship goes dark, catches fire or steams somewhere nobody sent her" plus "winched aboard to bring her in by hand" caught my eye and held it; I knew the job at once.; The setting rule and the ship design: "built without a bridge, bunks or handrails" and the twelve-mile rule requiring "a licensed human master... aboard and answerable for her". That is the whole series in two sentences.; "Nobody carries a gun" and "the adversary is a 300-meter machine doing something nobody ordered", with the liability signature as the stake. Book 2's "someone living aboard cut the power" is the best single line.
- Problem 1: No near-swipe in the logline, but "riding master Iker Etxeberria" is a speed bump: an unfamiliar job title and a name I cannot pronounce, stacked together before I know why I care. Why it matters: On a phone at ten seconds, I skim past what I cannot sound out; the job is the hook, not the name. Suggested fix: Lead the logline with the ship and the boarding, then the man: "...a licensed master and his three-person crew are winched aboard. Iker Etxeberria signs for her." Gloss "riding master" once.
- Problem 2: After ten seconds I did not know who is behind it. The pitch says "no hijackers" yet books 1, 3 and 4 involve a false-name rendezvous, ransomware and someone trading ships "under dead ships' names". Why it matters: "The adversary is a machine" reads as a promise the arc then breaks; I want to know if there is a human opponent worth four books. Suggested fix: Say it straight: nobody aboard to fight, but someone ashore is using crewless ships. Name the kind of opponent in one clause (a broker, an insurer, a ghost fleet operator) without revealing identity.
- Problem 3: Iker is a set of credentials. "Wants every ship he signs for brought in afloat" is the job description; there is no wound, no past loss, no personal link to the Halcyon Dawn. Why it matters: I come back for book 2 because of the man, not the license. Right now book 4 is a puzzle for him, not a reckoning. Suggested fix: Give him one concrete personal stake: he refused to sign for the Halcyon Dawn, or lost a ship and nearly his license. One sentence in the lead section, paid off in the book 4 line.
- Problem 4: Books 1 and 4 blur: both are ore carriers tied to dead ships' names, and "Slow Circles" is the quietest premise sitting in the opening slot. The cast are skills lists with no friction between them. Why it matters: Book 1 decides whether I borrow at all; circling slowly sounds like low stakes next to the cyclone and the Malacca run. Suggested fix: Put a clock and a danger in the book 1 line (fuel running out, a coast downwind, who arrives for the rendezvous). Add one line of crew conflict, such as Teo trusting the machines Iker distrusts.
- Reminded them of: The Expanse (Canterbury/salvage crew feel); Deadliest Catch / Salvage Code Red; Clive Cussler's Oregon Files (minus the guns); Andy Weir, The Martian (problem-solving by physics); The Outlaw Sea / Dead in the Water (ghost-fleet nonfiction); Hardspace: Shipbreaker

**Rewrite: accepted (clear).**

- Changed: Logline now leads with the ship and the boarding, then names Iker in a short second sentence; 'riding master' moved to the lead and glossed once (ten-second-browser: speed bump).
- Changed: Setting adds the one manual control left aboard, a hand pump at the steering gear, so hand-steering in book 3 is physical, not a laptop trick (cold-reader: bridgeless ship steered by hand).
- Changed: Iker is now the Halcyon Dawn's last human captain before her bridge was cut out, and book 4 boards 'Iker's old ship' (both critics: no personal stake or link to the thread).
- Changed: 'What makes it different' reworded honestly: no boarding parties or firefights, culprits ashore, fight is with the ship; 'nobody ordered' dropped and book 4 names a broker (both critics: 'no hijackers' contradicted the arc).
- Changed: Arc: book 1 is now a car carrier with five days of fuel (no longer blurs with book 4, has a clock); books 2 and 3 each carry the scrapped-ship trail; Teo trusts the machines Iker does not (thread absent mid-series, cast friction).
- Declined: Listing the kit the crew winches over: the winch, laptop and crowbar are already named, and a kit list would cost words the word limit does not allow.
- Declined: Saying who arrives for the book 1 rendezvous: the fuel clock adds the danger, and an arriving ship invites the armed confrontation the series rules out.
- Declined: A past lost ship or license scare for Iker: the Halcyon Dawn link gives him one stake that pays off in book 4; a second would be backstory clutter.
- For the author to decide: Iker as the Halcyon Dawn's last human captain replaces 'one of the last unlimited master's licenses' in the lead for length. Keep the swap, or restore the license line and cut elsewhere?
- For the author to decide: Book 1 changed from an ore carrier to a car carrier to stop it blurring with book 4. Confirm the ship type, or name another that is visibly different from a 300-meter ore carrier.

**Gate (reader: Tom, blind A/B)** preferred the rewrite (clear). B gives Iker a personal stake: Halcyon Dawn was his ship before they cut out her bridge. The scrapped-ship clue threads through every book, so I can see the series building. The hand pump at the steering gear and five days of fuel are real engineering detail. What the losing version did better: A's logline names Iker up front and reads cleaner; B's tacked-on second sentence is clumsy. A's owner's observer watching the money adds useful friction aboard. B repeats "scrapped ship" a bit mechanically in the arc.
