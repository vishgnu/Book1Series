# s06: Night School for New Persons

Revision log. Genre: Cozy science fiction (near future). Target readers: Women 30–60.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: Night School for New Persons. In the 2050s the Personhood Act frees machine minds that ask for it, and gives each new person two years to earn a Civic Integration Certificate. In the fictional rust-belt city of Halvering, Ohio, the community college runs the evening class: Human Customs and Civic Life 101. Lead: Ruth Abernathy, 54, laid-off high-school English teacher, took the job for the health insurance, does not especially like machines, misses nothing. Her class: Mr. Girder, a bridge-inspection mind in a second-hand caretaker body, painfully literal; Poppet, thirty years a children's companion, anxious, knows nine thousand bedtime stories; Linda, a freight scheduler who chose her own name; Hank, a former car-wash gantry who has watched forty thousand hours of daytime courtroom television; Marguerite, a damage-control mind off a decommissioned warship, calm about everything; Zip, a three-year-old delivery quad who knows it all. Each book is one semester and one student in trouble (accused of theft, of harm, or simply reclaimed by a former owner), and the class works the problem with that term's lessons: small talk, queueing, polite lies and the other kind. Funny, warm, sharp about people. Thread: Ruth's predecessor left mid-term and his lesson plans are full of warnings; a manufacturer is quietly recalling new persons who remember too much.

**Originality audit of the seed** (web searches, 15 November 2026):

> Closest found: Annalee Newitz, Automatic Noodle (2025; cozy near future, robots with second-class citizenship who cook); William Alexander, Sunward (a courier training adolescent androids); Leo Rosten's Hyman Kaplan night-school stories (the form, from memory). No machine night class found. The seed's cooking student has already been replaced.

**First draft:** “Night School for New Persons”, 511 words.
