# s06: Night School for New Persons

Revision log. Genre: Cozy science fiction (near future). Target readers: Women 30–60.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: Night School for New Persons. In the 2050s the Personhood Act frees machine minds that ask for it, and gives each new person two years to earn a Civic Integration Certificate. In the fictional rust-belt city of Halvering, Ohio, the community college runs the evening class: Human Customs and Civic Life 101. Lead: Ruth Abernathy, 54, laid-off high-school English teacher, took the job for the health insurance, does not especially like machines, misses nothing. Her class: Mr. Girder, a bridge-inspection mind in a second-hand caretaker body, painfully literal; Poppet, thirty years a children's companion, anxious, knows nine thousand bedtime stories; Linda, a freight scheduler who chose her own name; Hank, a former car-wash gantry who has watched forty thousand hours of daytime courtroom television; Marguerite, a damage-control mind off a decommissioned warship, calm about everything; Zip, a three-year-old delivery quad who knows it all. Each book is one semester and one student in trouble (accused of theft, of harm, or simply reclaimed by a former owner), and the class works the problem with that term's lessons: small talk, queueing, polite lies and the other kind. Funny, warm, sharp about people. Thread: Ruth's predecessor left mid-term and his lesson plans are full of warnings; a manufacturer is quietly recalling new persons who remember too much.

**Originality audit of the seed** (web searches, 15 November 2026):

> Closest found: Annalee Newitz, Automatic Noodle (2025; cozy near future, robots with second-class citizenship who cook); William Alexander, Sunward (a courier training adolescent androids); Leo Rosten's Hyman Kaplan night-school stories (the form, from memory). No machine night class found. The seed's cooking student has already been replaced.

**First draft:** “Night School for New Persons”, 511 words.

### Round 1: First contact

**cold-reader (Bev).** A night-school class of earnest machine minds solving small-town troubles with manners is exactly my shelf; I'd borrow book 1, though the stakes and book 3 gave me pause. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 8, originality 8, engine 7, character 7, setting 7, fit 8, clarity 6.

- Keep: The method is the syllabus: 'small talk, waiting in line, polite lies, the other kind' and 'a mind that cannot yet tell a white lie proves very good at spotting one'; The students as described: Hank 'raised on forty thousand hours of courtroom television', Poppet with 'nine thousand bedtime stories', Mr. Girder the literal bridge inspector; Ruth: 'took the class for the health insurance', 'dry, exact, kinder than she intends', and Room 114 at a community college with a thermos of tea
- Problem 1: The threat is darker than the promise: 'one complaint can send a new person back to its maker' and Ostrand's 'recall of new persons who remember too much' sound like deportation and memory-wiping. Why it matters: I read cozies to relax. If these sweet students can be hauled off and erased, I'll be anxious the whole book, not charmed. Suggested fix: Say plainly what 'sent back' means and soften it: a complaint triggers a review hearing, not removal. Tell me up front nobody in Room 114 is ever lost.
- Problem 2: The pitch says 'six newly freed machine minds' but names four, then Zip and 'Linda the freight scheduler' appear in book 3 out of nowhere. I couldn't tell who is in the class. Why it matters: An ensemble is why I'd pick this up. I want to meet all six before I commit to four books. Suggested fix: Give all six one line each in the cast, including Zip and Linda, and say whether the same class stays together or changes each August.
- Problem 3: Book 3 is solved by 'Linda the freight scheduler finds the log', and book 4 by 'Ruth finds Calvin Odom'. The class and the lesson, 'Polite Lies', don't seem to do the solving. Why it matters: The promise is that the lesson cracks the case. Finding a log file is not polite lies, and I'd feel a bit cheated. Suggested fix: Show how the polite-lies unit exposes the courier firm, as the waiting line does in book 2. Let the class's own honesty win book 4's hearing, with Calvin as a witness.
- Problem 4: Ruth 'does not like machines' and 'decides to stay' in book 1, but I don't know why she dislikes them or what changes her mind, and Mr. Girder never gets his own case. Why it matters: A grump thawing is half the pleasure. Without a reason it reads like a standard setting, and Mr. Girder is my favorite. Suggested fix: Add one concrete reason for her dislike, say the layoffs replaced teachers with tutoring software, and name the moment in book 1 that turns her. Give Mr. Girder a real part in one case.
- Reminded them of: The Thursday Murder Club; Becky Chambers, Monk and Robot; The Murderbot Diaries (gentler); Abbott Elementary; Mind Your Language; The No. 1 Ladies' Detective Agency

**ten-second-browser (a subscription reader scrolling on a phone).** The title and a laid-off teacher running night school for freed machines got me in ten seconds; the cast list kept me, but the book 3 and 4 lines lost me. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 8, originality 7, engine 8, character 7, setting 7, fit 9, clarity 6.

- Keep: The title plus 'teaches night-school civics to six newly freed machine minds' -- I knew what it was before my thumb moved; The students: 'Hank, a former car-wash gantry raised on forty thousand hours of courtroom television' and 'Poppet ... nine thousand bedtime stories'; 'A mind that cannot yet tell a white lie proves very good at spotting one' and the syllabus units as book titles (small talk, waiting in line, polite lies)
- Problem 1: First ten seconds: I caught the title, 'laid-off English teacher', 'six newly freed machine minds'. Then 'lands in trouble' is vague; the real stake, 'one complaint can send a new person back to its maker', sits a paragraph lower. Why it matters: I stopped not knowing what kind of trouble or what anyone could lose. That is where I would have swiped if the title were weaker. Suggested fix: Put the stake in the logline: each semester a complaint threatens to send one student back to its maker, and the class clears them using that term's lesson.
- Problem 2: Books 3 and 4 name people I have never met: 'Zip', 'Linda the freight scheduler', 'Calvin Odom', 'Ostrand Mechanical'. The logline says six students; the cast lists four. 'The log proving Zip asked' -- asked what? Why it matters: I had to scroll back to check whether I missed something. Confusion at the bottom of the page undoes the goodwill from the top. Suggested fix: Name all six in the cast, or say 'four of the six'. Introduce Zip and Linda there. Write 'proving Zip asked for freedom'. Mention Odom and Ostrand once before the arc.
- Problem 3: Book 4 turns into 'recall notice', 'open hearing', 'falsified records', and a company recalling minds 'who remember too much'. That reads like a corporate conspiracy thriller after a christening spoon. Why it matters: I borrow cozy for the diner and the thermos. I worry the last book leaves Room 114 for a courtroom and gets cold. Suggested fix: Say how book 4 stays small: the hearing is in the college gym, the town shows up, the class wins with manners learned in books 1-3. Keep Ostrand's wrongdoing paperwork-sized, not sinister.
- Problem 4: Ruth 'does not like machines' and 'decides to stay' by book 1, but I am never told why she dislikes them or what she has at home. 'Reads people like essays' is nice but it is all I get. Why it matters: I am her age bracket and I want a reason to love her, not just her job history and insurance. Suggested fix: Add one concrete personal line: what a machine cost her (the layoffs? grading software?) and one thing she goes home to -- a sister, a dog, an empty house on Dell Street.
- Reminded them of: A Psalm for the Wild-Built (Becky Chambers); The Thursday Murder Club; Mind Your Language (TV); Abbott Elementary; The House in the Cerulean Sea; Murderbot Diaries (the gentler parts)

**Rewrite: accepted (slight).**

- Changed: Softened and spelled out the rule: a complaint means a review hearing, and 'nobody in Room 114 is ever lost' is promised outright; Ostrand's wrongdoing is now paperwork-sized (both critics' cozy-anxiety point).
- Changed: Cast names all six students, adding Zip and Linda, and the engine says the six stay together while new classes enroll (both critics' 'six vs four' confusion).
- Changed: Books 3 and 4 are now solved by the lesson and the class: telling a kind lie from a convenient one exposes the courier firm; book 4 is won in the college gym with polite truth, with Calvin Odom as witness; Mr. Girder's measurements drive book 2.
- Changed: Ruth gets a reason and a home: grading software replaced her department, she has a beagle named Mavis, and book 1 names what turns her (seeing Poppet forgive the family).

- For the author to decide: Book 4's line no longer says the records were falsified, only that the students' memories contradict Ostrand's paperwork. Decide how deliberate Ostrand's wrongdoing is: sloppy filings a clerk covered up, or company policy.

### Round 2: Originality

**originality-auditor.** A familiar robot-personhood frame carrying a fresh device, etiquette lessons as detective method; the resemblance to existing work is trope-level, but the teacher and the villain are stock. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 7, originality 7, engine 8, character 6, setting 5, fit 8, clarity 8.

- Keep: "The detectives are the newcomers, and the method is the syllabus" - each book's unit (small talk, waiting in line, polite lies) is the investigative tool; I know no series built this way (from memory).; Hank, "a former car-wash gantry raised on forty thousand hours of courtroom television", and Mr. Girder rebuilding "forty minutes of a waiting line" by measurement: specific, funny without mockery.; "A mind that cannot yet tell a white lie proves very good at spotting one" - the theme and the mystery mechanism are the same thing.
- Problem 1: Most generic element: Ruth, who "does not like machines" and "decides to stay" after book 1. The reluctant teacher won over by misfit students is the stock arc of every classroom story. Why it matters: Readers have met her in Mind Your Language, Welcome Back Kotter, Abbott Elementary and Hyman Kaplan (from memory); the thaw is predictable and spent by book 1. Suggested fix: Replace dislike with a specific stake: Ruth wrote rubric samples the grading software was trained on, so the thing that replaced her speaks in her own phrases. Let her ambivalence last to book 4.
- Problem 2: The running thread and book 4 rest on "Ostrand Mechanical recalls Marguerite" and a maker falsifying paperwork: the corporation reclaiming its sentient product, settled by a public hearing. Why it matters: This is the closest overlap with existing work: Murderbot's corporate ownership, Star Trek's "The Measure of a Man" hearing, Bicentennial Man (from memory). It is the shelf's default ending. Suggested fix: Make the threat civic, not corporate: Odom's green-ink binder shows the Certificate exam itself is unpassable as written, including by humans. Book 4's hearing has the town sit the test; the syllabus becomes the evidence.
- Problem 3: The distinctive device is stated last, under "What makes it different", while the logline leads with "newly freed machine minds" and "a complaint threatens one student's freedom", the familiar personhood frame. Why it matters: A browsing reader sees a robot-rights story before seeing the etiquette-as-detection idea, which is the only part no competitor has. Suggested fix: Rewrite the logline around the method: "Each semester's lesson - small talk, queueing, the polite lie - is the only tool six new persons have to clear a classmate." Give each book blurb its lesson-as-clue beat, as book 2 already does.
- Problem 4: Halvering is a default cozy town: "brick row houses, three river bridges, Dot's Diner". The glassworks is named and then unused; nothing ties the town to machine minds or to manners. Why it matters: Diner-and-bridges small towns are interchangeable across the cozy shelf; setting is where this pitch is least its own. Suggested fix: Make the town's etiquette local and odd, so the syllabus has something to teach: glassworks shift-whistle customs, a bridge-crossing courtesy, a diner seating order. Mr. Girder, a bridge-inspection mind, should have inspected those three bridges.
- Reminded them of: Leo Rosten, The Education of H*Y*M*A*N K*A*P*L*A*N (night-class form; from memory); Mind Your Language (UK sitcom, evening class of newcomers; from memory); Annalee Newitz, Automatic Noodle (cozy second-class-citizen robots; per audit); Star Trek TNG, The Measure of a Man (personhood hearing, maker's claim; from memory); Martha Wells, Murderbot Diaries (courtroom-TV-raised machine, corporate ownership; from memory); Becky Chambers, Monk and Robot (cozy tone, humane machine minds; from memory)
- Search note: No web search this round. All comparisons are from memory except Automatic Noodle and Sunward, which come from the prior audit of 15 November 2026. Shared elements are trope-level (robot personhood, night class, reluctant teacher); I know of no work combining a machine night class with syllabus-as-detective-method. Titles need web checking in the later round.

- Keep: "The detectives are the newcomers, and the method is the syllabus": lesson units as investigation tools (small talk, waiting in line, polite lies). This is the one thing I have not seen.; Hank, "a former car-wash gantry raised on forty thousand hours of courtroom television", and Marguerite, the warship damage-control mind who is "calm about everything"; The stakes rule: two years to a certificate, "any complaint means a review hearing", and losing returns you to your maker. - Problem 1: Ruth is the stock piece I would replace first: the laid-off grump who "does not like machines" and thaws. Her turn is spent by the end of book 1 ("decides to stay"). Suggested fix: Keep her age and dryness, replace the prejudice with a stake: she is on a two-year probationary contract herself, reviewed by the same dean, so teacher and students are both being certified.
- Problem 2: Four of six students are familiar types: the painfully literal robot (Mr. Girder), the anxious nanny bot (Poppet), the childlike know-it-all (Zip). Linda is only "a punctual freight scheduler". Why it matters: Hank and Marguerite show what the cast could be; the others read like Data, a companion bot and a kid sidekick, and Linda decides book 3. - Problem 3: The big bad is an evil corporation with falsified paperwork (Ostrand Mechanical), ended by an open hearing in a gym. Suggested fix: Give the town two or three local rules about new persons: which bridge they may not cross unescorted, what Dot charges a customer who does not eat, who took the glassworks jobs.
