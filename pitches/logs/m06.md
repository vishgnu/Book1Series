# m06: As If Live: The Sound-Effects Mysteries

Revision log. Genre: Historical mystery. Target readers: Women and men 45–75.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: The Sound-Effects Girl. Cincinnati, 1947. Hazel Novak, 31, is the only woman sound-effects artist at the fictional radio station WQC, the Voice of the Queen City: by day she makes the doors, teacups and footsteps of the soap operas, by night the gunshots and stabbed cabbages of the thrillers. Her ear is her trade: she can tell a .38 from a backfire, crepe soles from leather, linoleum from pine. Radio is changing under her feet. Magnetic tape has just arrived from Germany, and for the first time a voice can be cut, moved and played back as if it were live, so a broadcast is no longer proof that anyone was anywhere. Each case turns on something heard: a death during a live show, a ransom call with the wrong streetcar bell behind it, a recording with one splice too many. The reader gets every sound she gets. Cast ideas: the station's chief engineer, who distrusts tape and loves it; her uncle, a police sergeant who half-listens to her; a soap company's sponsor man; a script girl who knows every actor's lies. Thread: the station's very first reel of tape, recorded by accident during a rehearsal, holds forty seconds of two people planning something; Hazel knows one voice and spends four books finding the other. Titles could be sounds (The Second Door, Footsteps on Gravel, One Splice Too Many).

**Originality audit of the seed** (web searches, 15 November 2026):

> Closest found: Cheryl Honigford, The Darkness Knows and two sequels (Chicago, 1938, radio-station murders, the lead is a radio actress); the film Radioland Murders (Chicago, 1939, killings during a live broadcast); Jean Sheldon, She Overheard Murder (a postwar Chicago radio actress). No sound-effects sleuth found. The seed has already been moved from Chicago in 1938 to Cincinnati in 1947 and the arrival of tape.

**First draft:** “The Sound-Effects Girl”, 514 words.

### Round 2: Originality

**originality-auditor.** A crowded shelf (old-time radio murder) entered through an uncrowded door: a Foley sleuth and tape killing the live-broadcast alibi; the frame around her is stock. Would read on: yes. Scores: hook 8, originality 7, engine 7, character 6, setting 7, fit 8, clarity 8.

- Keep: The tape premise: 'the year being on the air stopped working as an alibi' and 'a razor blade can now forge a live broadcast'. This is the piece no radio mystery in the audit owns.; The sound-effects table as detective method, with the clue printed 'as Hazel hears it': 'two door slams and Hazel made one', 'gravel and his yard is brick'.; Cincinnati in 1947 with a soap sponsor (Dunmore Soap), which fits the city's real industry and moves clear of the Chicago 1938-39 cluster.
- Problem 1: Book 1, 'An actor dies mid-broadcast', is the exact opening of The Darkness Knows territory and Radioland Murders: a death at a live radio drama, solved by a woman on staff. Why it matters: The first book sells the series; its premise is the one this shelf has met most often, and the tape idea is only a subplot in it. Suggested fix: Open on the tape alibi instead: a suspect was 'heard live' on WQC at the hour of a death across town; Hazel hears the second door slam on the air check and knows the broadcast was a reel.
- Problem 2: Most generic element: the dead mentor whose 'fall' may be murder, plus the policeman uncle who 'wants her quiet'. Both are standard cozy-historical furniture, and the running thread rests entirely on the first. Why it matters: A four-book thread built on 'did he really fall' is predictable from the pitch; readers of this shelf will guess the answer and the type of culprit. Suggested fix: Keep Gus dead but make the question audible, not 'did he fall': the reel holds a sound effect Gus made that matches a real unsolved crime, so the thread asks what he staged, for whom.
- Problem 3: Tape, the most distinctive element, drives only books 1 and 4. 'The Wrong Bell' and 'Footsteps on Gravel' are telephone-background clues that could sit in any 1940s mystery (from memory: a familiar golden-age and radio-drama device). Why it matters: The middle books lose the signature device, so the series reads as a general ear-witness mystery with a tape bookend rather than a tape-age series. Suggested fix: Give each middle book a distinct tape or transcription trick: book 2 a ransom call assembled from a dead man's aired lines; book 3 a transcription disc dubbed to tape to move a broadcast's date.
- Problem 4: The title 'The Sound-Effects Girl' follows the saturated 'The [Occupation] Girl' pattern, and 'war widow who kept the man's job' is the standard postwar heroine (from memory: common across 1940s-set women's historical mystery). Why it matters: The cover line signals a generic shelf-mate and hides the one thing only this series has: sound as evidence and tape as forgery. Suggested fix: Title the series on the device, e.g. 'As If Live' or 'A Hazel Novak Air Check Mystery', and give Hazel a specific stake in tape: she learns to splice, so her skill makes her a suspect.
- Reminded them of: Cheryl Honigford, The Darkness Knows (radio-station murder, woman on staff; shared setting type, not device); Radioland Murders (film; deaths during a live broadcast, closest to book 1); Jean Sheldon, She Overheard Murder (postwar radio actress); Blow Out (film; sound man finds a crime in a recording, from memory); The Conversation (film; evidence hidden in tape edits, from memory); Remember WENN (TV; 1940s radio station ensemble, from memory)

Honigford, Radioland Murders and Sheldon come from the supplied audit; Blow Out, The Conversation, Remember WENN and the remarks on title pattern and telephone-background clues are from memory and need checking. The resemblance to the audit titles is shared trope and setting type, not premise plus setting plus device: none has a sound-effects sleuth or the tape alibi.
