# m08: The Night Notary

Revision log. Genre: Urban amateur-sleuth mystery. Target readers: Women and men 40–65.

## Where it started

**Seed idea** (written by the series developer before any drafting):

> Working title: Witness My Hand. Gloria Ibarra, 47, a former superior-court clerk, works nights across Los Angeles County as a mobile notary: loan signings at kitchen tables, powers of attorney at hospital bedsides, affidavits in the county jail. She cannot give legal advice and she is not police. All the law asks of her is to check identity, watch the signature, take the thumbprint and write it in her journal, and to refuse if the signer does not understand or is being pushed. That refusal is her whole power. Each book starts with a signing that is wrong: a deed signed over at 2 a.m. by a man who is dead by morning, a jailhouse recantation, a dying widow's sudden marriage license. Her journal (names, times, thumbprints) is the evidence trail. Cast ideas: a night chaplain at the county hospital; a bail-bonds clerk who owes her favors; her 19-year-old nephew, who digitizes her journals; a sheriff's fraud detective who wishes she would go home; her father, whom she looks after by day. Thread: six years ago she notarized a forged signature that cost a family their house; the signing mill behind it is still working, and each case is a step closer to who runs it. Titles from notarial wording (Witness My Hand, Sworn Before Me, Under Penalty of Perjury, Personally Appeared).
