# The Riding Crew

**Genre:** Near-future science fiction techno-thriller · **Readers:** Men 35–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete boarding per book

**Logline.** In 2041, when a crewless cargo ship goes dark or steams somewhere nobody sent her, riding master Iker Etxeberria and his crew are winched aboard to bring her in by hand and learn why she did it. He signs for her.

**The setting.** Nine thousand cargo ships cross the oceans with nobody aboard, steered by signed satellite orders from control rooms in Singapore, Rotterdam and Houston. They are built without a bridge, bunks or handrails, with one manual control: a hand pump at the steering gear. One rule survives: a ship that stops answering her controllers may not come within twelve miles of any coast until a licensed human master is aboard and answerable for her.

**The lead.** Iker Etxeberria, 46, Basque, is a riding master. He was the Halcyon Dawn's last captain and signed the certificate that let her owners cut out her bridge. He wants every ship brought in afloat; each signature makes him liable for hull, cargo and coastline. He reads a ship by sound and talks in short sentences.

**The cast.** Mirren Tait, 31, engineer, once steered these ships from the Rotterdam room and still defends it. Adaeze Nwosu, 55, a Lagos harbor pilot until remote docking ended the trade, wants the machines seen failing. Teo Baptista, 22, deckhand, trusts the machines Iker does not.

**The series engine.** Every book is one ship and one question: why a vessel with no crew did what she did. The crew boards with seventy-two hours of supplies and a deadline set by weather, fuel or a coastline, and answers it with seamanship and physics. With nine thousand crewless hulls at sea, one goes silent every week, and few masters will sign for a stranger.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Slow Circles* — An LNG carrier steams slow circles off Namibia, burning her own cargo as fuel, ordered there under a dead ship's number: the Halcyon Dawn's. Iker brings her in; her charterer claims against his license.
2. *Cold Boxes* — A container ship lies dark off Madagascar, a cyclone two days out; the man living in one of her 600 cold boxes cut her power. He once rewired the Halcyon Dawn's transponder, and Iker lands him alive.
3. *Fifteen Knots* — A tanker of twice-sold oil runs for the Strait of Malacca, two control rooms fighting over her rudder, two navies waiting on Iker's signature. The crew steers by hand pump; Mirren traces the forged orders to Rotterdam.
4. *Halcyon Dawn* — Iker's old ship drifts laden toward Tasmania, three days out, every landfall signed on a copy of his license. Bringing her in means signing for all of them. He signs, and names the forger.

*Running thread:* The Halcyon Dawn left Brazil crewless two years ago and never arrived; someone is sailing her on Iker's license.

**Why readers come back.** Each book is a complete job: a strange ship, a hard boarding, a problem solved by people who know their trade. Book four settles the Halcyon Dawn and leaves more ships to board.

**What makes it different.** No boarding parties, no firefights: the crew's fight is with a 300-meter machine, and the only weapon is a signature. Nobody carries a gun.
