# The Riding Crew

**Genre:** Near-future science fiction techno-thriller · **Readers:** Men 35–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete boarding per book

**Logline.** In 2041, when a crewless cargo ship goes dark or steams somewhere nobody sent her, riding master Iker Etxeberria and his crew are winched aboard to bring her in by hand and learn why she did it. He signs for her.

**The setting.** Nine thousand cargo ships cross the oceans with nobody aboard, steered by satellite from control rooms in Singapore, Rotterdam and Houston. They are built without a bridge, bunks or handrails, with one manual control: a hand pump at the steering gear. Each obeys whoever sends her number. A ship that stops answering her controllers may not come within twelve miles of any coast until a licensed human master is aboard and answerable for her.

**The lead.** Iker Etxeberria, 46, Basque, was the Halcyon Dawn's last captain and signed the certificate that let her owners cut out her bridge. He wants every ship brought in afloat; each signature pledges his house in Bermeo against hull, cargo and coastline. He reads a ship by sound, says little, cooks for his crew, and signs alone.

**The cast.** Mirren Tait, 31, engineer, took the Halcyon Dawn's helm from Rotterdam the day Iker gave it up, and logged nothing when her scrapped number stayed live. Adaeze Nwosu, 55, a Lagos harbor pilot until remote docking ended the trade, wants the machines seen failing. Teo Baptista, 22, deckhand, learned the sea on simulators and wants a master's license.

**The series engine.** Every book is one ship and one question: why a vessel with no crew did what she did. The crew boards with seventy-two hours of supplies and a deadline set by weather, fuel or a coastline, and answers it with satellite links, ballast and towlines, explained plainly. Each week one more goes silent.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Slow Circles* — An LNG carrier steams slow circles off Namibia, burning her own cargo as fuel. Iker hears healthy engines; Mirren finds the order, sent under the Halcyon Dawn's dead number. She comes in whole.
2. *Cold Boxes* — A container ship lies dark off Madagascar, a cyclone two days out. Teo finds a man living in one of her 600 cold boxes: the Halcyon Dawn's electrician, minding cargo no manifest lists. Iker lands him alive.
3. *Fifteen Knots* — A tanker of twice-sold oil runs for the Strait of Malacca, two control rooms fighting over her rudder, two navies waiting. Adaeze steers by hand pump; Mirren traces the forged orders to her old Rotterdam watch chief and names him.
4. *Halcyon Dawn* — With the watch chief jailed, Iker's old ship drifts unsteered toward Tasmania, three days out. Iker lets his crew countersign, lands her, and meets the forger: his old chief mate, feeding the eleven crew Iker signed ashore.

*Running thread:* Sold for scrap two years ago, the Halcyon Dawn never reached the breakers; someone trades her under borrowed names and Iker's signature.

**Why readers come back.** Each book is a complete job: a strange ship, a hard boarding, a problem solved by people who know their trade. Book four settles the Halcyon Dawn; more ships wait.

**What makes it different.** No firefights: the crew's fight is with a 300-meter machine, and the only weapon is a signature. Nobody carries a gun.
