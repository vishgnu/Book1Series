# Master Aboard

**Genre:** Near-future science fiction techno-thriller · **Readers:** Men 35–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete boarding per book

**Logline.** In 2041, when a crewless cargo ship goes dark, riding master Iker "Etch" Etxeberria and his crew are winched aboard to bring her in and learn why. He signs for her.

**The setting.** Nine thousand cargo ships cross the oceans crewless, steered by satellite, built without bridge, bunks or handrails; the one manual control is a hand pump at the steering gear. Each obeys orders sent under her registry number, which should die when she is scrapped. A ship that stops answering may not come within twelve miles of a coast until a licensed human master is aboard and answerable.

**The lead.** Iker Etxeberria, 46, Basque, was the Halcyon Dawn's last captain and signed the certificate that put her eleven crew ashore. He wants every ship brought in afloat; each signature pledges his house in Bermeo against hull, cargo and coastline. He reads a ship by sound, is sometimes wrong, and signs alone.

**The cast.** Mirren Tait, 31, engineer, took the Halcyon Dawn's helm from Rotterdam the day Etch gave it up, and logged nothing when her scrapped number stayed live. Adaeze Nwosu, 55, a Lagos harbor pilot until remote docking ended the trade, wants the machines seen failing. Teo Baptista, 22, deckhand, learned the sea on simulators.

**The series engine.** Every book is one ship and one question: why a crewless vessel did what she did. The crew boards with seventy-two hours of supplies and a deadline set by weather, fuel or a coastline. Salt and cheap maintenance silence another ship each week; a few are sent astray on purpose.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Dead Slow* — An LNG carrier circles off Namibia, burning her cargo on orders under the Halcyon Dawn's dead number; Etch lands her whole, and the charterer claims his house.
2. *Cold Cargo* — A container ship lies dark off Madagascar ahead of a cyclone; Teo finds the Halcyon Dawn's electrician living in a cold box, and Etch lands him alive.
3. *Two Helms* — Two control rooms and two navies contest a tanker bound for the Malacca Strait; Adaeze steers by hand pump, and Mirren traces the forged orders to her Rotterdam watch chief.
4. *The Halcyon Dawn* — Etch's old ship drifts toward Tasmania; he lets his crew countersign, lands her, and finds his chief mate running her to pay the eleven sailors his certificate put ashore.

*Running thread:* Sold for scrap two years ago, the Halcyon Dawn never reached the breakers; someone trades her under Etch's signature.

**Why readers come back.** Each boarding is complete, solved by people who know their trade and explained plainly. Each ends on one new fact about the Halcyon Dawn: her dead number still sends orders; her crew never left the sea; the jailed man took orders. Book four settles her and makes Etch sign a report naming a friend; more ships wait.

**What makes it different.** No firefights, no navy heroes, no secret agency: four licensed civilians against a 300-meter machine; the only weapon is a signature, and being wrong costs a house.
