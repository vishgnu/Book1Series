# The Riding Crew

**Genre:** Near-future science fiction techno-thriller · **Readers:** Men 35–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete boarding per book

**Logline.** In 2041, when a crewless cargo ship goes dark, catches fire or steams somewhere nobody sent her, riding master Iker Etxeberria and his crew are winched aboard to bring her in by hand and learn why she did it.

**The setting.** Nine thousand cargo ships cross the oceans with nobody aboard, steered from control rooms in Singapore, Rotterdam and Houston. They are built without a bridge, bunks or handrails: hull, engine, sensor mast. One rule survives: a ship that stops answering her controllers may not come within twelve miles of any coast until a licensed human master is aboard and answerable for her.

**The lead.** Iker Etxeberria, 46, is a riding master from a Basque fishing family, holding one of the last unlimited master's licenses. He wants every ship he signs for brought in afloat; each signature makes him liable for hull, cargo and coastline. He reads a ship by sound and motion and talks in short sentences, shortest with fools.

**The cast.** Mirren Tait, 31, engineer, makes locked control systems take orders from a laptop and a crowbar. Adaeze Nwosu, 55, former Lagos harbor pilot, docks anything that floats. Teo Baptista, 22, deckhand, has never sailed on a crewed ship. Each job adds an owner's observer watching the money.

**The series engine.** Every book is one ship and one question: why a vessel with no crew did what she did. The crew boards with seventy-two hours of supplies and a deadline set by weather, fuel or a coastline, and answers it with seamanship and physics. With nine thousand crewless hulls at sea, one goes silent every week.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Slow Circles* — An ore carrier steaming slow circles in the South Atlantic is holding for a rendezvous ordered under a scrapped ship's name.
2. *Cold Boxes* — A container ship lies dark off Madagascar, 600 cold boxes alarming, a cyclone two days out; someone living aboard cut the power.
3. *Fifteen Knots* — A ransomware-locked tanker runs for the Strait of Malacca while two navies dispute her; the crew steers her by hand.
4. *Halcyon Dawn* — The crew boards the missing carrier in the Southern Ocean and finds who has traded her under dead ships' names.

*Running thread:* The Halcyon Dawn, a 300-meter ore carrier, left Brazil crewless two years ago and never arrived; book four answers where she went and who commands her.

**Why readers come back.** Each book is a complete job: a strange ship, a hard boarding, a problem solved by people who know their trade. Ballast, towlines and control systems are explained plainly. The same crew returns, and book four settles the Halcyon Dawn while leaving more ships to board.

**What makes it different.** No hijackers, no crew to rescue: the adversary is a 300-meter machine doing something nobody ordered. The heroes are working sailors with a winch, a laptop and a license. Nobody carries a gun.
