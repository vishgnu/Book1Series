# The Riding Crew

**Genre:** Near-future science fiction techno-thriller · **Readers:** Men 35–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete boarding per book

**Logline.** In 2041, when a crewless cargo ship goes dark, catches fire or steams somewhere nobody sent her, riding master Iker Etxeberria and his crew are winched aboard to bring her in by hand and learn why she did it. He signs for her.

**The setting.** Nine thousand cargo ships cross the oceans with nobody aboard, steered by signed satellite orders from control rooms in Singapore, Rotterdam and Houston. They are built without a bridge, bunks or handrails: hull, engine, sensor mast. Class rules leave one manual control: a hand pump at the steering gear. One rule survives: a ship that stops answering her controllers may not come within twelve miles of any coast until a licensed human master is aboard and answerable for her.

**The lead.** Iker Etxeberria, 46, Basque, is a riding master: the licensed captain put aboard a ship that has none. He was the Halcyon Dawn's last human captain and signed the certificate that let her owners cut out her bridge. He wants every ship he signs for brought in afloat; each signature makes him liable for hull, cargo and coastline. He reads a ship by sound and talks in short sentences.

**The cast.** Mirren Tait, 31, engineer, once steered these ships from the Rotterdam room and knows what controllers leave out of the log. Adaeze Nwosu, 55, was a Lagos harbor pilot until remote docking ended the trade. Teo Baptista, 22, deckhand, has never sailed on a crewed ship.

**The series engine.** Every book is one ship and one question: why a vessel with no crew did what she did. The crew boards with seventy-two hours of supplies and a deadline set by weather, fuel or a coastline, and answers it with seamanship and physics. With nine thousand crewless hulls at sea, one goes silent every week.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Slow Circles* — An LNG carrier steams slow circles in the South Atlantic, burning her own cargo as fuel, holding for a rendezvous ordered under a dead ship's number.
2. *Cold Boxes* — A container ship lies dark off Madagascar, a cyclone two days out; a man living in one of her 600 cold boxes cut the power.
3. *Fifteen Knots* — A tanker whose oil was sold twice runs for the Strait of Malacca, two control rooms countermanding each other; two navies wait on which flag Iker signs for.
4. *Halcyon Dawn* — The crew boards Iker's old ship in the Southern Ocean and finds her landfalls signed for on a copy of his license.

*Running thread:* The Halcyon Dawn, a 300-meter ore carrier, left Brazil crewless two years ago and never arrived; book four answers where she went and who signs for her.

**Why readers come back.** Each book is a complete job: a strange ship, a hard boarding, a problem solved by people who know their trade. Satellite links, ballast and towlines are explained plainly. Book four settles the Halcyon Dawn and leaves more ships to board.

**What makes it different.** No boarding parties, no firefights: the crew's fight is with a 300-meter machine, and the only weapon is a signature. Whoever signs for a ship answers for everything she does. Nobody carries a gun.
