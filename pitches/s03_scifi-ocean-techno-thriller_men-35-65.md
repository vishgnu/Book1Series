# The Riding Crew

**Genre:** Near-future science fiction techno-thriller · **Readers:** Men 35–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete boarding per book

**Logline.** In 2041, when a crewless cargo ship goes dark or steams somewhere nobody sent her, riding master Iker Etxeberria and his crew are winched aboard to bring her in by hand and learn why she did it. He signs for her.

**The setting.** Nine thousand cargo ships cross the oceans with nobody aboard, steered by satellite from control rooms in Singapore, Rotterdam and Houston. They are built without a bridge, bunks or handrails, with one manual control: a hand pump at the steering gear. A ship that stops answering her controllers may not come within twelve miles of any coast until a licensed human master is aboard and answerable for her.

**The lead.** Iker Etxeberria, 46, Basque, was the Halcyon Dawn's last captain and signed the certificate that let her owners cut out her bridge. He wants every ship brought in afloat; each signature pledges his house in Bermeo against hull, cargo and coastline. He reads a ship by sound, talks in short sentences, and signs alone.

**The cast.** Mirren Tait, 31, engineer, took the Halcyon Dawn's helm from Rotterdam the day Iker gave it up, and knows what controllers leave out of the log. Adaeze Nwosu, 55, a Lagos harbor pilot until remote docking ended the trade, wants the machines seen failing. Teo Baptista, 22, deckhand, learned the sea on simulators and wants a master's license.

**The series engine.** Every book is one ship and one question: why a vessel with no crew did what she did. The crew boards with seventy-two hours of supplies and a deadline set by weather, fuel or a coastline, and answers it with satellite links, ballast and towlines, explained plainly. Of nine thousand crewless hulls, one goes silent every week.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Slow Circles* — An LNG carrier steams slow circles off Namibia, burning her own cargo as fuel. Iker blames her engines; Teo finds the order, sent under the Halcyon Dawn's dead number. She comes in; her charterer claims Iker's house.
2. *Cold Boxes* — A container ship lies dark off Madagascar, a cyclone two days out. Teo finds a man living in one of her 600 cold boxes: the Halcyon Dawn's electrician, put ashore by Iker's certificate. Iker lands him alive.
3. *Fifteen Knots* — A tanker of twice-sold oil runs for the Strait of Malacca, two control rooms fighting over her rudder, two navies waiting. Adaeze steers by hand pump; Mirren traces the forged orders to her old Rotterdam watch chief and names him.
4. *Halcyon Dawn* — Iker's old ship drifts laden toward Tasmania, three days out, every landfall signed on a copy of his license. Iker lets his crew countersign, and meets the forger.

*Running thread:* The Halcyon Dawn left Brazil crewless two years ago and never arrived; one of her last crew sails her on Iker's license.

**Why readers come back.** Each book is a complete job: a strange ship, a hard boarding, a problem solved by people who know their trade. Iker gave a ship away; Mirren took her. Book four settles the Halcyon Dawn and leaves more ships to board.

**What makes it different.** No firefights: the crew's fight is with a 300-meter machine, and the only weapon is a signature. Nobody carries a gun.
