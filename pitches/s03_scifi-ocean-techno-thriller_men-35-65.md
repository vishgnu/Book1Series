# The Riding Crew

**Genre:** Near-future science fiction techno-thriller · **Readers:** Men 35–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete boarding per book

**Logline.** In 2041, when a crewless cargo ship goes dark, catches fire or steams somewhere nobody sent her, a licensed master and his crew are winched aboard to bring her in by hand and learn why she did it. Iker Etxeberria signs for her.

**The setting.** Nine thousand cargo ships cross the oceans with nobody aboard, steered from control rooms in Singapore, Rotterdam and Houston. They are built without a bridge, bunks or handrails: hull, engine, sensor mast. Class rules leave one manual control: a hand pump at the steering gear. One rule survives: a ship that stops answering her controllers may not come within twelve miles of any coast until a licensed human master is aboard and answerable for her.

**The lead.** Iker Etxeberria, 46, is a riding master: the licensed captain put aboard a ship that has none. From a Basque fishing family, he was the Halcyon Dawn's last human captain before her owners cut out her bridge. He wants every ship he signs for brought in afloat; each signature makes him liable for hull, cargo and coastline. He reads a ship by sound and motion and talks in short sentences, shortest with fools.

**The cast.** Mirren Tait, 31, engineer, makes locked control systems take orders from a laptop and a crowbar. Adaeze Nwosu, 55, former Lagos harbor pilot, docks anything that floats. Teo Baptista, 22, deckhand, has never sailed on a crewed ship and trusts the machines Iker does not.

**The series engine.** Every book is one ship and one question: why a vessel with no crew did what she did. The crew boards with seventy-two hours of supplies and a deadline set by weather, fuel or a coastline, and answers it with seamanship and physics. With nine thousand crewless hulls at sea, one goes silent every week.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Slow Circles* — A car carrier steaming slow circles in the South Atlantic, five days of fuel left, is holding for a rendezvous ordered under a scrapped ship's name.
2. *Cold Boxes* — A container ship lies dark off Madagascar, 600 cold boxes alarming, a cyclone two days out; someone living aboard, in a box booked to a scrapped ship, cut the power.
3. *Fifteen Knots* — A ransomware-locked tanker runs for the Strait of Malacca while two navies dispute her; the crew steers her by hand, and the ransom is payable to a scrapped ship.
4. *Halcyon Dawn* — The crew boards Iker's old ship in the Southern Ocean and finds the broker trading her under dead ships' names.

*Running thread:* The Halcyon Dawn, a 300-meter ore carrier, left Brazil crewless two years ago and never arrived; book four answers where she went and who commands her.

**Why readers come back.** Each book is a complete job: a strange ship, a hard boarding, a problem solved by people who know their trade. Ballast, towlines and control systems are explained plainly. Book four settles the Halcyon Dawn and leaves more ships to board.

**What makes it different.** No boarding parties, no firefights: the people behind it are ashore, and the crew's fight is with a 300-meter machine. The heroes are working sailors with a winch, a laptop and a license. Nobody carries a gun.
