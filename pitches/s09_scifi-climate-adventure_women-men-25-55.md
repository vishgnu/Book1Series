# Aurochs Country

**Genre:** Climate science fiction adventure · **Readers:** Women and men 25–55 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete mission per book

**Logline.** In 2084, ranger-veterinarian Hanka Sommer guards the Bruch, nine thousand square kilometers of rewilded fen where nobody may live. Each season brings one species and one case, and every dry gauge reading she signs hands land back to families like her mother's.

**The setting.** Between Elbe and Oder, aurochs, wisent, wild horses, moose and wolves move through drowned villages and birch-grown autobahn. Winter floods run off; hotter summers pull the water table down. Forty rangers on barges read gauges in church towers. The Charter: nobody lives here, nothing leaves, and rangers handle an animal only to undo human harm. Where ground stays dry a meter down through a whole flood season, it leaves the Charter and old owners may return.

**The lead.** Hanka Sommer, 34, reads a limping wisent at four hundred meters. She was nine when her father drowned sandbagging Zerbow's dike; each autumn she barges her mother to his grave. She wants the Bruch kept wild and still signs every dry reading that shrinks it. "Walls drown people. Water only rises."

**The cast.** Renate Sommer, 66, her mother, speaks for the Zerbow returners. Tomasz Wrona, 57, her trainer, would bury a finding to protect the Bruch. Ines Brandt, 41, hydrologist, wants sluices to hold winter floods in. Deniz Aydin, 23, first-year ranger, heir to the calving ground.

**The series engine.** One season, one species, one case per book. The falling water table pushes herds onto released ground, where breeders pay a year's wage for a wild-born Bruch calf. Every finding goes to the Elbe-Oder Assembly, where returners face downstream cities that need the fen as flood storage.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Winter Marsh* — A wisent herd quits its range, two cows cut by survey cable; Hanka frees them, tracks the cable to Ines Brandt, and still countersigns her survey: careless cable, true gauges. The aurochs calving ground leaves the Charter.
2. *Calving Ground* — A breeder claims nine calves as born on released ground. Hanka and Deniz match calf to dam by blood and milk and barge them home inside ten days, before the bonds fail. Tomasz leaves one track out of the report; she lets him.
3. *Fire Season* — Renate's settlers' campfire catches dried peat in wild-horse country; Hanka drives the herd uphill, then breaches an old dike without orders. The flood saves herd and peat, proves Ines right about held water, and costs Hanka a suspension.
4. *The Unbred* — An unchipped brown bear trails wintering moose through the survey camps. Hanka moves the camps, not the bear; snared hair and bar-worn teeth prove a pen-raised animal Tomasz released to scare surveyors. She names him, and her own silence.

*Running thread:* Whether the Charter survives the year-end vote; book 4 renews it for ten years, with Ines's sluices built, Zerbow's church hill returned to Renate and Tomasz's badge gone; Hanka, demoted, patrols the line below.

**Why readers come back.** A full case solved in every book, no cliffhangers. A mother and daughter on opposite sides of one grave plot. A recovering land kept by competent people.

**What makes it different.** The conflict comes from recovery, not collapse: people want back in. The law follows the water, so the strongest witness against the ranger's cause is her own honest report.
