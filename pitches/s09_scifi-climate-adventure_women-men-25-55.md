# Aurochs Country

**Genre:** Climate science fiction adventure · **Readers:** Women and men 25–55 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete mission per book

**Logline.** In 2084, ranger-veterinarian Hanka Sommer guards the Bruch, a rewilded fen between the Elbe and the Oder where nobody may live. Each season brings one species and one case, while the water falls and her family's drowned village rises inside the land she protects.

**The setting.** After the floods, nine thousand square kilometers of emptied countryside went back to water and grass. Back-bred aurochs, wisent, elk, wild horses and wolves move through drowned villages and birch-grown autobahn. Forty rangers watch from river barges, with sensor mycelium in the peat and drones that flock like starlings. The Charter's rule: nobody lives here, nothing leaves, and rangers handle an animal only to undo human harm.

**The lead.** Hanka Sommer, 34, ranger-veterinarian at Station Erlenhof, wants the Bruch kept wild. Her mother calls it the theft of Zerbow, the family village under reeds, and Hanka's own field reports keep proving the returners partly right. She files them anyway. She can read a limping wisent at four hundred meters and talks in short orders and field notes.

**The cast.** Renate Sommer, 66, her mother, speaker for the Zerbow returners. Tomasz Wrona, 57, a tracker who trusts his feet over any sensor and would leave a finding out to protect the Bruch. Deniz Aydin, 23, first-year ranger from Hamburg, better with drones than mud. Margit Kolbe, 61, station cook, who feeds settlers at the back door.

**The series engine.** Every book is one season, one species and one case, worked on foot, by barge and in bad weather: tracking, field medicine, animals that behave like animals. Missions keep arriving because the drying fen pushes herds toward 400 kilometers of unfenced edge, where horns and land are worth money, and the Charter turns every ranger report into evidence.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Winter Marsh* — Hanka follows a wisent trail through frozen marsh to bring missing hydrologist Ines Brandt out alive; Brandt's survey proves the water is falling, and Hanka countersigns it.
2. *Calving Ground* — Low water pushes the spring aurochs calving to the edge, within reach of horn poachers; the rangers take crew and buyer without a shot near the herd; Deniz earns his place.
3. *Fire Season* — Renate's settlers camp in wild-horse country and their fire catches dried peat; Hanka breaches an old dike to drown it, and her report wins them a hearing.
4. *The Unbred* — An unchipped brown bear shadows the autumn elk migration along the last deep channels; Hanka traces who released it, and the trail leads toward her own station.

*Running thread:* Whether the Charter survives the year-end vote as the water falls and Zerbow's roofs surface; book 4 decides both the vote and Zerbow, at a price to Hanka.

**Why readers come back.** A full case solved in every book, no cliffhangers. A crew worth a year and a station kitchen to come home to. Mud, ice, smoke and river light. A future where the land is recovering and competent people keep it that way.

**What makes it different.** The conflict comes from recovery, not collapse: the land is healing and people want back in. Cases turn on veterinary evidence, tracks and water levels, not guns. The strongest witness against the ranger's cause is her own honest report.
