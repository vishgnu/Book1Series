# Aurochs Country

**Genre:** Climate science fiction adventure · **Readers:** Women and men 25–55 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete mission per book

**Logline.** In 2084, wherever the rewilded Bruch fen dries, the land goes back to its old families. Ranger-veterinarian Hanka Sommer solves one wildlife case a season and signs every dry reading herself, even for her mother's drowned village.

**The setting.** Between Elbe and Oder, aurochs (the wild ox Europe bred back), wisent, wild horses and wolves roam drowned villages and birch-grown autobahn. Rangers on barges fly drones and read gauges in church towers. The Charter: nobody lives here, nothing leaves, and rangers touch animals only to undo human harm. Ground that stays dry through a flood season is released from the Charter, and its old families may move back.

**The lead.** Hanka Sommer, 34, reads a limping wisent at 400 meters. She was nine when her father drowned sandbagging Zerbow's dike; each autumn she barges her mother to his grave. She wants the Bruch wild and signs the readings that shrink it. "Four hundred twelve sandbags. He chalked it on the pump house."

**The cast.** Renate Sommer, 66, her mother, calls the Charter "the theft of Zerbow." Tomasz Wrona, 57, her trainer, keeps his real counts in a notebook nobody sees. Ines Brandt, 41, hydrologist, wants flood sluices and loses her own gear. Deniz Aydin, 23, first-year ranger, wants the badge; his grandparents fled to Hamburg with the calving ground's deed.

**The series engine.** One season, one species, one case. As summers dry the fen, herds wander onto released ground, where breeders pay a year's wage for a wild-born calf. Every finding goes to the Elbe-Oder Assembly, where returner families and downstream cities needing its flood storage split the seats almost evenly.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Wisent Winter* — Hanka treats two wisent snared in survey cable, traces it to Ines's crew, and still signs their honest gauges; the calving ground is released.
2. *Aurochs Spring* — A breeder claims nine calves; Hanka and Deniz prove them wild-born by blood and milk, and she lets Tomasz leave one track out.
3. *Wild Horse Summer* — A campfire lights dry peat; Hanka breaches a dike without orders to save the horses, counts the drowned nests, and names her mother as the camper.
4. *Bear Autumn* — Hanka moves the camps, not the bear trailing the surveyors; snared hair and bar-worn teeth prove Tomasz released it pen-raised to scare them off.

*Running thread:* Whether the Charter survives the year-end vote. Book 4 renews it for ten years: Ines's sluices built, Zerbow's church hill returned to Renate, Tomasz's badge gone, Hanka demoted for her silence.

**Why readers come back.** Every case closes, and each hands the next book a burden, such as the track Tomasz left out. A mother and daughter on opposite sides of one grave plot. Hurt animals are treated and recover; no named animal dies.

**What makes it different.** A ranger mystery in a rewilded future. Conflict comes from recovery, not collapse: people want back in. The law follows the water, so the strongest witness against Hanka's cause is her own honest report.
