# Aurochs Country

**Genre:** Climate science fiction adventure · **Readers:** Women and men 25–55 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete mission per book

**Logline.** In 2084, ranger-veterinarian Hanka Sommer patrols the Bruch, nine thousand square kilometers of rewilded fen between the Elbe and the Oder. Each season brings one species and one crime or rescue, and each case becomes evidence in the vote on its future.

**The setting.** After the floods, the emptied countryside went back to water and grass. Back-bred aurochs, wisent, elk, wild horses and wolves move through drowned villages and birch-grown autobahn. Forty rangers watch from river barges, with sensor mycelium in the peat and drones that flock like starlings. The Charter's rule: nobody lives here, nothing leaves, and rangers handle an animal only to undo human harm.

**The lead.** Hanka Sommer, 34, ranger-veterinarian at Station Erlenhof, wants the Bruch kept wild. That costs her peace at her mother's table, where the Bruch is still called the theft of Zerbow, the family village under reeds. She can read a limping wisent at four hundred meters and talks in short orders and field notes.

**The cast.** Tomasz Wrona, 57, a tracker who trusts his feet over any sensor. Deniz Aydin, 23, first-year ranger from Hamburg, better with drones than mud. Margit Kolbe, 61, station cook, keeper of the rosters and the last word. Ines Brandt, 41, the hydrologist whose numbers feed the vote.

**The series engine.** Every book is one season, one species and one case, worked on foot, by barge and in bad weather: tracking, field medicine, animals that behave like animals. Missions keep arriving because the Bruch has 400 kilometers of unfenced edge, horns and land worth money, and a Charter that turns every ranger report into evidence.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Winter Marsh* — Hanka follows a wisent trail through frozen marsh to bring missing hydrologist Ines Brandt out alive; Brandt's survey proves the water is falling.
2. *Calving Ground* — Horn poachers work the spring aurochs calving; the rangers take crew and buyer without a shot near the herd; Deniz earns his place.
3. *Fire Season* — Settler families light a peat fire in wild-horse country; Hanka breaches an old dike to drown it, and the settlers win a hearing.
4. *The Unbred* — An unchipped brown bear shadows the autumn elk migration; Hanka traces who released it, and her finding goes before the Charter vote.

*Running thread:* Whether the Charter survives the year-end vote as the water falls and Zerbow's roofs surface; book 4 answers with a ten-year renewal and new terms.

**Why readers come back.** A full case solved in every book, no cliffhangers. A crew worth a year and a station kitchen to come home to. Mud, ice, smoke and river light. A future where the land is recovering and competent people keep it that way.

**What makes it different.** The conflict comes from recovery, not collapse: the land is healing and people want back in. Cases turn on veterinary evidence, tracks and water levels, not guns. The lead's lost village is rising inside the land she protects.
