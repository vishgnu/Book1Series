# Aurochs Country

**Genre:** Climate science fiction adventure · **Readers:** Women and men 25–55 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete mission per book

**Logline.** In 2084, ranger-veterinarian Hanka Sommer guards the Bruch, nine thousand square kilometers of rewilded fen where nobody may live. Each season brings one species and one case, and every honest report hands back more of her family's drowned village.

**The setting.** Between Elbe and Oder, aurochs, wisent, wild horses, moose and wolves move through drowned villages and birch-grown autobahn. Winter floods still come; hotter summers drop the water table. Forty rangers on barges read gauges in church towers. The Charter: nobody lives here, nothing leaves, and rangers handle an animal only to undo human harm. Ground gauged a meter down through a full flood season leaves the Charter and old land titles revive; fen-ringed islands stay in.

**The lead.** Hanka Sommer, 34, reads a limping wisent at four hundred meters. She was nine when her father drowned sandbagging Zerbow's dike. Her mother calls the Bruch the theft of Zerbow, and every low reading Hanka signs moves the Charter line inward. She signs anyway. "Walls drown people. Water only rises."

**The cast.** Renate Sommer, 66, her mother, speaks for the Zerbow returners. Tomasz Wrona, 57, her trainer, would bury a finding to protect the Bruch. Ines Brandt, 41, hydrologist, wants sluices to hold winter floods in. Deniz Aydin, 23, first-year ranger; his Hamburg family holds title to the calving ground.

**The series engine.** One season, one species, one case per book: tracking and field medicine. The falling water table pushes herds onto released ground, where a certified Bruch-born calf fetches a year's wage. Every finding goes to the Elbe-Oder Assembly, where returners face downstream cities that need the fen as flood storage.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Winter Marsh* — A wisent herd quits its range, two cows cut by survey cable; Hanka tracks it to Ines Brandt, frees the cows, and countersigns the survey that releases the aurochs calving ground.
2. *Calving Ground* — A breeder takes nine calves as born on released ground; Hanka and Deniz match calf to dam by blood and milk, prove them Charter-born and barge them home before the bonds fail. Tomasz logs one track short; she lets him.
3. *Fire Season* — Renate's settlers' campfire catches dried peat in wild-horse country; Hanka drives the herd uphill, then breaches an old dike without orders. Her first testimony counts the drowned nests, proves Ines's numbers, wins them a hearing and costs her a suspension.
4. *The Unbred* — An unchipped brown bear trails wintering moose through the survey camps. Hanka moves the camps, not the bear; snared hair and bar-worn teeth on camera prove a pen-raised animal Tomasz released to scare surveyors. She names him, and her own silence.

*Running thread:* Whether the Charter survives the year-end vote; book 4 renews it for ten years, with Ines's sluices built, Zerbow's church hill returned to Renate and Tomasz's badge gone; Hanka, demoted, patrols the line below.

**Why readers come back.** A full case solved in every book, no cliffhangers. A mother and daughter on opposite sides of one grave plot. A recovering land kept by competent people.

**What makes it different.** The conflict comes from recovery, not collapse: people want back in. The law follows the water, so the strongest witness against the ranger's cause is her own honest report.
