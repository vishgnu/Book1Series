# Aurochs Country

**Genre:** Climate science fiction adventure · **Readers:** Women and men 25–55 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete mission per book

**Logline.** In 2084, wherever the rewilded Bruch fen dries, the law returns the land to its old families. Ranger-veterinarian Hanka Sommer solves one wildlife case a season and signs every dry reading herself, even for her mother's drowned village.

**The setting.** Between Elbe and Oder, aurochs (the wild ox Europe bred back), wisent, wild horses and wolves roam drowned villages and birch-grown autobahn. Rangers on barges fly drones, scan ear chips and read gauges in church towers. The Charter: nobody lives here, and rangers touch animals only to undo human harm. Ground dry through a flood season leaves the Charter, and families with pre-flood title may return.

**The lead.** Hanka Sommer, 34, reads a limping wisent at 400 meters. She was nine when her father drowned sandbagging Zerbow's dike; each autumn she barges her mother to his grave. She wants the Bruch wild and signs the readings that shrink it. "Walls drown people. Water only rises."

**The cast.** Renate Sommer, 66, her mother, calls the Charter "the theft of Zerbow." Tomasz Wrona, 57, her trainer, would bury a finding to protect the Bruch. Ines Brandt, 41, hydrologist, wants sluices to hold winter floods. Deniz Aydin, 23, first-year ranger; his Hamburg family holds title to the calving ground.

**The series engine.** One season, one species, one case. As summers dry the fen, herds wander onto released ground, where breeders pay a year's wage for a wild-born calf. Every finding goes to the Elbe-Oder Assembly, where returner families and downstream cities needing its flood storage split the seats almost evenly.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Wisent Winter* — Hanka frees two wisent cut by survey cable and still signs the surveyor's honest gauges; the aurochs calving ground is released and returners gain votes.
2. *Aurochs Spring* — A breeder claims nine calves; Hanka and Deniz prove them Charter-born by blood and milk, and she lets Tomasz leave one track out.
3. *Wild Horse Summer* — Renate's campfire lights dry peat; Hanka breaches a dike without orders, saving horses and peat, drowning nests she counts, proving Ines's sluices and earning a suspension.
4. *Bear Autumn* — Hanka moves the camps, not the bear trailing the surveyors; snared hair and bar-worn teeth prove Tomasz released it pen-raised, and she names him and her own silence.

*Running thread:* Will the Charter survive the year-end vote? Book 4 renews it for ten years: Ines's sluices built, Zerbow's church hill returned to Renate, Tomasz's badge gone, Hanka demoted.

**Why readers come back.** Every case closes, and each hands the next book a burden: breeders' trucks at the calving ground, Tomasz's missing track, a suspension. A mother and daughter on opposite sides of one grave plot. A recovering land kept by competent people.

**What makes it different.** A ranger mystery in a rewilded future; every book is subtitled "A Charter Ranger Novel." Conflict comes from recovery, not collapse: people want back in. The law follows the water, so the strongest witness against Hanka's cause is her own honest report.
