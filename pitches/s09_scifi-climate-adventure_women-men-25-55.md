# Aurochs Country

**Genre:** Climate science fiction adventure · **Readers:** Women and men 25–55 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete mission per book

**Logline.** In 2084, ranger-veterinarian Hanka Sommer guards the Bruch, nine thousand square kilometers of rewilded fen where nobody may live. Each season brings one species and one case, and every honest report she signs hands ground to the returners.

**The setting.** After the floods, the emptied land between Elbe and Oder went back to water and grass. Back-bred aurochs, wisent, elk, wild horses and wolves move through drowned villages and birch-grown autobahn. Forty rangers watch from river barges, with sensor mycelium in the peat and gauges in drowned church towers. The Charter's rule, valid only where the gauges read wet: nobody lives here, nothing leaves, and rangers handle an animal only to undo human harm.

**The lead.** Hanka Sommer, 34, of Station Erlenhof, wants the Bruch kept wild. Her mother calls it the theft of Zerbow, the family village under reeds, and every low reading Hanka signs moves the Charter line inward. She signs anyway. She can read a limping wisent at four hundred meters.

**The cast.** Renate Sommer, 66, her mother, speaker for the Zerbow returners. Tomasz Wrona, 57, the tracker who trained Hanka and would leave a finding out to protect the Bruch. Ines Brandt, 41, hydrologist, whose numbers nobody likes. Deniz Aydin, 23, first-year ranger whose flood-displaced Hamburg family is on the land waiting list.

**The series engine.** One season, one species, one case per book: tracking and field medicine. Missions keep arriving because the drying fen pushes herds onto ground the Charter no longer covers, and every case report goes to the Elbe-Oder Assembly, where returner families face downstream cities that need the fen as flood storage.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Winter Marsh* — Hanka follows a wisent trail through frozen marsh to bring hydrologist Ines Brandt out alive; countersigning Brandt's survey moves the Charter line off the aurochs calving ground.
2. *Calving Ground* — A licensed breeder takes newborn aurochs calves from the newly dry ground; Hanka matches calf to dam by blood and milk, returns them, and reports that the edge cannot be held.
3. *Fire Season* — Renate's settlers camp in wild-horse country and their fire catches dried peat; Hanka breaches an old dike without orders to drown it, and her report wins them a hearing.
4. *The Unbred* — An unchipped brown bear shadows the autumn elk migration toward the returners' survey camps; Hanka keeps bear and people apart, then signs the report naming Tomasz, who released it.

*Running thread:* Whether the Charter survives the year-end vote as the water falls and Zerbow's roofs surface; book 4 renews it for ten years, with Zerbow's high ground returned and Tomasz's badge gone.

**Why readers come back.** A full case solved in every book, no cliffhangers. A crew worth a year. A recovering land kept that way by competent people.

**What makes it different.** The conflict comes from recovery, not collapse: people want back in. Cases turn on veterinary evidence, tracks and water levels, not guns. The law follows the water, so the strongest witness against the ranger's cause is her own honest report.
