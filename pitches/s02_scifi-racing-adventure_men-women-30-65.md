# Ballast

**Genre:** Science fiction adventure (racing) · **Readers:** Men and women 30–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete race per book

**Logline.** No engines, no steering: in the 2190s a miners' cooperative races a balloon on the winds of Venus, Titan, Neptune and Saturn, ballasted with its members' own ore. Wind-reader Anselm Okoro flies to learn why his banned brother confessed to pilot error.

**The setting.** Band racing is the great sport: three-person crews under envelopes of heated gas, riding the jet streams of four worlds. A crew can only climb or sink to find a wind going its way, and every move costs ballast or fuel. A boat that spends both sinks into the dark.

**The lead.** Anselm Okoro, 48, was band racing's best wind-reader until his brother Tobias's envelope tore on the Great Band six years ago. Tobias signed for pilot error, took a life ban and will not say why. Anselm, now routing Pallas Cooperative ore barges, wants the finding overturned; the price is flying the Marigold under the same make of envelope. He talks like a forecast: short, numbered.

**The cast.** Dita Halloran, 19, burner hand and the members' elected delegate, who signs for every kilo of ore dropped; Hesper Lindqvist, 60, rigger, who bets against her own boat; Priya Raman, 34, the ore buyer's auditor, one signature from pulling the entry; Lucan Dray, 44, Corvane works pilot, who saw the tear and said nothing. "Two hundred kilos," Anselm says. "That's a family's month," says Dita. "One ninety, then."

**The series engine.** Every book is one race: a route the reader can hold, weather that breaks the plan, a failure repaired with what is aboard, a result. Someone always cheats, never the same way twice; once Marigold is accused. The membership votes on each entry, and only a purse pays for the next.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Acid Lap* — Venus Cloud Classic, four days: sunlight makes heat cheap, acid eats rigging, Hesper's patch holds, and a works boat hides a ballast tank.
2. *Cold Fuel* — Titan Endurance, eleven days: thick air makes lift cheap, slow wind makes every kilo count; Marigold's fuel load is protested, and Priya freezes the entry until Dita's ledger clears it.
3. *Blue Limit* — Neptune Sprint, three days in the fastest wind anywhere: cold splits a seam where Tobias's did, and proving a falsified wind feed costs a placing.
4. *The Great Band* — Nine days around Saturn's equator in ring shadow: Marigold and Dray's Corvane boat start level on points, and the title goes to the last day.

*Running thread:* Why Tobias signed for a fault that was not his; book 4 answers with a fabric lot number and a name Dray has kept six years, a Pallas member's.

**Why readers come back.** Each book is a whole race, so a reader can stop satisfied or start the next that night. The crew is good, the danger is weather and physics, the standings carry forward.

**What makes it different.** A space sport with no engines, no weapons and no steering. Tactics are two gauges, ballast and heat, and the underdog's ballast is its own ore, its entry paid with ore tithes and bake sales.
