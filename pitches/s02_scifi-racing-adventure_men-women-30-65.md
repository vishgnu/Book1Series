# Ballast

**Genre:** Science fiction adventure (racing) · **Readers:** Men and women 30–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete race per book

**Logline.** No engines, no steering: a miners' cooperative races a balloon on the winds of Venus, Titan, Neptune and Saturn, ballasted with its members' own ore. Wind-reader Anselm Okoro flies to learn why his banned brother confessed to pilot error.

**The setting.** Band racing: three-person crews riding the jet streams of four worlds. A burner heats the planet's own air, and hot gas lifts. Burn fuel or drop ballast to climb, vent to sink, and find a wind going your way. Fuel and ballast are all a crew can spend; with both gone, no boat climbs again.

**The lead.** Anselm Okoro, 48, was band racing's best wind-reader until he called the fast band and his brother Tobias's envelope tore in it. Tobias signed for pilot error and will not say why. Anselm quit, sure his call was the fault. Only an entrant may open the scrutineering files, so he flies the Pallas Cooperative's Marigold under the make that tore. He talks like a forecast: short, numbered.

**The cast.** Dita Halloran, 19, burner hand and members' delegate, who signs for every kilo of ore dropped; Hesper Lindqvist, 60, rigger, who bets against her own boat; Priya Raman, 34, the ore buyer's auditor, raised on a Pallas tithe, who can pull the entry; Lucan Dray, 44, Corvane works pilot, whose silence bought his seat. "Two hundred kilos," Anselm says. "That's a family's month," says Dita. "One ninety, then."

**The series engine.** Every book is one race: a route the reader can hold, weather that breaks the plan, a failure repaired with what is aboard, a placing. Someone always cheats, never the same way twice; once Marigold is accused. The membership votes on each entry, and only a purse pays for the next.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Acid Lap* — Venus, cheap sun heat and acid: Hesper's patch is Tobias's torn panel, its lot stamp cut out. Third, after a works boat's hidden tank is found; the purse covers Titan.
2. *Cold Fuel* — Titan, thick air and cheap lift: a false protest costs a day, so Marigold crosses slow polar air on short fuel, shedding ore. Second; Priya's audit shows Pallas bought that lot as scrap.
3. *Blue Limit* — Neptune, fastest wind anywhere: the seam splits where Tobias's did. With Tobias on the radio, Anselm quits the fast band to bring it home whole, second to fifth, and Dray says what he saw.
4. *The Great Band* — Saturn, ring shadow: level on points with Dray, the title turns on forty minutes. Dita votes on filing the name; Anselm takes it to Tobias first.

*Running thread:* Why Tobias signed for a fault that was not his; book 4 answers with a lot number and a name Dray has kept six years, a Pallas member's: Dita's mother. He signed so the fine would not close the cooperative.

**Why readers come back.** Each book is a whole race, so a reader can stop satisfied or start the next that night. The danger is weather and physics, and each book the brothers say a little more.

**What makes it different.** A space sport with no engines, no weapons and no steering. Tactics are two gauges, ballast and heat, and the underdog's ballast is its own ore, dense and dear, its entry paid with ore tithes and bake sales.
