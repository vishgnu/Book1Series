# Ballast

**Genre:** Science fiction adventure (racing) · **Readers:** Men and women 30–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete race per book

**Logline.** No engines, no steering: a miners' cooperative races a balloon on the winds of four worlds, ballasted with its members' own ore. Wind-reader Anselm Okoro flies to learn why his banned brother confessed to pilot error.

**The setting.** Band racing: three-person crews in a van-sized cabin under a stadium-sized envelope, riding jet streams for days. A burner heats the planet's own air. Burn fuel or drop ballast to climb, vent to sink, and find a wind going your way. Ballast is the entrant's own cargo, sold at the finish. Fuel and ballast are all a crew can spend; with both gone, a boat sinks into the dark.

**The lead.** Anselm Okoro, 48, called a crossing into the fast band, and his brother Tobias's Pallas Cooperative boat tore in the shear. Tobias signed for pilot error and will not say why. Anselm quit, sure his call was the fault. Only an entrant flying Corvane cloth may read the inspection files, so he flies Pallas's Marigold under the make that tore. He talks like a forecast: short, numbered.

**The cast.** Dita Halloran, 19, burner hand and members' delegate, signs for every kilo dropped. Hesper Lindqvist, 60, rigger, bets against her own boat. Priya Raman, 34, audits for the ore buyer. Lucan Dray, 44, Corvane works pilot, saw the tear and kept quiet. "Two hundred kilos," Anselm says. "That's a family's month," says Dita. "One ninety, then."

**The series engine.** Every book is one race: a route the reader can hold, weather that breaks the plan, a failure repaired with what is aboard, a placing, and one piece of the file. The membership votes on each entry, and only a purse buys passage to the next.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Acid Lap* — Venus, free sun heat and acid: Pallas cannot afford new cloth, so Hesper patches with a sound panel from Tobias's torn envelope, its lot stamp cut out. Third, after Anselm's fuel count exposes a rival's hidden tank.
2. *Cold Fuel* — Titan, minus 290, cheap lift: a false protest costs a day, so Marigold crosses slow polar air on short fuel. Second; Priya's audit shows Corvane sold that lot as scrap.
3. *Blue Limit* — Neptune, 1,100 mph wind: the patch tests sound and the members vote to fly it; the cold splits it. With Tobias on the radio, Anselm quits the fast band to bring it home whole, second to fifth, and Dray says who bought the scrap: Pallas.
4. *The Great Band* — Saturn, ring shadow cutting the sun heat: Marigold beats Dray to the title. Dita votes to file the name; the purse pays the fine, and Tobias's ban is lifted.

*Running thread:* Why Tobias signed for a fault that was not his. Book 4 answers: Pallas bought the scrap cloth to afford its entry, Dita's mother signed for it, and the fine would have closed the cooperative.

**Why readers come back.** Each book is a whole race: stop satisfied, or start the next that night. The danger is weather and physics, and each book the brothers say a little more.

**What makes it different.** No engines, no weapons, no steering: tactics are two gauges, ballast and heat. The underdog's ballast is its own ore, its entry paid with ore tithes and bake sales.
