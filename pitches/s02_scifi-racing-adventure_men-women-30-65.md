# Ballast: The Four Worlds Balloon Race

**Genre:** Science fiction adventure (racing) · **Readers:** Men and women 30–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete race per book

**Logline.** No engines, no steering: a miners' cooperative races a balloon on four worlds' winds, ballasted with its members' own ore. Wind-reader Anselm Okoro flies to learn why his brother took the blame for a crash; Dita Halloran, 19, signs for every kilo he drops.

**The setting.** Band racing: three-person crews in a van-sized cabin under a stadium-sized envelope, riding jet streams for days. A burner heats the planet's own air; under hydrogen skies, the fuel carried is oxygen. Burn or drop ballast to climb, vent to sink, find a wind going your way. With both gone, a boat sinks into the dark.

**The lead.** Anselm Okoro, 48, read the wind for his brother Tobias for twenty years. He called a crossing into the fast band and the Pallas Cooperative's boat tore. Tobias signed for pilot error, took the ban, and will not say why. Anselm quit, sure his call was the fault; now he flies Pallas's Marigold to find out.

**The cast.** Dita Halloran, 19, burner hand and members' delegate. Her mother Maren, 51, keeps Pallas's books. Hesper Lindqvist, 60, rigger, bets against her own boat, Pallas's only insurance. Lucan Dray, 44, rival works pilot, saw the tear and kept quiet for his seat. "Two hundred kilos," Anselm says. "That's a family's month," says Dita. "One ninety, then."

**The series engine.** Every book is one race: a clear route, weather that breaks the plan, a repair with what is aboard, a placing, and one plain answer about the tear. Hesper re-cuts the envelope for each new sky. The members vote on each entry; only a purse buys passage to the next.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Acid Sky: The Venus Race* — Acid eats the vent line and Hesper rebuilds it aloft; Marigold places third, and the torn cloth shows Anselm's call was sound.
2. *Cold Lift: The Titan Race* — The burner ices and dies over a methane sea; relit, Marigold places second, and Anselm learns Tobias knew the cloth was scrap.
3. *Blue Shear: The Neptune Race* — A seam splits in 1,100 mph wind; Anselm quits the fast band to finish fifth, and Dray names the scrap's buyer: Pallas.
4. *Ring Shadow: The Saturn Race* — Marigold beats Dray by forty minutes; Dita votes to report her mother, the purse pays the fine, and Tobias's ban is lifted.

*Running thread:* Why Tobias took the blame for a tear not his fault. Book 4 answers: Maren bought scrap cloth so Pallas could afford its entry; the fine would have closed the cooperative.

**Why readers come back.** Stop satisfied after any book, or start the next that night. Each finish answers one question and raises a sharper one: not Anselm's call; Tobias knew; Pallas bought it; who signed.

**What makes it different.** No weapons: tactics are two gauges, ballast and heat. Hard science, not a cozy float. The underdog's ballast is its own ore, and the underdog is not clean.
