# Ballast

**Genre:** Science fiction adventure (racing) · **Readers:** Men and women 30–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete race per book

**Logline.** No engines, no steering: in the 2190s, crews race balloons on the jet streams of Venus, Titan, Jupiter and Saturn, and a boat out of ballast and heat sinks. Wind-reader Anselm Okoro flies a miners' cooperative entry to learn why his brother's envelope tore.

**The setting.** Band racing is the great sport of the 2190s: three-person crews in gondolas under envelopes of heated gas, riding the jet streams of four worlds. No engines, no steering. A crew can only climb or sink to find a wind going its way, and every move costs ballast or burner fuel. A boat that spends both sinks into the dark.

**The lead.** Anselm Okoro, 48, was band racing's best wind-reader until the Great Band killed his brother and co-pilot Tobias six years ago. He has routed ore barges for the Pallas Cooperative since. He wants the inquiry reopened; the price is flying the Marigold under a secondhand envelope of the same make. He talks like a forecast: short, numbered, never louder than the burner.

**The cast.** Dita Halloran, 19, burner prodigy who meters heat by the gram and wants the route calls Anselm keeps; Hesper Lindqvist, 60, rigger, who patches seams by feel and rigged Tobias's last boat; Priya Raman, 34, the ore buyer's auditor, ground-side, who can pull the entry with one signature; Lucan Dray, 44, Corvane works pilot, last to see Tobias aloft.

**The series engine.** Every book is one race, launch to finish: a route the reader can hold in mind, weather that breaks the plan, a failure repaired with what is aboard, and a result. Someone always cheats, never the same way twice; once Marigold is the boat accused. The federation runs four races every season, and the cooperative needs each purse to enter the next.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Acid Lap* — Venus Cloud Classic, four days over acid cloud that eats rigging by the hour: Marigold needs a purse finish, and one works boat outclimbs its ballast.
2. *Cold Fuel* — Titan Endurance, eleven days in orange haze at minus 290 degrees: Dita and Anselm split over the route, and Marigold is the boat protested.
3. *Storm Rim* — Jupiter's Red Spot Rim, one lap of a storm wider than Earth: a seam splits where Tobias's did, and proving a rival's foul will cost a placing.
4. *The Great Band* — Nine days around Saturn's equator in ring shadow and 1,100-mile-an-hour wind: Marigold and Dray's Corvane boat start level on points, and the title goes to the last day.

*Running thread:* Why Tobias's envelope failed, and who had it signed off as pilot error; book 4 answers, in the air, with a fabric lot number and a name Dray has kept six years.

**Why readers come back.** Each book is a whole race with a finish line and a result, so a reader can stop satisfied or start the next that night. The crew is good at its work, the danger is weather and physics, and the standings carry forward.

**What makes it different.** A space sport with no engines, no weapons and no steering. Tactics come down to two gauges, ballast and heat, and the underdog entry is paid for with ore tithes and bake sales.
