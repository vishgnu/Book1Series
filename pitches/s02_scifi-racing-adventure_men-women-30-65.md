# Ballast

**Genre:** Science fiction adventure (racing) · **Readers:** Men and women 30–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete race per book

**Logline.** No engines, no steering: a miners' cooperative races a balloon on the winds of four worlds, ballasted with its members' own ore. Wind-reader Anselm Okoro flies to learn why his banned brother confessed to pilot error.

**The setting.** Band racing: three-person crews in a van-sized cabin under a stadium-sized envelope, riding four worlds' jet streams for days. A burner heats the planet's own air, and hot gas lifts; under hydrogen skies the fuel is carried oxygen. Burn fuel or drop ballast to climb, vent to sink, and find a wind going your way. Fuel and ballast are all a crew can spend; with both gone, a boat sinks into the dark.

**The lead.** Wind-reader Anselm Okoro, 48, called a crossing into the fast band, and his brother Tobias's envelope tore in the shear. Tobias signed for pilot error and will not say why. Anselm quit, sure his call was the fault. The scrutineering files open only to an entrant flying Corvane cloth, so he flies the Pallas Cooperative's Marigold under the make that tore. He talks like a forecast: short, numbered.

**The cast.** Marigold's other two: Dita Halloran, 19, burner hand and members' delegate, who signs for every kilo of ore dropped; Hesper Lindqvist, 60, rigger, who re-cuts the envelope for each world. Priya Raman, 34, audits for the ore buyer, which pays only for kilos that cross the line. Lucan Dray, 44, Corvane works pilot, saw the tear and kept quiet for his seat. "Two hundred kilos," Anselm says. "That's a family's month," says Dita. "One ninety, then."

**The series engine.** Every book is one race: a route the reader can hold, weather that breaks the plan, a failure repaired with what is aboard, a placing, and one piece of the file. The membership votes on each entry, and only a purse buys passage to the next.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Acid Lap* — Venus, free sun heat and acid: Hesper's patch is Tobias's torn panel, its lot stamp cut out. Third, after Anselm's fuel count exposes a works boat's hidden tank.
2. *Cold Fuel* — Titan, minus 290, cheap lift: a false protest costs a day, so Marigold crosses slow polar air on short fuel, shedding ore. Second; Priya's audit shows Corvane sold that lot as scrap.
3. *Blue Limit* — Neptune, 1,100 mph wind: the patch splits in the shear. With Tobias on the radio, Anselm quits the fast band to bring it home whole, second to fifth, and Dray says who bought the scrap: Pallas.
4. *The Great Band* — Saturn, ring shadow: level on points with Dray, Marigold wins the title by forty minutes. Dita votes to file the name; Tobias hears it first, and his ban is lifted.

*Running thread:* Why Tobias signed for a fault that was not his. Book 4 answers: Dita's mother signed the scrap receipt, and the fine would have closed the cooperative.

**Why readers come back.** Each book is a whole race: stop satisfied, or start the next that night. The danger is weather and physics, and each book the brothers say a little more.

**What makes it different.** No engines, no weapons, no steering: tactics are two gauges, ballast and heat. The underdog's ballast is its own ore, its entry paid with ore tithes and bake sales.
