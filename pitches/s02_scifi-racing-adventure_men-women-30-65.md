# Ballast: The Four Worlds Balloon Race

**Genre:** Science fiction adventure (racing) · **Readers:** Men and women 30–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete race per book

**Logline.** No engines, no steering: a miners' cooperative races a balloon on four worlds' winds, ballasted with its members' ore. Wind-reader Anselm Okoro flies to learn why his brother took the blame for a crash; Dita Halloran, 19, signs for every kilo dropped.

**The setting.** Band racing: three-person crews in a van-sized cabin under a stadium-sized envelope, riding jet streams for days. A burner heats the planet's own air, fuel chosen for each sky; under hydrogen, the fuel carried is oxygen. Burn or drop ballast to climb, vent to sink, find a wind going your way. With heat and ballast gone, a boat sinks.

**The lead.** Anselm Okoro, 48, read wind for his brother Tobias for twenty years. He called a crossing into the fast band; the Pallas Cooperative's boat tore. Tobias signed for pilot error, took the ban, and won't say why. Anselm quit, sure his call was at fault; now he flies Pallas's Marigold to find out. "He talks like a forecast," Tobias says.

**The cast.** Dita Halloran, burner hand and members' delegate: no ore goes overboard until she signs. Rigger Hesper Lindqvist bets her wages against Marigold; if the boat is lost, that payout is Pallas's only insurance. Rival pilot Lucan Dray saw the tear and kept quiet for his seat. "Two hundred kilos," Anselm says. "That's a family's month," says Dita. "One ninety, then."

**The series engine.** Every book is one race: a clear route, weather that breaks the plan, one hard choice, a placing, one plain answer about the tear. Pallas is four hundred miners on one asteroid; a purse buys a year's air. Members vote on each entry; only a purse buys passage to the next.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Acid Sky: The Venus Race* — Acid eats the vent line; Hesper patches it aloft with cloth from Tobias's torn envelope, Marigold places third, and that cloth shows Anselm's call was sound.
2. *Cold Lift: The Titan Race* — Marigold gives up the lead to lift a downed crew off a methane sea, places second; their rigger says Tobias knew the cloth was scrap.
3. *Blue Shear: The Neptune Race* — The Venus patch splits crossing into the fastest band; Anselm turns back, finishes fifth, and Dray names the scrap's buyer: Pallas.
4. *Ring Shadow: The Saturn Race* — Ring-shadow cold kills the lift; Dita signs away the last ore, Marigold beats Dray, and her vote makes Pallas report itself.

*Running thread:* Why Tobias took the blame for the tear. Book 4 answers: the members voted to fly scrap cloth to afford the entry, and Tobias voted yes. The purse pays the fine; his ban is lifted.

**Why readers come back.** Stop satisfied after any book, or start the next that night. Each finish answers one question and raises a sharper one.

**What makes it different.** No weapons: tactics are two gauges, ballast and heat. Four real skies, each flown by its own rules. The underdog is not clean.
