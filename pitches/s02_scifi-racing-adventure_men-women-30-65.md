# Ballast: The Four Worlds Balloon Race

**Genre:** Science fiction adventure (racing) · **Readers:** Men and women 30–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete race per book

**Logline.** A miners' cooperative races an engineless balloon through four worlds' skies, ballasted with its members' ore, while its wind-reader hunts the reason his brother took the blame when the last one tore.

**The setting.** Band racing: three-person crews in a van-sized cabin under a stadium-sized envelope, riding jet streams for days. Crews call the balloon a boat and each layer of wind a band. The burner heats whatever air the planet has; in a hydrogen sky the crew carries oxygen to burn. Burn or drop ballast to climb, vent to sink, find a wind going your way. With heat and ballast gone, a boat sinks.

**The lead.** Anselm Okoro, 48, read wind for his brother Tobias for twenty years. He called a crossing into the fast band; the Pallas Cooperative's boat tore. Tobias signed for pilot error, took the ban, and won't say why. Anselm quit, sure his call was at fault; now he pilots Pallas's Marigold to find out.

**The cast.** Dita Halloran, 19, burner hand and members' delegate: no ore goes overboard until she signs. Third seat, rigger Hesper Lindqvist bets her wages against Marigold: if the boat goes down, her winnings are Pallas's only insurance. Rival Lucan Dray saw the tear and said nothing, to keep his pilot's seat. "Two hundred kilos," Anselm says. "That's a family's month," says Dita. "One ninety, then."

**The series engine.** Every book is one race: a route, weather that breaks the plan, a hard choice, a placing, one plain answer about the tear. Pallas is four hundred miners on one asteroid; a purse buys a year's air. Members vote on each entry.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Acid Sky: The Venus Race* — Acid eats the vent line; Hesper patches it aloft, Marigold places third, and the old wreck shows the tear began in bad cloth, not Anselm's wind.
2. *Cold Lift: The Titan Race* — Marigold gives up the lead to lift a downed crew off a methane sea, places second; their rigger says Tobias knew the cloth was condemned.
3. *Blue Shear: The Neptune Race* — Hesper's patch splits at the fastest band; Anselm turns back, finishes fifth, and Dray names who bought the cloth: Pallas.
4. *Ring Shadow: The Saturn Race* — In ring-shadow cold, Anselm calls the fast-band crossing he has refused since the tear, Dita signs the last ore, Marigold wins, and her vote makes Pallas report itself.

*Running thread:* Why Tobias took the blame. Book 4 answers: a closed five-seat committee voted to fly condemned cloth, Tobias cast the deciding yes, then signed for pilot error so the fine fell on him, not on Pallas's air. The purse pays it; his ban is lifted.

**Why readers come back.** Each finish closes one question: the call was sound; Tobias knew; Pallas bought the cloth; the committee voted.

**What makes it different.** No weapons: tactics are two gauges, ballast and heat. Acid, methane cold, supersonic shear, ring shadow: each sky breaks the boat differently. The underdog is not clean.
