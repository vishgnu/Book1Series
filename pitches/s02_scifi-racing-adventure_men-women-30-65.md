# Ballast

**Genre:** Science fiction adventure (racing) · **Readers:** Men and women 30–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete race per book

**Logline.** No engines, no steering: in the 2190s a miners' cooperative races a balloon on the winds of Venus, Titan, Neptune and Saturn, ballasted with its members' own ore. Wind-reader Anselm Okoro flies to learn why his banned brother confessed to pilot error.

**The setting.** Band racing: three-person crews under stadium-sized envelopes, riding the jet streams of four worlds. A burner heats the planet's own air; hot gas lifts, even hydrogen in hydrogen. Burn fuel or drop ballast to climb, vent to sink, and find a wind going your way. A boat that spends both sinks into the dark.

**The lead.** Anselm Okoro, 48, was band racing's best wind-reader until his brother Tobias's envelope tore on the Great Band. Tobias signed for pilot error, took a life ban and will not say why. Only an entrant may open the scrutineering files, so Anselm, now a Pallas Cooperative barge router, flies the Marigold under the make that tore. He talks like a forecast: short, numbered.

**The cast.** Dita Halloran, 19, burner hand and members' delegate, who signs for every kilo of ore dropped; Hesper Lindqvist, 60, who rigged Tobias's last boat; Priya Raman, 34, the ore buyer's auditor, one signature from pulling the entry; Lucan Dray, 44, Corvane works pilot, who saw the tear and said nothing. "Two hundred kilos," Anselm says. "That's a family's month," says Dita. "One ninety, then."

**The series engine.** Every book is one race: a route the reader can hold, weather that breaks the plan, a failure repaired with what is aboard, a placing. Someone always cheats, never the same way twice; once Marigold is accused. The membership votes on each entry, only a purse pays for the next, and each race gives up one piece of Tobias's file.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Acid Lap* — Venus, four days of cheap sun heat and acid: Hesper's patch is Tobias's torn panel, its lot stamp cut out. Fourth, third once a works boat's hidden tank is found; the purse covers Titan.
2. *Cold Fuel* — Titan, eleven days at minus 290: a false protest costs a day, so Marigold crosses slow polar air on short fuel, shedding ore. Second; Priya's audit shows Pallas bought that lot as scrap.
3. *Blue Limit* — Neptune, three days in 1,100-mile-an-hour wind: the seam splits where Tobias's did. Anselm quits the fast band to bring it home whole, second to fifth, and Dray says what he saw.
4. *The Great Band* — Saturn, nine days in ring shadow: level on points with Dray, Marigold takes the title by forty minutes. The members vote to file the name; Tobias's ban lifts.

*Running thread:* Why Tobias signed for a fault that was not his; book 4 answers with a lot number and a name Dray has kept six years, a Pallas member's.

**Why readers come back.** Each book is a whole race, so a reader can stop satisfied or start the next that night. The crew is good, the danger is weather and physics, the standings carry forward.

**What makes it different.** A space sport with no engines, no weapons and no steering. Tactics are two gauges, ballast and heat, and the underdog's ballast is its own ore, its entry paid with ore tithes and bake sales.
