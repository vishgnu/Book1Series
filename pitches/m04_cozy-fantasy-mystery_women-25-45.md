# Keys Cut While You Wait

**Genre:** Cozy fantasy mystery · **Readers:** Women 25–45 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Hester Wardlow is sworn locksmith to a hill town whose doors are bound to hand, word, hour or promise. She has no magic; a retired burglar makes her tea. When a neighbor dies behind a locked door, the law sends only her.

**The setting.** Pellory is a hill town of nine hundred steps and a market on every landing. Each lock is charmed once, to a hand, a word, an hour or a promise. A charm knows its condition was met, never who met it: a pantry bound to breakfast opens for anyone at breakfast. By the Lock Law, only a sworn locksmith may open a room locked from inside, then swear how it was locked.

**The lead.** Hester Wardlow, 38, came home from twelve years fitting ships' strongboxes to her grandmother Odile's shop and Odile's oath, with one promise added: never open a door unwitnessed. Her cellar lock is bound to it. No charm takes from her hand, so she alone can lose her keys. She talks to locks like shy horses and to suspects less gently.

**The cast.** Bram Tolliver, 71, retired burglar, grades Hester's lockwork out of ten; she has never had an eight. Sergeant Dorcas Quill, 52, the lawful witness, complains on each stair and kept Odile's witness book. Idris Fenn, 36, the town binder, takes every lock Hester picks personally and leaves soup unasked.

**The series engine.** Each book opens on a door locked, or open, in a way no charm allows, and ends with the culprit named; every clue is on the page first. Deaths stay offstage; the tea does not. Cases keep coming: nobody else may open that door, and she cannot swear until she knows how.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Locked from Inside* — A clockmaker dies behind a hand-bound door Idris charmed; Hester proves whose hand it obeyed and finds a door of Odile's missing from Quill's witness book.
2. *Under Ward* — A will vanishes from a word-bound strongroom; Tamsin, 16, is blamed for knowing the word Odile taught her mother. Hester and Idris clear her; she stays as apprentice.
3. *The Spare Key* — A dead innkeeper's hour-bound door stands open, a key in Odile's style in the lock; Hester clears Bram, learns why Odile hired a burglar, and Idris binds her a lock.
4. *Double Throw* — A magistrate dies behind two bolts, one hour-bound, one promise-bound; to prove whose promise broke, Hester breaks her own, and tells Idris first.

*Running thread:* Whether Odile ever opened a door she had no right to. Hester's broken promise opens the cellar lock on Odile's ledger of unwitnessed doors: most hid a kindness, one hid the magistrate's fraud. Hester swears it into Quill's book; the last pages are blank for her.

**Why readers come back.** A puzzle you can solve from the armchair, a steep town you would rent a room in, and a shop where a retired burglar makes the tea. Each book closes its case and adds one person to Hester's table.

**What makes it different.** Hester is the one person the law lets through the door, so she never has to snoop. The magic is a short list of lock rules, and each solution is one rule, bent.
