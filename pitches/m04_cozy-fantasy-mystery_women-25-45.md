# The Sworn Locksmith of Pellory

**Genre:** Cozy fantasy mystery · **Readers:** Women 25–45 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Hester Wardlow keeps the key shop in a hill town where doors are charm-bound to hand, word, hour or promise; a retired burglar makes her tea. When a door locks as no charm allows, the law waits on her oath.

**The setting.** Pellory is a hill town of nine hundred steps, four days from any judge. A hand lock opens to any bound hand; a word lock to its word; an hour lock at its hour; a promise lock when its promise breaks. A charm knows its condition was met, never who met it. By the Lock Law, no one is charged until the sworn locksmith swears how the door opened.

**The lead.** Hester, 38, has no magic. She left at 26 when her grandmother Odile would not explain the locked cellar, and came home from ships' strongboxes too late to ask. She wants the shop to be hers. Her fingertips are salt-scarred, so she knocks; she talks to locks like shy horses.

**The cast.** Bram Tolliver, 71, retired burglar, grades Hester's lockwork out of ten; she has never had an eight. Sergeant Dorcas Quill, 52, her lawful witness, once watched Odile open a door and never wrote it down. Idris Fenn, 36, town binder, takes each picked lock personally and leaves soup unasked.

**The series engine.** Each book opens on a neighbor's worst morning: a door locked, or open, as no charm allows. The lock type names the suspects: whoever held the hand, word, hour or promise. Every clue comes first; deaths stay offstage.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Hand-Bound Clock Shop* — A clockmaker dies behind a door bound to three hands; Hester wrongly blames Idris's charm, finds the hand, and learns Odile's cellar lock holds her promise: never open a door unwitnessed.
2. *The Word-Bound Strongroom* — A strongroom is robbed and Tamsin, 16, is blamed; Hester finds the thief, gains an apprentice, and Idris binds the girl's first lock.
3. *The Hour-Bound Inn* — A cashbox vanishes through an hour-bound door; Hester clears Bram, learns why Odile hired a burglar, and Idris asks her to test his work.
4. *The Promise-Bound Cellar* — A magistrate dies behind a promise-bound bolt; Hester breaks her own promise to prove such locks spring from anywhere, breaking the killer's alibi and opening Odile's cellar.

*Running thread:* Whether Odile ever opened a door she had no right to. Her cellar ledger answers: most doors hid a kindness, one hid the magistrate's fraud. Hester re-swears, takes over its blank pages, Pellory stops calling the shop Odile's, and Idris stays for supper.

**Why readers come back.** An armchair-solvable puzzle and a steep town you would rent a room in. Each book closes its case, adds one person to Hester's table, and moves one step nearer Odile's cellar.

**What makes it different.** The law lets only Hester through the door, so she never has to snoop. The magic is four lock rules; each solution is one rule, bent.
