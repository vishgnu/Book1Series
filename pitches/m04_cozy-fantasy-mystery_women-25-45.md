# Keys Cut While You Wait

**Genre:** Cozy fantasy mystery · **Readers:** Women 25–45 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Hester Wardlow is sworn locksmith to a hill town whose doors are bound to hand, word, hour or promise. She has no magic; a retired burglar makes her tea. When a door locks as no charm allows, nobody is charged until she swears how.

**The setting.** Pellory is a hill town of nine hundred steps, a market on every landing. A hand lock opens to any bound hand; a word lock to its word; an hour lock at its hour; a promise lock the moment its promise breaks. A charm knows its condition was met, never who met it. By the Lock Law, only a sworn locksmith may open a door no charm explains, and nobody is charged until she swears how.

**The lead.** Hester Wardlow, 38, left at 26 when her grandmother Odile would not explain the cellar, and came home from ships' strongboxes too late to ask. Odile bound Hester's apprentice promise into the cellar lock: never open a locked door unwitnessed. She wants the shop to be hers, and hides behind rules she wants to deserve. She talks to locks like shy horses and to suspects less gently.

**The cast.** Bram Tolliver, 71, retired burglar, grades Hester's lockwork out of ten; she has never had an eight. Sergeant Dorcas Quill, 52, her lawful witness, once watched Odile open a door and never wrote it down. Idris Fenn, 36, town binder, takes each picked lock personally and leaves soup unasked; a four-book thaw.

**The series engine.** Each book opens a neighbor's worst morning: a door locked, or open, in a way no charm allows. Hester cannot swear until she knows how. Every clue comes first; deaths stay offstage.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Locked from Inside* — A clockmaker dies behind a door Idris bound to three hands. Hester blames his charm, is wrong, and reasons out which hand; Quill's witness book is one door short.
2. *Under Ward* — No death: a word-bound strongroom is robbed and Tamsin, 16, who calls picking cheating, is blamed. Hester vouches on her oath, finds the thief, gains an apprentice.
3. *The Spare Key* — An inn's cashbox vanishes through an hour-bound door, Odile's style of key in it. Hester clears Bram, learns why Odile hired a burglar; Idris binds her door to a word.
4. *Double Throw* — A magistrate dies behind two bolts, one hour-bound, one promise-bound. To prove a broken promise opens its lock from anywhere, Hester tells Idris, breaks her own, and the cellar lock springs ninety steps away.

*Running thread:* Whether Odile ever opened a door she had no right to. After the culprit is named, the cellar yields Odile's ledger of unwitnessed doors: most hid a kindness, one hid the magistrate's fraud. Hester re-swears; the last pages are blank for her, Pellory stops calling the shop Odile's, and Idris stays for supper.

**Why readers come back.** A puzzle you can solve from the armchair and a steep town you would rent a room in. Each book closes its case and adds one person to Hester's table, where Bram complains about the bread.

**What makes it different.** Hester is the one person the law lets through the door, so she never has to snoop. The magic is four lock rules; each solution is one rule, bent.
