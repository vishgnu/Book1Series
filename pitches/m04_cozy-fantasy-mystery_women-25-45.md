# The Sworn Locksmith of Pellory

**Genre:** Cozy fantasy mystery · **Readers:** Women 25–45 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** In the hill town of Pellory, nobody can be charged with a crime until locksmith Hester Wardlow swears how the door was opened. Each book brings her a door that could not have been.

**The setting.** Pellory is a hill town of nine hundred steps with a judge who climbs up twice a year. Doors are charm-bound four ways: a hand lock opens to any bound hand, a word lock to its word, an hour lock at its hour, a promise lock when its promise breaks. A charm knows its condition was met, never who met it. By the Lock Law, only the sworn locksmith may open a door no charm explains.

**The lead.** Hester, 38, has no magic. She left at 26 when her grandmother Odile would not explain the shop's locked cellar, and came home from ships' strongboxes too late to ask. She wants the shop to be hers. Her oath is bound into that cellar lock: she may open no one's door unwitnessed.

**The cast.** Bram Tolliver, 71, retired burglar, grades Hester's lockwork out of ten. Sergeant Ruth Pike, 52, her lawful witness, keeps a witness book one door short. Cal Marrow, 36, town binder, proud and prickly, rebinds her shop door tighter, unasked.

**The series engine.** Each book opens on a neighbor's worst morning: a door locked, or open, as no charm allows. The lock type names the suspects: whoever held the hand, word, hour or promise. Every clue is on the page before Hester swears; deaths stay offstage.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Clockmaker's Three Hands* — A clockmaker dies behind a door bound to three hands; Hester wrongly blames Cal's charm, then finds a forgotten fourth hand, bound in childhood.
2. *A Word in the Market* — No death: a strongroom is robbed and Cal blames Peg, 16; Hester shows the word sits in a market song and gains an apprentice.
3. *The Early Bell* — A cashbox vanishes through an hour-bound inn door; Hester clears Bram by proving the town bell rang early, learns why Odile hired a burglar, and quarrels with Cal.
4. *The Cellar Ninety Steps Away* — The town clerk dies behind a promise-bound bolt; Hester opens a door unwitnessed, her own cellar lock springs ninety steps away, and the killer's alibi of distance fails.

*Running thread:* Did Odile ever open a door unwitnessed? Her cellar holds a ledger of them: most hid a kindness, one the clerk's fraud. Hester re-swears, Pellory stops calling the shop Odile's, and Cal stays for supper.

**Why readers come back.** An armchair-solvable puzzle and a steep town you would rent a room in. Each book closes its case, adds one person to Hester's table, and moves nearer Odile's cellar.

**What makes it different.** The law lets only Hester through the door, so she never has to snoop. The magic is four lock rules; each solution is one rule, bent, and the reader holds the same four.
