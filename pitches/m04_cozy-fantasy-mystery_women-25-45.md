# The Sworn Locksmith of Pellory

**Genre:** Cozy fantasy mystery · **Readers:** Women 25–45 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** In the hill town of Pellory, nobody can be charged with a crime until locksmith Hester Wardlow swears how the door was opened. Each book brings her an impossible one.

**The setting.** Pellory climbs nine hundred steps; a judge climbs them twice a year. Doors are charm-bound four ways: a hand lock opens to any bound hand, a word lock to its word, an hour lock at its hour, a promise lock when its promise breaks. A charm knows its condition was met, never who met it. By the Lock Law, only the sworn locksmith may open a door no charm explains.

**The lead.** Hester, 38, has no magic. Ships' strongboxes salt-scarred her fingertips, so she knocks, and she talks to locks like shy horses. She left when her grandmother Odile would not explain the shop's cellar; Odile died before she came home. To make the shop hers she swore Odile's oath, bound into the cellar's promise lock: open any door unwitnessed and the cellar springs open.

**The cast.** Bram Tolliver, retired burglar, grades Hester's lockwork out of ten; she has never had an eight. Sergeant Ruth Pike, her lawful witness, keeps a book missing one of Odile's doors. Cal Marrow, town binder, takes every unexplained door personally and rebinds Hester's shop door tighter, unasked.

**The series engine.** Every case opens on a neighbor's worst morning: a door locked, or open, as no charm allows. The lock type names the suspects: whoever held the hand, word, hour or promise. All the clues are on the page before Hester swears. Deaths stay offstage.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Clockmaker's Three Hands* — A clockmaker dies behind a door bound to three hands; Hester wrongly blames Cal's charm, then finds a forgotten fourth hand, bound in childhood.
2. *A Word in the Market* — No death: a strongroom is robbed and Cal blames Peg, 16; Hester shows the word sits in a market song and gains an apprentice.
3. *The Early Bell* — A cashbox vanishes through an hour-bound inn door; Hester clears Bram by proving the town bell rang early, quarrels with Cal, and learns why Odile hired a burglar.
4. *The Cellar Ninety Steps Away* — The town clerk dies behind a promise-bound bolt; Hester opens a door unwitnessed, her cellar springs open ninety steps away, proving a promise lock reaches farther than the killer's alibi.

*Running thread:* Did Odile ever open a door unwitnessed? Her cellar holds a ledger of unwitnessed doors: most hid a kindness, one the clerk's fraud. Hester re-swears, Pellory stops calling the shop Odile's, and Cal stays for supper.

**Why readers come back.** A steep town you would rent a room in, with a burglar marking your homework. Each book closes its case, adds one person to Hester's table, and moves nearer Odile's cellar.

**What makes it different.** She never has to snoop: the law sends her in. Each solution is one of the four lock rules, bent, and the reader holds the same four.
