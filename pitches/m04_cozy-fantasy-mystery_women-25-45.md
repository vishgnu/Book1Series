# Keys Cut While You Wait

**Genre:** Cozy fantasy mystery · **Readers:** Women 25–45 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** In Pellory, where every door is charmed and the Lock Law lets only a sworn locksmith open a room locked from within, Hester Wardlow, 33, is first in when someone dies behind one, and finds how the lock was cheated.

**The setting.** Pellory is a hill town of nine hundred steps, slate roofs and a market on every landing, where each household charms its own doors. A lock can be bound to a hand, a word, an hour or a promise, never two at once, and none can be locked from the far side. A charm knows its condition was met, never who met it.

**The lead.** Hester Wardlow, 33, inherited her grandmother Odile's shop on Latchkey Stairs and its sign, Keys Cut While You Wait. She wants the trust Pellory gave Odile; the work makes her the one who opens a neighbor's worst morning. No magic: picks, wax and patience. She talks to locks like shy horses and to suspects less gently.

**The cast.** Bram Tolliver, 71, retired burglar, sweeps the shop and knows how any lock is really beaten. Sergeant Dorcas Quill, 52, the lawful witness, hates stairs. Idris Fenn, 36, the glazier next door, brings soup unasked. Odile's forty-one ledgers name every lock the shop made and who paid.

**The series engine.** Each book opens on an impossible locked room and ends with a door opened, the culprit named and the town a little safer. Readers learn the lock rules early and can spot the bent one first. Cases keep coming because the law sends for her: nobody else may open that door.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Locked from Inside* — A clockmaker dies behind a hand-bound door; Hester proves whose hand the lock obeyed, and Pellory stops calling the shop Odile's.
2. *Under Ward* — A guardian dies in a word-bound study and his ward Tamsin, 16, is blamed; Hester clears her and gains an apprentice.
3. *The Spare Key* — A spare key in Odile's style puts Bram at a dead innkeeper's hour-bound door; Hester clears him and learns why Odile hired a burglar.
4. *Double Throw* — A magistrate dies behind two bolts and two charms; to prove how, Hester opens the cellar lock she promised to leave shut.

*Running thread:* What Odile kept behind the cellar lock, bound to Hester's own promise: a ledger of every door she opened without a witness, its last pages blank.

**Why readers come back.** A puzzle you can solve from the armchair, a steep town you would rent a room in, and a shop where a retired burglar makes the tea. Each book closes its case and adds one person to the table behind Hester's counter.

**What makes it different.** Hester is the one person the law lets through the door, so she never has to snoop. The magic is a short list of lock rules, and each solution is one rule, bent.
