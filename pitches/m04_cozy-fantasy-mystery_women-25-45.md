# Keys Cut While You Wait

**Genre:** Cozy fantasy mystery · **Readers:** Women 25–45 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Hester Wardlow is sworn locksmith to a hill town whose doors are bound to a hand, a word, an hour or a promise. She has no magic; a retired burglar makes her tea. When a room is locked from inside, the law sends only her.

**The setting.** Pellory is a hill town of nine hundred steps, slate roofs and a market on every landing. Each lock is charmed once, to one thing: a hand, a word, an hour or a promise. A charm knows its condition was met, never who met it: a promise lock opens when the promise breaks, whoever broke it. By the Lock Law, only a sworn locksmith may open a room locked from inside.

**The lead.** Hester Wardlow, 33, inherited her grandmother Odile's shop on Latchkey Stairs and Odile's oath, with one promise added: never open a door unwitnessed. No charm takes from her hand, so she alone in Pellory can lose her keys. With picks, wax and patience she opens a neighbor's worst morning. She talks to locks like shy horses and to suspects less gently.

**The cast.** Bram Tolliver, 71, retired burglar, grades Hester's lockwork out of ten; she has never had an eight. Sergeant Dorcas Quill, 52, the lawful witness, hates stairs and witnessed for Odile for twenty years. Idris Fenn, 36, the town binder, takes every lock Hester picks personally, a romance across four books.

**The series engine.** Each book opens on an impossible locked room and ends with a door opened and the culprit named. Deaths stay offstage; the tea does not. Cases keep coming because the law sends for her: nobody else may open that door.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Locked from Inside* — A clockmaker dies behind a hand-bound door; Hester proves whose hand the lock obeyed, and Pellory stops calling the shop Odile's.
2. *Under Ward* — A guardian's will vanishes from a word-bound strongroom and his ward Tamsin, 16, is blamed; Hester clears her and gains an apprentice.
3. *The Spare Key* — A spare key in Odile's style puts Bram at a dead innkeeper's hour-bound door; Hester clears him and learns why Odile hired a burglar.
4. *Double Throw* — A magistrate dies behind two bolts, one hour-bound, one promise-bound; to prove whose promise broke, Hester breaks her own.

*Running thread:* Whether Odile ever opened a door she had no right to. Hester's broken promise opens the cellar lock on Odile's ledger of unwitnessed doors, each opened to hide a household's kindness, its last pages blank for Hester.

**Why readers come back.** A puzzle you can solve from the armchair, a steep town you would rent a room in, and a shop where a retired burglar makes the tea. Each book closes its case and adds one person to Hester's table.

**What makes it different.** Hester is the one person the law lets through the door, so she never has to snoop. The magic is a short list of lock rules, and each solution is one rule, bent.
