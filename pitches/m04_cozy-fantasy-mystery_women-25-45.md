# Keys Cut While You Wait

**Genre:** Cozy fantasy mystery · **Readers:** Women 25–45 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Hester Wardlow, 33, keeps a locksmith shop in a hill town of charmed doors, where a retired burglar makes the tea. She has no magic. When a neighbor dies behind a locked door, the law sends only her to find how the lock was cheated.

**The setting.** Pellory is a hill town of nine hundred steps, slate roofs and a market on every landing, where each household charms its own doors. A lock is bound to one thing: a hand, a word, an hour or a promise. A charm knows its condition was met, never who met it: a pantry bound to breakfast opens for anyone at breakfast. By the Lock Law, only a sworn locksmith may open a room locked from inside.

**The lead.** Hester Wardlow, 33, inherited her grandmother Odile's shop on Latchkey Stairs and its sign, Keys Cut While You Wait. She wants to believe Odile earned Pellory's trust, and she promised never to open the cellar lock that would tell her. No magic: picks, wax and patience. She talks to locks like shy horses and to suspects less gently.

**The cast.** Bram Tolliver, 71, retired burglar, sweeps the shop and grades Hester's lockwork out of ten; she has never had an eight. Sergeant Dorcas Quill, 52, the lawful witness, hates stairs and says so on each one. Idris Fenn, 36, the glazier next door, brings soup unasked, a romance that takes all four books.

**The series engine.** Each book opens on an impossible locked room and ends with a door opened and the culprit named. Deaths stay offstage; the tea does not. Readers learn the lock rules early and can spot the bent one first. Cases keep coming because the law sends for her: nobody else may open that door.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Locked from Inside* — A clockmaker dies behind a hand-bound door; Hester proves whose hand the lock obeyed, and Pellory stops calling the shop Odile's.
2. *Under Ward* — A guardian dies in a word-bound study and his ward Tamsin, 16, is blamed; Hester clears her and gains an apprentice.
3. *The Spare Key* — A spare key in Odile's style puts Bram at a dead innkeeper's hour-bound door; Hester clears him and learns why Odile hired a burglar.
4. *Double Throw* — A magistrate dies behind two bolts and two charms; to prove how, Hester opens the cellar lock she promised to leave shut.

*Running thread:* Whether Odile ever opened a door she had no right to. Behind the cellar lock, bound to Hester's own promise, is her ledger of every door opened without a witness, its last pages left blank for Hester.

**Why readers come back.** A puzzle you can solve from the armchair, a steep town you would rent a room in, and a shop where a retired burglar makes the tea. Each book closes its case and adds one person to the table behind Hester's counter.

**What makes it different.** Hester is the one person the law lets through the door, so she never has to snoop. The magic is a short list of lock rules, and each solution is one rule, bent.
