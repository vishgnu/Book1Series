# Cause of Failure

**Genre:** Techno-mystery · **Readers:** Men and women 40–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Structural engineer Elias Brandt, 52, sealed a drawing for a ramp that fell and killed two. Now he, a metallurgist and a retired crane operator tell Duluth insurers why things fell. Each time, the metal contradicts the paperwork.

**The setting.** Duluth and Lake Superior's working shore at 30 below: ore docks, grain elevators, ski lifts, dams. Northline Testing inspected most of it, the ramp included. The rule: no insurer pays until an engineer signs a cause, and the signature decides who pays.

**The lead.** Elias warned in writing that a ramp connection was unsafe, then sealed a late revision unchecked: Vance, his partner of twenty years, called it a title-block change. Suspended a year, he wants to know who drew the change. After each case he writes to Ruth Sorenson, a victim's widow; she has not answered.

**The cast.** Naomi Adeyemi, 29, metallurgist, quit Northline rather than retype a failed hardness result. Dutch Kowalczyk, 68, retired crane operator, set the ramp's girders. Renee Halloran, Lakehead Mutual's claims manager, hires them and fights every invoice. Martin Vance, a suspect, checks Elias's numbers for free; Elias rechecks each, to keep him in sight.

**The series engine.** Every book opens on a failure ruled an accident, five parties who could owe and 60 days to sign. Every other local engineer takes Northline work, so Renee hires Elias. Each case has a plausible wrong answer, pushed by one party's expert, and breaks on something one trade alone could read.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Factor of Safety* — A ski-lift tower falls. Naomi blames a retorque Northline skipped; a bolt stamp and cleavage faces clear Northline: the owner bought off-grade anchors, brittle at 30 below. Dutch's log: the ramp went up from an unsigned print.
2. *Load Path* — A grain bin splits in Superior. A rain gauge shows the seam was welded wet; Elias must name welders who meant no harm. Pike's ramp notes: the unsigned print came from Elias's own office.
3. *Fatigue Limit* — An ore-dock conveyor drops in Two Harbors. Beach marks show a slow shaft crack, until Dutch reads chain scars: Lakehead's largest policyholder overloaded it, voiding cover. Renee won't bury that and is fired. The print carries Vance's plot stamp.
4. *Single Point of Failure* — Renee, now independent, brings Tamarack Dam's torn spillway gate. Grease logs show the contractor billed for work never done. Northline's subpoenaed files: Pike hand-marked the change so a short girder would fit, the detailer drew it, Vance plotted it unread. The one engineer placed to stop it was Elias. Ruth answers.

*Running thread:* Who drew the late change to sheet S-401: Vance, the erector's detailer or Gordon Pike, the Northline inspector who trained Elias.

**Why readers come back.** People who know their trades take a failure apart in plain language, and the reader learns to read steel. Every book closes its case and turns the S-401 question once.

**What makes it different.** Three trades do the detecting; the clues are a bolt stamp, a fracture face, a rain gauge. The lead was not framed: he signed the sheet. No hacker, no gunfight.
