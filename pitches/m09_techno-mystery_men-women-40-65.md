# Cause of Failure: The Duluth Collapse Mysteries

**Genre:** Techno-mystery · **Readers:** Men and women 40–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Structural engineer Elias Brandt, 52, sealed a drawing for a ramp that fell and killed two. Now he, a metallurgist and a retired crane operator tell Duluth insurers why things fell and who knew. Each time, the metal contradicts the paperwork.

**The setting.** Duluth and Lake Superior's working shore at 30 below: ore docks, grain elevators, ski lifts, dams. Northline Testing inspected most of it, the ramp included. After a fatal failure OSHA holds the scene and every party's expert watches each test. The rule: no insurer pays until an engineer signs a cause, and the signature decides who pays.

**The lead.** Elias warned in writing that a ramp connection was unsafe, then sealed a late revision unchecked: his partner Martin Vance called it a title-block change. Suspension served, license restored, he wants to know who made the change. He speaks in short sentences, units attached. After each case he writes to Ruth Sorenson, a victim's widow; the letters come back unopened.

**The cast.** Naomi Adeyemi, 29, metallurgist, quit Northline rather than retype a failed hardness result; no lab in town will hire her. Dutch Kowalczyk, 68, retired crane operator, set the ramp's girders. Renee Halloran, Lakehead Mutual's claims manager, four years from her pension, hires them and fights every invoice. Gordon Pike, Northline inspector, trained Elias.

**The series engine.** Every book opens on a fatal failure ruled an accident, five parties who could owe and 60 days to sign. Every other local engineer takes Northline work, so only Elias is free of conflict. Each case has a plausible wrong answer and breaks on something one trade alone could read. Elias defends his finding under oath.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Cold Short* — A ski-lift tower kills a mechanic; a bolt stamp proves the owner bought off-grade anchors, brittle at 30 below. The unsigned ramp print came from Elias's office.
2. *Lack of Fusion* — A split grain bin buries a worker; a rain gauge proves the seam was welded wet and passed from a Northline desk. The print carries Vance's plot stamp.
3. *Beach Marks* — An ore-dock conveyor kills two dockhands; Dutch's chain scars overturn the lab and prove a concealed overload. Renee is fired for reporting it. The markup is Pike's.
4. *Fracture Critical* — A torn spillway gate drowns a fisherman; grease logs expose faked maintenance. Northline's files: Pike marked the change, Vance passed it unread, Elias sealed it. Ruth answers.

*Running thread:* Who changed sheet S-401, and what it costs Elias's restored license to find out.

**Why readers come back.** People who know their trades take a failure apart in plain language, and the reader learns to read steel. Every book closes its case and points the S-401 question at someone closer to Elias: his office, his partner, his teacher.

**What makes it different.** Three trades do the detecting; the clues are a bolt stamp, a fracture face, a rain gauge. The lead was not framed: he signed the sheet. No hacker, no gunfight.
