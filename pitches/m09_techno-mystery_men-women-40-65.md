# Cause of Failure

**Genre:** Techno-mystery · **Readers:** Men and women 40–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Structural engineer Elias Brandt, 52, sealed a drawing that killed two. Now, in Duluth, he, a metallurgist and a retired crane operator tell insurers why things fell. Each time, the metal shows the accident had an author.

**The setting.** Duluth and Lake Superior's working shore at 30 below: ore docks, grain elevators, ski hills and small dams, most built before 1970 on town budgets. One firm, Northline Testing, inspected most of it, and the ramp Elias sealed. Brandt Forensic rents Bay 6 of a former ship chandlery, ten-ton crane overhead. The rule: no insurer pays until an engineer signs a cause, and the signature decides who pays.

**The lead.** Elias Brandt warned in writing that a Carver Street ramp connection was unsafe, then sealed a late revision unchecked. The ramp fell, killing two. He served a year's suspension and wants to know who drew the change. After each case he writes to Ruth Sorenson, a victim's widow; she has not answered. He trusts fracture surfaces over paperwork and talks in short sentences, units attached.

**The cast.** Naomi Adeyemi, 29, a metallurgist who quit Northline's lab and tests from a van. Dutch Kowalczyk, 68, a retired crane operator who knows how steel really goes up. Renee Halloran, claims manager at Lakehead Mutual, hires them and fights every invoice. Martin Vance, Elias's old partner, checks his numbers for free.

**The series engine.** Every book opens on a failure ruled an accident and a client who wants that signed in 60 days. Lakehead insures most of what Northline inspected, and Elias is the only local engineer who will sign against Northline. Each case has a plausible wrong answer and breaks on something one trade alone could read.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Factor of Safety* — A ski-lift tower falls at Sawbill Ridge. Naomi finds off-grade anchor bolts, blaming the owner, until Dutch shows a retorque Northline signed was never done. The signer, Gordon Pike, also passed the ramp.
2. *Load Path* — A grain bin splits in Superior. An honest crew's cold weld takes the blame, until a rain gauge record Northline tried to bury shows Pike passed it from his desk. The ramp's form has the same gap.
3. *Fatigue Limit* — An ore-dock conveyor drops in Two Harbors. Beach marks show a slow shaft crack, until Dutch reads chain scars: Lakehead's largest policyholder overloaded it. It costs Renee her job. Vance's original S-401 shows the change came from outside.
4. *Single Point of Failure* — A spillway gate tears loose at Tamarack Dam; a new client blames ice. Copied grease logs expose a contractor billing for work never done, and Northline's subpoenaed files confirm the name Elias already has.

*Running thread:* Who drew the late change to sheet S-401: Vance, Pike or the erector's detailer. Book 4 answers it.

**Why readers come back.** People who know their trades take a failure apart in plain language, and the reader learns to read steel. Every book closes its case and earns one fact about S-401. The violence is over before page one.

**What makes it different.** Three trades do the detecting; the clues are a bolt stamp, a fracture face, a rain gauge record. The lead was not framed: he signed the sheet. No hacker, no gunfight.
