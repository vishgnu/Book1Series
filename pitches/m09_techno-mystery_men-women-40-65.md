# Cause of Failure

**Genre:** Techno-mystery · **Readers:** Men and women 40–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Elias Brandt, 52, a structural engineer blamed for a parking ramp collapse, works from a rented bay on the Duluth waterfront telling insurers why things fell. In every book, the metal shows the accident had an author.

**The setting.** Duluth and the working shore of Lake Superior: ore docks, grain elevators, ski hills and small dams, most built before 1970 and kept up on town budgets through winters of 30 below. Brandt Forensic rents Bay 6 of a former ship chandlery with a ten-ton crane. The rule: no insurer pays until an engineer signs a cause, and the signature decides who pays.

**The lead.** Elias Brandt, 52, was a principal at Thorsby Vance in Minneapolis until the Carver Street ramp fell, killing two, and a sealed drawing made the detail he flagged look approved. It cost him his partnership and savings. He wants the original sheet. He trusts fracture surfaces over paperwork and talks in short sentences, units attached.

**The cast.** Naomi Adeyemi, 29, a metallurgist with a van-mounted lab and no patience. Dutch Kowalczyk, 68, a retired crane operator who knows how steel really goes up. Renee Halloran, claims manager at Lakehead Mutual, hires them and fights every invoice. Martin Vance, Elias's old partner, still takes his calls.

**The series engine.** Every book opens on a failure ruled an accident and a client who wants a signature. The team recovers the parts, tests them and rebuilds the sequence; the reader sees each bolt stamp, fracture face and inspection log as they do. The evidence names a person. Old structures keep failing, so cases keep coming.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Factor of Safety* — A ski-lift tower falls at Sawbill Ridge; forged grade stamps on its anchor bolts show Elias how approved paperwork gets swapped.
2. *Load Path* — A grain elevator bin splits in Superior; a weld inspection signed on a rain day names an inspector from Elias's ramp.
3. *Fatigue Limit* — An ore-dock conveyor drops in Two Harbors; beach marks on its shaft date the cracking, and a fabricator's files hold the ramp's original drawings.
4. *Single Point of Failure* — A spillway gate tears loose at Tamarack Dam; copied grease logs explain it, and Elias proves who revised the sealed sheet.

*Running thread:* Who altered sheet S-401 of the Carver Street ramp after sealing, and how; book 4 answers both.

**Why readers come back.** You watch a failure taken apart by people who know their trades, in language anyone can follow, and you hold the same evidence they do. Every book closes its case, no cliffhanger. The violence is over before page one; what remains is the proof.

**What makes it different.** The detectives are an engineer, a metallurgist and a crane operator, and the clues are physical: a bolt stamp, a fracture face, a rain gauge record. No hacker, no gunfight. The reader learns to read steel.
