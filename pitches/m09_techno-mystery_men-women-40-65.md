# Cause of Failure

**Genre:** Techno-mystery · **Readers:** Men and women 40–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Structural engineer Elias Brandt, 52, sealed a drawing for a ramp that fell and killed two. Now, in Duluth, he, a metallurgist and a retired crane operator tell insurers why things fell. Each time, the metal contradicts the paperwork.

**The setting.** Duluth and Lake Superior's working shore at 30 below: ore docks, grain elevators, ski lifts, dams. One firm, Northline Testing, inspected most of it, the ramp included. The rule: no insurer pays until an engineer signs a cause, and the signature decides who pays. Wreckage is opened only at joint inspections, every party's expert watching.

**The lead.** Elias Brandt warned in writing that a Carver Street ramp connection was unsafe, then sealed a late revision unchecked because his partner handed it over. Suspended a year, he wants to know who drew the change. After each case he writes to Ruth Sorenson, a victim's widow; she has not answered. He trusts fracture surfaces over paperwork and talks in short sentences, units attached.

**The cast.** Naomi Adeyemi, 29, metallurgist, quit Northline rather than retype a failed hardness result. Dutch Kowalczyk, 68, a retired crane operator who knows how steel really goes up, set the ramp's girders. Renee Halloran, Lakehead Mutual's claims manager, hires them and fights every invoice. Martin Vance, Elias's old partner and a suspect, checks his numbers for free.

**The series engine.** Every book opens on a failure ruled an accident, five parties who could owe and 60 days to sign. Every other local engineer takes Northline work, so Renee hires Elias. Each case has a plausible wrong answer and breaks on something one trade alone could read.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Factor of Safety* — A ski-lift tower falls. Naomi blames a retorque Northline skipped; a bolt stamp and cleavage faces show the owner bought off-grade anchors, brittle at 30 below. Dutch's log: the ramp went up from an unsigned print.
2. *Load Path* — A grain bin splits in Superior. The weld was laid cold; Elias must name welders who meant no harm. A rain gauge record shows Northline passed it from a desk, like the ramp.
3. *Fatigue Limit* — An ore-dock conveyor drops in Two Harbors. Beach marks show a slow shaft crack, until Dutch reads chain scars: Lakehead's largest policyholder overloaded it, voiding cover. Renee won't bury that and is fired. The S-401 print carries Vance's plot stamp.
4. *Single Point of Failure* — Tamarack Dam's spillway gate tears loose; Renee, now independent, brings the case. Grease logs show the gate contractor billed for work never done. Subpoenaed Northline files hold Gordon Pike's redline: the detailer drew S-401 from it; Vance only plotted it. No engineer but Elias ever saw it. Ruth answers.

*Running thread:* Who drew the late change to sheet S-401, an unsigned hand-marked print: Vance, Northline's inspector or the erector's detailer. Book 4 answers it.

**Why readers come back.** People who know their trades take a failure apart in plain language, and the reader learns to read steel. Every book closes its case and earns one fact about S-401.

**What makes it different.** Three trades do the detecting; the clues are a bolt stamp, a fracture face, a rain gauge record. The lead was not framed: he signed the sheet. No hacker, no gunfight.
