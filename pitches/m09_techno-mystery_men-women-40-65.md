# Cause of Failure: The Duluth Collapse Mysteries

**Genre:** Techno-mystery · **Readers:** Men and women 40–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Structural engineer Elias Brandt, 52, sealed the drawing for a ramp that killed two. Now he signs the cause of Duluth's fatal failures, and the metal keeps contradicting the paperwork.

**The setting.** Duluth and Lake Superior's working shore at 30 below: ore docks, grain elevators, ski lifts, dams. Northline Testing inspected most of it, the ramp included. After a fatal failure OSHA holds the scene and every party's expert watches each test. The rule: no insurer pays until an engineer signs a cause, and the signature decides who pays.

**The lead.** Elias warned in writing that a ramp connection was unsafe, then sealed a late revision to sheet S-401 unchecked: his partner Martin Vance called it a title-block change. Suspension served, license restored, he wants to know who changed it. He speaks in short sentences, units attached. After each case he writes to Ruth Sorenson, a victim's widow; the letters come back unopened.

**The cast.** Naomi Adeyemi, 29, metallurgist, quit Northline rather than retype a failed hardness result; no lab in town will hire her. Dutch Kowalczyk, 68, retired crane operator, set the ramp's girders. Renee Halloran, Lakehead Mutual's claims manager, four years from her pension, hires them and fights every invoice. Gordon Pike, Northline inspector, trained Elias.

**The series engine.** Each book opens on a fatal failure ruled an accident, five parties who could owe and 60 days to sign. Every other local engineer depends on Northline work and cannot testify against it. Elias, shut out since the ramp, can; Lakehead hires him, and every cross-examination opens with the ramp. The case has a plausible wrong answer and breaks on something one trade alone could read.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Cold Short* — A ski-lift tower kills a mechanic, Naomi reads a bolt stamp on off-grade anchors Northline passed, and an unsigned S-401 turns up in Elias's own office.
2. *Lack of Fusion* — A split grain bin buries a worker, a rain gauge shows the seam was welded wet and passed from a Northline desk, and S-401 carries Vance's plot stamp.
3. *Beach Marks* — An ore-dock conveyor kills two dockhands, Dutch reads chain scars that reveal a concealed overload, Renee is fired for reporting it, and the handwriting on S-401 is Pike's.
4. *Fracture Critical* — A spillway gate drowns a fisherman, grease logs expose faked maintenance, Elias reopens his own discipline file to prove Vance ordered Pike's change, and Ruth answers.

*Running thread:* Who changed sheet S-401, and what the answer costs Elias's restored license.

**Why readers come back.** People who know their trades take a failure apart in plain language, and the reader learns to read steel. Each book closes its case in full and moves the S-401 question one step closer to Elias. Book 4 answers it and leaves the three of them in business.

**What makes it different.** The lead was not framed: he signed the sheet. Three trades do the detecting, and the evidence is something you can hold: a bolt stamp, a fracture face, a rain gauge. No hacker, no gunfight.
