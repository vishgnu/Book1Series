# Cause of Failure

**Genre:** Techno-mystery · **Readers:** Men and women 40–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Structural engineer Elias Brandt, 52, sealed a drawing that killed two. Now, in Duluth, he, a metallurgist and a retired crane operator tell insurers why things fell. Each time, the metal shows the accident had an author.

**The setting.** Duluth and Lake Superior's working shore at 30 below: ore docks, grain elevators, ski lifts, dams, most built before 1970 on town budgets. One firm, Northline Testing, inspected most of it. Brandt Forensic rents Bay 6 of an old chandlery, ten-ton crane overhead. The rule: no insurer pays until an engineer signs a cause, and the signature decides who pays.

**The lead.** Elias Brandt warned in writing that a Carver Street ramp connection was unsafe, then sealed a late revision unchecked because his partner handed it over. Suspended a year, he wants to know who drew the change. After each case he writes to Ruth Sorenson, a victim's widow; she has not answered. He trusts fracture surfaces over paperwork and talks in short sentences, units attached.

**The cast.** Naomi Adeyemi, 29, quit Northline's lab rather than retype a failed hardness result and wants Northline shut. Dutch Kowalczyk, 68, a retired crane operator who knows how steel really goes up, set the ramp's girders. Renee Halloran, Lakehead Mutual's claims manager, paid the ramp claim and fights every invoice. Martin Vance, Elias's old partner and a suspect, checks his numbers for free.

**The series engine.** Every book opens on a failure ruled an accident and a client who wants that signed in 60 days. Lakehead insures most of what Northline inspected; only Elias will sign against it. Each case has a plausible wrong answer and breaks on something one trade alone could read.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Factor of Safety* — A ski-lift tower falls at Sawbill Ridge. Naomi finds off-grade anchor bolts, blaming the owner, until Dutch shows a retorque Northline signed was never done. The signer, Gordon Pike, who trained Elias, also passed the ramp.
2. *Load Path* — A grain bin splits in Superior. The cold weld is real and Elias must name men who meant no harm; a rain gauge record shows Pike, short-staffed, passed it from his desk. The ramp's form has the same gap.
3. *Fatigue Limit* — An ore-dock conveyor drops in Two Harbors. Beach marks show a slow shaft crack, until Dutch reads chain scars: Lakehead's largest policyholder overloaded it. Renee backs the finding and loses her job. The S-401 revision carries Vance's plot stamp.
4. *Single Point of Failure* — Tamarack Dam's spillway gate tears loose; Renee, now independent, brings the case. Grease logs expose billing for work never done. On day 60 Elias holds his seal until Vance's numbers are checked. Northline's files show the detailer drew S-401 from Pike's redline. Ruth answers.

*Running thread:* Who drew the late change to sheet S-401: Vance, Pike or the erector's detailer. Book 4 answers it.

**Why readers come back.** People who know their trades take a failure apart in plain language, and the reader learns to read steel. Every book closes its case and earns one fact about S-401.

**What makes it different.** Three trades do the detecting; the clues are a bolt stamp, a fracture face, a rain gauge record. The lead was not framed: he signed the sheet. No hacker, no gunfight.
