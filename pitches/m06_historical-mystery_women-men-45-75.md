# The Hazel Novak Radio Mysteries

**Genre:** Historical mystery · **Readers:** Women and men 45–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Cincinnati, 1947: Hazel Novak, 31, the only woman on sound effects at station WQC, solves murders by ear, in the year tape arrives and a voice on the air stops proving anyone was there.

**The setting.** WQC fills the Alder Building's ninth floor, streetcars grinding below. Dramas go out live, so a voice on the air proves where a person stood. Then a German tape machine goes into service, and a voice can be cut, moved and aired as if live.

**The lead.** Hazel got the sound table in 1942 when the men left, and kept it. She can splice tape herself. She wants to be believed: when her teacher Gus Lindqvist died in a fall, she told the coroner what she heard; he wrote "accident." She talks dry: "That was a door. It wasn't mine."

**The cast.** Her deaf father Anton, a streetcar motorman, reads her lips over supper and knows when she lies. Dot Feeney times every show and keeps no secret past lunch. Ray Hauser, police dispatcher, courts her slowly from Book 2. Sergeant Frank Kessler, half deaf since Anzio, goes from doubting her to asking for her.

**The series engine.** Each murder turns on one wrong sound, printed as Hazel hears it, never meaning the same thing twice. Her ear opens a case; proof a jury can hold closes it. After Book 1 people bring her what they cannot explain. Every cover: a studio microphone and that book's sound.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Second Door on the Air* — An actor was on air when his wife died, but the recording holds two door slams and Hazel made one; Kessler suspects her.
2. *The Bell on the Dead Line* — An actress found dead Monday speaks on Tuesday's ransom call, over the bell of a streetcar line closed since spring; Hazel's father knows the car.
3. *The Gravel on the Wire* — A factory watchman dies with his telephone off the hook; the operator heard gravel, his yard is brick, and Kessler lets Hazel listen.
4. *The Clock in Studio A* — An announcer's recorded farewell is called a forgery after he dies; Hazel proves it real, and Kessler asks for her by name.

*Running thread:* Gus faked the sound of a banker's fatal crash so the man could flee with the bank's money; then Gus died. Books 1–3 turn up his reel, the banker's wife, and a second man in the studio. Book 4 answers whether he was a crook and whether he was pushed.

**Why readers come back.** Each book is a complete puzzle, every clue on the page before Hazel names it. Each settles one question about Gus and raises the next. Tape is explained once; the people matter more. Deaths stay offstage, and Hazel is right more often than she is heard.

**What makes it different.** A detective whose evidence is sound, written so the reader hears it too. And she could have faked every clue.
