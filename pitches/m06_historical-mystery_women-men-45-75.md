# As If Live: The Sound-Effects Mysteries

**Genre:** Historical mystery · **Readers:** Women and men 45–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Cincinnati, 1947: Hazel Novak, 31, the only woman making sound effects at radio station WQC, solves murders by ear, in the year tape arrives and a voice on the air stops proving anyone was there.

**The setting.** WQC fills the ninth floor of the Alder Building, streetcars grinding below. Its dramas go out live, so a voice on the air proves where a person stood. Then chief engineer Walter Pruitt, who taught Hazel to splice, uncrates a German tape machine, and a voice can be cut, moved and aired as if live.

**The lead.** Hazel got the sound table in 1942 when the men left and kept it. She can splice, so every forgery she exposes is one she could have made. She wants to be believed: when her teacher Gus Lindqvist died in a fall, she told the coroner what she heard; he wrote "accident." Her deaf father Anton, a streetcar motorman, reads her lips and knows when she lies. She talks dry: "That was a door. It wasn't mine."

**The cast.** Dot Feeney, her friend, keeps the stopwatch sheets and her confidences. Ray Hauser, police dispatcher, is only a voice on her telephone until Book 2. Sergeant Frank Kessler, half deaf since Anzio, distrusts what he cannot hear.

**The series engine.** Each book centers a murder on one wrong sound, printed as Hazel hears it, and the sound never means the same thing twice. After Book 1 people bring her what they cannot explain, and her own effects keep turning up in evidence.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Second Door* — An actor was on the air when his wife died across town; the air check holds two door slams and Hazel made one. Kessler suspects her.
2. *The Wrong Bell* — An actress found dead Monday speaks on Tuesday's ransom call, over the bell of a streetcar line closed since spring. Police say tape; Hazel hears a live bell, and her father knows the car.
3. *Footsteps on Gravel* — A Dunmore Soap watchman dies, telephone off the hook; the operator heard gravel and his yard is brick. The gravel is from Hazel's box; Kessler lets her listen.
4. *The Clock in Studio A* — Announcer Harlan Tice, the second man in Gus's studio, dies, his recorded farewell called a forgery. Hazel proves it uncut: the Studio A clock ticks under every second. Kessler asks for her by name.

*Running thread:* Gus left forty seconds of tape: a car going into water, built at her table. Books 1–3 turn up the reel, the banker's wife it fooled, and a second man. In Book 4 Hazel, once sure no one she loved could fool her ear, testifies to what he staged and whether he fell.

**Why readers come back.** Each book is a complete puzzle to solve by listening along; every clue is on the page before Hazel names it. You get 1947 radio from the studio floor, a Northside kitchen, deaths kept offstage, and a woman right more often than she is heard.

**What makes it different.** A detective whose evidence is sound, written so the reader hears it too. And she could have faked every clue.
