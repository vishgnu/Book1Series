# The Sound-Effects Girl

**Genre:** Historical mystery · **Readers:** Women and men 45–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Cincinnati, 1947: Hazel Novak, 31, the only woman making sound effects at station WQC, solves each case by ear, in the year magnetic tape arrives and a voice on the air stops proving anyone was there.

**The setting.** WQC fills the ninth floor of Cincinnati's Alder Building: a gravel box and a wheeled door frame in Studio A, streetcars grinding below. Its dramas go out live, and the city takes a live voice as proof of who stood where. Then the chief engineer uncrates a German tape machine, and a voice can be cut, moved and aired as if live.

**The lead.** Hazel Novak, 31, got the sound table in 1942 when the men left and has not given it back. She wants to keep it and be believed; each case costs her standing with whoever signs her pay. She can tell a .38 from a backfire, crepe soles from leather, linoleum from pine. She talks dry and exact.

**The cast.** Walter Pruitt, 54, chief engineer, shipped the tape machine home and trusts it less than he loves it. Police Sergeant Joe Hauser, 57, her uncle, half-listens. Leland Voss, 40, Dunmore Soap's sponsor man, reads every script. Dot Feeney, 23, script girl, tracks every actor's lies.

**The series engine.** Every book puts one sound at the center of a crime and prints it as Hazel hears it, so the reader can solve the case from the page. Cases keep arriving because a station touches actors, advertisers, politicians and police, and because a razor blade can now forge a live broadcast.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Second Door* — An actor dies mid-broadcast; the air check holds two door slams and Hazel made one. Pruitt plays her reel one.
2. *The Wrong Bell* — A ransom call for Dunmore's bookkeeper carries the bell of a streetcar line closed since spring; her uncle starts listening.
3. *Footsteps on Gravel* — A watchman dies, telephone off the hook; the operator heard gravel and his yard is brick. Hazel's night shows go to tape.
4. *One Splice Too Many* — A missing announcer's taped farewell has one cut too many; so does reel one, and the second voice gets a name.

*Running thread:* Reel one, recorded by accident in rehearsal, holds forty seconds of Gus Lindqvist, Hazel's teacher, dead of a fall three weeks later, settling a price with a voice too far from the microphone to name.

**Why readers come back.** Each book is a complete puzzle you can solve by listening along; every clue is on the page before Hazel names it. You get 1947 radio from the studio floor, deaths kept offstage, and a woman right more often than she is heard.

**What makes it different.** A detective whose evidence is sound, written so the reader hears it too, in the year radio's oldest alibi, being on the air, stopped holding. The clues are doors, bells, gravel and razor cuts.
