# The Hazel Novak Radio Mysteries

**Genre:** Historical mystery · **Readers:** Women and men 45–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Cincinnati, 1947: Hazel Novak, 31, the only woman on sound effects at station WQC, solves murders by ear the year tape arrives and a voice on the air stops proving anyone was there.

**The setting.** WQC fills the Alder Building's ninth floor, above streetcars grinding from the river toward the hills. Soap money pays for live dramas, so a voice on the air proves where a person stood. Then one captured German tape machine arrives, and a voice can be cut, moved and aired as if live.

**The lead.** Hazel got the sound table in 1942 when the men left, and kept it. She distrusts actors, paid to sound sincere. She wants to be believed: when her teacher Gus Lindqvist died, she told the coroner what she heard; he wrote "accident." Now she writes down every sound she cannot place. She talks dry: "That was a door. It wasn't mine."

**The cast.** Her deaf father Anton, a streetcar motorman, reads her face better than her lips across the Northside kitchen table and knows when she lies. Dot Feeney wants to direct; her stopwatch sheets break alibis. Ray Hauser, police dispatcher, courts her slowly. Sergeant Frank Kessler, deaf in his left ear since Anzio, doubts her, then asks for her by name.

**The series engine.** Each murder turns on one wrong sound, printed as Hazel hears it, never meaning the same thing twice. Her ear opens a case; proof a jury can hold closes it. After Book 1, people bring her the unexplained. Covers: a studio microphone and that book's sound.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Second Door* — An actor was on air when his wife died, but the station's disc of the broadcast holds two door slams and Hazel made one; Kessler suspects her.
2. *The Wrong Bell* — An actress found dead Monday speaks on Tuesday's ransom call, over the bell of a streetcar line closed since spring; Hazel's father knows the car.
3. *Footsteps on Gravel* — A machine-tool plant watchman dies, telephone off the hook; the operator heard footsteps on gravel, his yard is brick, and Kessler lets Hazel listen.
4. *The Clock in Studio A* — An announcer dies after recording the name of the man who was with Gus; it is called a forgery, until Hazel hears the studio clock ticking unbroken beneath it.

*Running thread:* In 1946 Gus faked a car crash in sound so a banker's wife would swear she heard her husband die; the banker fled with the money; Gus fell down the station stairs. Book 4 answers: was he a crook, and was he pushed?

**Why readers come back.** Every clue is on the page before Hazel names it. Each book settles one question about Gus and raises the next. Deaths stay offstage, and Hazel is right more often than she is heard.

**What makes it different.** A detective whose evidence is sound, written so the reader hears it too. And she could have faked every clue.
