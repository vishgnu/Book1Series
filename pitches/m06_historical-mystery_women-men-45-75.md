# As If Live: The Sound-Effects Mysteries

**Genre:** Historical mystery · **Readers:** Women and men 45–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Cincinnati, 1947: Hazel Novak, 31, the only woman making sound effects at radio station WQC, solves murders by ear, in the year tape arrives and a voice on the air stops proving anyone was there.

**The setting.** WQC fills the ninth floor of the Alder Building. Its dramas go out live, so a voice on the air proves where a person stood. Then the chief engineer uncrates a German tape machine, and a voice can be cut, moved and aired as if live.

**The lead.** Hazel got the sound table in 1942 when the men left and kept it. She can tell a .38 from a backfire, linoleum from pine. She wants to be believed; she can splice, so every forgery she exposes is one she could have made. She lives with her deaf father, a streetcar motorman, and talks dry: "That was a door. It wasn't mine."

**The cast.** Walter Pruitt, chief engineer, loves the machine that could replace her. Ray Hauser, police radio dispatcher, trades squad-car logs for her ear. Frank Kessler, homicide sergeant, suspects her first. Leland Voss, Dunmore Soap's sponsor man, can cancel any show she embarrasses.

**The series engine.** Each book centers a murder on one wrong sound, printed as Hazel hears it, and the sound never means the same thing twice. Cases arrive because a razor blade can now forge a live broadcast; her own effects keep turning up in evidence, so she must clear herself.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Second Door* — An actor was on the air when his wife died across town; the air check holds two door slams and Hazel made one. Kessler suspects her.
2. *The Wrong Bell* — An actress found dead Monday speaks on Tuesday's ransom call, over the bell of a streetcar line closed since spring. Police blame the ad agency's new tape machine; Hazel hears a live bell.
3. *Footsteps on Gravel* — A Dunmore watchman dies, telephone off the hook; the operator heard gravel and his yard is brick. The gravel is Hazel's own, and Voss pulls her show.
4. *The Clock in Studio A* — Announcer Harlan Tice, the second man, is found dead, his recorded farewell called a forgery. Hazel proves it uncut: the Studio A clock ticks under every second. Kessler asks for her by name.

*Running thread:* Hazel's teacher Gus Lindqvist died in a fall and left forty seconds of tape: a car going into water, built at her table. Books 1–3 turn up the reel, the banker's wife it was played to, and a second man in the studio. Book 4 says what Gus staged, for whom, and whether he fell.

**Why readers come back.** Each book is a complete puzzle to solve by listening along; every clue is on the page before Hazel names it. You get 1947 radio from the studio floor, deaths kept offstage, and a woman right more often than she is heard.

**What makes it different.** A detective whose evidence is sound, written so the reader hears it too. And she could have faked every clue.
