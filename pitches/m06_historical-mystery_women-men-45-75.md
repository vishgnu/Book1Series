# As If Live: The Sound-Effects Mysteries

**Genre:** Historical mystery · **Readers:** Women and men 45–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Cincinnati, 1947: Hazel Novak, 31, the only woman making sound effects at radio station WQC, solves murders by ear, in the year tape arrives and a voice on the air stops proving anyone was there.

**The setting.** WQC fills the ninth floor of Cincinnati's Alder Building: a gravel box and a wheeled door frame in Studio A, streetcars grinding below. Its dramas go out live, so the city takes a voice on the air as proof of where a person stood. Then the chief engineer uncrates a German tape machine, and a voice can be cut, moved and aired as if live.

**The lead.** Hazel Novak, 31, got the sound table in 1942 when the men left and has not given it back. She can tell a .38 from a backfire, linoleum from pine. She wants to be believed; she can splice, so every forgery she exposes is one she could have made. She lives in Northside with her deaf father, a streetcar motorman, and talks dry: "That was a door. It wasn't mine."

**The cast.** Walter Pruitt, 54, chief engineer, trusts the tape machine less than he loves it. Ray Hauser, 57, police radio dispatcher, trades squad-car logs for her ear. Leland Voss, 40, Dunmore Soap's sponsor man, can cancel any show she embarrasses. Dot Feeney, 23, script girl, keeps the stopwatch sheets.

**The series engine.** Every book puts one wrong sound at the center of a murder and prints it as Hazel hears it, so the reader can solve the case from the page. Cases keep arriving because a station touches actors, advertisers, politicians and police, and a razor blade can now forge a live broadcast.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Second Door* — An actor was on the air when his wife died across town; the air check holds two door slams and Hazel made one. His scene was a reel.
2. *The Wrong Bell* — A dead actress speaks on her own ransom call, over the bell of a streetcar line closed since spring; her words were cut from old broadcasts.
3. *Footsteps on Gravel* — A watchman dies, telephone off the hook; the operator heard gravel and his yard is brick. It is Hazel's own gravel, dubbed from a station disc.
4. *One Splice Too Many* — A missing announcer's taped farewell has one cut too many and no Studio A clock behind it; that clock also dates Gus's reel.

*Running thread:* Hazel's teacher Gus Lindqvist died in a fall and left forty seconds of tape: a car going into water, built at her table. A banker's wife heard it by telephone the night he vanished. Book 4 says what Gus staged, for whom, and who was paying whom.

**Why readers come back.** Each book is a complete puzzle you can solve by listening along; every clue is on the page before Hazel names it. You get 1947 radio from the studio floor, deaths kept offstage, and a woman right more often than she is heard.

**What makes it different.** A detective whose evidence is sound, written so the reader hears it too, in the year radio's oldest alibi, being on the air, stopped holding. She is also the one person in Cincinnati who could have faked every clue.
