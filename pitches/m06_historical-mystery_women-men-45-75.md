# The Hazel Novak Radio Mysteries

**Genre:** Historical mystery · **Readers:** Women and men 45–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Cincinnati, 1947: sound-effects woman Hazel Novak, 31, solves murders by ear, just as tape arrives and a voice on the air stops being an alibi.

**The setting.** WQC fills the Alder Building's ninth floor. Soap money pays for live dramas, so whoever is heard on the air was in Studio A, under its clock. Then the station's engineer, back from the Signal Corps, brings home one captured German tape machine, and a voice can be cut, moved and aired as if live.

**The lead.** Hazel got the sound table in 1942 when the men left, and kept it. She wants to be believed: when her teacher Gus Lindqvist died, the coroner wrote "accident." Now she writes down every sound she cannot place, and she can splice tape herself. She talks dry: "That was a door. It wasn't mine."

**The cast.** Her deaf father Anton, a streetcar motorman, reads her lips over supper and knows when she lies. Ginny Feeney keeps no secret past lunch; her stopwatch sheets break alibis. Ray Hauser, police dispatcher, hears every call in the city and courts Hazel slowly. Sergeant Frank Kessler wants evidence he can bag; he doubts her, then asks for her by name.

**The series engine.** Each murder turns on one wrong sound, never meaning the same thing twice. Her ear opens a case; proof a jury can hold closes it. After Book 1, strangers bring her sounds they cannot explain.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Second Door* — An actor was on air when his wife died, but the recording holds two door slams and Hazel made one; cleared, she finds Gus's last reel: the crash was faked.
2. *The Wrong Bell* — An actress dead Monday speaks on Tuesday's ransom call, over the bell of a streetcar line closed since spring; Anton knows the car, and the banker's wife admits she lied.
3. *Footsteps on Gravel* — A plant watchman dies, telephone off the hook; the operator heard footsteps on gravel, the plant yard is brick, and Hazel learns Gus was not alone on the stairs.
4. *The Clock in Studio A* — An announcer dies after recording who was with Gus; Hazel is accused of splicing the tape until she hears the studio clock ticking unbroken, proof it was never cut.

*Running thread:* In 1946 Gus faked a car crash in sound for a banker, whose wife swore she heard him die while he fled with the money. Then Gus fell down the station stairs, and Hazel heard a door close above him. Book 4 answers whether he was a crook and whether he was pushed.

**Why readers come back.** Every clue is on the page before Hazel names it. Each book settles one question about Gus and raises the next. Deaths stay offstage, and Hazel is right more often than she is heard.

**What makes it different.** A detective whose evidence is sound, written so the reader hears it too. And she could have faked every clue.
