# The Sound-Effects Girl

**Genre:** Historical mystery · **Readers:** Women and men 45–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Cincinnati, 1947: Hazel Novak, 31, the only woman making sound effects at radio station WQC, solves murders by ear, in the year tape recording arrives and a killer can be heard live on the air while standing somewhere else.

**The setting.** WQC fills the ninth floor of Cincinnati's Alder Building: a gravel box and a wheeled door frame in Studio A, streetcars grinding below. Its dramas go out live, so the city takes a voice on the air as proof of where a person stood. Then the chief engineer uncrates a German tape machine, and a voice can be cut, moved and aired as if live.

**The lead.** Hazel Novak, 31, war widow, got the sound table in 1942 when the men left and has not given it back. Gus Lindqvist, who taught her the trade, died in a fall. She wants to keep the table and be believed; each case costs her standing with whoever signs her pay. She can tell a .38 from a backfire, crepe soles from leather. She talks dry: "That was a door. It wasn't mine."

**The cast.** Walter Pruitt, 54, chief engineer, shipped the tape machine home; it could replace her. Police Sergeant Joe Hauser, 57, her uncle, got her the job and wants her quiet in it. Leland Voss, 40, Dunmore Soap's sponsor man, can cancel any show she embarrasses. Dot Feeney, 23, script girl, tracks every actor's lies.

**The series engine.** Every book puts one wrong sound at the center of a murder and prints it as Hazel hears it, so the reader can solve the case from the page. Cases keep arriving because a station touches actors, advertisers, politicians and police, and a razor blade can now forge a live broadcast.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Second Door* — An actor dies mid-broadcast; the air check holds two door slams and Hazel made one. Pruitt plays her a rehearsal reel with Gus on it.
2. *The Wrong Bell* — The ransom call for Dunmore's dead bookkeeper carries the bell of a streetcar line closed since spring. Hazel places the hum on Gus's reel: the Alder's freight elevator.
3. *Footsteps on Gravel* — A watchman dies, telephone off the hook; the operator heard gravel and his yard is brick. A studio log dates Gus's reel and puts Voss in Chicago.
4. *One Splice Too Many* — A missing announcer's taped farewell has one cut too many; so does Gus's reel, and the second voice gets a name.

*Running thread:* Three weeks before his fatal fall, a rehearsal tape caught Gus arguing over money with a man too far from the microphone to name. Book 4 names him and settles whether Gus fell.

**Why readers come back.** Each book is a complete puzzle you can solve by listening along; every clue is on the page before Hazel names it. You get 1947 radio from the studio floor, deaths kept offstage, and a woman right more often than she is heard.

**What makes it different.** A detective whose evidence is sound, written so the reader hears it too, in the year being on the air stopped working as an alibi. The clues are doors, bells, gravel and razor cuts.
