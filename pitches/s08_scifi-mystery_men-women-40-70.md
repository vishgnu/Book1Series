# The Century Unit

**Genre:** Science fiction mystery · **Readers:** Men and women 40–70 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** In Port Sable, bodies no longer age but memory holds only seventy years. Inspector Dessa Varga, 154, and archivist Midge Pell, 131, reopen one century-old homicide per book. Every witness is alive and misremembers.

**The setting.** Port Sable in 2157 is a harbor city of diary notaries, thirty-year marriages and forged keepsakes. Since 2049 the Forfeit renews the body and forfeits the past: memory reaches back seventy years. Courts refuse older testimony, so the Century Unit trusts paper, cloth, bone and dead devices. A homicide conviction means forty years confined, over half the memory a person keeps.

**The lead.** Inspector Dessa Varga, 154, runs the Unit. She wants to find Corin Hale, the sergeant who trained her and, as she tells it, walked out on her first homicide; his pencil corrections still mark her oldest notebooks. Each morning she rereads entries in her own hand she cannot believe. She asks for the receipt before the story, in short, dry questions, then waits.

**The cast.** Midge Pell, 131, revives dead formats and calls Varga's notebooks one more account. Ilan Okafor, 61, the Unit's baby, whose memory is still whole, dreads his first forgetting, nine years off. Anselm Roux, 160, pathologist, cannot tell his grief from retelling. Tobias Quill, 140, defense advocate, argues that a client with no memory of the act is not the one who did it, and believes it.

**The series engine.** Every book opens with an object surfacing from demolition or dredging. Pell must prove it is what it claims before it decays. Witnesses are certain and wrong, so Varga reads how each story drifted and who gained; Pell tests it and Quill attacks it. No suspect ever dies, so no case closes itself.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Larch Street Doorbell* — A doorbell camera bricked into a wall reopens Ines Marlow's 2041 death; her widower has remarried four times, and her daughter, 121, tells the story that cleared him.
2. *The Judge's Raincoat* — A fading thermal ferry ticket from 2026, in an auctioned raincoat's lining, places Judge Halvard Stroud beside a drowned clerk Roux once loved. The old report is signed C. Hale; Varga fights it until Okafor overrules her.
3. *The Clinic Queue* — A clinic ledger reopens the killing of nurse Ona Ferrant in the 2052 Forfeit queue; survivors tell one identical heroic story, and the order of names shows who stood behind her. Hale is listed; Varga hides it from Pell.
4. *The Constable in 2030* — A dredged, locked phone reopens Varga's first homicide; Okafor leads and Varga sits as a witness, her notebooks authenticated against her by Pell. The messages show she sent Hale away and who altered his Stroud report.

*Running thread:* Why Hale left in 2030, and whether Varga must answer for an act beyond her memory; answered in book 4, where she takes her own test.

**Why readers come back.** A complete, fair mystery in every book, and nobody is hurt on the page. Readers return for Varga and Pell arguing over proof, and for witnesses a century older than their alibis, met with patience, not contempt.

**What makes it different.** Cold cases where time has removed nobody: every suspect sits for interview, the one reliable witness is a dead phone, and the detective's own memory is on trial.
