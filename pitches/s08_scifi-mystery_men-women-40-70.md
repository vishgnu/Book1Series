# The Century Unit

**Genre:** Science fiction mystery · **Readers:** Men and women 40–70 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** In Port Sable in 2157, bodies no longer age but memory holds only seventy years. Inspector Dessa Varga, 154, and archivist Midge Pell reopen one century-old homicide per book. Every witness is alive and remembers only the retelling.

**The setting.** Port Sable is a harbor city behind tide gates, where freighters dock under kite sails and diary notaries stamp each year's journal. Since 2049 the Forfeit renews the body and forfeits the past: nobody remembers anything before 2087, and every case is older. Courts refuse older testimony, so the Century Unit trusts paper, cloth, bone and dead devices. A homicide conviction means forty years confined.

**The lead.** Inspector Dessa Varga, 154, runs the Unit. She wants to find Corin Hale, the sergeant who trained her at 27; she knows him only by pencil corrections in her oldest notebooks, which say he walked out on her first homicide. She asks for the receipt before the story, in short, dry questions, then waits.

**The cast.** Midge Pell revives dead formats and calls Varga's notebooks one more account. Ilan Okafor, 61, remembers everything and dreads his first forgetting, nine years off. Tobias Quill, defense advocate, argues that a client with no memory of the act is not the one who did it, and believes it.

**The series engine.** Every book opens with a twenty-first-century object surfacing from a wall, an auction or harbor mud. A marriage or an inheritance still rests on the old story, so Varga reads how it drifted and who gained. No suspect ever dies, so no case closes itself. Every cover shows that object against the 2157 skyline, subtitled *A Century Unit Cold Case*.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Larch Street Doorbell* — A doorbell camera mortared into a wall breaks a widower's 2041 alibi; a pencil note in the old file contradicts Varga's own notebook.
2. *The Judge's Raincoat* — A ferry ticket in a raincoat lining places a judge beside a clerk drowned in 2026; Hale signed the report, and one page was replaced.
3. *The Clinic Queue* — A clinic ledger reopens a nurse's killing in an early Forfeit queue; the order of names shows who stood behind her, and lists Hale.
4. *The Sealed Tin* — A phone in a sealed tin reopens Varga's first homicide; Okafor leads and Varga sits as a witness, her notebooks authenticated against her by Pell.

*Running thread:* Whether Varga drove out the man who trained her; each book adds one document, and book 4 answers it and finds Hale alive.

**Why readers come back.** A complete, fair mystery in every book, and nobody is hurt on the page. Readers return for Varga and Pell arguing over proof, and for witnesses a century older than their alibis, met with patience, not contempt. Each closed case adds one page to the Hale file, the reason to borrow the next.

**What makes it different.** Cold cases where time has removed nobody: the one reliable witness is a dead phone from our own century, and the detective's own memory is on trial.
