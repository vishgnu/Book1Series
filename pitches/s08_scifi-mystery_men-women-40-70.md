# The Century Unit

**Genre:** Science fiction mystery · **Readers:** Men and women 40–70 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** In Port Sable, bodies no longer age but memory holds only seventy years. Inspector Dessa Varga, 154, and archivist Midge Pell, 131, reopen one century-old homicide per book. Every witness is alive and misremembers.

**The setting.** Port Sable in 2157 is a harbor city of diary notaries, family story-keepers and forged keepsakes. The Forfeit, since 2049, renews the body but not the mind: clear memory reaches back seventy years, and anything older survives only as a story retold. So courts refuse older testimony, and the Century Unit trusts paper, cloth, bone and dead devices, each read in open court. A homicide conviction means forty years confined, over half the memory a person keeps.

**The lead.** Inspector Dessa Varga, 154, runs the Unit. She wants to find Corin Hale, the partner who, as she tells it, walked out on her first homicide. Her notebooks hold entries in her own hand she cannot believe. She asks for the receipt before the story, in short, dry questions, then waits.

**The cast.** Midge Pell, 131, archivist, who revives dead formats and hoards chargers. Sergeant Ilan Okafor, 61, the Unit's baby, whose memory is still whole. Anselm Roux, 160, pathologist, who knew some victims alive. Tobias Quill, 140, defense advocate, who argues that a client with no memory of the act is not the one who did it.

**The series engine.** Every book opens with an object surfacing: a letter in a wall, a coat at auction, a phone in harbor mud. Pell must prove it is what it claims before it decays. Witnesses are certain and wrong, so Varga reads how each story has drifted and who gained, and tells Pell what to test; the result must survive Quill in court. No suspect ever dies, so no case closes itself.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Larch Street Doorbell* — A doorbell camera with an unreadable memory card, bricked into a wall, reopens Ines Marlow's 2041 death; her widower has remarried four times, and her daughter, 121, learns which family story is true.
2. *The Judge's Raincoat* — A fading thermal ferry ticket from 2026, in an auctioned raincoat's lining, places Judge Halvard Stroud beside a drowned clerk; the old report is signed C. Hale.
3. *The Clinic Queue* — Wren Ferrant, 112, brings a letter about her mother, a nurse killed in the 2052 Forfeit queue; a clinic ledger in dead software shows Hale alive that year.
4. *The Constable in 2030* — A dredged, locked phone reopens Varga's first homicide; Okafor leads and Varga sits as a witness, her notebooks authenticated against her. The messages show she sent Hale away, where he went, and a name missing from the Stroud verdict.

*Running thread:* Why Hale left in 2030, and whether Varga must answer for an act beyond her memory; answered in book 4.

**Why readers come back.** A complete, fair mystery in every book: the clues are on the table and nobody is hurt on the page. Readers return for witnesses a century older than their alibis, met with patience, not contempt.

**What makes it different.** Cold cases where time has removed nobody: suspect, widower and victim's child all sit for interview, yet the one reliable witness is a dead phone, and the detective's own memory is on trial.
