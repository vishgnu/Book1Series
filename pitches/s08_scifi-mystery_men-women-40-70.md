# The Century Unit

**Genre:** Science fiction mystery · **Readers:** Men and women 40–70 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** In Port Sable, bodies no longer age but memory holds only seventy years. Inspector Dessa Varga, 154, and archivist Midge Pell, 131, reopen one century-old homicide per book. Every witness is alive and remembers only the retelling.

**The setting.** Port Sable in 2157 is a harbor city of diary notaries, thirty-year marriages and forged keepsakes. Since 2049 the Forfeit renews the body and forfeits the past: memory reaches back seventy years. Courts refuse older testimony, so the Century Unit trusts paper, cloth, bone and dead devices. A homicide conviction means forty years confined.

**The lead.** Inspector Dessa Varga, 154, runs the Unit. She wants to find Corin Hale, the sergeant who trained her at 27; she knows him only by pencil corrections in her oldest notebooks, which say he walked out on her first homicide. She asks for the receipt before the story, in short, dry questions, then waits.

**The cast.** Midge Pell, 131, revives dead formats and calls Varga's notebooks one more account. Ilan Okafor, 61, the Unit's baby, still remembers everything and is teased for it. Anselm Roux, 160, pathologist, cannot tell his grief from retelling. Tobias Quill, 140, defense advocate, argues that a client with no memory of the act is not the one who did it.

**The series engine.** Every book opens with an object surfacing from a wall, an auction or harbor mud. Pell must prove it is what it claims before it decays. A marriage or an inheritance still rests on the old story, so Varga reads how it drifted and who gained; Pell tests it and Quill attacks it. No suspect ever dies, so no case closes itself.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Larch Street Doorbell* — A doorbell camera mortared into a wall reopens Ines Marlow's 2041 death. Her widower is on his fifth wedding; her daughter, 121, cleared him at five. The camera's clock puts him on the step an hour earlier.
2. *The Judge's Raincoat* — A punched 2026 ferry ticket in a raincoat lining places Judge Halvard Stroud beside a drowned clerk Roux once loved. The report is signed C. Hale, one page replaced; Varga trusts it until Okafor shows her.
3. *The Clinic Queue* — A clinic ledger reopens the killing of nurse Ona Ferrant in the 2052 Forfeit queue; survivors tell one identical story, and the order of names shows who stood behind her. Hale is listed; Varga hides it.
4. *The Constable in 2030* — A phone dredged up in a sealed tin reopens Varga's first homicide; Okafor leads and Varga sits as a witness, her notebooks authenticated against her by Pell. The messages show she sent Hale away, and who replaced his page.

*Running thread:* Whether Varga drove Hale out in 2030 to bury his Stroud report; answered in book 4, where Hale is found alive.

**Why readers come back.** A complete, fair mystery in every book, and nobody is hurt on the page. Readers return for Varga and Pell arguing over proof, and for witnesses a century older than their alibis, met with patience, not contempt.

**What makes it different.** Cold cases where time has removed nobody: the one reliable witness is a dead phone, and the detective's own memory is on trial.
