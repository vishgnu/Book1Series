# The Century Unit

**Genre:** Science fiction mystery · **Readers:** Men and women 40–70 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** In Port Sable in 2157, where nobody has died of old age for 108 years and murder has no time limit, Inspector Dessa Varga, 154, reopens one century-old homicide per book. Every witness is alive and misremembers.

**The setting.** Port Sable is a harbor city of fog, ferries and brick row houses behind a sea wall. The Maintenance, which arrived in 2049, renews the body but not the mind: clear memory reaches back seventy years, and anything older survives only as a story retold. So the Century Unit, in the Customs House basement, trusts paper, cloth, bone and dead devices.

**The lead.** Inspector Dessa Varga, 154, was a 27-year-old constable in 2030. She wants to find Corin Hale, the partner who, as she tells it, walked out on her first homicide. Every case shows how far her memory has drifted from her notebooks. She asks for the receipt before the story, in short, dry questions, then waits.

**The cast.** Sergeant Ilan Okafor, 61, her partner, the Unit's baby, whose memory is still whole. Midge Pell, 131, archivist, who revives dead phones and hoards chargers. Anselm Roux, 160, pathologist, who knew some victims alive. Superintendent Calla Brandt, who wants the Unit's budget.

**The series engine.** Every book opens with an object surfacing: a letter in a wall, a coat at auction, a phone in harbor mud. Witnesses are certain and wrong; Pell restores the format, Roux reads the bone, and the evidence names the killer before the last page. The city keeps renovating, selling and dredging, and unsolved homicides never expire.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Larch Street Doorbell* — A doorbell camera found in a wall reopens Ines Marlow's 2041 death; her widower has remarried four times, and the Unit keeps its budget.
2. *The Judge's Raincoat* — An auctioned raincoat with a 2026 ferry ticket in its lining places Judge Halvard Stroud beside a drowned clerk; Okafor stops being the baby.
3. *The Clinic Queue* — Wren Ferrant, 112, brings a letter about her mother, a nurse killed in the 2052 Maintenance queue; a ledger shows Hale alive that year.
4. *The Constable in 2030* — A dredged phone reopens Varga's first homicide and makes her a witness; its messages show she sent Hale away, and where he went.

*Running thread:* Why Hale left in 2030, and where he has been for 127 years; answered in book 4.

**Why readers come back.** A complete, fair mystery in every book: the clues are on the table and nobody is hurt on the page. Readers return for the Unit's basement company and for witnesses a century older than their alibis, met with patience, not contempt.

**What makes it different.** Cold cases where time has removed nobody: suspect, widower and victim's child all sit for interview, yet the one reliable witness is a dead phone, and the detective's own memory is on trial.
