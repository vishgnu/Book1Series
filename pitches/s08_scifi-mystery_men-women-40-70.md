# The Century Unit

**Genre:** Science fiction mystery · **Readers:** Men and women 40–70 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** In Port Sable, 2157, bodies no longer age but memory holds only seventy years. Inspector Dessa Varga, 154, and archivist Midge Pell reopen one homicide over a century old per book. Every witness is alive and remembers only the retelling.

**The setting.** Port Sable is a harbor city of kite-sailed freighters and diary notaries. Since 2049 the Forfeit has stopped aging, but everyone keeps only their latest seventy years; death comes by accident, never by age. Courts refuse testimony older than memory, so the Century Unit trusts paper, cloth, bone and dead devices. A homicide conviction means forty years confined, most of a living memory.

**The lead.** Varga runs the Unit and trusts her own handwriting too much. She wants to find Corin Hale, the sergeant who trained her at 27, known only from pencil corrections in her oldest notebooks, which say he walked out on her first homicide. She asks for the receipt before the story, then waits.

**The cast.** Pell coaxes one small fact, a timestamp or a call log, from each dead device and calls Varga's notebooks one more account. Ilan Okafor, 61, remembers everything and is writing down his mother's kitchen before his first forgetting, nine years off. Defense advocate Tobias Quill argues that a client who cannot remember the act did not commit it, and believes it.

**The series engine.** Each book opens with a twenty-first-century object surfacing from a wall, an auction or harbor mud. A marriage or inheritance still rests on the old story, and no killer may keep what the killing won, so Varga traces how the story drifted and who gained. No suspect dies of old age, so no case closes itself.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *The Larch Street Doorbell* — A walled-up doorbell camera, its clock an hour off, breaks the alibi a daughter, now 121, gave her father at five; Hale's pencil marks the file.
2. *The Judge's Raincoat* — A ferry ticket in a raincoat lining places a judge beside a clerk drowned in 2026; Hale signed the report, one page since replaced.
3. *The Clinic Queue* — A ledger from the first Forfeit queue reopens a nurse's killing; the names' order shows who stood behind her, and its Hale proves the wrong man.
4. *The Sealed Tin* — A phone in a sealed tin reopens Varga's first homicide; Okafor leads, Varga is a witness, and Pell authenticates her notebooks against her.

*Running thread:* Did Varga drive out the man who trained her? The Hale file grows unevenly; book 4 answers it and finds Hale alive.

**Why readers come back.** A complete, fair mystery every book, nobody hurt on the page; Varga and Pell arguing over a ferry ticket, she reading the date, Pell the ink; witnesses a century older than their alibis, met with patience.

**What makes it different.** Cold cases where time has removed nobody: the one reliable witness is a dead phone from our century, and in book 4 the inspector's own notebooks testify against her.
