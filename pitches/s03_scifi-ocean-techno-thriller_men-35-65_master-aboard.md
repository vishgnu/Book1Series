# Master Aboard

**Genre:** Near-future science fiction techno-thriller · **Readers:** Men 35–65 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete boarding per book

**Logline.** In 2041, when a crewless cargo ship goes dark, riding master Iker "Etch" Etxeberria and his crew are winched aboard to bring her in and learn why. He pledges his house each time.

**The setting.** Nine thousand cargo ships cross the oceans crewless, built without bridge, bunks or handrails; the one manual control is a hand pump at the steering gear. Each obeys orders signed under her registry number, which should die when she is scrapped. A ship that stops answering may not come within twelve miles of a coast until a licensed human master is aboard and answerable.

**The lead.** Iker Etxeberria, 46, Basque, was the Halcyon Dawn's last captain and signed the certificate that let her sail crewless and put her eleven crew ashore. He wants every ship brought in afloat; each signature pledges his house in Bermeo against hull, cargo and coastline. He reads a ship by sound, is sometimes wrong, and signs alone.

**The cast.** Mirren Tait, 31, engineer, steered the Halcyon Dawn from a Rotterdam desk after Etch left her, and logged nothing when her scrapped number stayed live. Adaeze Nwosu, 55, a Lagos harbor pilot until remote docking ended the trade, wants the machines seen failing. Teo Baptista, 22, deckhand, trained on simulators and wants a master's license.

**The series engine.** Every book is one ship and one question: why she did what she did. The crew boards with seventy-two hours of supplies and a deadline set by weather, fuel or a coastline. Salt and cheap maintenance silence another ship each week; a few are sent astray on purpose.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Not Under Command* — An LNG carrier circles off Namibia, burning her cargo on orders under the Halcyon Dawn's dead number; Etch anchors her whole, and the charterer puts a lien on his house.
2. *Cold Cargo* — A container ship lies dark off Madagascar ahead of a cyclone; Teo finds the Halcyon Dawn's electrician living in a cold box, and Etch brings him off alive.
3. *Two Helms* — Two control rooms and two navies contest a tanker bound for the Malacca Strait; Adaeze steers by hand pump, and Mirren traces the forged orders to her Rotterdam watch chief.
4. *The Halcyon Dawn* — Etch's old ship drifts toward Tasmania; he lets his crew countersign, lands her, and finds his chief mate renting out her dead number to pay the eleven Etch put ashore.

*Running thread:* Sold for scrap two years ago, the Halcyon Dawn never reached the breakers; someone trades her under Etch's signature.

**Why readers come back.** Each book closes its own case and ends on one new fact about the Halcyon Dawn: her dead number still commands ships; one of her eleven never went ashore; the watch chief was paid from aboard her. Book four settles her and makes Etch sign a report naming a friend; more ships wait.

**What makes it different.** No firefights, no navy heroes, no secret agency: four licensed civilians against a 300-meter machine; the only weapon is a signature, and being wrong costs a house.
