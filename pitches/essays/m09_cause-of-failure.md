# Cause of Failure: The Duluth Collapse Mysteries

*A story essay for pitch [m09](../m09_techno-mystery_men-women-40-65_cause-of-failure-the-duluth-collapse-mysteries.md). It gives a feel for the series, not a decision about it: the scene and any detail not in the pitch are illustrative. The scene assumes close third person, past tense, which the pitch does not settle.*

---

At thirty below, steel rang. Elias Brandt had forgotten that during his suspension, and he remembered it now, at the foot of tower nine, with a hammer in his coat pocket that he was not allowed to use.

Nobody was allowed to use anything. OSHA held the scene. Yellow tape ran from the lift shack to a spruce. Inside it the tower lay down the slope on its side, haul rope slack across the snow, and a blue tarp covered the place where the mechanic had been found. Around the old footing stood five experts in five colors of parka, one for each party that might owe: the hill, the lift's maker, the concrete contractor, the maintenance firm, and Northline Testing, which had inspected it.

Northline had sent Leonard Haas. Haas had trained Elias. He nodded once and kept his gloves on.

"Wind gust," said the maker's man, to nobody. "Forty-one miles an hour at the top station. Ice in the sheave. It's in the log."

"It's in a log," said Elias.

Naomi Adeyemi knelt at the footing. Four anchor rods stood out of the concrete, two snapped flush, two bent over like grass. She did not touch them. She held a flashlight low and flat, so the light raked across the rod ends, and photographed each with a scale beside it.

"Talk," said Elias.

"Stamp's legible on three. They're not the grade on the drawing."

"How far off?"

"One grade. Maybe two. I want the lab." She looked at Haas, then away. "They were passed. Northline's initials are on the anchor inspection."

"Initials go on a lot of things," Haas said.

Elias wrote the time, the temperature and the name of everyone present, and what each had just said. Minus thirty-one Fahrenheit. Eleven-forty. He had sixty days to sign a cause. His signature would decide who paid, and every lawyer here would open the cross-examination with the ramp.

"Bag them," he said. "Chain of custody. Everybody watches."

---

That is the shape of every book: a failure everyone is ready to call an accident, a plausible wrong answer already written in somebody's log, and a piece of metal that disagrees.

**The city.** Duluth at thirty below, on Lake Superior's working shore: ore docks, grain elevators, ski lifts and dams, much of it inspected by Northline Testing, the ramp included. Steam lifts off the open lake, the hills are steep enough that every street is a ramp of its own, and the industry is old, heavy and still running. After a fatal failure OSHA holds the scene and every party's expert watches each test. No insurer pays until an engineer signs a cause, and the signature decides who pays.

**The lead.** Elias is fifty-two. He warned in writing that a ramp connection was unsafe, then sealed a late revision to sheet S-401 without checking it, because his partner Martin Kell called it a title-block change. The ramp killed two. Elias was not framed. He signed. He has served his suspension and his license is restored, and he wants to know who changed the sheet. He speaks in short sentences with the units attached. After every case he writes to Joan Lindahl, a victim's widow. The letters come back unopened.

**The team.** Naomi Adeyemi, twenty-nine, a metallurgist, quit Northline rather than retype a failed hardness result, and no lab in town will hire her now. Dutch Kowalczyk, sixty-eight, a retired crane operator, set the ramp's girders and knows how steel really goes up. Renee Halloran, claims manager at Lakehead Mutual and four years from her pension, hires them and fights every invoice. Leonard Haas, the Northline inspector who trained Elias, is on the other side of most tapes. Every other engineer in town depends on Northline work and cannot testify against it. Elias, shut out since the ramp, can.

**What a book feels like.** One fatal failure, five parties who could owe, and sixty days. The case always has a plausible wrong answer, and it always breaks on something only one trade can read: a fracture face for Naomi, a scar on a chain for Dutch, a load path for Elias. The reader learns to read steel along with the jury. The violence is over before the book opens, and nobody draws a gun or opens a laptop to save the day. The evidence is something you can hold, bag and carry into court.

**Where the four books go.** In *Cold Short* a ski-lift tower kills a mechanic, Naomi reads a bolt stamp on off-grade anchors that Northline passed, and an unsigned copy of S-401 turns up in Elias's own office. In *Lack of Fusion* a split grain bin buries a worker, and a rain gauge shows the seam was welded wet and passed from a Northline desk. S-401 carries Kell's plot stamp. In *Beach Marks* an ore-dock conveyor kills two dockhands, Dutch reads chain scars that reveal a concealed overload, and Renee is fired for reporting it. The handwriting on S-401 is Haas's. In *Load Path* a spillway gate drowns a fisherman and grease logs expose faked maintenance. Elias reopens his own discipline file to prove that Kell ordered the change and Haas drew it, and Joan answers his letter. The pitch frames the thread as who changed the sheet and what the answer costs Elias's license. It says book 4 leaves the three of them in business, but not what the price is.

**What stays with the reader.** An engineer who carries his own signature through four books and never pretends it was someone else's. The cold, the evidence bags, a widow's envelope that finally comes back opened, and a lesson in looking at a broken thing until it tells you who broke it.

## Before choosing this one

- **Strongest element:** a lead who is guilty, not framed, and evidence a reader can hold. It ranked 6 for distinctiveness, and failures never run out.
- **Main risk:** production. Metallurgy, structural engineering, OSHA and insurance procedure are the whole appeal, and this pitch ranked last on the slate for production fit. AI drafts will write confident engineering that is wrong, and a part-time editor cannot catch it without an engineer reading each book. The final reader also found the Kell-and-Haas answer guessable from book 2, and eight-plus fatal failures in one city will strain belief.
- **Shelf risk:** narrow and hard to advertise. A critic thought it skews to men 50-70 with trade backgrounds. *Beach Marks* may read to browsers as a beach book.
- **Still open, and left open here:** Haas's motive for drawing the change; who hires the team after Renee is fired in book 3; whether book 4 should implicate Elias's own conduct more directly; what the answer costs his license; whether a reinstated engineer signing causes for an insurer is credible (`logs/m09.md`, the author questions). The essay does not name the ramp's type or location.
- **To verify:** OSHA control of a fatal-failure scene, the "no pay until a cause is signed" rule and its sixty days, engineering license suspension and reinstatement in Minnesota, anchor-rod grade markings, and the metallurgy behind each title term. Duluth is real; every firm and facility should be invented.
- **Invented for this essay:** tower nine, the five parties' identities, the wind figure, the time and temperature, Haas at the scene, the hammer, the tarp and the inspection initials.
