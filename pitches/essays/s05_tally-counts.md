# The Tally Counts

*A story essay for pitch [s05](../s05_scifi-cozy-space_women-men-30-60_the-tally-counts.md). It gives a feel for the series, not a decision about it: the scene and any detail not in the pitch are illustrative. The scene assumes close third person, past tense, from Nell's side, which no style guide has settled yet.*

---

Dunmore's Orchard smelled of cut grass and warm filters, and the trees went up the curve of the ring until they hung overhead, upside down and heavy with fruit. Davi kept looking up at them. Nell kept looking at hands.

She sat at the end of the long table in the packing shed with her pocket book open and a stub of pencil, and the orchard came to her a family at a time. Name, hearth, how long. She saw each one, asked each one, and made one stroke. Four down and one across, the way her own trainer had made them.

"Two hundred and twelve," said the steward, a broad man named Tobin Ambry, when the last family had gone out into the evening light. He put the register on the table, open at a page of tidy names. "Same as last count. Same as the air bill."

"The gauges agree," Davi murmured. "Total draw fits two-twelve, near enough."

"The gauges tell you how much is breathed," said Nell. "They don't tell you who."

She ran her pencil down the strokes, bundle by bundle, and frowned.

"Mr. Ambry. I don't have you."

"I'm in the register, Census. Top of the page. You can take my word."

"I don't doubt you," said Nell. "I count you."

He stood, a little red, and gave his name and hearth and years like anyone else, and she made the stroke. Then she counted the bundles again, out loud this time, with Davi's lips moving alongside.

Forty-two fives and three.

"Two hundred and thirteen," said Davi.

Ambry's face did something complicated. "You've counted somebody twice."

"Then I'll find them twice." Nell closed the pocket book on her pencil. She got up, went behind the shed's little counter as if it were her own galley, and filled the kettle from the tap.

"Davi," she said, "we'll be here a while."

---

That is the method entire. A number that is wrong by one, a woman who will not take anyone's word for a person, and a kettle, because the answer will come from sitting in kitchens rather than from the gauges.

**The Reaches.** The Outer Reaches are orchard rings and sixty-person ice drums. Scrubbers lose some of every breath, so the Air Board ships make-up air, billed per head. Gauges show how much is breathed, never by whom, so every person must be seen and asked. The last orbital count cut a living town's air. The Reaches keep their Assembly seats only above four million heads, and the register stands 650 over. Every miscount is a bill, a vote and, somewhere, someone's lungs.

**The lead.** Nell Abara is fifty-three and twenty-nine years unbribed. She wants her pocket book, one stroke per person, to balance. Counting people spares her staying with them. Her late husband, Ade, asked her to stop, and she took one more district. Home is the cutter *Tally* and the galley he fitted for two. Her kindness is real but procedural, and the series is about the procedure slowly letting her stay for supper.

**The company.** Davi Serrano, twenty-two, is her apprentice. He was raised in the town whose air was cut and is sure better math would have saved it. Registrar Imogen Park, sixty-one, trained Nell and needs the four million. Oksana Bright, sixty-seven, skippers a mail barge, rearranges Nell's galley without asking, and stays for supper.

**What a book feels like.** Park sends the *Tally* wherever count and register disagree. Nell and Davi work through kitchens, ration books and who sings at whose funeral. Every wrong number has a sound reason, and the reason is always human: debt, grief, fear of a ledger. No one is murdered. The stakes are air and votes. Each book ends on the pocket book, with the new seat margin and one theory about the midwife struck out.

**Where the four books go.** In *One Heartbeat Over*, Dunmore's Orchard's 213th is a tug hand who played dead to dodge eleven years of air bills, and the town splits the bill 213 ways. In *Forty Borrowed Breaths*, Saint Brine's goats breathe air drawn for forty dead; Davi's bedtime gauge check, a habit from home, catches it, and Nell wins a livestock allowance. In *Ninety-Four Loaves*, Quiet Harbor bakes for ninety-four but lists ninety, because creditors once bought a ledger naming its children. Davi asks instead of calculating, and Nell enters four strokes, no names. In *Six Hundred Twelve Under Way*, the hauler *Clover Parish*, bound out of the Reaches, wants its 612 struck from home registers. Park asks Nell to round up, Davi makes the count, and Park signs it. Through all four runs the midwife Junia Vasko, forty-one, seen and asked at two hearths in one count year. In book 4 Nell finds that Junia keeps both, half a year each, and changes the form: one stroke, two half-shares of air. The seats hold by two: 650 over, plus five found, less 653 struck.

**What stays with the reader.** The sum comes out, honestly, by two. And at the end Nell sets Ade's galley for two again, with Oksana in the second chair.

## Before choosing this one

- **Strongest element:** production fit, ranked 3 of 20, and distinctiveness, 5. The setting is invented, the cast is small, the cases are gentle and settled by headcount, and the seat arithmetic adds up across four books.
- **Main risk:** reader pull, ranked 16. Air billing and seat margins with no crime give few readers a reason to borrow. The first chapter must make a miscount by one feel urgent. The running margin must stay exact in every book, and AI drafts will slip a number or flatten Davi into a type.
- **Shelf risk:** is it cozy mystery or science fiction? A critic in the log suggests the core readers are women 40-70 coming from cozy mystery, with a subtitle such as "A Cozy Space Mystery" to make the shelf plain. Readers who expect a body will not get one.
- **Still open, and left open here:** the exact two-hearth custom behind Junia, which must be fixed before clues are planted; whether Nell and Oksana are a romance or a companionship; whether Nell stays aboard the *Tally* after book 4; whether the *Clover Parish* leaving for good is the right ending; and the three Junia theories and which book strikes each (`logs/s05.md`, the author questions). The essay assumes book 2's forty dead come off the register, which the pitch's final sum requires.
- **Facts to verify:** little outside the invented world. Scrubber losses, make-up air billing and spin-gravity orchard rings should be plausible to a technical reader.
- **Invented for this essay:** the steward Tobin Ambry, the packing shed and its counter, the overhead fruit, the "Census" form of address, and Nell's tally style. They are texture, not canon.
