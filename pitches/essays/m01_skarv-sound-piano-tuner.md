# The Skarv Sound Piano Tuner Mysteries

*A story essay for pitch [m01](../m01_cozy-paranormal-mystery_women-45-75_the-skarv-sound-piano-tuner-mysteries.md). It gives a feel for the series, not a decision about it: the scene and any detail not in the pitch are illustrative. The scene assumes close third person, past tense, which the pitch does not settle.*

---

Rain had got into the church on Tern Island again. Signe could smell it before she was through the door: wet hymnals, cold cedar, a furnace thinking about it. Purser stayed in the Volvo. He had opinions about choir lofts.

The pastor met her at the foot of the loft stairs with his hands in his cardigan pockets. "The funeral's Friday," he said. "We'd like the grand done for it. Lenore would have wanted it right."

"Lenore wanted everything right," said Signe. "That was the trouble with her."

She climbed the stairs Lenore had fallen down on Sunday night. Eleven steps, steep, the rail rubbed pale by eighty years of altos. Up in the loft the grand stood under its quilted cover like a horse in a blanket. She folded the cover back, opened the fallboard, set Arne's tuning case on the bench and touched middle C.

The piano played back to her.

A hymn, too fast and far too loud, the way you play when you want someone downstairs to hear every note. The right hand hammered the tune. The left came down on the chords, and in the third bar it struck a low E-flat and the G above the octave together, a full tenth, clean, not rolled. Then nothing. Rain on the roof. The furnace ticking.

Signe sat down. She put her own left hand on the keys and stretched it, the way she had watched Lenore stretch hers at every rehearsal for nine years. Lenore had small, quick hands. She could reach an octave and not a hair more, and she rolled every tenth like a harpist and called it style.

Whoever had played this, angry, in an empty church, had not been Lenore.

Signe closed the fallboard without turning a single pin. Downstairs the pastor called up to ask whether Friday would be all right.

"We'll see about Friday," she said.

Then she went down to the dock to find Mateo, who had been her worst pupil and was now, heaven help the county, a deputy. He still could not count to four. But he could count fingers, and he could carry a hand span to the mainland.

---

That is the series in one visit. A piano tells Signe something once, and the only way to keep it telling is not to do the job she came to do. What she can pass on is never the music. It is a hand.

**The islands.** Skarv Sound is six cedar-dark islands in Washington's Salish Sea, a shuttered cannery and one car ferry, the *Kittiwake*, four round trips a day. The timetable is the islands' clock and their alibi book: if you were not on the boat, you were not across the water. Damp gets into every piano, so sooner or later every house lets the tuner in. The mainland sheriff reopens no ruled death without physical evidence, which means the gift can only ever point. Someone always wants the piano done by Friday.

**The woman.** Signe Halvorsen is sixty-one, a widowed church organist, driving her husband Arne's tuning round in his 1989 Volvo with his cat in the back. She wants to finish the round, not ask why he stopped it. She has not touched their own parlor upright since he died. She is dry with pupils, dry with grief and cross with the husband she misses. At first touch a piano gives her the last music played on it in anger, grief or fear: a few bars and the touch of the hands, never words or faces. Usually it is plain sorrow. Tuning erases it, so she destroys evidence by doing her job.

**The company.** Britt Halvorsen, Arne's sister, sells ferry tickets and cardamom buns with his thermos still behind her window, and she and Signe bicker instead of crying. Mateo Ruiz is twenty-six, tone-deaf and the law. Opal Tanaka, the deckhand, remembers every car that ever boarded and will not talk about Arne's last crossing. Desmond Pryce, the mainland tuner, is the friendliest threat in the series: he has no idea what he erases.

**What a book feels like.** Rain, ferry queues, too-loud Bach in an empty church. Each book is one death that was ruled something gentle and one piano that disagrees. Signe names the piece. Hands and timetables do the proving: a span, a stiffness, a finger that never moves, checked against who was on which boat. The reader holds every clue but the music, and the music only points.

**Where the four books go.** *Discord in the Choir Loft* opens with choir director Lenore Vance's death ruled a fall, and her grand holding a furious hymn in a left hand spanning a tenth when Lenore could reach an octave. In *Lullaby at Last Call* a tavern owner drowns after the last ferry. Gossip blames his widow, who always plays the closing lullaby, but the bar piano holds it in stiff, arthritic hands. *Abide with Malice* follows Walter Coyle, dead after cutting his daughter from his will, whose piano holds "Abide with Me" in a right hand that never uses its ring finger. *The Piano She Wouldn't Tune* ends the thread. The cannery caretaker dies beside a piano Arne starred in his ledger, and Signe's own parlor upright holds Arne's frightened last waltz, played four-hands. She knows the second pair of hands before she tunes it away. The answer to the running question is that Arne heard echoes too. He starred eleven pianos he could not prove, and his heart failed on the crossing he made to tell the sheriff. Opal promised never to tell Signe he was afraid. Four stars are settled. Seven wait.

**What stays with the reader.** The cost. To learn the truth about Arne, Signe has to tune away the last music he made, and she has spent four books learning exactly what tuning destroys. The series is gentle, but its central act is a small, deliberate loss, repeated in every house she enters.

## Before choosing this one

- **Strongest element:** a quiet gift that erases its own evidence, with a hand clue in every book that the reader can check. It ranked first for reader pull.
- **Main risk:** every solution rests on piano fact and the ferry timetable. AI drafts will drift on boat times and hand details unless the bible fixes them in tables. The natural death at the center of the thread may also feel like a letdown after three books of hints; one test reader said so.
- **Shelf risk:** paranormal cozy is a crowded shelf, though this one has no witches or talking cat. "Choir Loft" is a well-worn title phrase.
- **Still open, and left open here:** whether Signe always had the gift or it began with Arne's round; who the second pair of hands in the waltz is; whether Arne's death stays natural; whether playing in strong feeling overwrites an echo; who played the arthritic lullaby; how much of Opal's family history the series touches (`logs/m01.md`, the author questions).
- **Needs checking:** hand spans and what a tuner does to a church grand in one visit; island ferry practice and which sheriff covers fictional islands in Washington.
- **Invented for this essay:** Tern Island, the pastor, the eleven loft stairs, the Sunday-night fall, the E-flat tenth, Lenore rolling her tenths, nine years of rehearsals, and the echo starting at the first key she touches. They are texture, not canon.
