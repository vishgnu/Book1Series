# The Hob Passage

*A story essay for pitch [s01](../s01_scifi-space-adventure_men-women-35-65_the-hob-passage.md). It gives a feel for the series, not a decision about it: the scene and any detail not in the pitch are illustrative. The scene assumes close third person, past tense, which the pitch does not settle.*

---

The boarding trunk was cold enough to fog her visor, and the ladder ran up it in forty rungs. Imani Okonjo counted every one over the suit channel, the way she had since the fall.

"Thirty-eight. Thirty-nine. That's forty. You're aboard, Pilot."

"Thank you, Imani."

"Don't thank me. How's the knee?"

"Present."

The bridge of the *Corazon Vale* was too quiet for a liner. The captain's chair was empty, his cap hung on its arm, and two officers stood on either side of it, not looking at each other. One was First Officer Solano. The other, older, with a staff officer's braid, said, "The captain will want to be informed of every burn, Pilot. In his cabin. In person."

"Then someone should inform him," Maren Achterberg said. "I'll be at the radar."

She had the conn and nothing else. She could not order a steward to bring her tea. She sat at the long-range set and watched the Comb for forty minutes without speaking: the bright, tumbling front of it, ice the size of houses and ice the size of salt, all of it dragged across the lane by Hob's slow, lumpy pull. The tables said the gap would hold for another seven hours. The tables were three days old. Out there, a beacon lasted a week if it was lucky.

At the end of the forty minutes she drew a line on the plot, six hours out, and wrote a time beside it.

"That's not the table," Solano said.

"No. The Comb's nine minutes early." She stood, and her knee told her about it. "We burn at oh-four-twelve, six minutes at full, and go through at the trailing edge with four minutes in hand."

"Four minutes," said the older officer. "Is that safe?"

"It's closer than I'd choose."

"The captain..."

"The captain will be told our heading, our speed and our time at the inner line. So will you, and so will the first officer. That is all I'm able to tell anyone." She sat back down at the radar. "I'd use the next six hours to decide who will be giving the orders at oh-four-twelve. I'm not allowed to."

---

That is the series in one watch. The pilot cannot take sides, so she makes the clock take them, and the people aboard have to settle their quarrel inside the margin she gives them.

**The Passage.** Kettle Station melts a gas giant's ice ring into propellant, and the one gap in is the Passage, thirty-one hours of moving ice. Hob, a two-kilometer rubble moonlet, orbits there, and its gravity drags a breaker of ice, the Comb, across the lane faster than the tables update or the beacons survive. By treaty every ship takes a pilot, who boards by cutter and serves any flag. The world is small and physical: frost in a boarding trunk, a radar plot, a burn timed to the minute, and a dock at the end.

**The pilot.** Maren is forty-four and calls the lane six hours ahead from forty minutes of radar. Years ago she docked the indebted *Anna Reyes* on the minute, as the rule required, into a waiting seizure crew. Docking late would have saved its master's license. She answers his daughter's letters, and she is still looking for the transit that proves the rule was right. A ladder fall cost her a knee. Her highest expression of alarm is "closer than I'd choose."

**The company.** Imani Okonjo, fifty-one, cutter coxswain, counts the ladder rungs for her and jokes about everything except whether Maren blames her for the fall. Pilot Master Vera Castellan, sixty-three, trained Maren. Dev Pradhan, twenty-three, an apprentice, built the longest-lived beacon anyone has managed, nineteen days. Anders Sorensen, thirty-nine, an inspector, rechecks the tables by hand and suspects they are rigged.

**What a book feels like.** One transit, one ship, a crew in trouble and a closing lane. It is a sealed-ship story told by the one neutral stranger aboard. Maren solves the navigation with burns, margins and timing, and she settles the quarrel aboard only through heading, speed and arrival time. Nobody is arrested and nobody is ordered. Every book ends at the dock and adds one fact about Hob.

**Where the four books go.** In *Boarding Ground* the liner *Corazon Vale*'s captain is dying and the Comb is closing nine minutes early. Maren times the last burn so that Solano takes command with the ship clear. In *Dead Freight* she nurses a yawing grain hauler past the Comb and docks two hours late, on purpose. It is the delay she refused the *Anna Reyes*, and it lets eleven witnesses to table-rigging reach Sorensen. In *No Register* she threads an unmarked warship under Hob. When its captain demands she erase the radar record of a tender beside the moonlet, she files it. In *Pilot Away* Maren learns that Castellan ordered the burns. She withholds the conn, a pilot's one lever, until the logs reach every flag, then brings the *Corazon Vale* through the narrowest lane on record. The answer to the thread is that the Pilot Service itself has pushed Hob for forty years, so that no flag can own the lane. Maren judges the aim right and the secrecy wrong. Hob stays erratic after book 4, and the cutter keeps boarding, under open logs.

**What stays with the reader.** A woman who was once on time when she should have been late, and is late once, on purpose, when it counts. The rungs counted aloud in the dark. And a hero whose only power is to hold a ship steady while other people decide who they are.

## Before choosing this one

- **Strongest element:** a hero who commands nothing. The neutral pilot makes a fresh sealed-ship format, and the answer inside the Pilot Service is morally loaded rather than a stock villain. It ranked 7 for distinctiveness.
- **Main risk:** numbers and sameness. Every resolution is burns, margins and timing across a thirty-one-hour transit, which is heavy checking for one editor, and AI drafts will drift on times and distances. Navigation-only climaxes may start to feel alike by book 3.
- **Shelf risk:** there is no combat, and the tone is procedural, which undersells against action-led space opera in subscription stores (reader pull ranked 13).
- **Still open, and left open here:** whether withholding the conn in book 4 is inside Maren's "only steer" rule or her one deliberate breach; whether the pushing cost ships over forty years; whether Castellan also wrote the on-the-minute rule; why a pilot beats a ship's computer; whether Maren has always known who signed the *Anna Reyes* seizure (`logs/s01.md`, the author questions). The essay assumes what the pitch says, that withholding the conn is "a pilot's one lever."
- **To verify:** no real-world law or history is involved, but the physics is not yet checked. Hob's size and mass, a tender's thrust, the nine-minute drift and beacon lifetimes need one physics sheet before drafting.
- **Invented for this essay:** the boarding trunk and its forty rungs, the captain's cap, the older officer, the burn time and four-minute margin, the three-day-old tables and the week-long beacon life.
