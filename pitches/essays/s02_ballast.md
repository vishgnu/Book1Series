# Ballast: The Four Worlds Balloon Race

*A story essay for pitch [s02](../s02_scifi-racing-adventure_men-women-30-65_ballast-the-four-worlds-balloon-race.md). It gives a feel for the series, not a decision about it: the scene and any detail not in the pitch are illustrative. The scene assumes close third person, past tense, from Anselm's side, which no style guide has settled yet.*

---

On the second morning of the Venus race the cloud outside Marigold's port was the color of weak tea, and it smelled, faintly, through the cabin seals, of struck matches. Anselm Okoro had his forehead against the cold glass and one hand flat on the wall, feeling the boat.

She was riding well. Overhead, too big to see, the envelope ticked as it took the heat. Below them the yellow went down forever. Above them, a few hundred meters up, was the band everyone wanted. You could see it in the haze, which was combed into lines up there, like wet hair.

"Dray's climbing," said Hesper Dunne from the rigging board. She did not look up from her splice. "That's three boats this hour."

Anselm watched the lines. They were not quite parallel. Where the two winds met there was a seam, and in the seam the haze curled back on itself in small, slow fists. He had watched a seam like that once before, with Jonah's hand on the burner, and called it good.

"We go up slow," he said. "Under it. We let the band come to us at the turn."

"Slow costs ballast," said Dita Marchetti. She had the ore ledger open on her knee. Every sack in the belly hold had a name on it in grease pencil: the shift that had cut it, the family it was owed to.

"Two hundred kilos," Anselm said.

"That's a family's month."

He looked at the seam again. It had not changed. He had.

"One ninety, then."

Dita wrote the figure down and signed beside it the way the members had taught her, full name, no initials. Then she pulled the release. Under their boots the hold door groaned, and a family's month, less a little, fell into Venus without a sound.

Marigold rose. Somewhere above the cabin the vent line hissed, a sound too thin to be wind. Hesper's head came up. She put down the splice.

---

That is the series in small. Every climb is paid for in somebody's work, and the man deciding how much to pay no longer trusts what he reads.

**The sport.** Band racing is three people in a van-sized cabin under a stadium-sized envelope, riding a planet's jet streams for days. There are no engines and no steering. The burner heats whatever air the planet has, and in a hydrogen sky the crew carries oxygen to burn. To climb, burn or drop ballast. To sink, vent. Then find a wind going your way. With heat and ballast gone, a boat sinks, and on the giants there is no floor. Each sky breaks the boat differently: acid on Venus, methane cold on Titan, shear at Neptune, the cold under Saturn's rings.

**The lead.** Anselm, forty-eight, read wind for his brother Jonah for twenty years. He called the crossing into the fast band, and the Pallas Cooperative's boat tore. Jonah signed for pilot error, took the ban, and will not say why. Anselm quit, sure his call was at fault. Now he pilots Marigold to find out, making calls with an instrument he doubts: his own eye.

**The crew and the cooperative.** Dita is nineteen, the burner hand and the members' delegate, and no ore goes overboard until she signs. Hesper Dunne, the rigger, bets her wages against Marigold: if the boat goes down, her winnings are Pallas's only insurance. It is the bleakest joke in the series and its kindest act. Darius Dray, the rival, saw the tear and said nothing, to keep his pilot's seat. Behind them stands Pallas, four hundred miners on one asteroid who vote on each entry, for whom a purse buys a year's air.

**What a book feels like.** One race per book: a route, weather that breaks the plan, a hard choice, a placing and one plain answer about the tear. The tension is arithmetic and nerve: two gauges that only go down. Most of it happens in the cabin, three people reading haze and arguing kilos. Nobody fires a shot. The worst that can happen is to spend too early and sink, slowly, with time to think.

**Where the four books go.** In *Acid Lap: The Venus Race*, acid eats the vent line, Hesper patches it aloft, Marigold places third, and the old wreck shows the tear began in bad cloth, not in Anselm's wind. In *Cold Lift: The Titan Race*, Marigold gives up the lead to lift a downed crew off a methane sea and places second; their rigger says Jonah knew the cloth was condemned. In *Blue Shear: The Neptune Race*, Hesper's patch splits at the fastest band, Anselm turns back and finishes fifth, and Dray names who bought the cloth: Pallas. In *Ring Shadow: The Saturn Race*, in the cold of the ring shadow, Anselm calls the fast-band crossing he has refused since the tear, Dita signs the last ore, and Marigold wins. Then Dita's vote makes Pallas report itself. The answer to the thread is that a closed five-seat committee voted to fly condemned cloth. Jonah cast the deciding yes, then signed for pilot error so the fine fell on him and not on Pallas's air. The purse pays the fine, and his ban is lifted.

**What stays with the reader.** The underdog is not clean. The reader roots for four books for a cooperative that bought the cloth that tore, and the brother who took the blame was protecting a vote he helped win. Anselm's call was sound all along, and that is no comfort: he still has to fly the crossing again.

## Before choosing this one

- **Strongest element:** ballast is the members' own ore, so every tactical call costs a named family. "Two hundred kilos." "That's a family's month." "One ninety, then." Each book closes one question about the tear.
- **Main risk:** reader pull. The slate ranked it 18 of 20: planetary balloon racing has no established readership, sports fiction borrows poorly, and the race structure repeats. Production fit is 13: the action depends on heat and ballast numbers that must stay consistent, and AI drafts will drift them. The editor will need a running sheet per race.
- **Physics a technical reader will challenge:** what burns on Venus (carbon dioxide does not burn, so fuel and oxidizer must both be carried, or the sun does the heating) and on Titan (methane is everywhere, oxygen is not); how weak hot-gas lift is in a hydrogen sky, which the stadium-sized envelope only partly answers; whether Neptune's shear is really supersonic at that altitude; acid on Venus against fabric and lines; and how a cooperative whose purse "buys a year's air" pays passage to Neptune. None of this is verified.
- **Still open, and left open here:** whether Dray is witness or villain; whether Hesper's loyalty carries suspicion; committee or full membership; whether a named member (Dita's mother was once proposed) sat on the committee; whether the purse covers the fine in full; and why Hesper's Venus patch is still flying at Neptune (`logs/s02.md`, the author questions). The essay names only Jonah on the committee and assumes the ore is sold at the finish, which the pitch implies but does not state.
- **Invented for this essay:** the tea-colored cloud, grease-pencil names on ore sacks, the belly-hold release, haze lines as a sign of a band, the vent-line hiss on day two, and Dray climbing early. They are texture, not canon.
