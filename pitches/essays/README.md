# Story essays: one per pitch

Each essay gives a feel for one series in the slate, so the author can judge which series they would want to live with for four books and an estimated 260,000 words of Lektorat. Every essay has the same shape:

- an opening scene from early in book 1, showing the lead doing the work that drives every book;
- a walk through the world, the lead, the cast, what a single book feels like, and where the four books go (with spoilers, because the essays are for the author);
- a closing **Before choosing this one** section with the strongest element, the main risks, the open author questions it leaves open, the facts to verify and what the essay invented.

**They are not canon.** The pitch file stays the source text. Scenes, minor names and texture in the essays are illustrative. Every scene assumes close third person, past tense, unless its note says otherwise, because no style guide exists yet. The open questions in each log (`../logs/<id>.md`, "Questions the rewrites left for the author") are left open.

The slate position is the AI judges' combined ranking from `../README.md`. It is not a market test. Read the essays in any order, and note which ones you would want to read on before looking at the position.

## How to use them

1. **Read for appetite, not score.** Mark each essay *yes*, *maybe* or *no* by one question: would you happily read and edit four of these books beside your day job?
2. **Cut to three or four.** Keep only the *yes* pile, then check it against the business plan's niche advice (cozy paranormal or cozy fantasy mystery first, science-fiction adventure second).
3. **Test the shortlist on real readers.** Show the shortlisted essays' opening scenes, or the pitches, to people who read that genre, and ask which one they would borrow. The AI panel cannot replace this step.
4. **Run the business plan's month-1 checks on the finalist:** the hand-made category check of the top 100, a fact check of the pitch's real-world claims, and the two-or-three-style voice test.
