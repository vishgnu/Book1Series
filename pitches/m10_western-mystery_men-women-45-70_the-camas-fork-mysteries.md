# The Camas Fork Mysteries

**Genre:** Western mystery · **Readers:** Men and women 45–70 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Marty Voss has no badge, only the padlocks on the headgates that send Idaho's Camas Fork to its farms. When water runs where she locked it out, somebody is dead beside it.

**The setting.** Larkspur County, Idaho: potato cellars and dairies over black lava rock, nine inches of rain a year. Fourteen ditches split the Camas Fork, the oldest dug in 1884. First in time is first in right: in a dry year the oldest ditch fills before a newer one gets a drop.

**The lead.** Marty Voss, 54, has been the watermaster's ditch rider since 2002. As a deputy she served the 2001 shutoff that cost Vern Pruitt his farm, and she wants to know it was right. She reads a wet bank: what ran, how deep, when. "Four tenths low. What I think comes after."

**The cast.** Sheriff Caleb Ortega, 47, the trainee who drove her to the Pruitt shutoff; Dale Hinckley, 61, watermaster, who keeps the ditch ledger, minus its 1911 pages, in his late wife's recipe tins; Maite Aldecoa, 67, a Basque herder's granddaughter on the ditch first in line; Gene Voss, 58, Marty's brother, on the newest.

**The series engine.** The death is Ortega's case; the water is Marty's, and he acts only on what she has signed. Each break comes from what only a ditch rider may do: measure, lock, sign the log, testify. Every case turns on two records that cannot both be true, and the reader sees both. In a dry valley a place in line is worth killing for, every year.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *First in Time* — April: the old Pruitt farm's buyer lies dead in his potato cellar, Marty's padlock cut, yet the gauge chart says the water came six hours after he died.
2. *Call on the River* — July: ordered to lock out her brother Gene, Marty finds his neighbor dead beside a flume shaved to cheat the gauge; her log clears Gene, who stops speaking to her.
3. *Duty of Water* — Harvest: a dairy pond filling at night gives up Vern Pruitt, missing since 2001, but Marty's delivery sheets show the pond stood dry until 2003, so someone moved him.
4. *Futile Call* — Winter: Hinckley lies under ice at a locked headgate, his tins emptied; Marty's log says only her key opened it, and Gene, silent since July, drives her to the courthouse.

*Running thread:* In 1911 someone back-dated the Aldecoa claim ahead of the ditch that fed the Pruitt farm; Marty enforced that order in 2001. In book 4 she says under oath her shutoff was wrong.

**Why readers come back.** Each case closes in its own book, no cliffhangers. Each book turns up one missing 1911 page, moving a named ditch up the line. The July lockout costs Marty her brother; the winter gives him back.

**What makes it different.** Its detective cannot arrest anyone, only swear to what the water did, in cubic feet per second, and the reader can check every measurement.
