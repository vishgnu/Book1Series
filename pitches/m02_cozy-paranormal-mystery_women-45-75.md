# The Leaning Shadows Mysteries

**Genre:** Cozy paranormal mystery · **Readers:** Women 45–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Edie Farrow, 57, finds missing heirs for a Finger Lakes Surrogate's Court, helped by a sight no report can mention: the shadows of blood relatives lean toward each other. Each book: one unclaimed estate, one death that paid somebody.

**The setting.** Harrow County, New York: Lantern Lake, eleven miles long, with Corwin Falls at its foot, a stone courthouse, vineyards on the east slope and a dry canal bed behind Main Street. Six canal-era families have intermarried and feuded here since 1825. The rule: an estate with no will and no known heir stays frozen until the court finds blood kin.

**The lead.** Edie Farrow, 57, works on commission: no heir, no fee. A foundling left on the courthouse steps in March 1969, she has never seen her own shadow lean. She wants every estate to reach its family; proving on paper what she sees costs her months and friendships. Dry, patient, exact; she talks to file boxes.

**The cast.** Dot Kessler, 61, chief court clerk and Edie's oldest friend; Ambrose Teague, 74, county historian, who guards his archive like a dragon; Nora, 29, Edie's daughter, a genetic counselor who thinks her mother is simply observant; Walt Dombrowski, 63, dairy farmer and elected coroner.

**The series engine.** Every book names an heir and explains a death. Edie's sight supplies one fact: kin, or not. It never says how, shows nothing of marriage, adoption or love, and needs a light and both people in the room. The rest is censuses, baptism books, deeds and consented DNA. The court's docket never empties.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Where the Shadows Lean* — A stranger arrives grieving as Ruth Vandermark's lost daughter; her shadow stands straight beside Ruth's sister. Edie finds the true heir and opens her own foundling file.
2. *Noon Light* — Two strangers' shadows cross like siblings' at drowned vintner Carl Brandt's burial. Edie proves them his children and mails her own DNA kit.
3. *The Long Shadow* — Hollis Ostrander's brother expects the canal mansion until his shadow leans toward the gardener, Hollis's unrecorded son. Ambrose unlocks his archive for Edie's DNA match.
4. *By Lamplight* — Midwife Agnes Pruyn, 92, dies in a blackout, one ledger missing. Edie finds heir and thief; a 1969 entry leads to a kitchen where her shadow leans.

*Running thread:* who left a newborn at the courthouse in March 1969; book 4 answers it and gives Edie living kin with estates of their own.

**Why readers come back.** You see each shadow when Edie does, and every proof is a document on the table, so you can solve it alongside her. Church suppers, grape harvest, courthouse gossip. Every case closes, the right family gets the house, and nobody is harmed on the page.

**What makes it different.** The gift is a question, never an answer: it says two people are kin and stops. The detecting is real genealogy, by a 57-year-old researcher whose own family tree begins on a courthouse step.
