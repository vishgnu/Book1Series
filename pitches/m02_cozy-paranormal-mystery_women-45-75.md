# The Leaning Shadows Mysteries

**Genre:** Cozy paranormal mystery · **Readers:** Women 45–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Edie Farrow, 57, sees the shadows of close blood relatives lean toward each other. She traces heirs for a Finger Lakes county; every book brings one estate without a will and one suspicious death.

**The setting.** Harrow County, New York: Lantern Lake, Corwin Falls, vineyards. Six canal-era families, intermarried and feuding since 1825. When someone dies without a will, the county public administrator must prove blood kin at a hearing, or the estate passes to the state.

**The lead.** Edie is the administrator's genealogist, paid from the estate: no heir, no fee. A foundling left on the courthouse steps in 1969, her shadow has leaned toward one person only, her daughter; she has never searched, in case nobody was looking. A widow, she lives over the Lantern Lake bait shop. Dry, exact, she talks to file boxes.

**The cast.** Nora, 29, Edie's daughter, a genetic counselor who thinks her mother merely observant; Dot Kessler, 61, court clerk, who reads mail upside down; Ambrose Teague, 74, county historian, who swept the courthouse at 17; Walt Dombrowski, 63, dairy farmer and coroner.

**The series engine.** Shadows lean only between parent and child or siblings, half-siblings included. The sight gives one fact, kin or not, never how, and needs a light and both living people in the room. Each death is first ruled an accident; Edie's tree reveals the motive: who inherits. The proof, killer included, is censuses, baptism books and deeds; consented DNA only confirms.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Shadow on the Stairs* — Ruth Vandermark, 81, dies falling downstairs; her heir is brother Otto, in memory care. A stranger claims to be their half-sister, but her shadow stands straight beside his: an actress hired by Otto's guardian, who was draining Ruth's accounts. Edie opens her foundling file.
2. *Drowned at Noon* — Vintner Carl Brandt, a lifelong swimmer, drowns days before selling the vineyard. His sister expects everything, until the foreman's shadow leans toward hers: their father's unacknowledged son, owed half, and he knew. Edie mails her DNA kit.
3. *The Greenhouse Shadow* — Hollis Ostrander dies in his greenhouse, heater vent stuffed shut, a week before signing a will favoring his gardener. His brother's shadow leans toward the gardener, but the baptism book names another father; Edie must prove which page lies. She tells Nora about the sight.
4. *Death by Lamplight* — Midwife Agnes Pruyn, 92, dies in a blackout, one birth ledger missing; four families want it unread. Ambrose breaks a promise made at 17 to shield a frightened mother, and an entry leads to a kitchen where Edie's shadow leans a second way, Nora beside her.

*Running thread:* who left a newborn at the courthouse, and which two families hid her; book 4 answers, giving Edie living kin with estates of their own.

**Why readers come back.** You see each shadow when Edie does. Every book names an heir and explains the death. Thursday pie at the Lock Seven Diner, a courthouse cat named Affidavit. The right family gets the house; nobody is harmed on the page.

**What makes it different.** The gift is a question, never an answer. The detecting is real genealogy, by a researcher whose family tree begins on a courthouse step.
