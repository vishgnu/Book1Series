# The Leaning Shadows Mysteries

**Genre:** Cozy paranormal mystery · **Readers:** Women 45–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Edie Farrow, 57, sees the shadows of blood relatives lean toward each other. She finds lost heirs for the county court in a Finger Lakes town; every book brings one unclaimed estate and one suspicious death.

**The setting.** Harrow County, New York: Lantern Lake, with Corwin Falls at its foot, vineyards on the east slope and a dry canal bed behind Main Street. Six canal-era families have intermarried and feuded here since 1825. The rule: an estate with no will and no known heir stays frozen until the court finds blood kin.

**The lead.** Edie works on commission: no heir, no fee. A foundling left on the courthouse steps in March 1969, she has never seen her own shadow lean. She wants every estate to reach its family; proving on paper what she sees takes months and tries her friends' patience. Dry, patient, exact; she talks to file boxes.

**The cast.** Dot Kessler, 61, chief court clerk and Edie's oldest friend, who reads Edie's mail upside down; Ambrose Teague, 74, county historian, who guards his archive like a dragon; Nora, 29, Edie's daughter, a genetic counselor who thinks her mother is simply observant; Walt Dombrowski, 63, dairy farmer and elected coroner, who brings Edie coffee to death scenes.

**The series engine.** Every book names an heir and explains a suspicious death. Edie's sight supplies one fact: kin, or not. It never says how, shows nothing of marriage, adoption or love, and needs a light and both people in the room. The rest is censuses, baptism books, deeds and Ambrose's archive; consented DNA only confirms. The court's docket never empties.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Shadow on the Stairs* — Ruth Vandermark, 81, dies in a fall on her stairs. A stranger claims to be her lost daughter; her shadow stands straight beside Ruth's sister. Edie names heir and killer and opens her own foundling file.
2. *Drowned at Noon* — Vintner Carl Brandt, a lifelong swimmer, drowns off his own dock. Two strangers' shadows cross like siblings' at his burial. Edie names his children and his killer and mails her own DNA kit.
3. *The Greenhouse Shadow* — Hollis Ostrander dies in his greenhouse, the heater vent stuffed shut. His brother's shadow leans toward the gardener, Hollis's unrecorded son. Edie proves heir and killer; Ambrose unlocks his archive for her DNA match.
4. *Death by Lamplight* — Midwife Agnes Pruyn, 92, dies in a blackout, one birth ledger missing. Edie finds heir and thief; a 1969 entry leads to a kitchen where her shadow leans.

*Running thread:* who left a newborn at the courthouse in March 1969; book 4 answers it and gives Edie living kin with estates of their own.

**Why readers come back.** You see each shadow when Edie does, and every proof is a document on the table. Thursday pie at the diner, grape harvest, courthouse gossip. Every case closes, the right family gets the house, and nobody is harmed on the page.

**What makes it different.** The gift is a question, never an answer: it says two people are kin and stops. The detecting is real genealogy, by a 57-year-old researcher whose own family tree begins on a courthouse step.
