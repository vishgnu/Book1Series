# The Leaning Shadows Mysteries

**Genre:** Cozy paranormal mystery · **Readers:** Women 45–75 · **Series:** four books, 60,000–70,000 words each, one complete case per book

**Logline.** Edie Farrow, 57, sees the shadows of close blood relatives lean toward each other. She traces heirs for a Finger Lakes county; every book brings one unclaimed estate and one suspicious death.

**The setting.** Harrow County: Lantern Lake, Corwin Falls, vineyards. Six canal-era families, feuding since 1825. When someone dies without a will, the county must prove blood kin at a hearing, or the estate passes to the state.

**The lead.** Edie is the county's genealogist, paid from the estate: no heir, no fee. A foundling left on the courthouse steps in 1969, her shadow has leaned toward one person only, her daughter; she has never searched, in case nobody was looking. Widowed, she lives over the bait shop. Dry, exact, she talks to file boxes.

**The cast.** Nora, 29, Edie's daughter, a genetic counselor who thinks her mother merely observant; Dot Kessler, 61, court clerk, who reads Edie's mail upside down; Ambrose Teague, 74, county historian, who swept the courthouse at 17; Walt Dombrowski, 63, dairy farmer and coroner, who brings Edie coffee to death scenes.

**The series engine.** Shadows lean only between parent and child or siblings. The sight gives one fact, kin or not, never how, and needs a light and both living people in the room; the hearing puts every claimant in one lit courtroom. Each death looks accidental; Edie's tree shows who gained: money, land or silence. The proof is censuses, baptism books and deeds; consented DNA only confirms.

**The four-book arc.**
1. *Shadow on the Stairs* — Ruth Vandermark, 81, falls downstairs; her heir is brother Otto, in memory care. A stranger claims to be their half-sister, but her shadow never leans toward his: an actress hired by Otto's guardian, who killed Ruth to hide his thefts. Edie opens her foundling file.
2. *Drowned at Noon* — Vintner Carl Brandt, a lifelong swimmer, drowns days before selling the vineyard. The foreman's shadow leans toward Carl's sister's: he is their father's unacknowledged son, owed half. He wanted the sale; she drowned Carl to keep the land. Edie mails her DNA kit.
3. *The Greenhouse Shadow* — Hollis Ostrander dies in his greenhouse a week before signing a will favoring his gardener. His brother's shadow leans toward the gardener, but the baptism book names another father; Edie must prove which page lies. The brother, the gardener's father, killed to inherit alone. Edie tells Nora about the sight.
4. *Death by Lamplight* — Midwife Agnes Pruyn, 92, dies in a blackout, one birth ledger missing; four families want it unread, and Lyle Corwin killed to hide his entry. Ambrose breaks a promise made at 17, and an entry leads to a kitchen where Edie's shadow leans toward a stranger, Nora beside her.

*Running thread:* who left a newborn at the courthouse, and which two families hid her; book 4 answers, giving Edie living kin with estates of their own.

**Why readers come back.** You see each shadow when Edie does. Every case closes: an heir named, a death explained, nobody harmed on the page. Thursday pie with Dot at the Lock Seven Diner, a courthouse cat named Affidavit.

**What makes it different.** The gift is a question, never an answer. The detecting is real genealogy, by a researcher whose family tree begins on a courthouse step.
