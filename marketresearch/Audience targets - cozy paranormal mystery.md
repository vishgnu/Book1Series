# Audience targets: who reads the series, and how to reach them

The audience profile for the business plan next to this file. It covers age groups, gender, reading habits and reach channels for the recommended cozy paranormal or cozy fantasy mystery series, and for the science-fiction adventure runner-up. The research was done on 14 November 2026, and the detailed notes with all sources are in `notes/AI books on Amazon KDP/reader_demographics.md`. Evidence labels match the report: [primary], [secondary], [vendor], [inference] and [unverified].

## The short version

**No survey of cozy mystery, cozy fantasy or witch-cozy readers exists.** The common belief that cozy readers are "women 50+" comes from an agent's remark around 2017 and an unsourced model [unverified]. It is partly supported by data on mystery readers in general. The audiences below are therefore **hypotheses to test**, built from the best data available by genre.

A cozy paranormal mystery sits where two audiences overlap:

| | Primary target | Secondary target |
|---|---|---|
| **Who** | Women aged 45-75 who read mysteries | Women aged 25-45 who read fantasy |
| **Why them** | The largest, heaviest-reading audience for mystery. The core of Kindle+ series reading [inference from primary data] | Fantasy readers skew young and female. Cozy fantasy is growing among them [primary for fantasy; unverified for cozy fantasy] |
| **Entry point** | "A village mystery with a little magic" | "A cozy fantasy with a mystery to solve" |
| **Where to find them** | Amazon, deal newsletters, Facebook | Amazon, Instagram, BookTok, Goodreads |
| **Biggest risk** | Prefer print. Must find the book on Kindle | Most hostile to AI-written books |

**Plan:** launch to the primary target. Test the secondary target with separate ads from month 5 and let the numbers decide.

## 1. What the data shows by genre

### Mystery readers: older and mostly women

In a YouGov survey of 995 US adults in July 2022 [primary]:

| Age group | Share who read mysteries |
|---|---|
| 18-29 | 25% |
| 30-44 | 25% |
| 45-64 | 27% |
| **65+** | **38%** |

- **Gender:** 35% of women read mysteries against 21% of men. That makes roughly **64% of mystery readers women** [inference].
- **Age mix:** roughly **61% of mystery readers are 45 or older**, and about 31% are 65 or older [inference from the same survey]. Older studies agree: Sisters in Crime and Bowker found in 2010 that about 70% of mystery buyers were over 45 [secondary].
- **Income and region:** readership is almost flat across incomes and regions.
- **Mystery is the most-read genre** among women and among people aged 65 and over [primary].

### Fantasy readers: younger and slightly more women

From the same survey [primary]:

| Age group | Share who read fantasy |
|---|---|
| **18-29** | **21%** |
| 30-44 | 18% |
| 45-64 | 14% |
| 65+ | 11% |

- **Gender:** 18% of women against 13% of men, so roughly **60% of fantasy readers are women** [inference].
- **Age mix:** about **56% of fantasy readers are under 45** [inference].
- **Cozy fantasy** is described as read "primarily by women, age 25-55, though skewing younger", many of them former young-adult readers [unverified]. Romantasy is driving fantasy's growth [secondary].

### Science-fiction readers: more men, higher incomes

From the same survey [primary]:

- **Age:** flat across age groups, from 17% to 20%.
- **Gender:** 22% of men against 15% of women, so roughly **58% of sci-fi readers are men** [inference]. Among buyers of printed books, 43% of men and 23% of women buy sci-fi [primary].
- **Income:** readership rises from 15% under $50,000 a year to **25% at $100,000 or more**. Sci-fi is the only genre here with a clear income tilt.

**Sci-fi runner-up audience:** men and women aged 30-65 with higher incomes, reached through Amazon, BookBub and Reddit, plus Facebook interests in established series [inference].

### Who reads the most

A series earns its money from heavy readers, and they skew older and female [primary, YouGov August 2025]:

| | 18-29 | 30-44 | 45-64 | 65+ | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Read 20+ books a year | 7% | 5% | 10% | **17%** | 6% | **13%** |
| Read a book every day | 22% | 16% | 20% | **29%** | 19% | 24% |

Kindle Unlimited readers read about **9 books a month** on average [vendor, Written Word Media]. Amazon publishes no age or gender data for Kindle+ subscribers.

### Formats by age

Share of US adults who read each format in the past 12 months [primary, Pew, October 2025, 8,046 adults]:

| Format | 18-29 | 30-49 | 50-64 | 65+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Print | 66% | 64% | 61% | 65% |
| **Ebook** | **41%** | 33% | 26% | 23% |
| Audiobook | 32% | 33% | 23% | **13%** |

**What this means for the plan** [inference]:

- Older readers read the most, but fewer of them read ebooks. The paperback of every novel matters for the primary target. A large-print edition is worth testing.
- Younger readers are more ebook-heavy and suit Kindle+ well.
- Audio is the lowest priority for readers over 65.

## 2. Attitudes to AI books by age

- **56%** of US adults want any AI contribution to a book disclosed, and **28%** say AI should not be used at any stage [primary, YouGov/Black Château, August 2025].
- **Young adults are the most negative**, not the least. 61% of under-30s say AI will worsen creative thinking, against about 42% of people 65 and over [secondary, Pew 2025]. In Sweden, 72% of young adults are negative about AI-written books [secondary, Bokbarometern 2026].
- **Older readers are less sure they can spot AI writing.** 26% of people 65 and over say they cannot do it at all, against 5% of 18-29-year-olds [primary, YouGov August 2025].

**No age group sees AI authorship as a selling point.** The secondary target, young BookTok-style readers, is the most likely to react publicly and negatively. That makes the plan's openness rule more important for that audience, not less.

**Do not treat older readers' weaker AI detection as an opportunity.** KDP requires disclosure to Amazon, and 56% of readers want to be told. A series that hides AI risks the reviews of the heavy-reader audience it depends on. The plan's public line stays: "story, characters and editing by humans, written with AI".

## 3. How to reach each audience

| Channel | Primary: mystery women 45-75 | Secondary: fantasy women 25-45 | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Amazon store, also-boughts, Kindle+** | Core | Core | The first discovery channel for 68% of readers [vendor] |
| **Deal newsletters** (BookBub, Freebooksy, Bargain Booksy) | **Strong.** BookBub runs cozy mystery lists | Medium | Heavy, deal-driven readers. No published age data [vendor] |
| **Facebook groups and ads** | **Strong hypothesis.** Cozy reader groups exist, for example "Save Our Cozies" [secondary] | Medium | Facebook skews older, but no book-specific age data was found [inference] |
| **Instagram / Bookstagram** | Weak | **Strong** | In Germany, Instagram is the top social source of book tips for 16-29-year-olds [primary] |
| **TikTok / BookTok** | Weak | Medium to strong | Young and female. Also the most likely place for an AI backlash [secondary] |
| **Goodreads** | Medium | Medium | 46% of surveyed readers use it to find books [vendor] |
| **Reddit** (r/cozymystery, r/CozyFantasy) | Listening only | Listening only | Strict rules against self-promotion. Use it to learn what readers want |
| **Your own newsletter** | Core | Core | The only channel you own, and where you can survey your readers |

## 4. What ads can actually target

| Platform | Age and gender targeting | What you target instead |
|---|---|---|
| **Amazon Ads** (Sponsored Products) | **No** [primary] | Keywords, comparable books by ASIN (Amazon's product number), categories, automatic targeting |
| **BookBub Ads** | **No** [primary] | Comparable authors, categories such as Cozy Mysteries, ebook or audio, retailer and region |
| **Meta** (Facebook and Instagram) | **Yes.** With Advantage+ audiences only the minimum age is enforced, so use the original audience settings for a strict test [secondary; verify in Ads Manager] | Age, gender, US location, interests such as comparable authors or cozy mysteries |

On Amazon and BookBub, **the audience is defined by comparable authors**. Pick comparable authors to match each target [inference]:

- **Primary:** established witch-cozy and paranormal cozy mystery series with long backlists.
- **Secondary:** cozy fantasy authors whose books trend on Instagram and BookTok.

Build the list during the hand check of the category in weeks 1-2. Note which authors appear repeatedly in the top 20 and in the "also bought" rows of the bestsellers you read.

## 5. Testing the audiences

The age hypotheses can only be tested on Meta. Run the test around book 2's release in month 8, with book 1 promoted and book 2 just out [inference]:

1. **Set up four ad sets** with identical ads, women only, United States: ages 25-44, 45-54, 55-64 and 65+. Use the original audience settings so the age limits hold.
2. **Spend $5 a day on each for 10 days**, all pointing to book 1. That totals about $200, within the ads budget.
3. **Measure:** cost per click, click-through rate and, where possible, sales and Kindle+ pages by ad set. Amazon Attribution can track this, but its availability for KDP authors was not verified [unverified]. If it isn't available, run the ad sets one after another and compare sales and page reads in each period.
4. **Keep the winning age group or two**, and move the comparable-author lists on Amazon and BookBub toward that audience.
5. **Survey the newsletter** after the first 200 subscribers with three questions: age group, favourite genre, and how they found the series. It is the only way to get real data on who your readers are.

## 6. Germany

- No German data splits crime or fantasy buyers by age and gender.
- New Adult and Young Adult purchases grew strongly from 2021 to 2025, but young buyers fell in 2025 [secondary].
- Women read more regularly than men [secondary].
- English-language Kindle+ readers in Germany are a niche within a niche. Treat Germany as a later market for a German edition, aimed at the same two audiences through Instagram and the German Kindle and Tolino stores.

## What this rests on

- **No cozy-specific reader data exists.** Both targets are inferred from mystery-wide and fantasy-wide surveys, mainly one YouGov sample of 995 adults. Margins for subgroups are about ±7-10 points, so treat the shares as directional.
- **The secondary target's cozy-fantasy profile is unverified.**
- **Platform targeting details change often.** Check Meta's audience settings in Ads Manager before running the test.
