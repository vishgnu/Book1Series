# Build a series, not a book farm

Two people can build a part-time side income on Amazon KDP with AI help. The evidence points to a slow business that grows with the catalogue, not to passive income. The workable option is a human-led, AI-assisted series in a genre where readers already spend heavily: romance and romantasy, mystery/thriller, or cozy fantasy and LitRPG. It would be enrolled in KDP Select, so it is read through Kindle+ (the new name for Kindle Unlimited since October 2026), and it would be backed by an email list. The weak options are the ones AI made easy: low-content, activity and illustrated books, and fast AI self-help.

The verified numbers are discouraging for newcomers. In Written Word Media's 2025 survey, **about 80% of authors with 1-3 books earn under $100 a month**. Authors earning **$1,000-5,000 a month have about 35 titles on average**. A 2026 working paper finds that **books with substantial AI text are 20% of genre-fiction titles but take only 11.3% of revenue**, and that revenue per title is falling as the catalogue swells. Amazon allows AI but requires you to disclose AI-*generated* text, images and translations. US law gives purely prompted text no copyright, and German law points the same way. In 2026 the FTC made the best-known "AI publishing" course operator pay $1.5M over false earnings claims. A lean setup costs about $230-360 for the first year. A practical two-person setup costs about $690-950 a year before any human editing or cover design. Expect near-zero income in year one. Meaningful monthly income comes with a catalogue of roughly 25-35 titles, so it takes years. German residents also need a US tax form, a Finanzamt registration and, for non-fiction only, attention to the EU AI Act.

## Amazon allows AI if you disclose it, and copyright protects only the human part

### KDP's disclosure rule, upload limit and content rules

KDP's content guidelines require you to tell Amazon when a book contains **AI-generated text, images or translations**. "AI-assisted" content needs no disclosure. That means content you created yourself and then edited, refined, error-checked or brainstormed with AI tools. The page is explicit that AI-generated text stays AI-generated "even if you applied substantial edits afterwards" ([KDP Content Guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200672390)) [primary]. The page carries no date and shows no sign of a revision since the rule appeared in 2023. AI itself is not banned. Two claims from compliance blogs were **not found** on Amazon's pages: that disclosure is made per format, and that no public "AI" badge appears on the product page [unverified].

The upload limit is now **2 new titles per book format per week**, counted both when you create a title and when you submit it, resetting at 00:00 UTC on Sunday ([KDP, Create a Book](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G202172740)) [primary]. The 2023 limit of three a day is out of date. For two part-timers the limit does not matter. It does show that Amazon is pushing back against volume publishing.

KDP's **Guide to Kindle Content Quality** is the rule set AI publishers most often break ([KDP](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200952510)) [primary]. It bans content that "disappoints our customers", including:

- content that is too short, or not significantly different from another book in the Kindle Store;
- content that is excessively reused, recycled or repeated within or across books;
- content that is poorly translated, or does not give an enjoyable reading experience;
- companion guides such as summaries, study guides and workbooks, with limited exceptions.

Typos and formatting faults can lead to removal from sale or a public quality warning on the product page. The **metadata guidelines** ban unauthorised references to other authors or titles, covers that closely resemble another book, and review quotes or review requests in the description. They suggest a different pen name if yours resembles a popular author's ([KDP Metadata Guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201097560)) [primary].

On enforcement, the evidence is thin. An attorney blog reports a rise in KDP terminations tied to AI-assisted books ([AMZ Sellers Attorney](https://www.amazonsellers.attorney/blog/increase-in-kdp-account-terminations-over-ai-assisted-books-and-poor-customer-experience-concerns)) [secondary]. Claims of a January 2026 AI-detection push and an April 2026 "enforcement sweep" each come from a single vendor [unverified]. One guide says AI disclosure blocks Kindle+ enrolment. Nothing on Amazon's pages supports that, and Amazon's own AI translations are eligible, so treat it as false.

### KDP Select means 90 days of ebook exclusivity

Enrolling in KDP Select puts a book in Kindle+ and pays it from the KDP Select Global Fund. In return the ebook must be **exclusive to Amazon for each 90-day term**, apart from public libraries. Print and other formats can be sold elsewhere. Terms renew automatically, and you can cancel only within three days of a term starting. Exclusivity also covers any audio edition. Amazon bans any marketing that rewards Kindle+ readers for reading, such as bonus content or contest entries ([KDP Select](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GD9PMU58BV24QFZ7); [Royalties in Kindle+](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201541130)) [primary].

### Amazon now ships its own AI translation and narration

**Kindle Translate** launched on 6 November 2025 and is still an **invite-only beta**. It now translates from English into Spanish, German, French, Italian and Portuguese, and from each of those into English. Translated titles carry a public "Kindle Translate" label, can be enrolled in KDP Select, and cannot be edited directly ([Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/books-and-authors/amazon-kindle-translate-books-authors)) [primary].

**Virtual Voice** AI narration is free "at this time" and is an invite-only beta for the **US marketplace only**. It supports English, Spanish, Italian and French, **but not German**. List prices run **$3.99-$14.99 with a 40% royalty** ([KDP help](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GHJW2N8GLTQLK9TY)) [primary]. Since the Kindle+ rename, Virtual Voice audiobooks join Kindle+ automatically when the ebook is in KDP Select ([Written Word Media](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/kdp-global-fund-payouts/)) [secondary]. ACX, Audible's production platform, says audiobooks "must be narrated by a human unless otherwise authorized" ([ACX](https://help.acx.com/s/article/issues-with-producer)) [primary]. No evidence was found on what AI-translated or AI-narrated titles earn.

### US copyright protects the human share of the book

The US Copyright Office's report on copyrightability, published on 29 January 2025, concludes that "prompts alone … are unlikely to satisfy" the human-authorship requirement. Human contributions that are perceptible in the work stay protected, including the author's own text, selection and arrangement, and modifications ([Copyright Office, Part 2](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)) [primary]. Nothing newer on copyrightability is listed on copyright.gov. On **2 March 2026 the Supreme Court declined to hear Thaler v. Perlmutter** ([docket 25-449](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-449.html)) [primary]. The human-authorship requirement therefore stands. That case involved no claimed human input, so it did not decide how much human involvement is enough.

The business consequence follows directly [inference]. A book that was only prompted is a weak asset, because anyone can copy it. A book whose text the authors wrote and shaped is protectable. Under KDP's definitions it is also more likely to count as "AI-assisted" and need no disclosure.

### Germany and the EU

German copyright law protects only "persönliche geistige Schöpfungen", personal intellectual creations (§2(2) UrhG) [primary]. That points the same way as the US rule, though no German case law was checked.

The EU AI Act's **Article 50 transparency duties apply from 2 August 2026** (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Art. 113). The Digital Omnibus is now **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744**, published on 24 July 2026 and in force since 27 July. It delays the high-risk rules to 2 December 2027 for Annex III systems and 2 August 2028 for Annex I systems. It leaves Article 50 itself unchanged and gives AI *providers* until **2 December 2026** to mark output from systems already on the market [primary, read via the EU Publications Office because EUR-Lex was down].

For authors, the relevant duty is Article 50(4) on AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest. It does not apply where the text has had human review and someone holds editorial responsibility. The Commission's Article 50 guidelines list **"AI-generated fantasy novels" as outside the duty**. They give a self-published, unreviewed AI book on climate change as an example inside it, and they treat a commercial self-publisher as a professional "deployer". The human review must examine the substance, including fact-checking, and spell-checking is not enough. The responsible person should be named, for example in the edition notice [primary]. **For fiction the AI Act adds nothing. For non-fiction on public-interest topics, it requires real human review and a named editor.**

## Money concentrates in genre series, while AI floods the low-content shelves

### Survey data: most authors earn little, and income follows catalogue size

The most useful income data is **Written Word Media's 2025 indie author survey**. It has 1,346 self-reported responses and skews toward prolific commercial-fiction writers. **44% earn $100 or less a month, 13% earn over $5,000 and 8% earn over $10,000.** About **80% of authors with 1-3 books earn under $100 a month**. Average catalogue size rises with income band ([Written Word Media 2025](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/2025-indie-author-survey-results-insights-into-self-publishing-for-authors/)) [primary]:

| Monthly income | Average titles |
|---|---|
| Under $100 | About 10 |
| $500-999 | About 26 |
| $1,000-5,000 | About 35 |
| $5,000-10,000 | About 54 |

These are averages from a self-selected group and show correlation, not cause. The same survey finds that authors with an email list earn a median of about **$300 a month, against about $15 without one**. The 17- and 29-book figures in the first version came from the 2020 survey.

Written Word Media's **2026 mid-year survey** of 582 authors found 30% reporting a declining business and 23% a growing one. The decline was sharpest among authors earning $2,500-10,000 a month, and respondents blamed an Amazon algorithm change. Growing authors were more likely to be increasing output, running a newsletter, doing translations and using AI as a tool for ads, covers, translation and narration. Beginners saw AI "almost entirely negatively" because of discoverability ([Written Word Media 2026 mid-year](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/2026-mid-year-indie-author-survey/)) [primary]. The full 2026 survey asks about AI use, and its results, due in 2027, may give the first income-by-AI-use data.

The often-quoted **$13,500 median annual income** comes from the ALLi 2025 survey of 1,520 qualified respondents. It is gross income and covers only authors who spend over half their working time on publishing or earn over half their income from it. The **mean is $84,995**, about six times the median, so a few authors earn most of the money ([ALLi data](https://www.allianceindependentauthors.org/facts/)) [primary]. Both surveys over-sample engaged authors, and Written Word Media sells promotions, so the true median across all KDP publishers is almost certainly lower [inference].

### The AI flood is growing the catalogue faster than revenue

The strongest evidence on AI books is a 2026 working paper by **Chakrabarty, Liu, Ginsburg and Dhillon** ([arXiv 2607.20349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20349)) [primary]. It runs AI detection on **14,419 self-published genre-fiction titles** released from January 2023 to March 2026, with sales tracked to June 2026. **Books with substantial AI text were 20% of titles but 12.1% of sales and 11.3% of revenue.** Books with no AI were 62.9% of titles and 72.5% of revenue. Titles with any sales grew **19.2x while revenue grew 8.9x**, and revenue per book fell in six of eight genres.

The paper's limits matter:

- Revenue is gross consumer spending, and the data does not separate Kindle+ reads from purchases.
- Detection uses the Pangram tool, whose accuracy figures are the vendor's own.
- The results are observational, and the paper is still under review.
- The no-AI lead **narrowed** over time. In fantasy, where AI arrived latest, no-AI revenue per book **rose 35%**.
- Some AI books succeed. The top AI book grossed $643,000.

A separate NBER study by **Reimers and Waldfogel** estimates that **52.7% of new ebook releases in 2025 contained AI text, up from 23% in 2023**, based on 1,000-word previews. Monthly releases tripled to over 300,000 by late 2025, and AI books are "less used" ([NBER w34777](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34777)) [primary].

These are averages, so they cannot show that a disciplined, human-edited AI workflow fails [inference]. They do show that **AI-heavy output earns less per title than human writing on average**, and that every new title competes in a pool where revenue per title is shrinking.

### What a sale and a page read pay

Ebook royalties are **35% or 70%**. Since **7 July 2026 the 70% option covers list prices of $2.99-$12.99**, the first change since 2007. The 70% option deducts a delivery fee of **$0.15 per MB** in the US, which averages about $0.06 a sale ([KDP royalties](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200644210)) [primary]. Paperbacks pay **60% of list price at $9.99 or more and 50% below that**, minus printing cost ([KDP paperback royalty](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834330)) [primary]. Most genre paperbacks are priced above $9.99, so they get 60%.

Kindle+ pays from a monthly fund shared out by pages read. Amazon announces the fund size, which was **$71.1 million in October 2026**, but not a per-page rate [primary]. Written Word Media's tracker puts the US rate at **$0.0041-$0.0054 per page** from January 2025 to September 2026 ([Written Word Media](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/kdp-global-fund-payouts/)) [secondary]. Since October 2026 audiobook listening draws on the same fund, so the future rate is unknown.

At about $0.0048 a page, a reader who finishes a 300-page book earns the author about **$1.44**. A $4.99 sale at 70% earns about **$3.40** [inference]. Kindle+ therefore pays off mainly through long books and series binge-reading.

## A working setup costs hundreds of dollars until you pay for human editing

All prices below were checked on each vendor's own pricing page [primary]:

- **General chatbots:** Claude Pro costs **$20 a month**. ChatGPT Plus showed **€23 a month** from Germany, where the US price was not visible.
- **Fiction writing tools:** Sudowrite costs **$19, $29 or $59 a month**. NovelCrafter costs **$4-$20 a month** plus your own model API costs. NovelAI costs **$10-$25 a month**.
- **Cover tools:** Midjourney costs **$10 a month**. Adobe Firefly starts at **$9.99 a month**. Firefly is often called the safe choice for commercial use, but **Adobe's IP indemnity applies only to eligible enterprise customers**, not individual plans.
- **Formatting:** Atticus costs **$147 once** and runs on any platform. Vellum costs **$199.99 for ebooks only or $249.99 with print**, and runs only on Mac. Reedsy Studio's core features are free.
- **Keyword research:** KDSpy costs **$79** and Publisher Rocket **$199**, both one-time.
- **Grammar:** ProWritingAid costs **€120 a year**.

User feedback and a 70,000-word test report that the fiction tools **still lose character consistency and repeat phrases across a novel without human oversight** ([UC Strategies](https://ucstrategies.com/news/sudowrite-review-i-tested-the-22-month-ai-against-chatgpt-across-70000-words/)) [secondary]. That matters, because KDP's quality rules ban repetitive content. Human editing time is a real cost.

Reedsy's own data puts a human cover at **$880** on average across its marketplace. Its cost guide gives $930. For an 80,000-word novel, Reedsy puts a **copy edit at about $2,160 and a proofread at about $1,600** ([Reedsy covers](https://reedsy.com/blog/book-cover-design-cost/); [Reedsy cost guide](https://reedsy.com/blog/guide/how-to-self-publish-a-book/cost-to-self-publish/)) [vendor]. KDP supplies free ISBNs for print editions, and those work only on KDP. Ebooks need no ISBN. **Bowker's $125 for one or $295 for ten applies only to US publishers.** German partners would buy from the German ISBN agency, MVB, whose prices were not checked, or use KDP's free ISBN.

| Setup (first year, two people) | Components | Approximate cost [inference] |
|---|---|---|
| Lean | One chatbot, Reedsy Studio, KDP's free ISBN, an AI cover tool for a few months, free grammar tools, no editor | **About $230-$360** |
| Recommended | Two chatbots ($480, or about €550 at German prices), Atticus ($147), Midjourney or Firefly ($60-$120 a year), optional KDSpy ($79), optional ProWritingAid (about $120), with the partners editing each other's work | **About $690-$950**. **About $1,570-$1,830** with one human cover for book one |
| Comfortable (first title) | Sudowrite or NovelCrafter, Atticus or Vellum, Publisher Rocket, a human cover, a professional copy edit | **About $3,600-$5,000**. More with a proofread |

The totals are the report's own arithmetic on verified list prices. They exclude advertising, promotions, tax-adviser fees and German VAT where a price is shown without it. Professional editing is the biggest decision. It makes up most of the comfortable budget, and at the earnings levels above a single book is unlikely to pay it back quickly [inference].

## Discovery takes money and time, and Amazon's review rules are strict

No authoritative description of Amazon's ranking algorithm was found. The "30-day new-release boost" and similar ideas are practitioner lore. The best-supported points are economic: Kindle+ rewards page reads, and income rises with series and catalogue size (Written Word Media 2025) [primary; correlational].

**Email lists need care.** Written Word Media's data links an email list with far higher income. But KDP's content-quality guide lists "links to web forms that request customer information (e.g., email address …)" among **prohibited external links**. It warns that disruptive content can lead to account termination ([KDP](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200952510)) [primary]. Many authors link to their own website instead, but Amazon's page does not say whether that is allowed. **Ask KDP support how to point readers to a newsletter before putting any link in a book.**

Advertising arithmetic is unforgiving. A worked example shows that **a $3.00 royalty at a 10% conversion rate supports a maximum of about $0.30 per click** before an ad loses money ([Vappingo](https://www.vappingo.com/word-blog/?p=11834)) [vendor/affiliate]. Genre cost-per-click ranges circulate, but none was found on a citable page. An unknown author with one book is likely to convert below 10% [inference]. Advertising cost of sale understates the value of ads for a series, because a sale of book one leads to read-through ([ScribeCount](https://scribecount.com/author-resource/troubleshooting-author-resources/amazon-ads-dashboard)) [vendor]. That is the main reason to hold ads until two or three linked books exist [inference].

BookBub Featured Deals are the best-known paid promotion. BookBub's own price list puts genre-fiction deals at free or $0.99 at **about $290-$880 for the US only**, or $420-$1,110 for all regions. Deals priced above $0.99 cost $1,000-$4,200 ([BookBub pricing](https://www.bookbub.com/partners/pricing)) [primary]. BookBub itself gives two acceptance figures: about 25% overall and 10-15% for US deals ([BookBub selection process](https://insights.bookbub.com/how-bookbub-selection-process-works/); [BookBub support](https://support.bookbub.com/articles/submission-requirements-and-advice/)) [primary]. In practice a deal needs an established book with reviews [inference]. BookTok is divided over AI, and creators have faced public "AI accusation" pile-ons ([Good Story Co.](https://goodstoryco.substack.com/p/the-ai-accusation-heard-round-booktok)) [secondary].

**Review rules.** Amazon's Community Guidelines and KDP's review help page **allow free advance review copies**. They do not allow requiring a review, influencing it, or adding gift cards or other rewards. **Review swaps and reviews from family, friends or anyone with a personal connection are banned** ([Amazon Community Guidelines](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GLHXEX85MENUE4XF); [KDP Customer Reviews](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G202101910)) [primary]. Amazon's 2016 ban on incentivised reviews exempted books. The **FTC's fake-review rule** has been in force since 21 October 2024 (16 CFR Part 465). It bans fake and AI-generated reviews, incentives tied to a positive or negative review, and undisclosed insider reviews. The penalty is **$53,088 per violation**, the January 2025 level, which the FTC left unchanged in 2026 ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials); [Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-08-22/html/2024-18519.htm)) [primary]. The two partners must never review each other's books, and friends and family should not review them either.

## The biggest risks are dilution, account loss and paid courses

**Dilution** comes first. Over half of new ebook releases now contain AI text, and revenue per title is falling (Chakrabarty et al.; Reimers & Waldfogel) [primary]. A new title starts in a more crowded pool. The Kindle+ fund is now also shared with audiobooks, which may thin the per-page rate further [inference].

**Reader distrust** is real. A YouGov poll of 1,000 US adults in August 2025 found that **56% want AI-written books disclosed and 61% would feel less fulfilled** after learning a book was AI-written ([YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/52728-reality-checks-talkshow-ai-books-desiree-duffy)) [primary]. The Swedish Publishers' Association's Bokbarometern 2026, run by Kantar with 1,031 interviews, found **two-thirds of readers negative about AI-written books and 3% positive** ([Svenska Förläggareföreningen](https://forlaggare.se/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Bokbarometern-2026.pdf)) [primary].

**Other retailers** are less hostile than the first version said. Each policy below was read on the retailer's own site [primary]:

- **Apple Books** accepts AI-generated books with an "AI Generated by" credit and needs no disclosure for AI-assisted work.
- **IngramSpark** and **Draft2Digital** accept AI-assisted books with real human editing. IngramSpark requires attribution.
- **Kobo** rejects books that are "wholly or partially" AI-generated.
- **Google Play Books** has no AI-specific policy.

A human-led, AI-assisted book can therefore be sold widely if the partners leave KDP Select later. A mostly AI-generated one would lose Kobo [inference].

**Account loss** is the risk most likely to wipe out the business. KDP's terms let Amazon suspend or terminate an account, withhold royalties for three months, forfeit them for guideline breaches and bar a new account. Amazon sends appeal instructions with the notice ([KDP Terms](https://kdp.amazon.com/terms-and-conditions)) [primary]. Its Sales Ranking page warns that reads or purchases from accounts manipulating the system can mean "termination of your account and loss of royalties" ([KDP](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201648140)) [primary]. Written Word Media names abnormal page-read spikes as a trigger ([Written Word Media](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/kdp-account-health/)) [secondary]. The widely repeated claim that "90% are never reinstated" has no source. Clean traffic, honest disclosure and no reader-incentive schemes are the main defences, and even then an account can be lost [inference].

**Courses and services** are the risk the evidence documents best. On **13 April 2026 the FTC announced that Publishing.com and its principals, Christian and Rasmus Mikkelsen, would pay $1.5 million**. The FTC alleged false earnings claims for "AI Publishing Academy" and "Publishing Accelerator". It also alleged refunds conditioned on positive testimonials and reviews from employees and relatives that were not disclosed. It said most buyers never earned the promised income. The final order was approved on 2 July 2026 ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/04/publishingcom-pay-15-million-misleading-consumers-about-how-much-income-they-could-earn-using); [FTC case page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/242-3055-publishingcom-matter)) [primary]. According to the complaint, the main course typically cost **$1,995 and the Accelerator upsell $9,800**. That is ten times the recommended first-year tool budget. Scammers have also used Amazon trademarks to sell fake "KDP" services, and Amazon has since won a multi-million-dollar judgment against some of them ([Writer Beware](https://writerbeware.blog/2023/07/07/how-scammers-are-using-amazon-and-amazon-trademarks-to-rip-writers-off/)) [secondary]. Warning signs include income promises, free-video sales funnels, refunds tied to testimonials and paid reviews that aren't disclosed.

## A realistic plan: one series, human-led, measured in titles

The strategy below is the report's synthesis [inference], built from the evidence above.

**Choose the lane.** Write commercial genre fiction, as a series, in a subgenre both partners actually read. On the demand data, the candidates are romance/romantasy, mystery/thriller and cozy fantasy/LitRPG. Fantasy is where the no-AI revenue premium is strongest. Skip low-content, AI colouring and activity books, and fast self-help. That is where AI users crowd, and where the evidence shows saturation without earnings.

**Keep the authorship human.** Use AI for brainstorming, outlining, continuity checks, line suggestions and covers, and have the partners write and substantially shape the prose. That keeps the text protectable under US and German law and inside KDP's "AI-assisted" definition. Disclose honestly when any text, image or translation is AI-generated. An AI cover counts as AI-generated.

**Split the work.** One partner can own drafting and the other editing, continuity and the story bible, with roles swapping for marketing. Two readers checking each other's work partly replaces the $1,600-$2,160 professional edit the lean budget can't afford, though not fully.

**Distribute through Amazon first.** Enrol in KDP Select, whose page-read pay favours long series, and accept 90-day ebook exclusivity. Print can still go wide. Ask KDP how to point readers to a newsletter before adding any link, because direct sign-up form links are prohibited. Because the books are human-led, going wide later remains an option on every major retailer except possibly Kobo.

**Hold paid marketing.** Keep ads off until at least three books in the series are live. Then test small, using the break-even arithmetic above, and stop any campaign that cannot beat it.

**Time to income** is best counted in titles. The 2025 survey averages are under $100 a month at about 10 titles, $500-999 at about 26 and $1,000-5,000 at about 35. That comes from mostly human-written, self-reported data. The AI revenue gap suggests an AI-assisted catalogue may need more titles, not fewer. No source measures how fast two part-timers can produce publishable novels, so here are two illustrative paces:

| Pace (both partners together) | 26 titles | 35 titles |
|---|---|---|
| One novel a month | About 2 years | About 3 years |
| One novel every two months | About 4 years | About 6 years |

**Plan for year one to earn little or nothing.** Treat it as building the asset and learning the craft. Re-assess after the first complete three-book series, using actual Kindle+ read-through rather than hope.

**Startup budget.** Plan on about $690-$950 for the first year for two people, or about $1,570-$1,830 with a professional cover for book one. Spend on advertising only after book three. No course is needed. The lean route at about $230-$360 works if the partners accept AI covers and do all the editing themselves.

**German and EU admin.**

- **US tax.** KDP's tax interview produces a W-8 form. Without a treaty claim, KDP withholds 30% from US royalties ([KDP](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201274750)). The US-Germany treaty rate for copyright royalties is **0%** ([IRS Table 1](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-lbi/tax-treaty-table-1.pdf)) [primary]. Withholding applies to Amazon.com ebook sales and to Kindle+ fund payments, but not to Amazon.de sales.
- **Finanzamt.** Register for a tax number. Writing is a named freelance activity under §18 EStG, which suggests "freiberuflich" rather than a trade. Whether a high-volume AI-assisted operation still counts as writing is a question for a Steuerberater.
- **VAT.** The Kleinunternehmer exemption under §19 UStG applies up to **€25,000 turnover in the prior year and €100,000 in the current year** [primary]. An income tax return is still required.
- **Still open.** How two partners should be structured, and how EU VAT applies to ebook sales through Amazon, need a Steuerberater.
- **Translation.** Kindle Translate's German-English pair would let German partners publish in both markets from one manuscript. It is still invite-only, translated titles carry a public label, and nothing is known about what they earn. Virtual Voice does not support German.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. "Which AI book niche pays?" matters less than "what will still earn when AI output is unlimited?" The verified data says the answer is reader trust and series read-through, not volume. Books made mostly by AI are a fifth of genre-fiction titles but take about a ninth of the revenue. Amazon, other retailers, US and German copyright law, the EU AI Act and the FTC all draw their line in the same place: human authorship and honest disclosure. AI's real advantage for two part-timers is lower cost and faster drafting, which shortens the long road to a catalogue of roughly 25-35 titles. It does not remove that road. Anyone selling a shortcut is, on the FTC's record, the likeliest way to lose money.

One question decides whether this plan is sound, and it can be answered cheaply. It is how fast the partners can produce a novel they would be willing to sign. Writing one book with the recommended setup will show that. Before the first upload, also ask KDP how to link a newsletter, and ask a Steuerberater about the partnership structure.
