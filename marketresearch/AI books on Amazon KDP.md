# Build a series, not a book farm

Two people can build a part-time side income on Amazon KDP with AI help, but the evidence points to a slow, catalogue-driven business, not passive income. The workable option is a human-led, AI-assisted series in a genre where readers already spend heavily (romance and romantasy, mystery/thriller, cozy fantasy and LitRPG), enrolled in Kindle Unlimited and backed by an email list. The weak options are the ones AI made easy: low-content, activity and illustrated books, and fast AI self-help. The best available numbers are discouraging for newcomers. In the most-cited author survey, **roughly 80% of authors with 1-3 books earn under $100 a month**, and **$1,000+ a month tends to coincide with about 20-30 titles** (secondary, self-reported data). A 2026 study reports that **books with substantial AI text make up about 20% of titles but only about 11% of revenue**, and that revenue per title is falling as the catalogue swells. Amazon allows AI but requires you to tell it about AI-*generated* text, images and translations. US law gives purely AI-generated text no copyright. In April 2026 the FTC fined the best-known "AI publishing" course operator $1.5M over false earnings claims. A lean setup costs about $300-450 for the first title; a practical two-person setup costs about $700-950 a year before any human editing or cover design. Expect near-zero income in year one. Meaningful monthly income coincides with roughly 17-30 titles, so it is measured in years. German residents also need to deal with US tax withholding forms, registration with the Finanzamt and, from August 2026, the EU AI Act's transparency rules.

## Every figure here arrived second-hand, and some conflict

**Read this before any number below.** All four researchers behind this report were blocked by network restrictions from opening primary sources: Amazon KDP help pages, copyright.gov, ftc.gov, the Written Word Media and ALLi survey reports, the Authors Guild, Publishers Weekly and the academic preprint on AI books. **No figure in this report was checked against its primary page.** Everything comes from search-engine summaries of those pages or from secondary sites. Many of those sites are vendors or affiliates selling KDP tools, AI writing software or courses, so they have a commercial interest in making KDP look lucrative and easy. Several prices and dates conflict between sources, and the report flags each conflict. Anyone making money decisions should re-check the KDP content guidelines, royalty pages and tool prices directly.

To keep the evidence levels apart, the report labels claims as follows:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[primary, via summary]** | The source is the authority itself (Amazon, FTC, a survey publisher, or law-firm summaries of a Copyright Office report or court ruling), but only a search summary was read. This is the strongest evidence available here, and it is still not verified. |
| **[secondary]** | News, industry blogs or analysts reporting someone else's data. |
| **[vendor/affiliate]** | A site selling a competing tool, service, course or affiliate links. Treat as marketing. |
| **[inference]** | Arithmetic or reasoning by the researchers or the report writer, not a sourced fact. |
| **[unverified/conflicting]** | Single-source or contradicted by another source. |

Vendor pages repeatedly circulated "passive income" figures such as "$800-$3,000/month for 4-10 books", "$200-$1,200+/month" per niche and "+180% low-content uploads". The researchers traced none of them to data, so the report leaves them out.

## Amazon allows AI if you disclose it, and copyright protects only the human part

### KDP's disclosure rule and upload cap date from 2023

Since **September 2023**, KDP has required publishers to tell Amazon when a book contains **AI-generated text, images (including cover and interior art) or translations**. "AI-assisted" use does not need disclosure. That covers human-created work that was brainstormed, grammar-checked or refined with AI ([Authors Guild](https://authorsguild.org/news/amazons-new-disclosure-policy-for-ai-generated-book-content-is-a-welcome-first-step/); [ADT Magazine](https://adtmag.com/Articles/2023/10/02/AWS-Requires-KDP-authors-to-disclose-AI.aspx)) [secondary]. Compliance blogs add three points. Heavy human editing of AI-generated text does not turn it into "AI-assisted". Disclosure is made per title and per format. The answer stays internal to Amazon, with no public "AI" badge on the product page ([Univers Studio](https://univers.studio/blog/ai-generated-content-kdp/); [ScribeCount](https://scribecount.com/author-resource/protecting-books/kdp-ai-content-policy-the-compliance-guide)) [vendor/affiliate]. AI itself is not banned. Failing to disclose AI-generated content is the violation ([Inkrify](https://inkrify.com/blog/staying-compliant-with-amazon-kdp-ai-content-policy-in-2026/)) [vendor/affiliate]. **Nobody read the official KDP wording (help topic G200672390)**, and nothing shows whether Amazon revised it in 2025-2026.

Amazon also announced a **limit of three new titles per day per account** in 2023. It told The Guardian the limit could be adjusted ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/100246-amazon-puts-cap-self-publishing-combat-flood-ai.html); [ALLi](https://selfpublishingadvice.org/self-publishing-news-kdp-ai-new-limits-on-titles/)) [secondary]. One blog says the cap still applies in 2026, and another dates it to 2024. The 2023 date is better supported, and the current number is unverified. For two part-timers the cap does not matter. It matters as a signal that Amazon is pushing back against volume publishing.

On enforcement, the evidence is thin. An attorney blog reports a rise in KDP account terminations tied to AI-assisted books ([AMZ Sellers Attorney](https://www.amazonsellers.attorney/blog/increase-in-kdp-account-terminations-over-ai-assisted-books-and-poor-customer-experience-concerns)) [secondary]. A late-2025 wrongful suspension was later reversed, which shows that false positives happen ([GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/news/20251213-amazon-suspension/)) [secondary]. Claims of a January 2026 AI-detection escalation and an April 2026 "enforcement sweep" each come from a single vendor source ([ScribeCount](https://scribecount.com/author-resource/protecting-books/kdp-ai-content-policy-the-compliance-guide); [AI Policy Desk](https://www.aipolicydesk.com/blog/amazon-kdp-ai-disclosure-compliance-guide-2026)) [unverified/conflicting]. One guide says AI disclosure blocks Kindle Unlimited enrolment. No credible source supports that, and Amazon's own AI translations are KU-eligible, so treat it as false. Amazon's rules on "disappointing content", summaries and workbooks of other people's books, and impersonating real authors were **not retrieved at all**. That gap matters, because those are the categories where AI publishers most often get into trouble.

### Amazon now ships its own AI tools for translation and audio

**Kindle Translate** launched on 6 November 2025 as a free, invite-only beta for ebooks. It started with **English-Spanish and German-English**, and a reported expansion on 30 June 2026 added French, Italian and Portuguese. Translated titles carry a public "Kindle Translate" label and are eligible for KDP Select and Kindle Unlimited ([Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/md/news/books-and-authors/amazon-kindle-translate-books-authors); [Publishers Lunch](https://lunch.publishersmarketplace.com/2025/11/amazon-launches-ai-translation-for-self-published-ebooks/)) [primary, via summary]. **Virtual Voice** AI-narrated audiobooks have been in beta since 2023. A KDP help page reports a **$3.99-$14.99 list price and a 40% royalty on à la carte sales** ([KDP help](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GHJW2N8GLTQLK9TY)) [primary, via summary]. An October 2026 report says Amazon rebranded Kindle Unlimited and made Virtual Voice free for authors ([NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/science/amazon-rebrands-kindle-unlimited-to-kindle-adds-ai-narrated-audiobooks/story)) [unverified/conflicting]. Elsewhere the audio market is restrictive. ACX reportedly requires human narration unless AI narration is authorised ([InkFluence](https://www.inkfluenceai.com/blog/ai-narration-audible-acx-policy-2026)) [vendor/affiliate]. No evidence was found on what AI-translated or AI-narrated titles actually earn.

### US copyright protects the human share of the book

The US Copyright Office's January 2025 report on copyrightability concluded that **prompts alone do not make the user the author**, because the same prompt gives different outputs. Human contributions that are perceptible in the work stay protected, including the author's own text, selection and arrangement, and modifications ([Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2025/02/07/u-s-copyright-office-issues-report-on-artificial-intelligence-and-copyrightability/); [Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/copyright-office-publishes-report-on-copyrightability-of-ai-works/)) [primary, via summary]. On **2 March 2026 the Supreme Court declined to hear Thaler v. Perlmutter**, so the D.C. Circuit's human-authorship requirement stands. That case involved no claimed human input, so it did not decide how much human involvement is enough ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/supreme-court-denies-review-in-ai-authorship-case); [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/the-final-word-supreme-court-refuses-to-hear-case-on-ai-authorship)) [primary, via summary]. The business consequence follows directly [inference]. A prompted-only book is a weak asset in the US, because anyone can copy it. A book whose text the authors wrote and shaped is protectable. Under KDP's definitions it is also more likely to count as "AI-assisted" and need no disclosure.

### Germany and the EU: AI Act transparency rules apply from August 2026, and German copyright and tax questions are still open

The EU AI Act's **Article 50 transparency duties largely apply from 2 August 2026**. The "Digital Omnibus", approved by Parliament on 16 June and the Council on 29 June 2026, delays the high-risk rules to December 2027 but leaves Articles 50(1), (3) and (4) in place ([Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/yes-august-2-still-matters-the-eu-approved-a-high-risk-ai-delay-but-most-trans.html); [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/global-data-hub/2026/the-digital-omnibus-proposal/gdh---the-digital-omnibus-changes-to-the-ai-act)) [secondary, law-firm]. The grace period under Article 50(2) for marking AI output from systems already on the market is reported as either **2 December 2026 or 2 February 2027**; sources conflict ([Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/viewpoints/102nfqm/you-talkin-to-me-operationalising-the-eu-ai-acts-transparency-obligations)). A voluntary Code of Practice on labelling AI-generated content was reportedly finalised in June 2026 ([Addleshaw Goddard](https://www.addleshawgoddard.com/en/insights/insights-briefings/2026/technology/eu-ai-act-code-practice-marking-labelling-ai-generated-content/)). One researcher recalled, without a source, that Article 50(4) covers AI text published to inform the public on matters of public interest and exempts text under human editorial responsibility. If that is right, the duty rarely touches fiction, but it is **unverified**. Nobody researched whether a KDP author counts as a "deployer" under the Act, or how German copyright law treats AI output. Both questions need a lawyer before any non-fiction or heavily AI-generated project.

## Money concentrates in genre series, while AI floods the low-content shelves

### Survey data: most authors earn little, and income follows catalogue size

The most useful income data comes from the **Written Word Media 2025 indie author survey (1,346 responses, self-reported, skewed toward prolific commercial-fiction writers)**. As reported second-hand, **44% earn $100 or less a month**, 13% earn over $5,000 and 8% earn over $10,000 ([Written Word Media 2025](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/2025-indie-author-survey-results/)) [primary, via summary; figures from a secondary analysis]. The same data reportedly shows **about 80% of authors with 1-3 books earning under $100 a month**, and authors earning $5,000-7,500 a month had a median catalogue of **about 30 titles**. Authors with 10+ books and an engaged email list were the most likely to earn full-time income, though the survey itself warns this is correlation ([Written Word Media 2025](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/2025-indie-author-survey-results-insights-into-self-publishing-for-authors/amp/)). Older survey data (year unverified, pre-2024) puts authors earning **$250-999 a month at about 17 books** on average and those earning **$1,000-4,999 at about 29** ([Goodreads blog](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/20735990-self-publishing-survey)) [secondary].

The often-quoted **$13,500 median annual income** comes from the ALLi 2025 survey. It covers only authors who spend over half their working time on publishing or earn over half their income from it. It is not a beginner benchmark ([ALLi](https://www.allianceindependentauthors.org/?p=483); [Self-Publishing Advice](https://selfpublishingadvice.org/2025-author-income-survey/)) [primary, via summary]. One report puts the mean in the same sample above $80,000 [unverified]. A gap that size means a few authors earn most of the money. Both surveys over-sample engaged authors, and Written Word Media sells promotions, so the true median across all KDP publishers is almost certainly lower [inference]. **No survey splits income by AI use.**

### The AI flood is growing the catalogue faster than revenue

The strongest quantitative evidence on AI books is a 2026 preprint. As described in news coverage, it runs AI detection on **14,419 self-published genre-fiction titles (2023 to June 2026)** matched to a panel of daily ebook sales. **Books with substantial AI text were 20% of titles but 12.1% of sales and 11.3% of revenue.** Books with no AI were 62.9% of titles and 72.5% of revenue. The number of titles with any sales grew **19.2x while revenue grew 8.9x**, so revenue per title is shrinking ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-generated-books-are-flooding-amazon-and-tanking-sales-for-human-authors/); [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.20349.md)) [secondary; paper not opened]. Coverage that appears to describe the same study (Reimers & Waldfogel, NBER-linked) adds that **about half of 2025 books sold on Amazon contain AI text, up from 23% in 2023**, and that revenue per book fell in six of eight genres ([Office Chai](https://officechai.com/ai/half-the-ebooks-published-on-amazon-are-now-written-by-ai/)) [secondary]. Amazon reportedly said its internal metrics did not show that growth but declined to share figures ([AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/study-ai-books-gain-amazon-share-as-catalog-outpaces-revenue)). These are averages, so they cannot show that a disciplined, human-edited AI workflow fails [inference]. They do show that **AI-heavy output earns less per title than human writing on average**. They also show that each new title now competes in a pool where revenue per title is shrinking.

### What a sale and a page read pay

Ebook royalties are **35% or 70%**. The 70% option has historically required a **$2.99-$9.99 list price** and charges a delivery fee of **$0.15 per MB** in the US ([KDP help](https://kdp.amazon.com/help/topic/G200644210)) [primary, via summary]. Two secondary sites say the 70% band widened to **$12.99 from 7 July 2026** ([Writeo](https://writeo.app/news/amazon-kdp-royalty-cap-12-99-july-2026)) [unverified/conflicting]. Paperback royalty is 60% of list price minus printing cost ([Author Imprints](https://www.authorimprints.com/amazon-kdp-royalty-pricing/)). Kindle Unlimited pays from a monthly fund divided by total pages read (KENP), so the rate floats. Trackers put it at roughly **$0.0042-$0.0050 per page in 2025-2026**, for example about $0.00482 in April 2026 ([profitable.app](https://profitable.app/kdp/stats/kenp-rate); [KDP help](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G5HHMLVVEW48MQYW)) [secondary; trackers conflict]. At about $0.0045, a reader who finishes a 300-page book earns the author about **$1.35**, against about $3.40 for a $4.99 sale at 70% [inference]. Kindle Unlimited therefore pays off mainly through long books and series binge-reading.

## A working setup costs hundreds of dollars until you pay for human editing

Tool prices below come from affiliate and comparison sites, often undated or conflicting [vendor/affiliate]. **General chatbots (ChatGPT Plus, Claude Pro) were listed at $20/month** in April 2026 ([InkFluence](https://www.inkfluenceai.com/compare/inkfluence-ai-vs-novelcrafter)). Fiction-specific tools cost about **$19/$29/$59 a month for Sudowrite**, about **$4-$20 a month for NovelCrafter** plus your own model API costs, and **$10-$15 a month for NovelAI** ([Activepieces](https://www.activepieces.com/blog/sudowrite-pricing-2026-which-plan-should-you-get.md); [Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/novelcrafter/pricing)). User feedback and a 70,000-word test both report that these tools **still lose character consistency and repeat phrases across a novel without human oversight** ([UC Strategies](https://ucstrategies.com/news/sudowrite-review-i-tested-the-22-month-ai-against-chatgpt-across-70000-words/); [Sudowrite feedback](https://feedback.sudowrite.com/p/ai-consistency-and-workflow-limitations-overview)). Human editing time is a real cost, not an optional extra.

Cover tools run about **$10/month for Midjourney** and **$5-10/month for Adobe Firefly**. Firefly is most often cited as the safest for commercial use because it is trained on licensed content, though nobody checked Adobe's indemnity terms ([Magic Hour](https://magichour.ai/blog/cost-of-ai-image-generators); [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/tools/ai-design/best-adobe-firefly-alternatives)). A human cover averages **$880** (mostly $625-$1,250) across 9,600+ Reedsy collaborations ([Reedsy](https://reedsy.com/blog/book-cover-design-cost)) [vendor/affiliate]. For formatting, **Atticus costs $147 once** and runs cross-platform, **Vellum costs $199.99-$249.99** and runs only on Mac, and Reedsy Studio is free ([Publishing.co.uk](https://www.publishing.co.uk/guides/how-much-does-atticus-cost/); [Makerstack](https://makerstack.co/reviews/vellum-write-review/)). For keyword research, **KDSpy costs $79 and Publisher Rocket $199**, both one-time ([iWrity](https://www.iwrity.com/tools/best/kdp-keyword-research-tools)). Human editing for an 80,000-word novel reportedly runs about **$2,160 for a copy edit and $1,600 for a proofread** ([Chapter, citing Reedsy](https://blog.chapter.pub/reedsy-pricing/)) [secondary]. KDP supplies free ISBNs for print editions, which work only on KDP. Ebooks need no ISBN, and Bowker sells **one ISBN for $125 or ten for $295** ([TCK Publishing](https://www.tckpublishing.com/how-to-get-an-isbn-number/); [Reedsy ISBN guide](https://reedsy.com/blog/guide/isbn-number/)).

## Discovery takes money and time, and Amazon's review rules are strict

No authoritative description of Amazon's ranking algorithm was found. The "30-day new-release boost" and similar ideas are practitioner lore. The best-supported points are economic: Kindle Unlimited rewards page reads, and income correlates with series and catalogue size ([Written Word Media 2025](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/2025-indie-author-survey-results-insights-into-self-publishing-for-authors/amp/)) [primary, via summary; correlational]. Vendor claims that series earn "3-5x" more per reader cite no data.

Advertising arithmetic is unforgiving. One practitioner guide puts genre cost per click at **about $0.20-$0.40 for romance and mystery and $0.40-$0.80 for fantasy and thriller** (unsourced). A worked example shows that **a $3.49 royalty at an 8% conversion rate supports a maximum of about $0.28 per click** before the ad loses money ([PublishDrive](https://www.publishdrive.com/amazon-ads-for-authors); [Vappingo](https://www.vappingo.com/word-blog/?p=11834)) [vendor/affiliate]. An unknown author with one book is likely to convert below that rate. A few hundred dollars of test spend can easily produce no profitable campaign, though nobody has measured how often beginners lose money [inference]. Advertising cost of sale (ACoS) understates the value of ads for a series, because a sale of book one leads to read-through ([ScribeCount](https://scribecount.com/author-resource/troubleshooting-author-resources/amazon-ads-dashboard)). That is the main reason to hold ads until two or three linked books exist [inference]. A marketing consultant advises building an email list before paying for Facebook or Amazon ads ([Self-Publishing Advice](https://selfpublishingadvice.org/ricardo-fayet-on-marketing/)).

BookBub Featured Deals, the best-known paid promotion, cost about **$570-$940 per fiction deal** by 2025 third-party estimates. BookBub says it accepts **about 25%** of submissions, while third parties claim 4-20% ([Writers Helping Writers](https://writershelpingwriters.net/2025/04/i-tried-a-bookbub-featured-deal/); [BookBub Insights](https://insights.bookbub.com/?p=65)). In practice a deal needs an established book with reviews [inference]. BookTok is divided over AI, and creators have faced public "AI accusation" pile-ons ([Ypulse](https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2025/06/24/gen-z-favorite-booktok-is-going-through-a-wave-of-ai-controversy/); [Good Story Co.](https://goodstoryco.substack.com/p/the-ai-accusation-heard-round-booktok)).

Reviews cannot be bought, swapped or traded. Amazon has banned incentivised reviews (outside Vine) **since October 2016** ([Marketplace Pulse](https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/incentivized-reviews-will-lead-to-selling-suspension-to-amazon)). Consultants list review swaps and friends-and-family reviews as prohibited, and one tool claims that free advance copies given in exchange for reviews count as incentivised [unverified against Amazon's text] ([Goat Consulting](https://www.goatconsulting.com/merchandising/amazon-product-review-guidelines)). The **FTC's fake-review rule, in force since 21 October 2024**, bans fake and AI-generated reviews, paid reviews and undisclosed insider reviews, with civil penalties of **about $51,744 per violation** at 2024 rates ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)) [primary, via summary]. Two partners must never review each other's books, and friends and family should not review them either.

## The biggest risks are dilution, reader distrust, account loss and paid courses

**Dilution** comes first. With AI text reportedly in about half of new books and revenue per title falling ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-generated-books-are-flooding-amazon-and-tanking-sales-for-human-authors/)), a new title starts in a more crowded pool. Kindle Unlimited's fixed monthly fund also spreads thinner as more AI titles enrol, a point Book Riot raises without data ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/can-kindle-unlimited-survive-ai/)) [inference].

**Reader distrust** is real but depends on how the question is asked. In a YouGov/Black Chateau poll of 1,000 US adults (August 2025), most respondents wanted disclosure, and **61% said they would feel less fulfilled** after learning a book was AI-written ([Journal of Cyber Policy](https://journalofcyberpolicy.com/?p=343982)) [secondary]. A Swedish survey reportedly found two-thirds of readers negative about AI-written books and 3% positive ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/pudgycat/two-thirds-of-swedes-reject-ai-books-and-the-3-percent-who-want-them-are-a-statistical-error-4347)) [unverified]. Other retailers are stricter than Amazon. Apple Books uses an "AI Generated by" credit. Draft2Digital reportedly turns away most AI books it receives. Kobo lists AI-generated content among its rejection reasons, and IngramSpark bars AI-only books ([Indie Author Magazine](https://indieauthormagazine.com/draft2digital-ceo-kris-austin-details-how-ai-is-actually-affecting-the-industry/); [Inkfluence](https://www.inkfluenceai.com/blog/which-stores-allow-ai-written-books-2026)) [vendor/affiliate; verify on each platform]. A heavily AI-generated catalogue is therefore close to locked into Amazon [inference].

**Account loss** is the risk most likely to wipe out the business. Authors report sudden KDP terminations, sometimes with royalties withheld ([Writers Weekly](https://writersweekly.com/angela-desk/amazon-kdp-terminates-another-authors-account-and-keeps-her-royalties); [AuthorMedia](https://www.authormedia.com/why-amazon-is-terminating-innocent-authors/)) [secondary]. The widely repeated claim that "90% are never reinstated" has no source. Abnormal page-read, download or review spikes are cited as termination triggers ([Written Word Media](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/kdp-account-health/)). Clean traffic and honest disclosure are the main defences, and even then an account can be lost [inference].

**Courses and services** are the risk the evidence documents best. On **13 April 2026 the FTC announced that Publishing.com and its principals would pay $1.5 million** over allegedly false earnings claims for "AI Publishing Academy" and "Publishing Accelerator". The FTC also alleged refund guarantees that were illusory, some conditioned on writing positive testimonials, and reviews by employees that were not disclosed. It said most buyers never earned the promised income ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/04/publishingcom-pay-15-million-misleading-consumers-about-how-much-income-they-could-earn-using); [FKKS](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102mps2/ftc-charges-publishingcom-with-false-earnings-claims-illusory-refund-guarantees)) [primary, via summary]. The course reportedly cost **about $1,995** ([Publishers Marketplace](https://lunch.publishersmarketplace.com/2025/01/ftc-investigates-company-that-teaches-people-to-self-publish-ai-books/)). That is more than double the recommended first-year tool budget above. Scammers have also used Amazon trademarks to sell fake "KDP" services ([Writer Beware](https://writerbeware.blog/2023/07/07/how-scammers-are-using-amazon-and-amazon-trademarks-to-rip-writers-off/)). Warning signs include income promises, free-video sales funnels, refunds tied to testimonials and paid reviews that aren't disclosed.

## A realistic plan: one series, human-led, measured in titles

The strategy below is the report's synthesis [inference], built from the evidence above.

**Choose the lane.** Write commercial genre fiction, as a series, in a subgenre both partners actually read. On the demand data, the candidates are romance/romantasy, mystery/thriller and cozy fantasy/LitRPG. Skip low-content, AI colouring and activity books, and fast self-help: that is where AI users crowd, and where the evidence shows saturation without earnings.

**Keep the authorship human.** Use AI for brainstorming, outlining, continuity checks, line suggestions and covers, and have the partners write and substantially shape the prose. That keeps the text protectable under US law and probably inside KDP's "AI-assisted" definition. Disclose honestly when any text, image or translation is AI-generated. An AI cover counts as AI-generated.

**Split the work.** One partner can own drafting and the other editing, continuity and the story bible, with roles swapping for marketing. Two readers checking each other's work partly replaces the $1,600-$2,880 professional edit the lean budget can't afford, though not fully.

**Distribute through Amazon.** Enrol in KDP Select/Kindle Unlimited, whose page-read pay favours long series. The researchers did not retrieve Select's exclusivity terms, so read them first. Put a link to an email-list sign-up in every book from book one.

**Hold paid marketing.** Keep ads off until at least three books in the series are live. Then test small, using the break-even arithmetic above, and stop any campaign that cannot beat it.

**Time to income** is best counted in titles. The survey proxies say: under $100/month at 1-3 books, about $250-999/month around 17 books, and about $1,000+/month around 20-30 books. All of that comes from mostly human-written, self-reported data. The AI-heavy revenue gap suggests an AI-assisted catalogue may need more titles, not fewer. How fast two part-timers can produce publishable novels is the variable no source measures, so here are two illustrative paces. At one finished novel a month between the two partners, 17 titles takes about 17 months and 29 titles about two and a half years. At one novel every two months, those milestones move out to about three and five years. **Plan for year one to earn little or nothing.** Treat the first year as building the asset and learning the craft. Re-assess after the first complete three-book series, using actual Kindle Unlimited read-through rather than hope.

**Startup budget.** Plan on about $700-$950 for the first year for two people, or about $1,550-$1,850 with a professional cover for book one, plus advertising money spent only after book three. No course is needed. The lean route at about $300-$450 works if the partners accept AI covers and do all the editing themselves.

**German and EU admin.** Submit a W-8BEN with a tax ID to avoid KDP's default **30% US withholding**. Author sources commonly list Germany's treaty rate as **0%**, but confirm the rate in the KDP tax profile. Royalties from non-US stores such as Amazon.de are reportedly not subject to US withholding ([KDP help G201274750](https://kdp.amazon.com/help/topic/G201274750); [Vappingo](https://www.vappingo.com/word-blog/kdp-for-non-us-authors/)) [primary, via summary / vendor]. Register with the Finanzamt for a tax number. Kleinunternehmer status can remove the need to charge VAT, but an income tax return is still required, and long-running losses can draw scrutiny ([Selbststaendigkeit.de](https://selbststaendigkeit.de/buchhaltung-fuer-gruender/kleinunternehmer-steuer/); [Finanztip forum](https://www.finanztip.de/community/forum/thema/30861-buchautor/)) [secondary]. Three questions are open and need a Steuerberater: whether the income counts as a Gewerbe (trade) or as freelance (freiberuflich) work, how two partners should be structured, and how EU VAT applies to digital sales. Kindle Translate's **German-English** pair is a lead worth checking for German partners, who could publish in both language markets from one manuscript. Its invite status as of late 2026 and its earnings are unverified, and translated titles carry a public label.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. "Which AI book niche pays?" matters less than "what will still earn when AI output is unlimited?" The data says the answer is reader trust and series read-through, not volume. Books made mostly by AI make up a fifth of genre-fiction titles but take about a tenth of the revenue. Amazon, other retailers, US copyright law and the FTC all draw their line in the same place: human authorship and honest disclosure. AI's real advantage for two part-timers is lower cost and faster drafting, which shortens the long road to a catalogue of roughly 17-30 titles. It does not remove that road. Anyone selling a shortcut is, on the FTC's record, the likeliest way to lose money.

Two questions decide whether this plan is sound, and both can be answered cheaply before money is spent. The first is how fast the partners can produce a novel they would be willing to sign. Writing one book with the recommended setup will show that. The second is whether the primary sources still say what the summaries reported. The KDP content guidelines, royalty and Select terms, and the German tax treatment can each be checked in an afternoon. This report could not check them, so its figures are a starting point for that check, not a final answer.
