## 1. KDP content guideline: "AI-generated" vs "AI-assisted"; what is disclosed, where, shown to readers?

### Takeaway
Since Sept 2023 KDP requires publishers to tell Amazon when a submission contains AI-generated text, images or translations; AI-assisted content (human-created, then edited/refined/brainstormed with AI) need not be disclosed. Secondary sources say the disclosure is internal and not shown to readers. I could not verify the exact official wording.

### Cited Findings
- Amazon introduced the AI-disclosure requirement and the "AI-generated" / "AI-assisted" definitions in September 2023; the 3-titles-per-day cap was announced about a week later. Still the framework in 2026 per secondary sources — [search summary citing Authors Guild/news coverage](https://authorsguild.org/news/amazon-adds-to-kdp-generative-ai-policy-caps-daily-self-publishing-uploads/) (only a snippet was visible to me)
- Disclosure is required for AI-generated text, images (incl. cover and interior art) and translations; AI-assisted use (brainstorming, grammar checks, refining your own human-written content) does not require disclosure; extensive editing of AI-generated content does not change its classification (third-party claims) — [Univers Studio](https://univers.studio/blog/ai-generated-content-kdp/); [Inkrify](https://inkrify.com/blog/staying-compliant-with-amazon-kdp-ai-content-policy-in-2026/); [BuiltWritten](https://www.builtwritten.com/blog/disclose-ai-in-your-book-kdp-policy-2026)
- Disclosure is made per title and per format (ebook and paperback separately) — [Authors Guild-related search results / ScribeCount](https://scribecount.com/author-resource/protecting-books/kdp-ai-content-policy-the-compliance-guide) (blog, unverified against Amazon)
- Secondary sources say the disclosure is internal, not displayed to readers, and no public "AI-generated" badge exists on product pages — [Univers Studio](https://univers.studio/blog/ai-generated-content-kdp/) (blog, not official). Note: Kindle Translate titles DO get a public "Kindle Translate" label (see section 7).
- AI is not banned; undisclosed AI-generated content is the violation — [Inkrify](https://inkrify.com/blog/staying-compliant-with-amazon-kdp-ai-content-policy-in-2026/) (blog)

### Inferences
- The "AI-generated vs assisted" line turns on who created the expression; a heavily AI-drafted-then-human-edited manuscript is likely still "AI-generated" under Amazon's wording as reported.

### Gaps
- Exact official wording and any 2025-2026 revisions of the guideline: not read (kdp.amazon.com unreachable).
- Whether any reader-facing AI label has been added since 2023: no official confirmation found.

## 2. Publishing volume limits

### Takeaway
A limit of 3 new titles per day per account was announced Sept 2023 via the KDP forum; Amazon said it could be adjusted. Secondary sources say it still applies in 2026; no official confirmation of changes found.

### Cited Findings
- Amazon announced the cap in a KDP forum post; told The Guardian the limit is 3 titles/day and may be adjusted; previously no limit existed; users hitting the limit are notified (KDP confirmed to ALLi) — [Authors Guild](https://authorsguild.org/news/amazon-adds-to-kdp-generative-ai-policy-caps-daily-self-publishing-uploads/); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/100246-amazon-puts-cap-self-publishing-combat-flood-ai.html); [Self-Publishing Advice (ALLi)](https://selfpublishingadvice.org/self-publishing-news-kdp-ai-new-limits-on-titles/)
- One blog says the cap remains in force in 2026 — [Univers Studio](https://univers.studio/blog/kdp-ai-content-policy-2026/). Another source dates it to late 2024, which conflicts with Authors Guild/news (2023); the 2023 date is better supported.

## 5. US Copyright Office position and Thaler v. Perlmutter

### Takeaway
Purely AI-generated output is not copyrightable in the US; prompts alone are insufficient; human-authored contributions (own text, selection/arrangement, modifications) remain protectable. SCOTUS denied cert in Thaler on 2 March 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Copyright Office, "Copyright and AI, Part 2: Copyrightability" (Jan 2025): with current technology prompts alone don't give sufficient control to make the user the author; models vary outputs for identical prompts; existing law is adequate; AI material in a work does not affect protection of the human-authored whole; human input perceptible in output is protected — [Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2025/02/07/u-s-copyright-office-issues-report-on-artificial-intelligence-and-copyrightability/); [Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/copyright-office-publishes-report-on-copyrightability-of-ai-works/); [Manatt](https://manatt.com/insights/newsletters/copyright-office-releases-new-report-on-copyrightability-of-ai-works)
- Supreme Court denied certiorari in Thaler v. Perlmutter (No. 25-449) on 2 March 2026 with no opinion; the D.C. Circuit holding (human authorship required) stands; case involved no claimed human input so degree of human involvement not decided — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102mlpl/supreme-court-denies-certiorari-in-thaler-v-perlmutter-human-only-rule-for-ai/); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/supreme-court-denies-review-in-ai-authorship-case); [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/the-final-word-supreme-court-refuses-to-hear-case-on-ai-authorship)

### Inferences
- An author who writes the text and uses AI only for suggestions keeps protection for human-authored parts; a book generated by prompting alone has little or no US protection, so third parties could copy it.

### Gaps
- Registration guidance specifics (disclosure/disclaimer of AI material, March 2023 guidance), Part 2 primary text, post-March 2026 developments (e.g. Allen v. Perlmutter): not retrieved.

## 6. Germany / EU

### Takeaway
EU AI Act Art. 50 transparency duties are mostly applicable from 2 Aug 2026; marking-of-output duty (50(2)) timing is disputed in sources. German copyright position not researched.

### Cited Findings
- Digital Omnibus: Parliament approved 16 June 2026, Council adopted 29 June 2026; mainly delays high-risk rules to Dec 2027; Art. 50(1), (3), (4) not delayed (apply from 2 Aug 2026) — [Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/yes-august-2-still-matters-the-eu-approved-a-high-risk-ai-delay-but-most-trans.html); [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/global-data-hub/2026/the-digital-omnibus-proposal/gdh---the-digital-omnibus-changes-to-the-ai-act)
