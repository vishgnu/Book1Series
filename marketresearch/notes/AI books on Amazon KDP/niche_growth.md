# Genre and format growth 2023-2026: which niches are still accelerating?

Research date: 14 November 2026. Context: two German-resident partners planning AI-assisted genre fiction on KDP (US + DE). Builds on the earlier verified report on branch `claude/jolly-heisenberg-pi9vn8` (romance ~55% / MTS ~33% of Kindle top 100; cozy fantasy and LitRPG "hottest niches"; fantasy has the strongest no-AI revenue premium).

Labels: **[primary]** read on the issuing body's own page/document this session; **[secondary]** press/blog reporting someone else's data; **[vendor]** a party selling a tool/report/service, or an affiliate (Author Media links K-lytics as an affiliate); **[inference]** my arithmetic or reasoning; **[unverified]** not traced to a source or contradicted.

Important source caveat: almost all **subgenre** growth numbers for Kindle come from K-lytics, which I could only read through Author Media's summaries (affiliate). K-lytics' own public pages (k-lytics.com, /blog) show nothing newer than June 2024; the reports are paywalled. Circana / AAP / Börsenverein give only **broad** categories (romance, fantasy, sci-fi, "New Adult"), mostly **print**. No one publishes audited Kindle subgenre sales.

**Read across the table [inference]:** genuine *market-level* acceleration in late 2026 is only visible in **sci-fi**, **manga**, and **faith-based** books. Romance/romantasy, the 2021-2025 engine, is flat-to-down in US print in 2026 and decelerating in Germany. The Kindle-specific "hot niches" (cozy fantasy, LitRPG, techno-thriller) rest on one affiliate-relayed vendor dataset and are snapshots of demand-to-competition, not proof of exponential growth. **Nothing found grows "near-exponentially" except supply** (AI-era catalogue 38x since 2023 vs revenue 8.9x).

### 2.5 Saturation and AI crowding

- **Chakrabarty, Liu, Ginsburg, Dhillon, "Generative AI floods and dilutes the market for books", arXiv 2607.20349 (Jul 2026)**, PDF read this session [primary] https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.20349 :
  - 14,419 self-published genre-fiction ebooks released Jan 2023-Mar 2026.
  - By 2026 Q1 the cumulative released catalogue was **38.3x** its 2023 Q1 level; selling titles 19.2x; unit sales 7.3x; revenue 8.9x.
  - Substantial-AI books = 20% of books, 12.1% of sales, 11.3% of revenue; ~10% of the top 5%.
  - New-release composition: General Romance 28.8%, Speculative/Adventure Romance 15.5%, Fantasy/Supernatural/Horror 15.2%, General/Contemporary 11.4%, Mystery/Thriller/Crime 10.7%, Crime/Suspense Romance 9.6%, Sports Romance 5.8%, Sci-Fi/Adventure/Dystopian 3.1%.
  - AI share of top-25 slots rose in every cluster, **most steeply in Crime/Suspense Romance, Sci-Fi/Adventure/Dystopian, and Mystery/Thriller/Crime**.
  - Launch-window revenue per selling title, 2023 vs 2025 cohort: fell in 6 of 8 clusters; rose only in **Fantasy/Supernatural/Horror (+21%; +35% for no-AI books)** and Sci-Fi (+4%).
  - Dilution of no-AI books is stronger in KU-heavy genres.

## 3. Hype without data (found claimed, not evidenced)

- **Monster / alien romance**: no sales or growth data found anywhere public; only a paid community report. [unverified]
- **Why-choose / reverse harem, mafia romance**: no figures; K-lytics romance summary doesn't mention them. [unverified]
- **"Romantasy is the fastest-growing subgenre in 2026"** (selfpublishing.com, PublishDrive blogs): contradicted by Circana (softened H2 2025; romance −25.4% Q1 2026). True for 2023-H1 2025 only.
- **Paranormal women's fiction, healing/slice-of-life fantasy, dark academia, short spicy novellas**: no data. (Paranormal *romance* has WWM earner over-representation, which is not growth data.)
- **Cozy mystery "exploding"**: rests on a podcast summary of Google search interest. [unverified]
- **Cozy LitRPG = 18% of new series**: community tool, not audited. [unverified]
- **Alibaba-hosted "Dark Romance +22%, Romantasy +31%"** and similar listicle numbers: no traceable source. [unverified]

- **Goodreads/StoryGraph trend data**: neither publishes shelf-growth statistics; only "most read" lists.
- **"Exponential" demand anywhere in fiction**: not found. The only exponential curve in the data is AI-era title supply (38x in 3 years).
