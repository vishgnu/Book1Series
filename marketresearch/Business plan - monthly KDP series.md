# Business plan: one AI-drafted book a month on Amazon KDP

A plan for two German-resident partners, each working about 20 hours a week, to publish one genre-fiction novel a month on Amazon KDP. AI writes the first draft of every book. The partners plan it, rewrite it, edit it and sell it. The plan builds on the verified report next to this file and on two research notes from 14 November 2026: `niche_growth.md` and `rapid_release.md` in the notes folder. Evidence labels match the report: [primary], [secondary], [vendor], [inference] and [unverified].

Earlier versions are in the git history. One had a weekly AI-drafted novella. The other had a monthly novel written by the partners themselves.

## The plan in one page

**AI does the heavy lifting, and the partners own the quality.** Each month AI drafts a 60,000-70,000-word novel scene by scene from the partners' outline. The partners then rewrite it into the series voice, revise it, proofread it and publish it. That takes an estimated **90-145 hours** of the team's roughly 173 hours a month [inference]. The 30-80 spare hours go into a second revision pass and marketing, not more books.

**Three consequences follow from AI-drafted text, and the plan accepts them:**

- **Disclosure.** KDP counts AI-drafted text as "AI-generated" even after heavy edits, so every book is disclosed to Amazon [primary]. Amazon allows it.
- **Copyright** covers only what the partners wrote or reshaped [primary]. The series concept, characters, world and rewritten prose are the protectable asset, so the rewrite has to be real.
- **Lower average earnings.** In the AI-books study, books with substantial AI text earned about **half as much per title** as books without AI [primary, Chakrabarty et al.; inference on the ratio]. The plan's answer is a monthly pace instead of a weekly one, so each book gets a real edit, the same edit that separates the AI books that succeed from the rest.

**The product** is one series of cozy paranormal or cozy fantasy mysteries in English. Each novel solves one case, a three-book arc carries a running thread, and each completed arc is also sold as a collection. Science-fiction adventure is the runner-up.

**The money** is slow. Books 1-3 launch together in month 3, then one book follows each month. That puts 12 books live by month 12 and 24 by month 24. The base case earns about $1,100 in year one and $3,800 in year two, reaching about $400 a month by month 24 [inference]. Year one costs about $3,300-4,400.

**The checkpoints** are in months 3, 6, 12 and 24. Each one has clear numbers that decide whether to continue, adjust or stop.

## 1. Where the growth is

The research found no niche with near-exponential growth in demand. **What grows exponentially is supply.** The self-published genre-fiction catalogue grew 38x from early 2023 to early 2026, while revenue grew 8.9x (Chakrabarty et al., arXiv 2607.20349) [primary]. The Kindle+ fund grows about 10% a year [secondary]. A new series wins by taking readers from other books, not by riding a wave.

| Niche | Growth evidence | Direction | AI crowding | Fit for an AI-drafted monthly novel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Science fiction: adventure, dystopian, space exploration** | US print +22.1% in 2025, the largest adult-fiction gain [secondary, PW citing Circana]. Kindle sci-fi adventure +49%, dystopian +45% over 12 months [vendor, K-lytics] | **Accelerating** | AI books moving into top-25 slots fastest. Avoid sci-fi romance and space marines, which are falling | **Good.** The 70,000-80,000 words readers expect fit the time budget |
| **Cozy fantasy** | Best sales-to-competition ratio of 64 SF&F sub-markets [vendor, K-lytics] | Rising, from one snapshot | Low competition, small absolute demand | **Good** |
| **Fantasy, all** | The only genre cluster where books without AI earned more per title, +35% [primary] | Mixed: print −8.7%, Kindle niches up | Lowest AI share so far | Good, but AI-drafted books compete against that premium |
| **LitRPG and progression fantasy** | Ratio 14.8. A breakout series and mainstream deals in 2026 [vendor; secondary] | Rising | Medium | Weak. Readers expect 100,000+ words |
| **Mystery and thriller** | Rising share of Kindle lists [secondary]. Techno-thriller +49%, PI mystery +43% [vendor] | Rising | One of the steepest AI rises | **Good** |
| **Dark romance, dark themes** | 2025: psychological thriller +29%, dark fantasy +23%, horror +13% [primary, Circana]. No 2026 figures | Grew in 2025, unknown since | Crime and suspense romance is the most AI-crowded cluster | Good format fit. Carries content-policy risk |
| **Romance and romantasy, all** | Largest pool. US print +3.9% in 2025, then −25.4% in Q1 2026 [secondary] | **Plateauing** | The most crowded | Good format fit, against the biggest AI crowd |
| **Germany: New Adult** | Revenue +9.6% in 2025 against −2.7% for the whole market [primary, Börsenverein] | Slowing in 2026 | Big publisher pipelines | A later option, with German print distribution |
| **Christian fiction** | US religious publishers +9.3% in 2025 [primary, AAP]. Christian fantasy +36% [vendor] | Rising | Little evidence | Weak with AI drafting. Readers are sensitive to authenticity [inference] |
| **Manga, activity books, self-help** | Manga +45% in Q1 2026 [primary]. Self-help −26% | Mixed | Activity books and self-help are the most AI-flooded | **Avoid** |

No figures were found for monster, why-choose or mafia romance, paranormal women's fiction or short "spicy" novellas. Claims that those are exploding are hype until shown otherwise. Most Kindle subgenre numbers come from K-lytics through an affiliate site.

### The recommended niche: cozy paranormal or cozy fantasy mystery

This niche combines four supported signals [inference]:

- cozy fantasy's strong demand relative to competition;
- mystery's rising share of the Kindle lists;
- a self-contained case in each book, which suits AI drafting from a tight outline;
- recurring characters and settings, which a series bible can keep consistent across AI drafts.

Typical examples are a witch who runs a tea shop and solves village murders, or a retired adventurer whose inn draws trouble. The tone is gentle and humorous, with low violence and recurring characters.

**Runner-up: science-fiction adventure**, for example a salvage crew taking one job per book. It has the strongest growth data, and its longer books fit the time budget. AI books are moving into its top slots fastest, so the competition will be other AI-drafted books.

**The partners must actually read the chosen genre.** Readers in every genre punish books that miss its conventions, and AI drafts drift toward generic versions of a genre. The first weeks include a hands-on check of the category, described in section 7.

## 2. The product

| Element | Decision |
|---|---|
| Unit | Novel of **60,000-70,000 words**, about 300-350 Kindle pages. 70,000-80,000 for sci-fi adventure |
| Structure | One case per book. A three-book arc with a running thread. Recurring cast and setting |
| Series | One series under one shared pen name. Consider a second series only after book 12 |
| Collections | After each arc, a three-book collection as an ebook. Publish one collection per completed arc and no other repackaging. KDP bans "excessively reused" content and has no published box-set rule [primary rule; inference on its application] |
| Paperbacks | Every novel, priced at $9.99 or more to earn KDP's 60% print royalty [primary] |
| Language | English for the US, UK and other English-language stores first. A German edition later, for a series that has proven itself in English: Kindle Translate if invited, or AI translation with a human edit, disclosed as an AI translation |
| Ebook pricing | Book 1 at $0.99-$2.99. Books 2 and on at $3.99-$4.99. Collections at $7.99-$9.99, all within the 70% royalty band of $2.99-$12.99 [primary] |
| Distribution | KDP Select (Kindle+) for the ebooks, with 90-day exclusivity [primary] |

**What a reader is worth** [inference]: about 200 words fill one Kindle+ page, at about $0.0047 a page. A full Kindle+ read of a 65,000-word novel pays about **$1.53**. A $3.99 sale pays about **$2.75**. The business depends on readers carrying on through the series, so read-through, the share of readers who buy or borrow the next book, is the number to watch.

## 3. How the work gets done each month

### The workflow

1. **Outline.** The partners write the case, the clues, the twist, the arc thread and a scene-by-scene plan. That is the most important human input, for quality and for copyright, because AI does not plot a fair mystery well on its own.
2. **AI draft.** AI drafts scene by scene from the outline, with the series bible, style guide and earlier books as context. Use one chat model, or a drafting tool such as Sudowrite or NovelCrafter that holds a story bible.
3. **Human rewrite.** Partner A rewrites every chapter into the series voice. Dialogue, humour, character moments and the ending get the most attention, because that is where AI prose sounds generic.
4. **Revision.** Partner B checks structure, clue fairness, pacing and continuity, with AI critique as a first pass.
5. **Second revision pass.** This step uses the spare hours. One partner reads the whole book as a reader and fixes anything flat, repetitive or generic.
6. **Proofread.** An AI grammar pass first, then each partner proofreads the other's chapters.
7. **Production.** Formatting, cover, blurb, keywords and upload.

### The time budget

The team has **about 173 hours a month**. Estimated hours per 65,000-word novel [inference; editing speeds from the Editorial Freelancers Association, AI drafting plus human rewrite at 1,500-3,000 finished words an hour]:

| Step | Who | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| Outline and scene plan | Both | 8 |
| AI draft and human rewrite | A | 22-43 |
| Revision | B | 27-52 |
| Proofread | Both | 17-26 |
| Production | B | 6 |
| Marketing, through the month | Both | 17 |
| **Subtotal** | | **About 97-152 hours** |
| Second revision pass and extra marketing, from the spare time | Both | 20-75 |

Spend the spare hours on quality first. Readers decide whether the series is worth continuing, and the AI study shows that average AI books lose that test.

### Roles

- **Partner A, story lead:** series bible, outlines, prompting the AI draft and the rewrite into the series voice.
- **Partner B, editor and publisher:** revision, continuity, formatting, covers, metadata, KDP operations, newsletter and ads.
- Both proofread each other's chapters, and both are responsible for the book being good.

Swap roles for one arc a year. That spreads the skills and limits burnout.

### Quality gates for every book

A book goes out only if it passes all five:

1. **Nothing reused.** No scenes, descriptions or stock phrases repeat from earlier books, checked by AI comparison against the manuscript archive. AI drafts recycle phrasing, and KDP bans content that is "excessively reused … across books" or "not significantly different" from another book [primary].
2. **A fair mystery.** The case is complete and its clues play fair, while the arc thread continues.
3. **Read in full.** Both partners have read every line, and both would sign it.
4. **Continuity checked.** The series bible is updated, and the continuity check is clean.
5. **Disclosed and clean.** The KDP disclosure says AI-generated text, and AI-generated images if used. The cover, title and description refer to no other authors or books [primary].

## 5. Publishing operations

- **Account.** One KDP account for the business. KDP's terms allow Amazon to bar people from opening new accounts after a termination. Ask KDP support how two partners should hold one account before setting it up [unverified].
- **Release.** Publish on the same day each month. Put the next book on pre-order at each release, so every book links forward, and keep the Amazon series page complete.
- **Disclosure.** Answer "AI-generated" for text and for AI images. Keep a short log per book of what AI produced and what the partners wrote, rewrote and decided. If KDP ever questions a book, or if copyright is ever disputed, that log is the evidence.
- **KDP Select.** Enrol each ebook. Use the five free-promotion days or a Kindle Countdown Deal per 90-day term on book 1. Never reward readers for reading in Kindle+, which Amazon bans [primary].
- **Other stores.** Kobo rejects AI-generated books. Apple accepts them with an "AI Generated by" credit. IngramSpark requires attribution, and Draft2Digital looks for real human editing [primary]. For paperbacks sold outside Amazon, check each retailer's current rules before uploading.

## 6. Marketing

Paid marketing works only once there is a series to read through, so it follows the catalogue.

### Months 1-2, before launch

- Build the author website and the newsletter.
- Write a short **reader magnet**, a free prequel story offered through BookFunnel and sold nowhere else.
- Link the back of every book to the **website's home page** and to social media profiles, never directly to a sign-up form. KDP prohibits links to web forms that collect email addresses, and explicitly allows links to social media and to the rest of the series [primary]. Ask KDP support to confirm the home-page link before the first upload.
- Recruit 20-40 advance readers through genre communities and BookFunnel. They get free copies with no conditions, which Amazon allows. They must never be friends or family, and must never be asked for a positive review [primary].

### Month 3, launch

Publish books 1-3 together. Email the list and the advance readers. Run free days or a Countdown Deal on book 1 and list it on genre promotion sites such as Freebooksy, at roughly $50-150 per listing [unverified].

### Every month after launch

- One release with a pre-order for the next book.
- Two newsletters: a release email and a story or behind-the-scenes email.
- Three to five social posts a week showing setting, characters and tone.
- A monthly newsletter swap with authors in the same genre.

**Decide the public AI stance before launch.** Amazon's disclosure is internal. 61% of US adults would feel less fulfilled to learn a book was AI-written [primary, YouGov], and BookTok has run public "AI accusation" pile-ons [secondary]. Never deny AI use. A truthful line such as "plotted, edited and finished by two humans, drafted with AI tools" is safer than silence that gets exposed later [inference]. Avoid promotion channels that ban AI-assisted books.

### From month 5, with four or more books live

Test Amazon ads on book 1 only. The break-even rule is maximum cost per click equals royalty × conversion rate. At a $2.75 royalty and a 10% conversion rate, that is about $0.28 per click [inference]. Book-1 ads can run a little above break-even only if measured read-through pays for it. Start at $5 a day and stop any campaign that cannot show read-through.

### From about month 9

Apply for a BookBub Featured Deal on book 1 or the first collection. A US-only genre deal costs about $290-880 [primary]. BookBub accepts roughly 10-25% of submissions [primary]. Its policy on AI-generated books was not checked, so read it before applying.

### Revenue [inference]

The model assumes books 1-3 launch in month 3, then one a month. That gives 12 books live by month 12 and 24 by month 24. Each live title earns a flat amount per month from the month after release. Collections and paperbacks add 20% on top.

The amounts per title are assumptions with two anchors:

- **Survey data:** authors with about 10 books average under $100 a month in total, or about $10 a title. Authors with about 26 books average $500-999, or about $20-40 a title [primary, Written Word Media 2025]. Those authors are mostly human-written.
- **The AI discount:** AI-heavy books earn about half as much per title on average [primary; inference]. The base case assumes the second revision pass recovers part of that gap.

| Scenario | Per live title per month | Month 12 run-rate | Year 1 revenue | Month 24 run-rate | Year 2 revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | $5 | About $70 | About $400 | About $140 | About $1,300 |
| Base | $15 | About $200 | About $1,100 | About $410 | About $3,800 |
| High | $45 | About $600 | About $3,400 | About $1,240 | About $11,300 |

Year one loses money in every scenario. The base case covers ongoing costs in year two. The study also shows the upside: a minority of AI-drafted books earned six figures each [primary]. The high case is reachable only if the series is genuinely good, which is what the time budget protects.

## 9. Checkpoints

| When | Measure | Continue if | Otherwise |
|---|---|---|---|
| Month 3, before launch | Hours per book, quality | Book 3 took 150 hours or less, and both partners would sign it | Find the bottleneck. Usually the outline is too loose or the drafting set-up needs changing. Delay launch rather than ship weak books |
| Month 6, about 6 books live | Read-through from book 1 to 2 and from 2 to 3. Average rating. Reviews mentioning AI or repetition. Kindle+ pages per day | Book 1 to 2 at 40% or more. Rating at 4.0 or more. No pattern of "sounds AI" reviews | Rework book 1's opening, cover and blurb. If reviews call out AI prose, rewrite more and cut the AI share. If nobody reads past book 1, change the series concept |
| Month 12, 12 books live | Run-rate, newsletter size | Run-rate of $200 a month or more and rising. Newsletter growing every month | Cut costs to the minimum. Rethink the niche with the category data gathered so far |
| Month 24, 24 books live | Run-rate against hours worked | Run-rate of $400 a month or more and rising | Stop new production, keep the catalogue on sale and reassess |

The read-through targets are planning assumptions, not sourced benchmarks [inference]. Stop releases at once if KDP sends a content-quality warning, and fix every flagged issue before the next release.

## 10. Risks

| Risk | Why it matters | Counter-measure |
|---|---|---|
| Lower earnings for AI-drafted books | About half the revenue per title on average [primary] | The second revision pass, a strong human voice and fair-play plotting |
| Reader backlash | 61% of US adults would feel less fulfilled by an AI-written book [primary, YouGov]. Public pile-ons on BookTok | A decided, truthful public stance. Never deny AI use |
| KDP quality enforcement | AI drafts recycle phrasing, and KDP bans repeated or "not significantly different" content. Account loss can forfeit royalties [primary] | The reuse check, honest disclosure and clean traffic |
| Weak copyright | AI-drafted text is protected only where humans wrote or reshaped it [primary] | A real rewrite and the per-book log. Treat the series concept and characters as the core asset |
| Fewer stores | Kobo rejects AI-generated books. Other stores add conditions [primary] | Amazon-first by design. Check each retailer before going wide |
| Burnout | A novel a month beside day jobs | The spare hours, role swaps and permission to stretch to six weeks |
| Courses and services | The FTC made Publishing.com pay $1.5M over false earnings claims [primary] | Buy no courses or "done for you" services |

## 11. German setup

- **Partnership.** Two people running a business together usually form a German civil partnership (GbR). Put the split of costs, revenue, rights to the pen name and the books, and the exit terms in writing [unverified; confirm with the Steuerberater].
- **Tax.** Writing is a named freelance activity under §18 EStG [primary]. Whether an AI-drafted series still counts as writing ("schriftstellerisch") rather than a trade (Gewerbe) is a question for the Steuerberater. The VAT small-business exemption applies up to €25,000 turnover in the prior year and €100,000 in the current year [primary].
- **US tax.** Complete the KDP tax interview with a W-8BEN form. Germany's treaty rate on US royalties is 0% [primary].
- **EU AI Act.** The Commission's guidelines place AI-generated fiction outside the Article 50(4) labelling duty [primary]. Recheck it only for non-fiction on public-interest topics.
- **German copyright.** It protects only personal intellectual creations (§2(2) UrhG) [primary], the same direction as the US rule.
- **German edition.** New Adult grew 9.6% in Germany in 2025 [primary], but ebooks are only 6.3% of German book revenue. A German edition of a proven series needs print and the Tolino stores, not only Kindle.

## What this plan rests on

- **The hours estimate is not measured.** No time-logged study of AI-assisted fiction writing was found. The partners' log for book 1 replaces it.
- **The AI discount is an average.** It comes from one observational study whose AI detector's accuracy figures are the vendor's own. A well-edited AI-drafted series may do better or worse.
- **Per-title revenue is assumed.** Kindle+ per-page rates after October 2026 are unknown, because audiobooks now share the fund.
- **The category check could not be automated.** The partners do it by hand in weeks 1-2.
- **Several practical points are unconfirmed.** Box-set rules, the home-page link in books, account set-up for two partners, BookBub's AI policy and German tax treatment each need a question before launch.
