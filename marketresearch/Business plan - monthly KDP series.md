# Business plan: one book a month on Amazon KDP

A plan for two German-resident partners, each working about 20 hours a week, to write, publish and market one genre-fiction novel a month on Amazon KDP, with AI as an assistant. It builds on the verified report next to this file and on two research notes from 14 November 2026: `niche_growth.md` and `rapid_release.md` in the notes folder. Evidence labels match the report: [primary], [secondary], [vendor], [inference] and [unverified].

This plan replaces an earlier one-book-a-week version, which is still in the git history.

## The plan in one page

**One book a month is a full novel of 55,000-65,000 words, written by the partners themselves.** The team has about 173 hours a month. A 60,000-word novel takes an estimated **120-180 hours** with human drafting, AI assistance and a real edit [inference]. That fits, with slack at the faster end. The monthly pace matches what successful rapid-release authors actually did: books of 50,000 words or more every two to four weeks [secondary].

**The monthly pace changes the AI question.** The weekly plan needed AI to write first drafts. A monthly book can be written by the partners, with AI used for brainstorming, outlining, critique, continuity and grammar. KDP calls that "AI-assisted", which needs no disclosure [primary]. That brings four gains:

- **Copyright:** the whole text is protected in the US and Germany.
- **Retailers:** every store stays open, including Kobo, which rejects AI-generated books.
- **Revenue:** these books sit on the side of the AI study where books earn more per title [primary].
- **Fantasy premium:** fantasy is the one genre where books without AI earned *more* per title, up 35% [primary].

The one exception is covers. An AI-generated cover must be disclosed as an AI image, even when the text is human [primary].

**The product** is one series of cozy paranormal or cozy fantasy mysteries in English. Each novel solves one case, a three-book arc carries a running thread, and each completed arc is also sold as a collection. Science-fiction adventure is the runner-up.

**The money** is slow. Books 1-3 launch together in month 4, then one book follows each month. That puts 11 books live by month 12 and 23 by month 24. The base case earns about $1,600 in year one and $5,900 in year two, reaching about $660 a month by month 24 [inference]. That is less early income than the weekly plan's model. It rests on stronger evidence, and the books are a protectable asset.

**The checkpoints** are in months 4, 7, 12 and 24. Each one has clear numbers that decide whether to continue, adjust or stop.

## 1. Where the growth is

The research found no niche with near-exponential growth in demand. **What grows exponentially is supply.** The self-published genre-fiction catalogue grew 38x from early 2023 to early 2026, while revenue grew 8.9x (Chakrabarty et al., arXiv 2607.20349) [primary]. The Kindle+ fund grows about 10% a year [secondary]. A new series wins by taking readers from other books, not by riding a wave.

| Niche | Growth evidence | Direction | AI crowding | Fit for a monthly novel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Science fiction: adventure, dystopian, space exploration** | US print +22.1% in 2025, the largest adult-fiction gain [secondary, PW citing Circana]. Kindle sci-fi adventure +49%, dystopian +45% over 12 months [vendor, K-lytics] | **Accelerating** | Rising fastest in top-25 slots. Avoid sci-fi romance and space marines, which are falling | **Good.** Readers expect 70,000-90,000 words, at the top of the time budget |
| **Cozy fantasy** | Best sales-to-competition ratio of 64 SF&F sub-markets [vendor, K-lytics] | Rising, from one snapshot | Low competition, small absolute demand | **Good** |
| **Fantasy, all** | The only genre cluster where books without AI earned more per title, +35% [primary] | Mixed: print −8.7%, Kindle niches up | Lowest AI share so far | **Good**, and it rewards human writing |
| **LitRPG and progression fantasy** | Ratio 14.8. A breakout series and mainstream deals in 2026 [vendor; secondary] | Rising | Medium | **Weak.** Readers expect 100,000+ words |
| **Mystery and thriller** | Rising share of Kindle lists [secondary]. Techno-thriller +49%, PI mystery +43% [vendor] | Rising | One of the steepest AI rises | **Good** |
| **Dark romance, dark themes** | 2025: psychological thriller +29%, dark fantasy +23%, horror +13% [primary, Circana]. No 2026 figures | Grew in 2025, unknown since | Crime and suspense romance is the most AI-crowded cluster | Good format fit. Carries content-policy risk |
| **Romance and romantasy, all** | Largest pool. US print +3.9% in 2025, then −25.4% in Q1 2026 [secondary] | **Plateauing** | The most crowded | Good format fit, against the biggest crowd |
| **Germany: New Adult** | Revenue +9.6% in 2025 against −2.7% for the whole market [primary, Börsenverein] | Slowing in 2026 | Big publisher pipelines | A later option, with German print distribution |
| **Christian fiction** | US religious publishers +9.3% in 2025 [primary, AAP]. Christian fantasy +36% [vendor] | Rising | Little evidence | Medium |
| **Manga, activity books, self-help** | Manga +45% in Q1 2026 [primary]. Self-help −26% | Mixed | Activity books and self-help are the most AI-flooded | **Avoid** |

No figures were found for monster, why-choose or mafia romance, paranormal women's fiction or short "spicy" novellas. Claims that those are exploding are hype until shown otherwise. Most Kindle subgenre numbers come from K-lytics through an affiliate site.

### The recommended niche: cozy paranormal or cozy fantasy mystery

This niche combines four supported signals [inference]:

- cozy fantasy's strong demand relative to competition;
- fantasy's revenue premium for books without AI, which human-written books can claim;
- mystery's rising share of the Kindle lists;
- a format where each book is a self-contained case inside a long series, which suits a monthly release.

Typical examples are a witch who runs a tea shop and solves village murders, or a retired adventurer whose inn draws trouble. The tone is gentle and humorous, with low violence and recurring characters.

**Runner-up: science-fiction adventure**, for example a salvage crew taking one job per book. It has the strongest growth data. Its readers expect longer books, so it would use the whole monthly time budget, and AI books are moving into its top slots fastest.

**The partners must actually read the chosen genre.** Readers in every genre punish books that miss its conventions. The first weeks include a hands-on check of the category, described in section 7.

## 2. The product

| Element | Decision |
|---|---|
| Unit | Novel of **55,000-65,000 words**, about 275-325 Kindle pages |
| Structure | One case per book. A three-book arc with a running thread. Recurring cast and setting |
| Series | One series under one shared pen name. Consider a second series only after book 12 |
| Collections | After each arc, a three-book collection as an ebook. Publish one collection per completed arc and no other repackaging. KDP bans "excessively reused" content and has no published box-set rule [primary rule; inference on its application] |
| Paperbacks | Every novel, priced at $9.99 or more to earn KDP's 60% print royalty [primary]. Print can also be sold outside Amazon |
| Language | English for the US, UK and other English-language stores first. A German edition later, for a series that has proven itself in English: Kindle Translate if invited, or a human translator |
| Ebook pricing | Book 1 at $0.99-$2.99. Books 2 and on at $3.99-$4.99. Collections at $7.99-$9.99, all within the 70% royalty band of $2.99-$12.99 [primary] |
| Distribution | KDP Select (Kindle+) for the ebooks, with 90-day exclusivity [primary] |

**What a reader is worth** [inference]: about 200 words fill one Kindle+ page, at about $0.0047 a page. A full Kindle+ read of a 60,000-word novel pays about **$1.41**. A $3.99 sale pays about **$2.75**. The business depends on readers carrying on through the series, so read-through, the share of readers who buy or borrow the next book, is the number to watch.

## 3. How the work gets done each month

### The time budget

Each partner works about 20 hours a week, so the team has **about 173 hours a month**. Estimated hours per 60,000-word novel [inference; editing speeds from the Editorial Freelancers Association, drafting at 800-1,200 human words an hour]:

| Step | Who | AI's role | Hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plot outline and scene list | Both | Brainstorming, plot-hole checks | 8 |
| Human drafting | A, with B drafting some chapters | Suggestions on stuck scenes, research | 50-75 |
| Structural and line revision | B | Critique, continuity check against the series bible | 25-48 |
| Proofread, after an AI grammar pass | Both, on each other's chapters | First pass | 16-24 |
| Formatting, cover, blurb, keywords, upload | B | Blurb draft, cover image if the AI route is used | 6 |
| Marketing, through the month | Both | Copy drafts | 17 |
| **Total** | | | **About 120-180 hours** |

At the slow end the month is full, with no slack. Three safeguards keep the schedule safe:

1. **Three finished books before the first release.** They absorb a slipped month.
2. **A staggered pipeline.** In any month, A drafts book N+1, B revises book N, and one book goes live. Each book spends about two months in the pipeline.
3. **Shorter books as the release valve.** If a book runs long, cut it to 55,000 words rather than skip a month. Only bring in a paid proofreader once revenue can carry it. At 1.2-2.0 US cents a word, that costs $720-1,200 per novel [primary, EFA rates].

### Roles

- **Partner A, story lead:** series bible, arc and book outlines, main drafting.
- **Partner B, editor and publisher:** revision, continuity, formatting, covers, metadata, KDP operations, newsletter and ads. B also drafts some chapters.
- Both proofread each other's chapters, and both are responsible for the book being good.

Swap roles for one arc a year. That spreads the skills and limits burnout.

### Where AI fits, and where it stops

To keep a book "AI-assisted" under KDP's definition [primary]:

- **AI may:** brainstorm, outline, suggest options, critique drafts, check continuity, check grammar and spelling, research and draft marketing copy.
- **AI may not:** write the book's prose. AI-generated text stays AI-generated even after heavy editing, so pasting AI paragraphs and rewriting them would require disclosure.
- **Covers:** an AI-generated cover image must be disclosed. Either accept that disclosure, which is only for the image, or use human-made covers.

Keep a short log per book of what AI did. If KDP ever questions a book, that log is the evidence.

### Quality gates for every book

A book goes out only if it passes all four:

1. Both partners have read every line, and both would sign it.
2. It tells a complete case with a satisfying ending, while the arc thread continues.
3. The series bible has been updated, and the continuity check is clean.
4. The cover, title and description refer to no other authors or books [primary].

## 5. Publishing operations

- **Account.** One KDP account for the business. KDP's terms allow Amazon to bar people from opening new accounts after a termination. Ask KDP support how two partners should hold one account before setting it up [unverified].
- **Release.** Publish on the same day each month. Put the next book on pre-order at each release, so every book links forward, and keep the Amazon series page complete.
- **Disclosure.** Answer "no AI-generated text". Answer "AI-generated image" if the cover uses AI.
- **KDP Select.** Enrol each ebook. Use the five free-promotion days or a Kindle Countdown Deal per 90-day term on book 1. Never reward readers for reading in Kindle+, which Amazon bans [primary].
- **Paperbacks.** Publish one with every novel. Each one adds an extra product page and royalty, and it can later go to bookshops through IngramSpark, which accepts AI-assisted books.

## 6. Marketing

Paid marketing works only once there is a series to read through, so it follows the catalogue.

### Months 1-3, before launch

- Build the author website and the newsletter.
- Write a short **reader magnet**, a free prequel story offered through BookFunnel and sold nowhere else.
- Link the back of every book to the **website's home page** and to social media profiles, never directly to a sign-up form. KDP prohibits links to web forms that collect email addresses, and explicitly allows links to social media and to the rest of the series [primary]. Ask KDP support to confirm the home-page link before the first upload.
- Recruit 20-40 advance readers through genre communities and BookFunnel. They get free copies with no conditions, which Amazon allows. They must never be friends or family, and must never be asked for a positive review [primary].

### Month 4, launch

Publish books 1-3 together. Email the list and the advance readers. Run free days or a Countdown Deal on book 1 and list it on genre promotion sites such as Freebooksy, at roughly $50-150 per listing [unverified].

### Every month after launch

- One release with a pre-order for the next book.
- Two newsletters: a release email and a story or behind-the-scenes email.
- Three to five social posts a week showing setting, characters and tone.
- A monthly newsletter swap with authors in the same genre.

Human-written books are an advantage in a market wary of AI. State it plainly and truthfully, for example "written by two people, not a machine". Don't overclaim if AI covers are used.

### From month 6, with four or more books live

Test Amazon ads on book 1 only. The break-even rule is maximum cost per click equals royalty × conversion rate. At a $2.75 royalty and a 10% conversion rate, that is about $0.28 per click [inference]. Book-1 ads can run a little above break-even only if measured read-through pays for it. Start at $5 a day and stop any campaign that cannot show read-through.

### From about month 9

Apply for a BookBub Featured Deal on book 1 or the first collection. A US-only genre deal costs about $290-880 [primary]. BookBub accepts roughly 10-25% of submissions [primary], so expect rejections and reapply.

### Revenue [inference]

The model assumes books 1-3 launch in month 4, then one a month. That gives 11 books live by month 12 and 23 by month 24. Each live title earns a flat amount per month from the month after release. Collections and paperbacks add 20% on top.

The amounts per title are assumptions anchored to survey data. Authors with about 10 books average under $100 a month in total, or about $10 a title. Authors with about 26 books average $500-999, or about $20-40 a title [primary, Written Word Media 2025].

| Scenario | Per live title per month | Month 12 run-rate | Year 1 revenue | Month 24 run-rate | Year 2 revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | $8 | About $100 | About $500 | About $210 | About $1,900 |
| Base | $25 | About $300 | About $1,600 | About $660 | About $5,900 |
| High | $75 | About $900 | About $4,700 | About $2,000 | About $17,800 |

Year one loses money in every scenario except the high one. The base case covers ongoing costs during year two. The value builds in the catalogue: each book keeps earning, and German translations can later sell the same series again.

## 9. Checkpoints

| When | Measure | Continue if | Otherwise |
|---|---|---|---|
| Month 4, before launch | Hours per book, quality | Book 3 took 180 hours or less, and both partners would sign it | Lengthen the cycle to six weeks, or cut books to 50,000-55,000 words |
| Month 7, about 6 books live | Read-through from book 1 to 2 and from 2 to 3. Average rating. Kindle+ pages per day | Book 1 to 2 at 40% or more. Rating at 4.0 or more. Revenue rising each month | Rework book 1's opening, cover and blurb. If nobody reads past book 1, change the series concept |
| Month 12, 11 books live | Run-rate, newsletter size | Run-rate of $250 a month or more and rising. Newsletter growing every month | Cut costs to the minimum. Rethink the niche with the category data gathered so far |
| Month 24, 23 books live | Run-rate against hours worked | Run-rate of $600 a month or more and rising | Stop new production, keep the catalogue on sale and reassess |

The read-through targets are planning assumptions, not sourced benchmarks [inference].

## 10. Risks

| Risk | Why it matters | Counter-measure |
|---|---|---|
| Slow start | Little income for about 18 months | Plan for it. Measure read-through, not early revenue |
| Burnout | A novel a month, every month, beside day jobs | The three-book buffer, role swaps, and permission to stretch to six weeks |
| AI drifting into the prose | Under deadline it is tempting to paste AI paragraphs, which turns the book into "AI-generated" and changes disclosure and copyright | A clear rule and a log per book |
| KDP account loss | Amazon can withhold royalties and bar new accounts [primary] | Clean traffic, no incentives in Kindle+, honest metadata |
| Amazon dependence | Exclusive ebooks, one account | Newsletter as the direct line to readers. Paperbacks and later German editions sold beyond Amazon |
| Crowding | AI-heavy books flood every genre | Human authorship as a selling point. The fantasy and mystery niche, where the no-AI premium is strongest |
| Courses and services | The FTC made Publishing.com pay $1.5M over false earnings claims [primary] | Buy no courses or "done for you" services |

## 11. German setup

- **Partnership.** Two people running a business together usually form a German civil partnership (GbR). Put the split of costs, revenue, rights to the pen name and the books, and the exit terms in writing [unverified; confirm with the Steuerberater].
- **Tax.** Writing is a named freelance activity under §18 EStG [primary]. That fits better now that the partners write the prose themselves. The VAT small-business exemption applies up to €25,000 turnover in the prior year and €100,000 in the current year [primary].
- **US tax.** Complete the KDP tax interview with a W-8BEN form. Germany's treaty rate on US royalties is 0% [primary].
- **EU AI Act.** It does not apply to fiction [primary, Commission guidelines].
- **German edition.** New Adult grew 9.6% in Germany in 2025 [primary], but ebooks are only 6.3% of German book revenue. A German edition of a proven series needs print and the Tolino stores, not only Kindle. Human translation costs about €19 per standard page [secondary].

## What this plan rests on

- **The hours estimate is not measured.** No time-logged study was found. The partners' own log for book 1 replaces it.
- **Per-title revenue is assumed.** Kindle+ per-page rates after October 2026 are unknown, because audiobooks now share the fund.
- **The category check could not be automated.** The partners do it by hand in weeks 1-2.
- **Several practical points are unconfirmed.** Box-set rules, the home-page link in books, account set-up for two partners and German tax treatment each need a question to KDP or the Steuerberater before launch.
