# Business plan: one book a week on Amazon KDP

A plan for two German-resident partners, each working about 20 hours a week, to write, publish and market one AI-assisted genre-fiction book a week on Amazon KDP. It builds on the verified report next to this file and on two new research notes from 14 November 2026: `niche_growth.md` and `rapid_release.md` in the notes folder. Evidence labels are the same as in the report: [primary], [secondary], [vendor], [inference] and [unverified].

## The plan in one page

**The weekly target is possible, but only in one shape.** Two part-timers can finish about **20,000-25,000 publishable words a week** with AI drafting and a real human edit [inference, from editing speeds and AI drafting estimates in `rapid_release.md`]. One book a week therefore means a **22,000-28,000-word novella**, not a full novel. The same hours could instead produce a 45,000-50,000-word book every two weeks. That longer format has better evidence behind it. This plan follows the weekly target and includes a checkpoint to switch to the longer format without changing the workload.

**The product** is one long-running series of short, self-contained novellas. Each novella solves one case or adventure, and a story arc runs across every four books. Each completed arc is then sold again as a collected edition. The recommended genre is **cozy paranormal or cozy fantasy mystery**, written in English for the US market first. Science-fiction adventure is the runner-up.

**Three trade-offs come with a weekly pace:**

- **Disclosure.** Drafting a book a week requires AI to write the first draft. KDP counts that text as "AI-generated" even after heavy editing, so every book must be disclosed as such [primary].
- **Copyright.** Only the human-written and human-revised parts of the text are protected [primary].
- **Earnings per title.** AI-heavy books earn less per title on average, and short books earn less per Kindle+ reader than long ones [primary; inference].

**The money** is modest in year one. About 44 novellas would be live by month 12. A base case earns about $3,700 in year one against about $4,000 of costs, reaching a run-rate of about $700 a month by month 12 [inference]. The low case is about $200 a month and the high case about $2,000. Nothing found supports exponential growth in demand. The only exponential curve in the data is the number of books competing for readers.

**The checkpoints** are at weeks 20, 30 and 52. Each one has clear numbers that decide whether to continue, change format or stop.

## 1. Where the growth is

The research looked for niches with near-exponential growth and found none. **What grows exponentially is supply.** The self-published genre-fiction catalogue grew 38x from early 2023 to early 2026, while revenue grew 8.9x (Chakrabarty et al., arXiv 2607.20349) [primary]. The Kindle Unlimited fund, now Kindle+, grows about 10% a year [secondary, Written Word Media's monthly figures]. A new series wins by taking readers from other books, not by riding a wave.

| Niche | Growth evidence | Direction | AI crowding | Fit for weekly novellas |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Science fiction: adventure, dystopian, space exploration** | US print +22.1% in 2025, the largest adult-fiction gain [secondary, PW citing Circana]. K-lytics reports Kindle sci-fi adventure +49% and dystopian +45% over 12 months [vendor] | **Accelerating** | Rising fastest in top-25 slots. Avoid sci-fi romance and space marines, which are falling | Medium. Readers expect longer books |
| **Cozy fantasy** | K-lytics: best sales-to-competition ratio of 64 SF&F sub-markets, 23.1 [vendor] | Rising, from one snapshot | Low competition, but small absolute demand | Good. Episodic and gentle |
| **Fantasy, all** | The only genre cluster where books without AI earned *more* per title, +35% [primary] | Mixed: print −8.7%, Kindle niches up | Lowest AI share so far | Good, if cozy |
| **LitRPG and progression fantasy** | K-lytics ratio 14.8. A breakout series and mainstream publishing deals in 2026 [vendor; secondary] | Rising | Medium | **Poor.** Readers expect 100,000+ words |
| **Mystery and thriller** | Rising share of Kindle lists [secondary]. Techno-thriller +49% and PI mystery +43% [vendor] | Rising | One of the steepest AI rises | **Good for mystery.** Self-contained cases suit episodes |
| **Dark romance, dark themes** | 2025: psychological thriller +29%, dark fantasy +23%, horror +13% [primary, Circana]. No 2026 figures | Grew in 2025, unknown since | Crime and suspense romance is the most AI-crowded cluster | Good format fit. Carries content-policy risk |
| **Romance and romantasy, all** | Still the largest pool. US print +3.9% in 2025, then −25.4% in Q1 2026 [secondary] | **Plateauing** | The most crowded of all | Good format fit, but you start against the biggest crowd |
| **Germany: New Adult** | Revenue +9.6% in 2025 against −2.7% for the whole market [primary, Börsenverein] | Slowing in 2026 | Big publisher pipelines | Weak for KDP. Ebooks are only 6.3% of German book revenue |
| **Christian fiction** | US religious publishers +9.3% in 2025 [primary, AAP]. K-lytics: Christian fantasy +36% [vendor] | Rising | Little evidence either way | Medium. Readers are sensitive to authenticity |
| **Manga, activity books, self-help** | Manga +45% in Q1 2026 [primary]. Self-help −26% | Mixed | Activity and self-help are the most AI-flooded | **Avoid** |

No figures were found for monster, why-choose or mafia romance, paranormal women's fiction or short "spicy" novellas. Claims that those are exploding are hype until shown otherwise. Most Kindle subgenre numbers come from K-lytics through an affiliate site, because K-lytics' own reports are paywalled.

### The recommended niche: cozy paranormal or cozy fantasy mystery

This niche combines four supported signals [inference]:

- cozy fantasy's strong demand relative to competition;
- fantasy's premium for books without AI, which rewards a clearly human voice;
- mystery's rising share of the Kindle lists;
- a format where self-contained short cases are normal.

Typical examples include a witch who runs a tea shop and solves village murders, or a retired adventurer whose inn draws trouble. The tone is gentle and humorous, with low violence and recurring characters.

**Runner-up: science-fiction adventure in episodic form**, for example a salvage crew taking one job per book. It has the strongest growth data, but readers expect longer books and AI books are moving into its top slots fastest.

**Not recommended for this plan:** romance and romantasy, which are the most crowded, and LitRPG, which needs much longer books.

**The partners must actually read the chosen genre.** Readers in every genre punish books that miss its conventions, and no data can replace that. The plan's first two weeks include a hands-on check of the category, described in section 7.

## 2. The product

| Element | Decision |
|---|---|
| Unit | Novella of **22,000-28,000 words**, about 110-140 Kindle pages |
| Structure | One case or adventure per book. A four-book arc with a light running mystery. Recurring cast and setting |
| Series | One series under one shared pen name. At a weekly pace, a second series would split the readership too early |
| Collected editions | After each arc, a four-book collection of about 100,000 words as ebook and paperback. KDP has no statement on box sets. Publish one collection per completed arc and no other repackaging, because KDP bans "excessively reused" content [primary rule; inference on its application] |
| Language | English for the US, UK and other English-language stores first. German later, via Kindle Translate once a series has proven itself. That service is invite-only and its translations carry a public label |
| Pricing | Book 1: $0.99. Books 2 and on: $2.99, which earns the 70% royalty. Collections: $6.99-$8.99, also at 70% |
| Distribution | KDP Select (Kindle+) for the ebooks, with 90-day exclusivity. Paperbacks of the collections only, because single novellas are too thin to sell well in print |

**What a reader is worth** [inference]: about 200 words fill one Kindle+ page, at about $0.0047 a page. A full Kindle+ read of a 25,000-word novella pays about **$0.59**. A $2.99 sale pays about **$2.09**. The business depends on readers carrying on through the series, so read-through, the share of readers who buy or borrow the next book, is the number to watch.

## 3. How the work gets done each week

### The time budget

Each partner works about 20 hours, so the team has **about 40 hours a week**. Estimated hours per 25,000-word novella [inference, scaled from `rapid_release.md`]:

| Step | Who | AI's role | Hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plot outline from the series plan | A | Brainstorming and options | 2 |
| AI draft by scene, then human rewrite into the series voice | A | Writes the first draft | 12-16 |
| Structural and line revision | B | Critique, continuity check against the series bible | 8-10 |
| Proofread, after an AI grammar pass | A and B, each other's chapters | First pass | 5-6 |
| Formatting in Atticus | B | None | 1 |
| Cover from the series template | B | Image generation | 1-2 |
| Blurb, keywords, categories, upload | B | Drafts the blurb | 1-2 |
| Marketing: newsletter, social, ads, promotions | A and B | Copy drafts | 4-5 |
| **Total** | | | **About 34-44 hours** |

The weekly pace fits only at the low end of the estimate. Any extra revision, illness or holiday pushes it over. Three safeguards keep it on track:

1. **A buffer of six finished novellas before the first release.** It absorbs slipped weeks.
2. **A staggered pipeline.** In any week, Partner A drafts book N+2, Partner B revises book N+1, and book N goes live. Each book gets three weeks in the pipeline, while one comes out each week.
3. **A paid proofreader as the release valve.** A freelance proofread costs 1.2-2.0 US cents a word, or about $300-500 per novella [primary, Editorial Freelancers Association rates]. It is only worth it once revenue can carry it.

### Roles

- **Partner A, story lead:** series bible, arc and book outlines, AI-assisted drafting and the rewrite into the series voice. The voice is what makes the books human-authored and protectable.
- **Partner B, editor and publisher:** revision, continuity, formatting, covers, metadata, KDP operations, newsletter and ads.
- Both proofread each other's chapters, and both are responsible for the book being good.

Swap roles for one arc every few months. That spreads the skills and limits burnout, which is the most common failure in rapid-release stories [secondary].

### Quality gates for every book

A book goes out only if it passes all five:

1. It contains no scenes, descriptions or phrases reused from earlier books, checked by AI comparison against the full manuscript archive. KDP bans content that is "excessively reused … across books" and content "not significantly different" from another book [primary].
2. It tells a complete story with a satisfying ending. Cliffhanger complaints in reviews hurt short Kindle Unlimited books [secondary].
3. Both partners have read every line.
4. The KDP disclosure is answered honestly: AI-generated text, and an AI-generated cover if one is used.
5. Nothing on the cover, title or description refers to other authors or books [primary].

## 5. Publishing operations

- **Account.** One KDP account for the business. KDP's terms allow Amazon to bar people from opening new accounts after a termination. Ask KDP support how two partners should hold one account before setting it up [unverified].
- **Release day.** Publish at the same time every week. Use pre-orders for the next book so each release links forward, and keep the Amazon series page complete.
- **The limit.** KDP allows 2 new titles per format per week, so one novella plus an occasional collection fits [primary].
- **Disclosure.** Answer "AI-generated" for text and for AI images. Keep a short internal log per book of what AI did and what the partners did. If KDP ever questions a book, that log is the evidence.
- **KDP Select.** Enrol each ebook. Use the five free-promotion days per 90-day term for book 1 of each arc. Never offer readers rewards for reading in Kindle+, which Amazon bans [primary].

## 6. Marketing

The order matters. Paid marketing works only once there is a series to read through.

### Weeks 1-10, before the first release

- Build the author website and the newsletter.
- Write a short **reader magnet**, a free prequel story offered through BookFunnel and sold nowhere else.
- Put a link in the back of every book to the **website's home page** and to social media profiles, never directly to a sign-up form. KDP prohibits links to web forms that collect email addresses. KDP explicitly allows links to social media and to the rest of the series [primary]. Ask KDP support to confirm the home-page link before the first upload.
- Recruit about 20-40 advance readers through genre communities and BookFunnel. They get free copies with no condition attached, which Amazon allows. They must never be friends or family, and must never be asked for a positive review [primary].

### Week 11, launch

Publish books 1-3 together. Send the newsletter and alert the advance readers. Run free-promotion days on book 1 and list it on paid promotion sites in the genre, such as Freebooksy, at roughly $50-150 per listing [unverified].

### Every week after launch

- One new release, one newsletter and three to five social posts showing setting, characters and tone.
- Do not hide AI use. Readers who discover hidden AI react badly. The disclosure on Amazon is internal, so decide openly how to talk about it in public.
- Join newsletter swaps with authors in the same genre each month.

### From about week 20

Once eight or more books are live, test Amazon ads on book 1 only. Use the break-even rule: maximum cost per click equals royalty × conversion rate. At a $2.09 royalty and a 10% conversion rate, that is about $0.21 per click [inference]. Book-1 ads can run somewhat above break-even only if measured read-through pays for it. Start at $5 a day and stop any campaign that cannot show read-through.

### From about month 9

Apply for a BookBub Featured Deal on the first collection, at about $290-880 for a US-only deal [primary]. BookBub accepts roughly 10-25% of submissions [primary], so expect rejections.

Without ads or promotion sites, year one costs about $2,000.

### Revenue, first year [inference]

The model assumes three books at launch in week 11, then one a week. That gives about 5 books live by month 3, 22 by month 7 and 44 by month 12. Each live title earns a flat amount per month from the month after release, and collections add 25% on top. The amounts per title come from the evidence that most short-catalogue authors earn very little and AI-heavy books average about 250 copies each (`rapid_release.md`). They are assumptions, not data.

| Scenario | Earnings per live novella per month | Month 12 run-rate | Year 1 revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | $4 | About $200 | About $1,000 |
| Base | $15 | About $730 | About $3,700 |
| High | $40 | About $1,950 | About $9,900 |

The base case roughly breaks even in year one. The low case loses about $2,000-3,000. Year two decides the business. At the base rate, a catalogue of 90 novellas would earn about $1,700 a month by the end of year two, if per-title earnings hold [inference]. The research gives no reason to expect them to rise on their own. The survey data says income grows with catalogue size, and the AI study says revenue per title is falling.

## 9. Checkpoints

| When | Measure | Continue if | Otherwise |
|---|---|---|---|
| Week 10 | Hours per novella, quality | 40 hours or less, both partners would sign it | Switch to 45,000-50,000 words every two weeks, with the same hours |
| Week 20, about 10 books live | Read-through from book 1 to 2 and from 2 to 3. Average rating. Kindle+ pages per day | Book 1 to 2 at 35% or more. Rating at 4.0 or more. Revenue rising each month | Fix the opening book and blurb. If reviews complain about length, move to the longer format. If nobody reads past book 1, change niche |
| Week 30 | Monthly revenue against costs. Burnout | Revenue covers monthly costs | Cut ads and tools to the minimum. Consider the two-week schedule |
| Week 52 | Run-rate, newsletter size, read-through across arcs | Run-rate of $500 a month or more and still rising | Stop new production, keep the catalogue on sale and reassess |

The read-through targets are planning assumptions, not sourced benchmarks [inference]. Stop the weekly pace at once if KDP sends a content-quality warning. Fix every flagged issue before the next release.

## 10. Risks

| Risk | Why it matters | Counter-measure |
|---|---|---|
| KDP quality enforcement | Templated weekly AI novellas are exactly what "too short", "repetitive" and "not significantly different" target. Account loss can forfeit royalties [primary] | The quality gates. A complete story in each book. No reuse. Honest disclosure. Clean traffic only |
| Lower earnings for AI-heavy books | Disclosed AI-heavy books average about half the revenue per title of books without AI [primary] | A strong human voice in the rewrite, and the fantasy and mystery niche, where the no-AI premium is strongest |
| Reader backlash | 61% of US adults would feel less fulfilled by an AI-written book [primary, YouGov] | Quality and openness, not concealment. Build a newsletter readership that trusts the series |
| Burnout | Sustaining 20 hours a week of work beside day jobs | The six-book buffer, role swaps and the switch to the two-week schedule |
| Weak copyright | AI-drafted text is protected only where humans wrote or reshaped it | Real rewriting. Treat series concepts and characters as the main asset |
| Amazon dependence | Exclusive ebooks, one account, one store. Kobo rejects AI-generated books [primary] | Keep the newsletter as the direct line to readers. Paperbacks of the collections can sell elsewhere |
| Courses and services | The FTC made Publishing.com pay $1.5M over false earnings claims [primary] | Buy no courses or "done for you" services |

## 11. German setup

- **Partnership.** Two people running a business together usually form a German civil partnership (GbR). Put the split of costs, revenue, rights to the pen name and the books, and the exit terms in writing [unverified; confirm with the Steuerberater].
- **Tax.** Writing is a named freelance activity under §18 EStG [primary]. Whether a high-volume AI-assisted operation still counts as writing is a question for the Steuerberater. The VAT small-business exemption applies up to €25,000 turnover in the prior year and €100,000 in the current year [primary].
- **US tax.** Complete the KDP tax interview with a W-8BEN form. Germany's treaty rate on US royalties is 0% [primary].
- **EU AI Act.** It does not apply to fiction [primary, Commission guidelines]. Recheck it only if the partners ever publish non-fiction on public-interest topics.

## What this plan rests on

- **A weekly novella is a choice, not the evidence-based optimum.** Every documented rapid-release success released every two to four weeks with books of 50,000 words or more. One author dropped 30,000-word books as unworkable [secondary]. The week-10 and week-20 checkpoints exist to test the weekly format against that evidence.
- **The hours estimate is not measured.** No time-logged study of AI-assisted fiction writing was found. Your own log for book 1 replaces it.
- **Per-title revenue is assumed.** Kindle+ per-page rates after October 2026 are unknown, because audiobooks now share the fund.
- **The category check could not be automated.** The partners do it by hand in weeks 1-2.
- **Several practical points are unconfirmed.** Box-set rules, the home-page link in books, account set-up for two partners and German tax treatment each need a question to KDP or the Steuerberater before launch.
